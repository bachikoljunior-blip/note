## New failure evidence

### FE-205 — Named game benchmarks can measure semantic lookup rather than strategic computation
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10410

`Equilibrium Residuals Expose Three Regimes of Matrix-Game Strategic Reasoning in Language Models` removes textbook game names and evaluates freshly generated anonymous zero-sum payoff matrices. The base model's success falls to `34%` on 2×2, `18%` on 3×3, and `2%` on 5×5 matrices despite perfect performance on familiar named textbook games in the paper's diagnostic. Adding three solved few-shot examples makes 2×2 performance worse, `34%→10%`.

**Failure implication:** a benchmark containing named or semantically familiar games can overstate algorithmic strategic reasoning by allowing name→solution retrieval. Few-shot examples can increase template matching instead of inducing the intended computation.

**Scope guard:** this is a controlled matrix-game setting with the paper's Qwen3.5-9B base and its output contract. It does not imply every named benchmark is memorized or that few-shot prompting generally harms reasoning.

### FE-206 — Best-of-k selection and output contracts can materially change the apparent level of strategic reasoning
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10410

On the same procedurally generated 5×5 games, SFT reports `61%±9pp` success under best-of-4 oracle exploitability selection but `33%±7pp` pass@1; VERGE reports `37%±22pp` best-of-4 versus `21%±18pp` pass@1. Separately, a zero-shot Qwen3-32B format-stress baseline under the strict JSON-only output contract has only `8%` valid outputs and mean success near `1%` across 2×2–7×7, which the authors explicitly decline to interpret as an isolated reasoning-capacity test.

**Failure implication:** inference-time oracle selection and serialization validity are part of the measured system. A headline best-of-k score can overstate deployment-ready single-sample competence, while a brittle output interface can understate latent reasoning ability.

**Scope guard:** best-of-k is not illegitimate when the deployed system actually samples and verifies multiple candidates; the failure is treating it as interchangeable with pass@1. The Qwen3-32B result is a format-stress diagnostic, not proof that the model lacks strategic reasoning.

### FE-207 — Verifiable-reward training can produce high-transfer checkpoints without stable seed-level evidence
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10410

For VERGE on unseen 5×5 games, the three reported training seeds achieve `58%`, `40%`, and `14%` best-of-4 success. A larger 150-game replication gives VERGE `0.42±0.18` at 5×5 and `0.16±0.15` at 7×7, versus SFT `0.63±0.01` and `0.28±0.05`. The authors therefore frame exploitability reward as a diagnostic mechanism rather than evidence of stable mean superiority.

**Failure implication:** selecting a favorable verifier-RL checkpoint can create a strong-looking transfer result that is not representative of the training recipe's expected behavior. Multi-seed reporting is necessary before promoting a high-transfer checkpoint to a general training claim.

**Positive control / scope guard:** VERGE still learns nontrivial approximate computation and outperforms the base model in this setting. The negative evidence is specifically about seed stability and overinterpreting a selected checkpoint, not about verifier rewards being ineffective.

### FE-208 — Tool-schema representation can dominate measured tool-use capability
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04107

TSCG-Agentic-Bench holds the model/task/tool catalog fixed while changing how schemas are represented. For Mistral 7B at 20 tools, the natural JSON-style baseline is `35.0%` accuracy while a conservative deterministic representation is `80.0%`; at 50 tools it is `30.0%→75.3%`. Gemma 3 4B at 50 tools moves `24.3%→87.5%`. The paper's decomposition shows that for several small models the large JSON-baseline gain is primarily a **format effect**, not compression itself; against text baselines the gain can vanish or reverse.

**Failure implication:** a tool-use benchmark can mistake protocol/serialization compatibility for model capability. Comparing models or agents under only one schema representation can create artificial capability cliffs, and attributing a JSON→compressed improvement to token reduction alone can be wrong.

**Scope guard:** this is the paper's tool-schema benchmark and deterministic compiler. It does not show JSON is always inferior; frontier models exhibit different format responses, and the paper reports native function-calling advantages in some cells.

### FE-209 — A single 'optimized' tool-schema policy can invert across models; more aggressive compression can actively harm a well-adapted model
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04107

At 20 tools, Qwen3 14B's natural accuracy is `90.2%`. Conservative TSCG raises it to `99.3%` (`+9.1pp`), while balanced TSCG lowers it to `84.1%` (`−6.1pp`). Parameter F1 shows the same split: conservative `98.3%` versus balanced `81.0%`. At 10/20/50 tools the balanced profile is below the natural baseline by `8.7/6.1/5.0pp`, while conservative is neutral-to-positive. The paper explicitly identifies model-specific operator-response profiles rather than a universal-best configuration.

**Failure implication:** an infrastructure optimization validated on one model/version can become a regression on another, even within the same general tool protocol. Tool-schema transformations therefore need per-model/version regression tests rather than hard-coded global policy.

**Scope guard:** this does not imply conservative compression is universally best; other models benefit from the balanced profile. The evidence is for configuration non-universality and model-dependent interaction effects.

### FE-210 — Continuous LLM-judge monitoring can false-alarm catastrophically if repeated fixed-alpha tests are treated as anytime-valid
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15474

`Who Drifted: the System or the Judge?` studies continuous evaluation where the reference judge itself can change. Re-running a rolling z-test at fixed `α=0.05` on a drift-free stream false-alarms on `75%` of streams; `16/60` of its detections fire before the injected change point even exists. A fixed human-labelled anchor/e-process design instead detects a real silent judge version bump as judge drift in `60/60` runs with zero judge→system misattribution at the featured configuration, and correctly attributes a strict-prompt judge change in `110/120` contaminated runs at guard width 300.

**Failure implication:** repeated classical significance tests and unpinned LLM judges can make a healthy system look degraded, or make judge changes look like product changes. Continuous eval needs time-uniform error control plus an independent anchor for evaluator drift.

**Scope guard:** this is one monitoring construction on HelpSteer2 and TL;DR with two concrete judge changes. It does not establish that every rolling monitor false-alarms at 75%, nor that the proposed anchor settings are universally optimal.
