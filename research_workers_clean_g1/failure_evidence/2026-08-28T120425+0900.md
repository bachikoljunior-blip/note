## New failure evidence

### FE-337 — Full-trace process judges can shift blame toward later evidence simply because later steps and the outcome are visible, not because those steps caused the result
**Primary source:** “What Process Evaluation of Coding Agents Actually Measures: Action, Task, and Step Are Three Different Levels,” arXiv:2608.22960v1, 24 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22960

The paper holds the judge model and rubric fixed and changes only its information set: a prefix-only judge sees a pre-fixed 60% trajectory prefix with the outcome withheld, while the full-trace judge sees the complete trajectory and realized outcome. On the controlled common subset, making downstream steps visible moves the original judge’s selected attribution later by mean normalized displacement **+0.533 [95% CI +0.399,+0.656]**, with **19/23** episodes moving later (`p=4e-6`). Two additional judges reproduce positive displacement: **+0.434** and **+0.394**, with no detected between-judge heterogeneity (`p=0.181`). Across the larger reported contrast, mean selected position moves from **0.214** under prefix-only to **0.751** under full-trace, with median displacement **+0.667**.

The same full-trace judge puts **0.900 [0.829,0.971]** of top-1 verdicts on steps that touch or reveal a gold file, even though only 32.9% of verdicts fall inside the replay estimator’s directly comparable window set. The authors interpret the controlled information-set displacement as collider/post-treatment bias: full-trace judging can look informative while selecting semantically salient late evidence rather than identifying a certified step-local causal contribution.

The study also reports an important limitation/negative result for its own causal estimator: on the file-localization traces, the identifiable step-local effects do not localize reliably at feasible replay cost, and a pre-execution endpoint forecast has leave-one-repository-out AUROC **0.481**. Thus the result is not “their causal scorer solves attribution”; it is evidence that common process scores answer different estimands and must not be conflated.

**Failure implication:** root-cause/debugging systems should not treat a full-trace LLM judge’s selected step as causal ground truth merely because it has access to more evidence. Later observations and the final outcome can systematically pull attribution toward downstream semantic relevance. Counterfactual repair or prefix-conditioned replay should verify any proposed root cause before automated rollback or optimizer changes.

**Scope guard:** 499 file-localization episodes from 12 repositories overall; the controlled attribution cross-judge comparison is only 23 common episodes, with three judge models and fixed prompts. The paper does not establish that all full-trace judges are biased by the same magnitude, nor that causal localization is generally impossible.
