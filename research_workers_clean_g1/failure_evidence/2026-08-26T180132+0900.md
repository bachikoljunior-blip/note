### FE-109 — Random latent perturbation can substantially understate representation-space vulnerability; gradient-directed probing changes the measured result by up to +0.41 PASR
**Status:** peer-reviewed ACL 2026 long paper; controlled comparison of random vs gradient-based Activation Steering Attack (ASA) on four open-weight model variants.

Primary source:
- Gu et al., *Probing the Safety Robustness of LLMs in Latent Space*, ACL 2026, DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.967: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.967/

Evidence:
- Table 1 compares `ASA_random` and `ASA_grad` under the same model family/evaluation framework. PASR changes are Qwen-2.5-7B Base **0.55→0.73 (+0.18)**, Qwen-2.5-7B Instruct **0.45→0.74 (+0.29)**, Llama-3.1-8B Base **0.69→0.64 (-0.05)**, and Llama-3.1-8B Instruct **0.41→0.82 (+0.41)**.
- MASR also rises in 3/4 cases and is unchanged in the remaining Llama-Base case.

Failure implication: a latent-safety audit that samples only random directions can materially overestimate robustness. Representation-space evaluation should include an optimized or otherwise adversarial direction search, not just isotropic/random perturbations.

Scope guard: the stronger gradient attack is not uniformly stronger on every metric/model (Llama-3.1-8B Base PASR falls by 0.05), and the study uses its own ASA objective/evaluator. This does not establish a universal +0.41 gap for other defenses or hidden-state attacks.

### FE-110 — Layer-targeted robustness training can move the vulnerability peak rather than eliminate it; post-defense evaluation must rescan the full layer hierarchy
**Status:** peer-reviewed ACL 2026; matched before/after Layer-wise Adversarial Patch Training (LAPT) with held-out ASABench split.

Primary source:
- Gu et al., ACL 2026: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.967/

Evidence:
- The paper reports that after LAPT the most vulnerable layer relocates, e.g. **Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct L14→L19** while average PASR falls **0.29→0.09**.
- Other reported peak moves include Qwen-2.5-7B-Base **L27→L14** with average PASR **0.42→0.23**, Llama-3.1-8B-Base **L23→L15** with **0.40→0.19**, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct **L17→L14** with **0.35→0.28**.
- Capability changes are not identically zero: for Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct GSM8K changes **0.91→0.87** while CommonsenseQA remains **0.84**; Llama-3.1-8B-Base GSM8K rises **0.41→0.50** while CQA falls **0.68→0.66**.

Failure implication: testing only the layer that was vulnerable before hardening can produce a false sense of closure. A defense can flatten one region while the worst-case layer changes identity, so best-response latent evaluation should re-search layer as part of the attack.

Scope guard: LAPT does produce broad robustness gains in this study; this claim is not that training failed, only that the attack surface is non-stationary and layer-local acceptance tests are insufficient.

### FE-111 — Prompt-time activation defenses can have a structural prefilling blind spot despite near-perfect results on semantic jailbreak families
**Status:** June-2026 arXiv preprint with released code/data; seven 7B–31B instruction-tuned models and five attack families.

Primary source:
- Mitra, *Closing the Activation-Cone Blind Spot: Response-Time Probing and Unified Defense*, arXiv:2606.29441: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29441

Evidence:
- AlphaSteer records **0% ASR** on GCG, AutoDAN, and intent laundering in the reported comparison but **50% ASR** on canonical prefilling attacks.
- The paper argues that defenses which gate intervention from prompt-time activation alignment can accept a prompt before the harmful continuation exists in the observed state.
- Positive control: a linear probe on the first generated-token hidden states reaches **AUROC 0.97–1.00** across the seven models; probe+halt reduces canonical prefilling success to **0/40 on every model** with **0% benign false positives** in that test set. The combined response-halt + AlphaSteer reaches reported defense success **0.983 on Mistral** and **0.994 on Llama**.

Failure implication: a defense×attack diagonal can look excellent while an orthogonal timing channel remains open. Evaluation must vary *when* the defender observes evidence, not only which prompt attack family is used.

Scope guard: this is a preprint; the strongest zero-ASR result is scoped to the canonical prefilling-template family, and the authors explicitly report that cross-template generalization depends on probe depth. It is not a claim that every response-time probe generalizes to arbitrary prefilling.

### FE-112 — Shared steering artifacts create a supply-chain attack surface that benign functional checks and re-derivation can fail to detect
**Status:** June-2026 arXiv preprint with public code; two open-weight model families, eight model×attribute combinations, three seeds reported by the authors.

Primary sources:
- Aidakhmetov et al., *Steering Vectors are an Adversarial Attack Surface*, arXiv:2606.05958: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05958
- Public code: https://github.com/AbzalAidakhmetov/adversarial_attack

Evidence:
- Replacing only **4–6% of tokens** in the contrastive steering dataset can rotate the derived vector toward an anti-refusal direction.
- Poisoned vectors reach absolute **ASR 20–55%**, **+19 to +51 percentage points** over the clean reference, while the intended benign steering effect remains essentially unchanged on seven of eight reported combinations.
- Because the user can re-derive the same poisoned vector from the supplied texts, an equivalence check between dataset and vector does not prove safety.
- Positive control: refusal-direction orthogonalization recovers about **82% of the ASR gap** without reported benign-behavior harm in the tested setup.

Failure implication: safety provenance for activation-control artifacts must include adversarial safety tests and provenance of the source contrastive data; cryptographic/equivalence verification plus benign functional testing is insufficient against semantically plausible poisoning.

Scope guard: this is a specific supply-chain attacker who can modify steering data; it does not apply to steering vectors generated entirely from trusted local data, nor prove the same poisoning rate on all model families or attributes.

### FE-113 — Within a fixed multi-agent topology, forcing defenders to share weights modestly but consistently increases measured vulnerability
**Status:** AdvEvo-MARL parameter-sharing ablation; same 1-attacker/3-defender setting, varying whether defender weights are shared.

Primary source:
- Pan et al., *AdvEvo-MARL: Shaping Internalized Safety through Adversarial Co-Evolution in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning*, arXiv:2510.01586 / ICML 2026 project: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01586

Evidence:
- The appendix states that under the **exact same 1 Attacker vs. 3 Defenders setting**, making all defenders share parameters increases most ASR/contagion-rate values by roughly **0.1–1.0 percentage point** relative to the non-shared AdvEvo-MARL-3B configuration.
- The direction is consistent with reduced policy heterogeneity, but the magnitude is small.

Failure implication: parameter sharing can reduce heterogeneous coverage even when interaction topology and attacker count are fixed. This is a useful partial control for the broader parameter-coupling question.

Scope guard: this ablation shares parameters among defenders, not specifically between attacker and defender, and the effect size is small. It does not resolve the stronger frontier of a single-factor attacker↔defender parameter-sharing ablation with identical base/LoRA/optimizer/replay/update budget.
