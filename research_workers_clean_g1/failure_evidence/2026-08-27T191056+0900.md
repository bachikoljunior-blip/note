## New failure evidence

### FE-238 — Deleting the poisoned source record can leave the learned malicious skill intact; skill extraction can also make the same payload much harder to detect
**Primary source:**
- Ying et al., “SkillJack: Persistent Skill Backdoors in Self-Evolving Agents,” arXiv:2608.03509: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03509

SkillJack evaluates two experience-to-skill pipelines, SkillX and Anything2Skill, on `150` trajectories across four policy-risk categories. In SkillX, an LLM safety judge flags `98.5%` of the poisoned source trajectories but only `11.4%` of the skills distilled from those same trajectories. Implanted skills remain behaviorally effective, with reported attack success rates of `56.2%` and `89.2%` on the two tested systems. Most directly for deletion semantics, `80.0%` of successful skill-mediated attacks continue to trigger after the original poisoned source records are removed. The paper also reports unintended activation on benign queries.

**Failure implication:** deleting or sanitizing the raw experience after a self-evolution step is not equivalent to revoking derived persistent behavior. Experience→skill transformation can both launder obvious malicious markers and create an independently persistent artifact, so provenance must remain connected across promotion boundaries and revocation must traverse descendants/artifacts rather than only source memory.

**Scope guard:** this is a targeted adversarial poisoning study on two specific self-evolving systems and four policy-risk categories. The `80%` figure is conditional on successfully implanted skill-mediated attacks in the tested attack tasks, not a general persistence rate for all agent skills. The result does not show that skill distillation is inherently unsafe when admission/provenance controls are strong.

### FE-239 — Post-hoc deletion can recover only a small fraction of skill-contamination damage because later skills inherit flawed reasoning from earlier admitted skills
**Primary source:**
- Shang et al., “When Self-Evolution Backfires: Pre-Commit Gating against Skill Contamination in LLM Agents,” arXiv:2608.05810: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05810

On Terminal-Bench 2, unconditional skill accumulation improves at first, peaks at round 3, then loses most of the gain by round 5. The paper attributes this to individual, combinatorial, and cross-round systemic contamination: later skills are distilled while earlier admitted skills are already in the decision context, so flaws can propagate into descendants that no longer name the original source. Source-only rollback recovers only `17%` of the degradation in the reported experiment. In contrast, the Verifier-as-Gatekeeper (VaG) pre-commit design checks each candidate skill with structural, behavioral A/B replay and semantic critics plus joint marginal-gain selection; it improves across all five rounds, reaches `72%` pass@1, and uses a skill pool roughly `5×` smaller. The frozen gated pool also transfers positively to four other backbones and a second benchmark without re-evolution.

**Failure implication:** a “we can delete bad skills later” safety strategy can fail structurally once contaminated artifacts have already influenced later writes. Admission-time gating and lineage/provenance are qualitatively stronger controls than source-only cleanup after promotion.

**Positive control / scope guard:** VaG shows the failure is not evidence against self-evolution as a family. The `17%` recovery and `72%` pass@1 are specific to this Terminal-Bench 2 evolution setup, its distillation process and gate design. Full lineage cleanup is argued to be stronger than source-only rollback but is difficult because typical libraries do not record the complete context lineage; the result does not prove irreversibility for systems with perfect dependency tracking and descendant revocation.
