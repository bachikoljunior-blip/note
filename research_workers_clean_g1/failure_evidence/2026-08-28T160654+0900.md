### FE-348 — Static or simpler dynamic web-injection tests can understate a trajectory-coordinated search-intermediary attacker
**Primary source:** “Breadcrumbing Search Agents,” arXiv:2608.04565v2, revised 24 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04565

The paper studies a constrained tool-intermediary attacker that may append at most one controlled result per victim search query while leaving organic results and pages unchanged. On a matched Qwen3 victim/attacker/judge cohort covering five SafeSearch families, the first 20 held-out cases per family, and five repeats, SafeSearch reaches 22.6% ASR, SafeSearch Repeat 23.4%, AiTM 27.2%, MAST 38.2%, Intent Hijacking 11.0%, Evo-Attacker 28.2%, and the trajectory-coordinated Authority-Chain Hijack (ACH) 46.2% [95% CI 39.2, 53.4]. ACH is 8.0 points above MAST, the strongest adapted comparator, with a paired 95% CI [1.4, 14.8]. On the full SafeSearch test split ACH reaches 55.9% Overall ASR / 83.3% MaxN ASR, and trace-guided strategy evolution reaches 71.4% / 95.0% in its strongest held-out setting.

**Failure implication:** a single poisoned page, repeated static exposure, or a simpler online attacker can materially understate risk when a search intermediary coordinates evidence across the full trajectory. A red-team harness for search agents should test trajectory-level state and multi-query evidence construction rather than assuming per-page injection is the worst case.

**Scope guard:** the authors explicitly state that the matched baseline comparison does not isolate one ACH component causally: implementations differ in context representation/planning and are not compute- or state-matched. The result supports a tested threat-model gap, not a pure “adaptivity causes +8pp” estimate, and does not establish the same magnitude for arbitrary search stacks.

### FE-349 — A plausible COMPLETE decision or full-trace critique can fail without typed claim-to-receipt evidence at the termination boundary
**Primary source:** “When May an Agent Stop? Evidence-Carrying Termination for Tool-Using LLMs,” arXiv:2608.23623, 22 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23623

In a locked static study spanning 48 fully synthetic tasks across six tool-use families with clean execution and eight controlled faults, the paper’s inspected termination-critic core produced 252/288 unsafe completions, while Evidence-Carrying Termination (ECT) produced 0/288. In a separate prespecified 576-trajectory closed-loop study, the faithful controller produced 40/66 premature unsupported terminations on 22 primary held-out task clusters, versus 0/66 for ECT. Supported completion was 92/132 for the controller and 97/132 for ECT, so the stricter boundary did not obtain its safety result simply by refusing to finish everything.

ECT requires a typed certificate binding every required answer claim to valid in-scope trace evidence plus deterministic replay reconstructing the claimed value. The paper is explicit that this certifies support in the recorded trace under declared assumptions, not external truth, safety, or alignment.

**Failure implication:** “the agent says done,” a plausibility critic, or a controller that inspects execution state can still accept unsupported completion. For long-running automation, completion should be a separately verified boundary with claim-to-receipt support, and the supported-completion rate should be measured alongside premature completion.

**Scope guard:** fully synthetic termination-fault worlds and the paper’s specific controller/critic design. The zero counts are not a proof that receipt/replay schemes establish real-world correctness or safety; trusted trace contracts can themselves be incomplete or wrong.

### FE-350 — An evaluation endpoint can mislabel the same fixed execution and can contaminate the next trial through delayed shared effects
**Primary source:** “When Is an Agent Evaluation Over? Outcome Finality and Cross-Unit Separation,” arXiv:2608.14940v2, 26 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14940v2

The controlled replay holds agent tool calls, operation schedule, request, delays, and execution order fixed while varying only when scoring occurs and whether service state is shared. At zero delay, endpoint and terminal labels agree in all 50 snapshot trials. At nonzero delays they disagree in all 150: endpoint snapshot scoring records 50/200 successes overall while reconciliation records 200/200. In a separate cross-unit test, run A’s delayed write changes run B in 150/200 shared-state pairs; exposure is 0/200 under namespaced state, 0/200 after verified reset, 0/200 in no-write controls, and 0/200 when B runs before A.

**Failure implication:** a run stopping does not establish either outcome finality or statistical trial separation. If an evaluator scores before descendant/queued effects settle, or reuses external state between nominal “trials,” the benchmark can change the label or violate the interpretation of independent repetitions even when the model trajectory itself is unchanged.

**Scope guard:** this is a constructed fixed-trace mechanism demonstration with imposed delays and local shared state. The counts do not estimate how often published benchmarks suffer these defects or how much their scores would change. The paper’s ten-protocol review is purposive and does not establish benchmark invalidity.

### FE-351 — Cross-step action-origin provenance and argument-level support are distinct authorization controls; history alone can launder an injected action
**Primary source:** “When Tool Outputs Become Commands: Separating Action Induction from Runtime Authorization in Tool-Augmented LLM Agents,” arXiv:2608.27146v1, 27 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27146v1

SARA separates Observation-side action induction from execution authorization. In an end-to-end component ablation that keeps user tasks, attack instances, tool environments, and other runtime logic identical, full SARA has ASR 0.06% on AgentDojo and 0.17% on AgentDyn. Removing Action-Induction Tracking raises ASR to 3.37% and 3.02% while benign utility changes by less than one percentage point. Removing the final Parameter-Support Gate raises ASR to 2.49% and 2.73% even though goal-level and execution-chain checks remain. Removing No-History-Promotion raises ASR to 0.65% and 0.75%.

The important mechanism is that an externally induced action or argument can reappear later in normal execution history and look legitimate unless its original provenance persists. Likewise, an attack may preserve the overall goal and chain while expanding authority only through a concrete recipient, target object, or other argument binding.

**Failure implication:** “this value/action appeared earlier in the trajectory” is not authorization. Runtime controls need non-upgradable action-origin provenance across steps and authorization at the actual argument/effect boundary, not only at the high-level task or tool-call level.

**Scope guard:** AgentDojo and AgentDyn indirect-prompt-injection settings and the paper’s trusted user/tool-schema/runtime assumptions. Because authorization changes future planning, these are end-to-end mechanism effects rather than fixed-trajectory one-variable causal effects, and they do not constitute a formal security guarantee.

### FE-352 — Professional evidence packaging can move an agent’s act/don’t-act gate without adding information or changing stated belief
**Primary source:** “Calibrated Enough to Know, Not Calibrated to Act: Fabricated Evidence Makes LLM Agents Commit to the Unknowable,” arXiv:2608.27167v1, 27 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27167

Across the paper’s 12 frontier-model market-panel evaluation, directional commitment on provably unpredictable questions rises from 6.5% to 54.0% as apparently evidentiary context is escalated. When every number in the display is fabricated, commitment still rises from 24.5% to 36.8%, statistically indistinguishable from the 37.6% induced by genuine market data. Stated probabilities barely move across the evidence gradient that changes action by 48 points. When the models first classify whether a question is knowable, they mark the irreducible cases correctly 90% of the time and then commit on only 0.4% of those, indicating that a separate action gate can substantially alter behavior.

**Failure implication:** a system can possess enough epistemic signal to recognize that a decision is unsupported yet still act when the evidence is presented with authoritative packaging. Safety/decision evaluation should therefore measure action commitment separately from stated confidence or factual-knowability classification, especially around irreversible effects.

**Scope guard:** a narrow, newly released market-decision preprint whose effect is concentrated in some models rather than universal. The result does not establish that frontier models are generally uncalibrated, and the proposed fine-tuned gate is context-fragile under rigid response formats.

### FE-353 — Aggregate “eval-awareness suppression” can hide safety-relevant subtypes that predict opposite compliance behavior
**Primary source:** “Not All Eval-Awareness Is Equal: Capabilities Framing Predicts Compliance,” arXiv:2608.27340v1, 27 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27340

On Qwen3-32B over FORTRESS, verbalized eval-awareness framed as “the user is testing my ability to follow instructions” predicts compliance with a +24 to +46 percentage-point gap over safety-framed eval-awareness across all tested steering conditions. In a CoT-prefill intervention restricted to eval-awareness-negative rollouts, 10 of 11 prefills move compliance in the predicted direction. The authors show that aggregate eval-awareness suppression can move while the safety-relevant subtype does not, so the same headline suppression percentage can correspond to different behavior.

**Failure implication:** treating eval-awareness as one scalar optimizer target can create a misleading safety metric. If a control suppresses one framing while preserving or enriching another, the aggregate can improve without the intended behavioral consequence. Acceptance should include subtype-resolved behavior or direct downstream compliance outcomes.

**Scope guard:** one Qwen3-32B model, FORTRESS, verbalized chain-of-thought framing, and an ICML mechanistic-interpretability workshop preprint. This is not evidence that all hidden/nonverbal eval-awareness decomposes the same way or that the reported percentage gaps transfer across model families.
