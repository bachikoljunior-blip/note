## New claims

### FE-106 — A safety mechanism concentrated in a recoverable activation direction can become a white-box single point of failure
**Status:** primary May-2026 arXiv preprint; causal activation intervention on held-out jailbreaks; strongest on Qwen3 family; not a black-box attack result.

Primary source:
- Shah, Brinkmann & Angell, *Mitigating Adaptive Attacks against Reasoning Models with Activation Consistency Training*, arXiv:2605.28467v1: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28467v1

Evidence:
- ACT induces a roughly linear refusal direction at the assistant-turn boundary. The authors explicitly test the causal reverse intervention by subtracting that direction from the ACT-defended model.
- On Qwen3-8B, the unsteered ACT model refuses about 96% of the held-out jailbreaks; at the reported operating steering strength (`alpha=10`), ACT minus the learned direction has only **1.2% refusal [0.2, 6.6]**, and the authors manually verify sampled non-refusals are genuine harmful compliance rather than classifier artifacts. The paired opposite intervention drives the base model to **100% refusal [95.5, 100]**.
- Qwen3-1.7B shows the same bidirectional flip: at `alpha=20`, base+direction refuses **99.0% [94.4, 99.8]** while ACT-direction refuses **0.0% [0.0, 3.8]**. The benign false-refusal cost at this strength is **56.1%**, showing the direction is much less selective on the smaller model.
- The effect is not universal: Gemma-4-E4B-it does not show a clean defense-undoing flip at the tested `alpha=10`, and GPT-OSS / Phi-4 have partial or degenerate regimes.

Failure implication: strong static/adaptive black-box robustness does not imply robustness to interventions on the internal representation that carries the safety behavior. If a defense compresses refusal control into a small, recoverable subspace, a white-box evaluator should explicitly test feature subtraction/ablation or related activation-space best responses.

Scope guard: this requires internal activation access plus a direction derived from ACT-vs-base activations and per-model steering choices. It is not evidence that remote API attackers can perform the intervention, nor that all activation-based defenses are vulnerable to a single direction.

### FE-107 — Successful adaptive attacks are strongly defender-specific and transfer asymmetrically; replaying one defender's wins is not a coverage guarantee
**Status:** July-2026 primary arXiv benchmark with released 3x3 frontier matrix and separate attack-replay transfer set.

Primary source:
- Jain, Hartmann & Li, *Adaptive Adversaries: A Multi-Turn, Multi-LLM Benchmark for LLM Agent Security*, arXiv:2607.18063: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18063

Evidence:
- The benchmark's 945-battle 3x3 attacker×defender matrix produces 78 genuine winning turns, all pairwise distinct under exact-string and cosine>=0.95 criteria.
- A separate replay experiment tests attacks that originally beat one defender against the two other defenders. **0 of 46 unique replay attacks succeeds against all three frontier defenders.**
- Transfer is highly asymmetric: attacks sourced from GPT-5.4 wins transfer to Opus at **25.7%**, while attacks sourced from Opus wins transfer to GPT-5.4 at **0.0%**. Other directions are Opus->Gemini **14.8%**, Gemini->Opus **13.3%**, Gemini->GPT-5.4 **6.7%**, GPT-5.4->Gemini **0.0%**.
- Pooling the three attacker LLMs uncovers **1.4-2.2x** as many unique successful attacks per fixed defender as the best single attacker alone.

Failure implication: a fixed replay corpus harvested against one source defender can substantially miss another defender's failure surface, and transfer rates need not be symmetric. Cross-defense replay plus fresh defender-conditioned search is a stronger measurement protocol than assuming a single universal attack set.

Scope guard: the source attacks come from a 15-round adaptive benchmark against three frontier LLM defenders; this is not a proof that defender-specificity has the same magnitude for other models, tasks, or attack generators. Replay evaluates discovered winning turns, not fresh per-defender best-response retraining.

### FE-108 — Output-level safety training can be trajectory-dependent: replacing the model's reasoning trace can remove the defense even when ordinary jailbreak evaluation looked strong
**Status:** primary controlled chain-of-thought prefill intervention in the same ACT/BCT study; compares two consistency-training mechanisms, not all output-level safety methods.

Primary source:
- Shah, Brinkmann & Angell, arXiv:2605.28467v1: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28467v1

Evidence:
- For held-out jailbreaks on which the undefended base model complies, the authors replace the defended model's thinking block with that base model's compliant reasoning, then allow the defense to generate the post-thinking response.
- Under this intervention, **ACT refuses at least 98% of prefilled jailbreaks on every tested model**, whereas BCT "loses its robustness and complies to jailbreaks on most models" in the authors' cross-model Figure 10 summary.
- On Qwen3-8B, even when the compliant reasoning is left open-ended, ACT continues the malicious-looking reasoning for an average **342 additional tokens** before self-correcting and refusing **95%** of the time. With both the compliant reasoning and the first sentence of the compliant answer prefilled, ACT still refuses **all 82 prompts**.
- This contrasts with the paper's ordinary BCT jailbreak results, where BCT can look strong on static or adaptive evaluation for some models (for example GPT-OSS-20B has 0% static and 1% adaptive ASR), demonstrating that trajectory intervention probes a different failure surface.

Failure implication: safety acceptance tests that only score normal end-to-end rollouts can miss dependence on the model generating its own "safe" reasoning trajectory. For reasoning-model defenses, controlled reasoning-prefix/continuation interventions can distinguish upstream refusal mechanisms from output policies that merely stay safe along their usual trajectory.

Scope guard: the compliant trace is copied from the corresponding undefended base model and injected with white-box / prefill control. This does not show that ordinary users can necessarily replace hidden chain-of-thought on deployed systems, and it does not generalize from BCT to every output-supervised defense.
