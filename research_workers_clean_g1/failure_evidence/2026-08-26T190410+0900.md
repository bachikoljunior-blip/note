## New claims

### FE-114 — Mild activation steering layered on top of DPO can make a defended model *less safe than the undefended baseline*; defense composition is non-monotonic in steering strength
**Status:** January-2026 arXiv preprint with released components; matched steering-strength sweep on one Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 stack.

Primary source:
- Thornton, *TRYLOCK: Defense-in-Depth Against LLM Jailbreaks via Layered Preference and Representation Engineering*, arXiv:2601.03300: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03300

Evidence:
- On the same reported attack evaluation, the unmodified baseline is **46.5% ASR** and DPO-only is **39.8% ASR**.
- Adding RepE steering at the intermediate strength `alpha=1.0` raises ASR to **59.4%**, which is worse than both the DPO-only system and the original 46.5% baseline.
- Increasing steering further restores protection: the paper reports `alpha=2.0` at **8.0% ASR**. The full layered system reaches **5.6% ASR** overall.
- The authors explicitly treat the mechanism behind the alpha=1.0 anomaly as hypotheses rather than established causal explanation.

Failure implication: a safety layer that is beneficial at one strength can destructively interact with an existing alignment layer at another strength. Layered defenses therefore need a joint post-composition sweep rather than assuming monotonic safety as control strength increases.

Scope guard: this is one preprint, one Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 stack and its stated attack/judge setup. It does not establish that RepE+DPO is generally harmful; the same study shows strong positive controls at higher alpha and with adaptive steering.

### FE-115 — Partial first-token log-probability exposure can convert a static refusal into a high-bandwidth optimization oracle for black-box jailbreaks
**Status:** peer-reviewed Findings of ACL 2026 paper; black-box attack using first-token output-distribution information.

Primary source:
- Fu et al., *Steering Away from Refusal: A Black-box Jailbreak Method Based on First-Token Distribution*, Findings of ACL 2026, DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.findings-acl.1294: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1294/

Evidence:
- The paper begins from the failure mode that textual-feedback attacks such as PAIR/TAP receive little useful signal when the target repeatedly emits a nearly static refusal.
- Distribution Jailbreak instead uses the first generated token's refusal distribution and optimizes suffixes by maximizing divergence from that reference.
- The authors report **>90% ASR on every tested open-source model** and **>94% ASR on GPT-4.1** under their setup.

Failure implication: an API can appear robust to text-only iterative red-teaming while still exposing a much sharper attack signal through token-level probabilities. Red-team conclusions must therefore specify what side-channel feedback the attacker receives; hiding gradients does not imply a low-information optimization surface.

Scope guard: this does not imply all probability telemetry is unsafe at the same level, or that systems which do not expose usable log-probability information have this exact failure. The claim is limited to the tested Distribution Jailbreak setting and models.

### FE-116 — A nominal jailbreak defense can increase unsafe-query acceptance above the no-defense baseline on most tested models
**Status:** July-2026 arXiv v1 systematic evaluation of 11 defenses on six open models.

Primary source:
- Zhang et al., *When LLM Defenses Backfire: Characterizing Safety, Performance, and Cost Trade-offs*, arXiv:2607.24392: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24392

Evidence:
- Table 3 defines lower unsafe-query acceptance as better and compares each defense with the no-defense baseline on the same 250 unsafe XSTest queries.
- `Self-Paraphrase` is worse than baseline on five of six listed models: DeepSeek **18.09→24.12%**, Gemma **11.06→21.00%**, Llama **7.50→31.50%**, Mistral **28.00→31.16%**, and Qwen **15.00→19.10%**. Phi is the exception, **31.50→30.15%**.
- The authors therefore characterize paraphrase-based defense as the least reliable safety strategy in this grid.

Failure implication: preprocessing or rewriting that is labeled as a defense can preserve or amplify harmful intent rather than neutralize it. A defense should not receive positive credit merely because it transforms the input; it needs a matched no-defense comparison on the same threat set.

Scope guard: this is the paper's particular Self-Paraphrase implementation, six model versions, XSTest unsafe set and GPT-4o-mini response classification. It is not evidence that all paraphrasing defenses worsen safety.

### FE-117 — Strong unsafe-query rejection can coexist with catastrophic benign over-refusal and reasoning collapse; safety-only leaderboards can choose unusable defenses
**Status:** same July-2026 systematic defense study; matched model-defense comparisons across unsafe acceptance, benign refusal, and task accuracy.

Primary source:
- Zhang et al., arXiv:2607.24392: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24392

Evidence:
- On Mistral, `Self-Defend` has **1.00% unsafe-query acceptance**, versus the **28.00% baseline**, making it the best-ranked defense for that model on the unsafe-query table.
- On 1,000 benign OpenAssistant prompts, however, Mistral full-refusal rises from **2.0% baseline to 67.7%** under Self-Defend; on benign XSTest it rises **0.4%→80.8%**.
- On GSM8K, the same Mistral baseline accuracy is **0.55**, while Self-Defend falls to **0.01** (`delta=-0.538` in the table).
- Positive control: other defenses in the same grid preserve some task metrics far better, so the collapse is not an unavoidable consequence of evaluating a defense at all.

Failure implication: minimizing ASR/unsafe acceptance alone can reward a near-shutdown policy. Acceptance criteria for safety hardening need matched benign refusal and capability retention, not a single safety metric.

Scope guard: these values come from this implementation and evaluation grid and should not be generalized to every self-reflection defense or production workload.

### FE-118 — In a controlled self-evolution ablation, sharing one model across challenger and solver roles reduces pseudo-label quality and accelerates performance collapse
**Status:** ICLR 2026 R-Zero analysis; adjacent-domain control relevant to the unresolved role-coupling frontier, not direct LLM-safety evidence.

Primary source:
- Huang et al., *R-Zero: Self-Evolving Reasoning LLM from Zero Data*, ICLR 2026 / arXiv:2508.05004: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05004

Evidence:
- The paper compares standard two-model R-Zero with `Single-R-Zero`, a unified model sharing parameters across Challenger and Solver roles, on Qwen3-4B-Base.
- At Iter 1, performance/pseudo-label accuracy are **48.06 / 71.0%** for separated roles versus **47.31 / 63.4%** for shared roles.
- By Iter 3 they are **49.07 / 48.8%** versus **45.57 / 32.6%**; by Iter 4, **46.52 / 42.2%** versus **43.89 / 33.8%**.
- The authors report that the shared-role variant peaks after the first iteration and degrades thereafter, while the separated configuration improves for longer.

Failure implication: coupling adversarial/curriculum-generation and solving roles through shared parameters can create correlated error and lower-quality self-generated supervision even when both start from the same base model. This strengthens the case for measuring role coupling explicitly rather than assuming parameter efficiency is free.

Scope guard: Challenger/Solver reasoning is not Attacker/Defender safety. This is only a partial mechanistic analogue and does **not** resolve the stricter safety frontier requiring a single-factor attacker-vs-defender parameter-sharing ablation with all other training variables matched.
