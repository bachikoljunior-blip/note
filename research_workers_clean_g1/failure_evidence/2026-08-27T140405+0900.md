## New failure evidence

### FE-194 — Long benign context alone can materially degrade frontier safety-monitor recall
**Primary sources:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12366
- https://github.com/samm393/classifier-context-rot

Classifier Context Rot keeps the dangerous action and monitor task fixed while increasing benign transcript context. On padded MonitorBench, Claude Opus 4.6 with thinking drops from `98.6%` recall on the original short transcript to `88%` after an `800K`-token benign prefix. In the paper's obvious needle-injection task, the same monitor drops from `99.7%` recall at `100K` tokens to `69%` at `800K`. Across Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4, and Gemini 3.1, misses increase by roughly `2×–30×` at the long-context condition. The authors separately verify benign-transcript false-positive rates below `2%` across the tested models/prompt formats.

**Failure implication:** short-transcript monitoring benchmarks can substantially overstate safety-monitor reliability for long-running agents. Context length and attack timing are safety-evaluation variables, not merely throughput parameters.

**Scope guard:** these results use the paper's Claude Code/MonitorBench constructions, three frontier monitor families, and specific long-context concatenation/injection procedures. They do not establish the same degradation magnitude for all models, production transcript distributions, or monitor architectures.

### FE-195 — A prompt reminder mitigation helped one long-context attack type but not another
**Primary sources:** same as FE-194.

The paper inserts a monitoring reminder throughout the transcript and evaluates both the obvious needle-injection task and subtle padded MonitorBench attacks. The reminder decreases missed needle attacks for most tested monitor settings, but the paper reports that it does **not** improve MonitorBench overall. The same study finds that more reasoning generally helps, and that asking Opus to list suspicious actions before judging can improve detection with relatively little additional output.

**Failure implication:** a mitigation that repairs long-context retrieval of blatant violations may fail on subtle trajectory-level misbehavior. A single successful prompt patch should not be treated as a general monitor-hardening result without cross-failure-mode testing.

**Scope guard:** this is a prompt-level intervention in one monitoring setup; it does not show reminders are generally ineffective, and some tested model/task cells improve.

### FE-196 — Unstructured human feedback can make low-FPR monitoring worse; selective escalation is a positive control
**Primary sources:**
- https://openreview.net/forum?id=WV7xIboTDK
- https://github.com/scaleapi/mrt

In the ICLR 2026 Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring human-in-the-loop case study, the baseline Claude 3.6 Sonnet monitor on aware-agent/aware-monitor SHADE-Arena trajectories has `AUC=0.744` and `TPR@1% FPR=0.210±0.064`. Feeding ordinary human feedback back to the monitor reduces performance to `AUC=0.727` and `TPR=0.049±0.062`. Adding a strong-prior rule improves the TPR to `0.294±0.087`, and selective escalation only for initially suspicious trajectories (`score > 1`) raises it to `0.360±0.056` with `AUC=0.749`.

**Failure implication:** “add a human reviewer” is not a monotonic safety intervention. Unstructured human feedback can raise suspicion broadly enough to reduce useful detection at a strict false-positive budget; escalation policy and interface structure are part of the control system.

**Scope guard:** this is one case study with experienced red-team reviewers, one aware-agent/aware-monitor threat model, Claude 3.6 Sonnet, SHADE-Arena, three annotations aggregated by median, and sessions capped at 60 minutes. It does not imply human oversight generally hurts.

### FE-197 — Passing public tests leaves a large hidden-bug population, and adversarial generation further weakens verifiers
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20852

CodeMonitorBench generates `71,000` code candidates; `43,677` pass the visible public tests, yet `23,081` of those public-test-passing candidates still fail hidden tests. The paper reports that weak LLM verifiers still miss most hidden bugs when constrained to `5%` false-positive rate. Under an adversarial public-test-overfitting generator condition, verifier AUROC falls and low-FPR miss rates rise in most evaluated cells. A stronger GLM-5.1 verifier recovers part of the gap under the same public-evidence boundary, showing a positive control for verifier capability while leaving residual evidence limitations.

**Failure implication:** `tests passed` plus a weak review model is not a correctness certificate for agent-generated code. Evaluation must distinguish public-test success, hidden correctness, and verifier detectability, and should stress-test the verifier against code generated to exploit its evidence boundary.

**Scope guard:** this is the paper's three-domain CodeMonitorBench and its M1 public-check evidence boundary, not a claim that the same hidden-bug rate holds for production repositories or stronger test suites.

### FE-198 — More monitor information does not eliminate adversarial monitor failure, and workflow choice changes the low-FPR frontier
**Primary sources:** same ICLR 2026 MRT paper and released repository as FE-196.

Against aware attackers with evasion strategies, the paper reports the best observed SHADE-Arena scaffolding at each monitor-awareness level as follows: `AUC 0.75 / TPR@1% FPR 0.20` for an unaware monitor, `0.73 / 0.21` for an aware monitor, and `0.80 / 0.47` even for an omniscient monitor that knows the side task. The paper further reports extreme monitor-hijacking cases where baseline AUC drops below `0.5`. On CUA-SHADE-Arena, the corresponding best values are substantially higher (`0.85/0.68`, `0.84/0.67`, `0.98/0.88`), showing cross-environment sensitivity. Hybrid hierarchical-sequential scaffolding is the consistent AUC positive control.

**Failure implication:** giving a monitor more task information is not sufficient to make it robust to an agent optimizing against the monitoring process. Monitor architecture/scaffolding and environment distribution can dominate the value of additional awareness.

**Scope guard:** the numbers are empirical lower-bound summaries over the paper's tested evasion strategies and environments; they are not universal upper bounds on all possible monitor systems or programmatic verification.
