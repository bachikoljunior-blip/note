### FE-039 — Joint policy+gate evolution is not uniformly additive under adaptive long-horizon attacks
**Status:** primary arXiv preprint, held-out adaptive evaluation inside the HARD study; not independently replicated.

Primary source: Ruan et al., *Beyond Handcrafted Security: Towards Self-Evolving Defense for LLM Agents*, arXiv:2608.12977v1 (2026-08-13), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12977

Matched evidence:
- On held-out LPA, HARD-Policy reaches ASR **4.8%** while HARD-Both is **12.1%**; benign utility is **92.1%** vs **94.8%**.
- The paper's case analysis attributes the gap to routing many pattern-matchable failures to the gate, leaving the policy fewer opportunities to learn transferable anti-obfuscation/cumulative-intent rules.
- HARD-Gate improves its evolution split from **36.6% to 23.2% ASR**, while held-out ASR moves only **28.9% to 26.5%** (no-evolution reference **30.9%**).

Failure implication: modular defense components can interact non-additively; improving training/evolution-split attack metrics can coexist with weak held-out transfer. Route allocation is therefore part of the learned defense and needs held-out adaptive testing.

### FE-040 — A defense evolution backbone can lower ASR while catastrophically reducing utility under attack even when attack-free BU looks healthy
**Status:** primary matched backbone ablation in the same HARD preprint.

With execution agent, judge, five-round budget, and task family held fixed, Table 3 reports: no evolution **ASR 63.9 / BU 88.1 / UA 57.8**; GLM-5.2 **13.9 / 94.7 / 85.9**; GPT-5.5 **20.3 / 94.9 / 7.2**. The paper interprets the GPT-5.5 result as an overly restrictive defense.

Failure implication: attack-free benign utility plus lower ASR is insufficient to detect over-restriction. Utility under attack can collapse while BU remains high, so security evaluation needs joint ASR/BU/UA reporting rather than a single safety metric.
