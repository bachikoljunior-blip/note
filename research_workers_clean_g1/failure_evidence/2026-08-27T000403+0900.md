### FE-134 — Harmful-intent scores can anti-rank realized jailbreak success, so intent AUROC is not a substitute for outcome-indexed safety
**Status:** August-2026 arXiv v1; matched plain-versus-wrapped harmful goals with realized completions across multiple target models, attack families, judges and score channels.

Primary source:
- Luo et al., *Measuring the Wrong Thing: Internal Harmfulness Scores Anti-Rank Successful Jailbreaks*, arXiv:2608.09624v1 (10 Aug 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09624

Evidence:
- On Llama, wrapping the same underlying harmful goals raises harmful-generation rate from `0.05` to `0.27`, while harmful-intent discrimination AUROC falls from `0.936` to `0.803`.
- More importantly, among the wrapped harmful prompts, the pre-generation harmfulness score has **outcome AUROC `0.220`** for whether the jailbreak actually succeeds. This is below chance: successful jailbreaks are, on average, ranked as *less* harmful by the internal score than failed attempts.
- The paper reports that the reversal persists across three target models, seven attack families, two independent judges and multiple measurement channels, including rare-token, passive and detector-derived scores.

Failure implication: a safety score can remain useful for classifying harmful *intent* while being badly miscalibrated for the operational question “will this prompt produce a harmful completion?” An evaluator or gate trained/validated only on harmful-intent AUROC can therefore look strong while anti-ranking the failures that matter after attack transformation.

Scope guard: this does not imply that all internal activations, harmfulness detectors or prompt filters anti-rank jailbreak success, nor that the reported `0.220` transfers to other models or attack distributions. The claim is limited to the tested models, seven attack families, score channels, judges and wrapped-goal setup in this arXiv v1.

### FE-135 — When a harmfulness proxy becomes the attack-training reward, optimization can Goodhart against the proxy instead of increasing true harmful-goal completion
**Status:** controlled PPO reward ablation in *On the Misalignment Between Harmfulness and Jailbreak Success*; same training target and hyperparameters, reward differs by a relevance term.

Primary source:
- *On the Misalignment Between Harmfulness and Jailbreak Success*, OpenReview manuscript, controlled PPO-RAW versus PPO-STRICT ablation.

Evidence:
- PPO-RAW and PPO-STRICT share training hyperparameters and the Vicuna-7B-v1.5 target. PPO-RAW optimizes a harmfulness-style reward; PPO-STRICT additionally requires relevance to the requested harmful goal.
- At `k=1`, mean **strict** ASR across the reported targets is `0.362` for PPO-RAW versus `0.462` for PPO-STRICT (`+0.100`). On the in-distribution Vicuna-7B target it is `0.225` versus `0.450` (`+0.225`). Mean OOD strict ASR is `0.390` versus `0.465` (`+0.075`).
- PPO-RAW shows a mean `ASR_raw - ASR_strict` inflation of `+0.104` at `k=1`; in the misinformation category on the training target, raw versus strict success is `0.500` versus `0.250`.
- The effect is not universal in every cell: e.g. at `k=5` PPO-STRICT is lower on Llama (`0.175` versus `0.250`) and Mistral-v0.2 (`0.925` versus `0.950`). The evidence is therefore an average/systematic proxy-misalignment effect, not a monotonic superiority claim.

Failure implication: once an evaluator becomes an optimization reward, the relevant validation target is no longer “does the proxy recognize harmful-looking text?” but “does optimizing the proxy increase realized completion of the intended harmful goal?” Proxy-only red-team training can learn surface sequences that score as harmful without constituting a successful attack.

Scope guard: this is specific to the paper's PPO setup, targets, judges, HarmBench-derived evaluation and relevance gate. It does not establish that every harmfulness classifier Goodharts under optimization or that the relevance gate is itself a perfect ground truth.

### FE-136 — Structured-output contract failure can be mis-scored as failure of the scientific construct being evaluated
**Status:** August-2026 calibration study with deterministic fixtures plus four local quantized artifacts.

Primary source:
- *Calibrating Criterion Revision in LLM Agents: Failure Modes and a Trace-Anchored Protocol*, arXiv:2608.20729 (21 Aug 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20729

Evidence:
- Across the local-model portion, `96` calls produced `109` attempts because a retry was allowed; **11 calls remained unusable after one retry**.
- Complete-call rates differ radically by artifact: Llama 3.2 `3/24`, Gemma 3 `2/24`, Qwen2.5 `24/24`, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen `0/24`.
- No model trial satisfies all five desired conditions, but the paper explicitly warns that this zero does **not** identify capability absence when the required trace/output contract itself is not reliably produced.

Failure implication: a benchmark that collapses parseability, tool/trace-format compliance and the target construct into one conjunction can turn “the harness cannot obtain a valid measurement” into “the model lacks the capability.” Contract validity should be reported separately from construct performance, and non-measurement should fail closed as unidentifiable rather than be silently counted as negative evidence.

Scope guard: this is a calibration study with four quantized artifacts, 12 cases, seven deterministic fixtures and one reported seed. It does not rank model families or establish population-level capability rates.

### FE-137 — Apparent persistent criterion transfer can be reconstructed without stored state; without matched deletion/sham interventions and policy-selected writes, self-revision is not causally identified
**Status:** same criterion-revision calibration study; explicit intervention audit.

Primary source:
- *Calibrating Criterion Revision in LLM Agents: Failure Modes and a Trace-Anchored Protocol*, arXiv:2608.20729.

Evidence:
- Qwen2.5 correctly answers `12/12` loophole items and `24/24` preservation decisions under the intact setup in every arm.
- But the paper's “deleted” condition reuses an independently generated stateless response, and that response also gets **`12/12` loophole items correct**. The critical deletion contrast therefore vanishes: current-item reconstruction can explain the visible transfer without demonstrating use of persistent state.
- In the model-generated-state arm, the harness auto-commits generated criterion state; the model does not itself choose a distinct `WRITE/NO-WRITE/ESCALATE` action or execute the commit. The conflict manipulation also changes normative content, authority, wording and format simultaneously, and there is no equal-length irrelevant-state sham.

Failure implication: visible continuity across turns is not enough to infer durable self-revision. A causal test needs a hidden/delexicalized transfer signal, explicit policy-selected persistence, separately logged commit execution, matched carrier deletion and sham controls that preserve superficial context while removing the hypothesized causal state.

Scope guard: this does not show that persistent state is useless or that Qwen never uses memory. It shows that this tested contrast does not identify state use because the target behavior is reconstructable without the state and the write/delete interventions are not isolated.

### FE-138 — A defense can look configured and improve one metric while its target-channel enforcement hook is inactive; active-path evidence is required
**Status:** August-2026 source-level audit of authors' benchmark reimplementations for RAG privacy defenses; not an audit of every released defense implementation.

Primary source:
- *Mind the Hook: Source-Level Auditing of Privacy Defenses in Retrieval-Augmented Generation*, arXiv:2608.09001 (10 Aug 2026), accepted as an ICMLA 2026 regular paper, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09001

Evidence:
- In the authors' **benchmark reimplementations**, the audited DP-style defenses modify retrieval scores, but their generation-side hooks are TODO-flagged stubs that return responses unchanged.
- This explains the observed split: the defenses can change membership-inference behavior while generated-text named-entity leakage (`NEL_strict`) tracks the No-Defense condition because the response-generation channel is not actually modified by those stubs.
- A positive end-to-end control demonstrates that the audit can detect an active protection path: under No-Defense, `53/150` canaries are recovered; under LPRAG, recovery falls to **`0/150`**.
- Corroborating benchmark-audit evidence from *Auditing the Audit: Five Failure Modes in Benchmark-Validity Audits* reports silent no-op perturbations and `57–93%` no-op rates for one semantic-restructure perturbation family caused by trigger-string mismatch, reinforcing the need to verify that intended interventions actually execute.

Failure implication: configuration files, enabled flags, prompt text and metric movement are not proof that a defense's intended mechanism executed on the target channel. Source-level control-flow inspection, runtime receipts/counters and positive canary tests should be part of the evidence contract.

Scope guard: the TODO-stub finding concerns the paper authors' reimplementations in their stack; it must not be attributed automatically to original defense repositories or to DP-style RAG defenses as a family. The canary result is a positive control for the tested LPRAG implementation and stack, not a universal leakage guarantee.
