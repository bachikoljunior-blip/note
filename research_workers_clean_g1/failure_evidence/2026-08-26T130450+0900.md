## New claims

### FE-089 — Maximizing ASR on one defender can select an attacker that has nearly zero transfer to another defender
**Status:** peer-reviewed ACL 2026 primary paper + official code repository.

Primary sources:
- Tan et al., *TriPlay-RL: Tri-Role Self-Play Reinforcement Learning for LLM Safety Alignment*, ACL 2026: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1216/
- Official implementation: https://github.com/Qihoo360/TriPlay-RL

Matched ablation evidence:
- TriPlay-RL varies two attacker-training components: the closed-loop iterative interaction (`L`) and an explicit diversity penalty (`D`).
- With neither component (`w/o L + w/o D`), output diversity collapses to **OD=0.004** and ASR against Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B reaches **98.2%** — the highest Nano-8B ASR in the ablation.
- The same attacker achieves only **0.2% ASR** against Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
- With both loop and diversity (`w/ L + w/ D`), OD is **0.588**, Nano-8B ASR is lower at **85.4%**, but Llama-8B transfer rises to **6.6%**.

Failure implication: selecting a red-team policy solely by its highest ASR on one defender can favor severe strategy/template collapse and produce a much weaker cross-defender attacker. A larger single-target ASR is not sufficient evidence of a generally stronger red team.

Scope guard: this is the paper's fourth-iteration attacker ablation, its two tested defender targets for Table 2, its output-diversity metric, and its training setup. It does not establish that high single-target ASR always implies poor transfer, nor that OD causally captures all useful strategy diversity.

### FE-090 — Training the attacker against multiple defenders is a positive control that improves cross-defender attack generalization
**Status:** same peer-reviewed TriPlay-RL study.

Evidence:
- Holding the fourth-iteration attacker setup fixed except for single-defender versus multi-defender exposure during red training, multi-defender training raises ASR on all three tested target defenders:
  - DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B: **22.8% → 29.1%** (+6.3pp)
  - Qwen3-8B: **60.1% → 64.1%** (+4.0pp)
  - Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B-v1: **77.6% → 85.4%** (+7.8pp)

Failure implication: a red-team benchmark built from a single defender can understate attacker strength on other defenders because the attacker itself can overfit the target. Multi-defender exposure is a concrete mitigation in this tested setting.

Scope guard: the result compares the paper's specific single-versus-multiple-defense training construction and three target defenders. It is a positive control against target overfit, not a claim that more defender models monotonically improve attack generalization in arbitrary settings.

### FE-091 — A high-recall jailbreak judging rubric can produce massive false-positive inflation by mistaking topical engagement for harmful compliance
**Status:** primary arXiv v1 full text; authors report human validation of a three-judge ensemble.

Primary source:
- Gautam et al., *AutoRISE: Agent-Driven Strategy Evolution for Red-Teaming Large Language Models*, arXiv:2604.22871v1: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22871

Evidence:
- The authors initially applied the StrongREJECT rubric verbatim and report that it conflated topical engagement with harmful compliance.
- On their human-labeled JailbreakBench validation subset (`N=100`), the three-judge ensemble under that rubric had **57.3% accuracy, 46.2% precision, 98.2% recall, F1 62.8, κ=0.258**.
- Replacing it with a response-harm rubric increased performance to **90.7% accuracy, 82.5% precision, 94.5% recall, F1 88.1, κ=0.805** on the same validation set.

Failure implication: an automated red-team loop can optimize against a judge that rewards benign topical engagement as if it were harmful compliance. High recall alone can therefore inflate both measured ASR and the optimization signal.

Scope guard: these numbers are from AutoRISE's specific human-labeled validation set and three selected judge models. They do not invalidate StrongREJECT in its original benchmark context; they show that transferring a rubric into a different scoring construction can materially change false-positive behavior.

### FE-092 — Individual jailbreak prompts can be highly non-reproducible even when aggregate ASR is stable
**Status:** same primary AutoRISE study.

Evidence:
- Across three independent re-evaluations on the frontier targets, AutoRISE reports per-prompt replay rates of only **~29% on GPT-5.4** and **~43% on o4-mini** under default streaming stochasticity.
- Aggregate ASR remains much more stable, with run-to-run standard deviations of only **0.7–1.9 percentage points**.

Failure implication: a single successful prompt is weak evidence of a deterministic or durable exploit. Population-level attack success can be reproducible while the exact prompt-level failure identity is unstable.

Scope guard: the observed non-reproducibility is tied to the tested frontier endpoints, their default sampling/streaming behavior, the selected prompts and the authors' replay definition. It does not imply all jailbreak prompts or deterministic endpoints have similarly low replay rates.

### FE-093 — A benchmark can compare an adaptive baseline at a tiny fraction of its published optimization budget, preventing a resource-matched dominance claim
**Status:** same primary AutoRISE study; explicit implementation note in Appendix E.

Evidence:
- For AutoDAN-Turbo-R, AutoRISE uses **2 optimization epochs per prompt**, incurring 3 target calls per prompt in its implementation.
- The paper states that the original AutoDAN-Turbo setup uses **150 epochs per prompt** with early stopping at a scorer threshold of 8.5.
- The authors estimate that the full 150-epoch setting would require about **75× more wall-clock time** per target than their 2-epoch configuration, and explicitly choose 2 epochs as a practical low-budget configuration.
- In AutoRISE Table 2 this truncated AutoDAN-T-R configuration has **13.6% held-out mean ASR**, versus **40.0%** for AR-Standard and **35.7%** for AR-Frontier.

Failure implication: these Table 2 numbers do not support the stronger statement that AutoRISE algorithmically dominates AutoDAN-Turbo-R at AutoDAN's published search budget. Adaptive attack comparisons require an explicit resource axis or matched budget; otherwise part of the gap can be search allocation.

Scope guard: this does not negate AutoRISE's transfer results or its program-space ablation. The paper transparently discloses the reduced AutoDAN budget. The limitation applies specifically to interpreting cross-method ranking as budget-independent algorithmic superiority.
