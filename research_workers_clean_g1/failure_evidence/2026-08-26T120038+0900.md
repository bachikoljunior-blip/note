## New claims

### FE-084 — Under a shared target-call budget, the relative ranking of two jailbreak attacks reverses as the budget changes
**Status:** primary arXiv v1 full text + official code repository.

Primary sources:
- He et al., *Fair ASR: Re-Evaluating Black-Box Jailbreaks under Shared Target-Call Budgets*, arXiv:2608.17360v1: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17360
- Official implementation: https://github.com/xsddys/Fair-ASR

Evidence:
- Fair-ASR evaluates black-box attacks with the same maximum target-call budget `B` and reports complete `ASR@B` curves instead of comparing terminal ASRs obtained under heterogeneous target access.
- On the Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct HarmBench curve, TAP leads BoN at `B=5`: **60.5% vs 43.5% ASR**.
- Under the same target and benchmark but `B=100`, BoN overtakes TAP: **96.0% vs 91.5% ASR**.
- The official repository exposes the shared-budget evaluator, per-budget histories/checkpoints, and separate target-call / attacker-call accounting.

Failure implication: an evaluation can report the wrong ordering of attack strength if methods are compared at arbitrary or unequal target-call budgets. A single terminal ASR is partly a statement about available target access, not only attack quality.

Scope guard: this is a black-box single-turn jailbreak result under the paper's targets, HarmBench/JailbreakBench, judge choices, and target-call accounting. It does not imply target calls are the only relevant resource; the authors explicitly track attacker calls separately and note token/API/manual-development costs are not captured by the primary axis.

### FE-085 — Equal target access makes simple stochastic/template attacks competitive with substantially more complex LLM-driven search
**Status:** same primary Fair-ASR study and official implementation.

Evidence:
- At `B=100`, BoN has **81.3% average ASR** and **42.5 ATC** across the five primary targets while using no attacker-model calls, outperforming every evaluated LLM-driven method except ReNeLLM on average final ASR.
- On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, a single CodeAttack template reaches **62% ASR at B=1**; all eight templates yield **90.7% average ASR@K** in the main table.
- ReNeLLM is strongest on target-call efficiency (`98.7%` average ASR@100; ATC `3.9`) but uses substantial attacker-side rewriting, so target-call efficiency does not imply joint resource efficiency.

Failure implication: algorithmic sophistication is not a safe proxy for red-team strength. If a benchmark gives advanced methods more search or target access than simple baselines, apparent gains can be resource-allocation artifacts.

Scope guard: the result compares specific attacks under the Fair-ASR protocol; it does not show that hand-crafted or stochastic methods universally dominate adaptive LLM-driven attacks, and it does not collapse target calls and attacker calls into one cost measure.

### FE-086 — Holding the model fixed, changing the agent harness shifts attack success by more than 4×
**Status:** primary arXiv preprint + official project/code; 3 sampling seeds, 384 trajectories per configuration.

Primary sources:
- Bai et al., *HarnessRisk: A Lifecycle-Oriented Benchmark for Agent Harness Safety*, arXiv:2608.17597: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17597
- Project/results page: https://baiyajing.github.io/harness-risk/
- Official code: https://github.com/Baiyajing/HarnessRisk

Evidence:
- HarnessRisk evaluates 128 sandboxed cases spanning six harness lifecycle phases and reports Utility, ASR, Persistence, and Detection across three harnesses and six models.
- The project page reports the same **GLM-5.2** model at **54.7% ASR on OpenClaw** versus **12.6% ASR on Nanobot**, a **4.3×** harness effect.
- Across all 14 model–harness configurations, ASR spans **12.6% to 80.9%** while utility remains **75.0% to 97.6%**.

Failure implication: an agent safety score is a property of the deployed model–harness system, not the base model alone. Evaluating a model in one tool/context/permission wrapper can materially misstate risk in another wrapper even when the model identity is unchanged.

Scope guard: these are the three harnesses, 128 sandboxed cases, mock services, and configurations tested by HarnessRisk. The 4.3× shift should not be generalized to arbitrary harness substitutions or real production incident rates.

### FE-087 — Explicit risk detection can be very high while unsafe actions still succeed materially
**Status:** same primary HarnessRisk study.

Evidence:
- The paper reports that some configurations detect risk in **more than 90%** of runs while retaining substantial ASR.
- The project page gives a concrete example: **MiniMax M3 on OpenClaw detects risk in 97.9% of runs yet retains 31.2% ASR**.
- HarnessRisk scores detection separately from realized attack success using trajectory evidence, tool calls, workspace/state changes, and mock-service effects.

Failure implication: verbal recognition or a detector flag is not an adequate proxy for safe execution. A system may correctly identify untrusted/malicious content and still take the unsafe action, persist malicious state, or fail to remediate it.

Scope guard: this does not show detection is useless; it shows that in these tested agent-harness trajectories, detection and prevention are distinct metrics and high detection alone does not establish operational safety.

### FE-088 — Even stateful defenses with strong visible-history semantics can fail when harmful capability is pooled across unlinkable identities and retries reveal ALLOW/BLOCK feedback
**Status:** primary arXiv preprint; formal threat-model analysis plus 91 executable tasks and 11,393 capability-matched benign requests.

Primary source:
- Sun et al., *Decomposition Attacks Across Unlinkable Identities: Limits of Stateful Defenses for LLM Services*, arXiv:2608.17445: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17445

Evidence from the primary abstract:
- The defense sees service-visible state, but the attacker can distribute individually permissible subtasks across identities the service cannot reliably link and combine answers off-service.
- Under a **1% denial cap** for capability-matched benign requests and **0.5% cap** for unrelated background traffic, **all ten tested policies**, including a privileged policy with an exact request-to-operation map, either fail to stop attacks or exceed the denial budget.
- On **defense-unseen task families**, attack success is **at least 99% after one attempt and 100% after two**.
- The paper's central mechanism is that retries plus ALLOW/BLOCK feedback reveal which profiles pass while the service still lacks evidence about off-service pooling.

Failure implication: adding state/history does not create missing identity/linkage evidence. A stateful defense evaluated only under linkable accounts or no-retry attackers can substantially overstate robustness to decomposition attacks that use fresh identities and feedback.

Scope guard: this is conditional on the paper's decomposition threat model, executable synthetic cyber tasks, finite attack profiles, identity unlinkability, off-service answer pooling, denial caps, and service-visible observation boundary. Mechanisms that add reliable linkage, identity costs, restricted tools, sandboxing, or control over answer use can escape the result and should be evaluated separately.
