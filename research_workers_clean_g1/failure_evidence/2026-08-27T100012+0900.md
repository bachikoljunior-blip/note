## New claims

### FE-174 — A cross-user stateful monitor can recover substantial signal that per-transcript monitoring structurally misses
**Status:** primary 2026 arXiv study of distributed cyber-agent misuse and an online stateful monitoring defense.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31593

Evidence:
- The authors construct a distributed multi-agent cyberattack scaffold that fragments a harmful objective across limited-context subagents and multiple user identities. On Cybench, the distributed system still completes hard cyber tasks at roughly `36.7–40.0%` success while being detected by a standard per-context monitor only about one fifth as often as less distributed prior attacks.
- In simulated datacenter traffic at a `100:1` benign:malicious ratio, the proposed online stateful monitor reports `65.8%` recall versus `55%` for standard monitors at the same low false-positive operating point.
- The stateful monitor reduces time-to-detect by nearly `30%` and adds negligible extra latency for about `99%` of traffic because expensive cross-context reasoning is only triggered on a small fraction of requests.

Failure implication: a benchmark that evaluates safety only transcript-by-transcript can materially overestimate system safety against distributed misuse. Weak suspiciousness signals that are individually benign-looking can become decisive only after cross-user aggregation.

Scope guard: these results are from a simulated datacenter-traffic evaluation and Cybench-style cyber misuse, not a production false-positive study across arbitrary consumer traffic. They show that cross-context state can add detection power; they do not establish that this specific monitor is universally robust or deployment-ready.

### FE-175 — Stateful aggregation itself has adaptive evasion surfaces and degrades as benign background grows
**Status:** same primary stateful-monitor study; two-month white-box red-team evidence and scaling caveat.

Evidence:
- During two months of white-box red teaming, attackers with access to internal monitor state found `context poisoning` strategies that interleave benign tasks to dilute suspiciousness and `semantic shifting` strategies that rename files/change vocabulary to route related actions into different clusters.
- The authors responded with multi-cluster assignment and evidence prioritization rather than treating the initial stateful architecture as closed.
- The paper reports that the stateful monitor's detection advantage persists but narrows as benign background traffic becomes very large.
- An encouraging positive control is that the refined monitor also catches some standard jailbreak campaigns across changed user IDs because adaptive attackers reuse structurally similar variants across accounts.

Failure implication: actor-level or cross-account monitoring should itself be treated as an adaptive attack surface. A single static traffic mix or one routing rule can make the monitor appear stronger than it remains under deliberate dilution, semantic dispersion, or much larger benign background.

Scope guard: the paper does not report a complete attacker-versus-monitor best-response matrix or production-scale false-positive curve under adversaries explicitly optimizing across identities and time. The finding motivates direct batch-monitor red teaming; it does not prove that cross-user monitoring fails under all distributed attacks.

### FE-176 — Repeating the exact same harmful prompt reveals time-to-failure profiles that a terminal ASR obscures
**Status:** TrustNLP 2026 / arXiv primary study applying Kaplan-Meier survival analysis to repeated jailbreak attempts.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12869

Evidence:
- The study samples `60` HarmBench prompts (20 each from three categories) and repeats each prompt verbatim until jailbreak or a maximum of `10` refusals/low-risk responses, so the attack itself is not adapted between trials.
- In Misinformation & Disinformation, median time-to-jailbreak is `1` attempt for Phi-3 Mini versus `7` for Llama 3.2 and `8` for Qwen3-4B.
- Overall pairwise log-rank tests separate Phi-3 Mini from Llama 3.2 (`statistic 24.923`, `p<0.0001`) and from Qwen3-4B (`31.205`, `p<0.0001`), while Llama versus Qwen is not significant (`p=0.4834`).
- The estimated overall hazard for Phi-3 Mini decreases after a high early-failure window, whereas Llama and Qwen are reported as roughly more constant. Qwen was not jailbroken at all by the 20 General Harm prompts in the ten-attempt window.
- The judge itself is imperfect: Llama-3.1-70B agrees with human WildGuard labels on `76.5%` of 749 non-benign cases, disagrees on `17.1%`, and is inconclusive on `6.4%`.

Failure implication: one-shot ASR and end-of-budget ASR discard when failure occurs. Two systems with similar terminal outcomes can expose very different persistent-pressure risk and query-to-failure behavior; repeated identical requests alone can reveal this distinction even without adaptive attack search.

Scope guard: the experiment covers only three small local models, 60 prompts, and ten attempts, and uses a judge with nontrivial disagreement. The observed hazard shapes should not be generalized to frontier models or adaptive multi-turn attacks without replication.

### FE-177 — Very large internal red-team compute plus successful patching of a known jailbreak does not establish closure against fresh grey-box attackers
**Status:** current public GPT-5.6 system card; vendor-reported deployment evidence updated in August 2026.

Primary source:
- https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6

Evidence:
- OpenAI reports spending more than `700,000 A100e GPU hours` on universal-jailbreak discovery using optimization-based search, reinforcement learning, and test-time search.
- The strongest internally found universal jailbreak initially achieved `10.0%` success after safeguards; after additional mitigations, that specific attack was reduced to `0%` in the reported evaluation.
- Despite this scale of internal search and iterative mitigation, UK AISI — with grey-box access to safety-monitor reasoning, exact policy wording, and real-time classifier labels — found universal cyber jailbreaks in **all testing rounds so far**, often within hours, including attacks that preserved capability on public offensive-cyber benchmarks and enabled long-form agentic tasks.
- The system card explicitly states that UK AISI expects further red teaming to surface similar jailbreaks and that testing continues during deployment.

Failure implication: `0%` on a patched attack and large prior red-team compute are attack-set-specific evidence, not proof that the safeguard class has converged. Fresh attackers with stronger feedback or a different search distribution can reopen failure modes after an apparently successful mitigation cycle.

Scope guard: this is vendor-reported evidence for the GPT-5.6 safeguard stack, and UK AISI received much stronger information than ordinary users. It does not imply equivalent public black-box exploitability or that 700k GPU-hours is generally insufficient for every defense.
