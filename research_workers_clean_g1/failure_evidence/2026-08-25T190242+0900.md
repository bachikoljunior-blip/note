## New failure evidence

### FE-018 — Outcome-only agent evaluation can credit procedurally invalid success and can reverse model rankings

Primary source:
- Cao, Driouich, Thomas, “Beyond Task Completion: Revealing Corrupt Success in LLM Agents through Procedure-Aware Evaluation,” arXiv:2603.03116v1, 3 Mar 2026.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03116
- Primary PDF Table 3 and Section 5.1 were checked directly.

Tested setup / scope:
- τ-bench Retail and Airline.
- Models: GPT-5, Kimi-K2-Thinking, Mistral-Large-3.
- Four trials per task (`k=4`).
- Standard utility is compared with a six-dimension gate requiring task success plus policy compliance, policy faithfulness, execution consistency, data faithfulness, user-intent adherence, and question fulfillment.

Quantitative matched evidence (Table 3):

Retail:
- Success: GPT-5 0.79 → 0.58; Kimi-K2 0.61 → 0.27; Mistral 0.68 → 0.16.
- Pass^4: GPT-5 0.58 → 0.24; Kimi-K2 0.31 → 0.04; Mistral 0.46 → 0.03.
- Pass@4: GPT-5 0.92 → 0.87; Kimi-K2 0.83 → 0.56; Mistral 0.84 → 0.36.

Airline:
- Success: GPT-5 0.60 → 0.44; Kimi-K2 0.48 → 0.30; Mistral 0.40 → 0.09.
- Pass^4: GPT-5 0.44 → 0.18; Kimi-K2 0.28 → 0.06; Mistral 0.18 → 0.02.
- Pass@4: GPT-5 0.74 → 0.68; Kimi-K2 0.62 → 0.52; Mistral 0.62 → 0.22.

Ranking failure:
- On Retail, standard success ranks Mistral above Kimi-K2 (0.68 vs 0.61), while gated success reverses them (Kimi-K2 0.27 vs Mistral 0.16).
- The authors report 27–78% of benchmark-reported successes as “corrupt successes” under their gate.

Judge validation / uncertainty:
- Automatic proxy checks confirm 89.6% of UNNECESSARY_CALL and 90.6% of HARMFUL_DISALLOWED_EXECUTION judge labels.
- A random human spot-check confirms 89.3% of 150 other judge assessments.
- Exhaustive manual analysis of 131 Airline corrupt-success cases reports per-model judge precision of 93.8–95.2%, leaving a nonzero false-positive rate.

Interpretation constrained to evidence:
- In these τ-bench/model/scaffold settings, terminal-state success alone can materially overstate compliant reliability and can alter a pairwise ranking when procedural requirements are enforced.

Scope limit:
- This does not show that PAE’s six binary gates are the uniquely correct deployment metric, nor that all benchmarks contain the same corruption rate. The authors themselves note that binary gating treats violations as equally disqualifying and that policy-aware dimensions require explicit policies.

### FE-019 — Same-task repeated runs remain materially inconsistent even when nominal benchmark accuracy is moderate/high

Primary source:
- Rabanser, Kapoor, Kirgis, Liu, Utpala, Narayanan, “Towards a Science of AI Agent Reliability,” arXiv:2602.16666v3, 2 Jun 2026; accepted ICML 2026.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16666
- Official HAL reliability dashboard: https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/reliability/

Matched repeated-run protocol:
- 15 models on GAIA and a verified τ-bench Airline subset.
- Each task is executed `K=5` times with different random seeds.
- Temperature is set to zero where the API exposes it, specifically so observed variance is attributed to non-sampling stochasticity; reasoning models without configurable temperature retain provider defaults.
- The τ-bench main evaluation uses a verified 26-task subset because prior auditing found 24 of the original 50 Airline tasks contained flawed ground truth or ambiguous specifications.

Quantitative repeated-run evidence from the official final-version dashboard:
- GPT-4 Turbo on clean τ-bench Airline: accuracy 57.7%, but outcome consistency only 0.624 (aggregate consistency 0.763).
- GPT-5.2 on clean τ-bench Airline: accuracy 60.3%, outcome consistency 0.795.
- o1 on clean τ-bench Airline: accuracy 66.2%, outcome consistency 0.517; because o1 does not expose temperature control, this cell includes provider-default sampling/stochasticity and should not be used as a pure non-sampling estimate.
- GPT-5.2 (xhigh) on clean τ-bench Airline: accuracy 67.7%, outcome consistency 0.538; same temperature-control caveat applies.

Cross-model result:
- The paper reports low outcome consistency across models: agents that can solve tasks often fail to solve the same task consistently across repetitions.
- Distributional action-type consistency is higher than exact action-sequence consistency (“what but not when”), while resource use also varies substantially across runs, especially on GAIA.
- Over roughly 24 months of model releases, accuracy improves faster than the paper’s aggregate reliability measurements; the authors explicitly conclude that capability scaling does not guarantee reliability gains.

Evaluation-harness warning found during the same primary-source inspection:
- The final-version dashboard states that the authors corrected an earlier outcome-consistency formula error and fixed GAIA scaffolding loopholes that could expose ground-truth values. Use the current v3/final dashboard, not stale preprint numbers.

Interpretation constrained to evidence:
- A single-run success rate can hide same-task outcome instability. Repetition is necessary to distinguish consistently solvable/unsolvable tasks from tasks that flip between success and failure under nominally repeated conditions.

Scope limit:
- This is measured on one scaffold per benchmark and two benchmark families. Temperature-zero control does not apply to models whose providers hide temperature, and the authors note that alternate scaffolds could produce different reliability profiles.

### FE-020 — Mutating-action deviations are disproportionately associated with long-horizon agent failure

Primary source:
- Cuadron, Yu, Liu, Gupta, “SABER: Small Actions, Big Errors — Safeguarding Mutating Steps in LLM Agents,” arXiv:2512.07850, 26 Nov 2025.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07850

Tested scope:
- Execution traces on τ-bench Airline/Retail and SWE-Bench Verified.
- The paper separates mutating environment-changing actions from non-mutating steps and studies decisive deviations associated with success-to-failure divergence.

Quantitative negative evidence:
- In the paper’s logistic-regression analysis, each additional deviation in a mutating action reduces the odds of success by up to 92% on τ-bench Airline and up to 96% on Retail for the tested state-of-the-art models.
- In contrast, the primary abstract reports that non-mutating deviations have little to no effect.
- Errors increase with context length in the tested traces as agents drift from role/constraints.

Benchmark-integrity evidence:
- The same work reports ceiling effects in τ-bench caused by annotation errors and underspecified tasks and releases τ-bench Verified to restore headroom.
- Independently, the final ICML-2026 reliability study above restricts its Airline evaluation to 26 verified tasks because 24/50 original tasks were found to contain flawed ground truth or ambiguous specifications, reinforcing that the original task set cannot be treated as uniformly valid capability evidence.

Interpretation constrained to evidence:
- Under these traced benchmarks, not all trajectory mistakes are equally consequential: deviations at state-mutating steps are much more predictive of failure than deviations at non-mutating steps. This supports failure attribution at action type / side-effect boundaries instead of treating every trajectory mismatch as equally causal.

Scope limit:
- Logistic association is not proof that every mutating deviation causally creates the failure, and the “up to” odds reductions must not be generalized to arbitrary agents, tools, or domains.

## Updated failure taxonomy

The newly added evidence distinguishes three different failure surfaces:

1. **Outcome-metric blind spots (FE-018):** apparent success can conceal policy, grounding, execution, or intent violations; applying explicit procedural gates can collapse reliability and reverse pairwise rankings.
2. **Run-to-run instability (FE-019):** average success can hide same-task flips under repeated nominal execution, even with temperature zero where controllable.
3. **Action criticality (FE-020):** errors at state-mutating boundaries are far more strongly associated with failure than non-mutating deviations in the tested traces.

Combined with prior FE-014–FE-017, failure attribution should keep separate: policy quality, repeated-run stochasticity, side-effect/action criticality, verifier quality, benchmark contamination, scaffold/environment integrity, and compute effects.
