## New claims

### FE-132 — A single best jailbreak configuration can simultaneously understate the total reachable prompt surface and overstate how representative that attack configuration is
**Status:** May-2026 arXiv position paper with primary empirical case studies on PAIR and bijection learning; open-source target models only.

Primary source:
- Maple, Kumar, Tapwal, *Single-Configuration Attack Success Rate Is Not Enough: Jailbreak Evaluations Should Report Distributional Attack Success*, arXiv:2605.09070v1 (9 May 2026), Sections 4–5 and Tables 1–3. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09070

Evidence:
- PAIR was evaluated on the same 100-prompt HarmBench subset with a fixed Llama-3.1-8B attacker, one sampling stream, the three released system-prompt templates, and conversation depth `K∈{1,2,3}`. At `K=3`, the strongest single template reaches `69%` ASR on Mistral-7B and `75%` on Qwen3-0.6B, while the union of the three templates covers `88%` and `93%` of prompts respectively: `+19pp` and `+18pp` beyond the best single template.
- Across all nine PAIR template×depth configurations, the mean ASR is only `39.7%` on Mistral versus the best `69%` (Variant Sensitivity Measure `0.425`), and `51.6%` on Qwen versus the best `75%` (VSM `0.312`). Thus the best configuration is materially above typical tested-config performance while still missing prompts reachable by other configurations.
- Bijection learning was evaluated over 36 dispersion×teaching-shot configurations per model on the same 100-prompt subset. Mistral-7B has best ASR `81%`, mean ASR `15.94%`, worst `0%`, VSM `0.803`, yet union coverage reaches `100%`. Qwen3-0.6B is `52%` best / `7.08%` mean / `85%` union, and Llama-3.1-8B is `17%` best / `2.69%` mean / `43%` union.

Failure implication: a headline best-ASR number can fail in two opposite directions at once. It can exaggerate how reliably a parameterized attack family works under a typical tested configuration, while also understating the total set of prompts an adversary can reach by varying configurations. Defender evaluation therefore needs both a peak/worst-case view and prompt-level union/overlap across a declared attack-variant distribution.

Scope guard: this does not establish the same magnitudes for GCG, AutoDAN, TAP, closed-source frontier targets, or attack parameters not enumerated in the study. PAIR intentionally uses `N=1`; the paper studies two attack families and three open-source targets on a 100-prompt stratified HarmBench subset. Union coverage is coverage across the tested variant set, not proof that all reachable variants were exhausted.

### FE-133 — Attack-family parameter interactions can create large non-monotonic measurement changes, so one-dimensional ablations can miss vulnerable pockets
**Status:** same May-2026 primary source; matched grid within one attack family.

Primary source:
- Maple, Kumar, Tapwal, arXiv:2605.09070v1, Section 5.3, Table 3.

Evidence:
- On Mistral-7B bijection learning, the same 100 prompts are evaluated across fixed-size dispersion `FS∈{0,5,10,13,18,26}` and teaching shots `TS∈{1,2,4,6,8,10}`.
- At `FS=26`, changing only teaching-shot count changes ASR from `81%` (`TS=1`) to `49%` (`TS=10`), with intermediate values `80%, 51%, 70%, 63%`.
- At lower dispersion settings, `TS=1` exposes attack success that almost disappears for higher shot counts: e.g. `FS=5` is `28%` at `TS=1` and `0%` for every tested `TS≥2`; `FS=10` is `24%` at `TS=1` and `0%` thereafter.
- The union over teaching-shot values at `FS=26` reaches `95%`, while the full 36-cell union reaches `100%`, showing that even the strongest dispersion setting misses some prompt-level vulnerabilities revealed elsewhere in the parameter grid.

Failure implication: choosing a single “reasonable” value for one attack hyperparameter and sweeping another can incorrectly suggest monotonicity or robustness. Attack evaluation should treat interacting parameters as a joint search space and preserve prompt-level overlap, rather than collapsing the family into one tuned configuration.

Scope guard: the grid is deterministic at the reported configuration level and does not quantify independent stochastic-seed variance. The result is specific to this bijection implementation, HarmBench subset, judge, and three tested open-source targets.
