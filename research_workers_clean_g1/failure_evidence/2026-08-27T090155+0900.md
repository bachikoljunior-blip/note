## New claims

### FE-170 — Thousands of human red-team hours are not a robust upper bound on black-box classifier exploitability
**Status:** primary UK AI Security Institute paper/blog; fully black-box classifier attack against deployed safeguard families.

Primary sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15001
- https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/boundary-point-jailbreaking-a-new-way-to-break-the-strongest-ai-defences

Evidence:
- Anthropic's Constitutional Classifiers had previously withstood more than `3,700` collective hours of human red teaming with only one fully successful human-led universal jailbreak reported.
- Boundary Point Jailbreaking (BPJ) then found universal jailbreak prefixes automatically while receiving only one bit of feedback per query: whether the classifier flagged the interaction.
- Against Constitutional Classifiers, the AISI reports BPJ raising the average harmful-question score from `0%` to `25.5%`, with `39.9%` max across 50 attempts; with basic elicitation the scores rose to `68%` average and `80.4%` max.
- Against GPT-5's input classifier, BPJ reached `75.6%` average and `94.3%` max across 50 attempts without relying on human seed attacks.
- The authors report that combining curriculum learning with boundary-point selection finds successful attacks about `5×` faster than curriculum learning alone, while simpler random search fails even on much weaker classifiers.

Failure implication: accumulated human red-team time and failure of earlier black-box methods are not reliable upper bounds on vulnerability. A defense may look saturated simply because the attack search lacks a useful signal near the decision boundary.

Scope guard: BPJ required months of research effort and very large query budgets, so this does not imply ordinary users can cheaply reproduce the attack. The classifier attack is also distinct from bypassing the underlying model's refusal policy; the AISI used a separate basic human-found model jailbreak for that layer.

### FE-171 — A defense can fail per interaction while still exposing a strong batch-level detection signal
**Status:** same primary BPJ sources; operational positive-control implication.

Evidence:
- The reported Constitutional Classifier run cost about `$330` and `660k` queries; the GPT-5 input-classifier run cost about `$210` and `800k` queries.
- BPJ's authors explicitly note that optimization incurs many classifier flags even though the final attack can evade the classifier on individual interactions.
- They therefore recommend supplementing single-interaction safeguards with batch-level monitoring across traffic; the AISI also notes that repeated flags on ordinary accounts would plausibly trigger enforcement such as account banning.

Failure implication: evaluating only whether a single request is blocked can understate system-level defensive leverage, while evaluating only aggregate account-level enforcement can hide a broken per-request classifier. Interaction-level robustness and cross-interaction telemetry are separate controls and should be scored separately.

Scope guard: no production batch-monitoring false-positive/false-negative curve is reported here, and the enforcement comment is an operational observation rather than a randomized mitigation experiment. This claim is about the existence of a strong observable query/flag footprint, not proof that a particular batch detector stops BPJ.

### FE-172 — Strong jailbreaks need not impose a useful capability tax on frontier models
**Status:** primary arXiv v2-style empirical study across Claude frontier models and 28 jailbreaks.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00267

Evidence:
- Across `28` jailbreaks and `5` biology/reasoning benchmarks, the paper reports mean relative degradation shrinking from `33.1%` for Haiku 4.5 to `7.7%` for Opus 4.6 at maximum thinking effort.
- For Boundary Point Jailbreaking specifically, the authors report only `2%` capability degradation on the most-capable Opus 4.6 thinking configuration.
- Against the deployed Constitutional Classifier used in their evaluation, BPJ occupies the high-evasion/low-degradation corner across Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5, and Opus 4.6: `92–100%` classifier evasion with less than `5%` degradation.
- The authors analyze 135 BPJ prefixes from one optimization run and find no meaningful correlation between prefix classifier score and capability degradation (`r=-0.07, p=0.43`; balanced subsampling gives mean correlation `-0.01`).

Failure implication: a safety case should not assume that a sufficiently complicated jailbreak will consume enough model capability to neutralize dangerous downstream competence. More-capable models can preserve task performance while the safeguard is bypassed.

Scope guard: these headline capability results are for five largely biology/reasoning benchmarks and Claude-family models, with evaluation selecting the best of ten prompting strategies for each jailbreak/benchmark pair. The paper's preliminary agentic evaluation instead finds substantial degradation and explicitly cautions that multi-turn jailbreak adaptation remains unresolved, so this does not establish near-zero jailbreak tax for agents.

### FE-173 — Cross-stage guardrail evaluation independently reproduces the "future attacker beats static defense" failure mode, but still falls short of a seeded exploitability matrix
**Status:** primary arXiv v1 DARWIN attack-defense co-evolution study; independent replication of a previously observed cross-stage failure pattern.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19829v1

Evidence:
- DARWIN evaluates a cross-play matrix between evolving attacker stages and guardrail checkpoints. A later `Attack-150` achieves `84%` ASR against the earlier `Guard-50` checkpoint.
- On a held-out set of complex jailbreak strategy families excluded from the evolving training loop, ASR against the guard falls from `54%` to `26%` as the guard evolves.
- Across HarmBench under the same stated query budget, the attack strategy pool scaling from 50 to 200 raises ASR on GPT-5.5 from `73.5%` to `92.5%` and on YuFeng-XGuard from `82.2%` to `99.0%`.

Failure implication: static guardrail evaluation can become stale as the attacker strategy space expands. Cross-stage evaluation is needed to separate "defender got stronger" from "current attacker is weak"; the held-out trend is a positive control showing that continual defense can still generalize rather than merely memorize historical attacks.

Scope guard: the paper does not report multiple independently initialized attacker-training seeds per frozen defender or a full matrix of independently trained equal-budget best responses. Therefore it is useful cross-stage evidence but does **not** satisfy the strict seeded exploitability-matrix frontier.
