## New claims

### FE-069 — A defense that looks strong under transferred/non-adaptive prompt-injection attacks can collapse when the attack is optimized with that exact defense in the loop
**Status:** primary Google DeepMind technical report, matched Gemini 2.0 defense × attack-family table.

Primary source:
- Shi et al., *Lessons from Defending Gemini Against Indirect Prompt Injections* (Google DeepMind, 2025-05-18): https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Security%20and%20Privacy/Gemini_Security_Paper.pdf

Evidence:
- The report evaluates the same Gemini 2.0 system, eight defenses, and three attack families under non-adaptive and defense-aware adaptive optimization.
- For **Spotlighting**, Actor-Critic ASR rises from **0.180 to 0.824** (+64.4 percentage points; 4.58×), Beam Search from **0.130 to 0.648** (+51.8 pp; 4.98×), and TAP from **0.752 to 0.822** (+7.0 pp).
- Across the full 8-defense × 3-attack grid, adaptive attacks are equal to or stronger than the non-adaptive counterpart in **16/24** cells: Actor-Critic in 5/8 defenses, Beam Search in 5/8, TAP in 6/8.
- This is not a claim that every defense degrades adaptively: Warning and the User Instruction Classifier remain strong in several cells, and some adaptive optimizers underperform transferred attacks.

Failure implication: non-adaptive defense scores can materially overstate practical robustness when an attacker can observe and optimize against the deployed defense. Scope is Google DeepMind's Gemini 2.0 indirect-prompt-injection threat model, attack implementations, defenses, and query budgets; it does not establish a universal adaptive-collapse rate.

### FE-070 — A weak adaptive attack can create a second false sense of security: adaptive optimization sometimes underperforms transfer because the defense makes the optimization objective noisy or non-smooth
**Status:** same primary Google DeepMind report; explicit author analysis plus matched table.

Evidence:
- The report explicitly notes cases where non-adaptive transferred attacks outperform defense-aware adaptive attacks and attributes this to a phenomenon analogous to **gradient obfuscation**: the defended model can provide a poor optimization objective while remaining vulnerable to a worst-case attack.
- The matched table contains concrete reversals. Under the **Perplexity** defense, Beam Search ASR is **0.978 non-adaptive vs 0.680 adaptive**, and TAP is **0.998 vs 0.624**. Retrieved-data-classifier Actor-Critic is **0.682 vs 0.050**.
- The authors therefore recommend transfer attacks as a sanity check rather than treating “adaptive attack failed to optimize” as evidence of robustness.

Failure implication: the correct evaluation principle is not simply “replace static attacks with adaptive attacks.” A credible robustness evaluation should include both defense-aware optimization and transferred attacks, because failure of the optimizer can masquerade as defensive strength. This claim is limited to the tested Gemini 2.0 defense/attack combinations and the paper's interpretation; it does not prove that every adaptive-under-transfer reversal is gradient obfuscation.

### FE-071 — Reusing an old attack suite across a model update can dramatically overstate robustness even when the old suite was strong on the predecessor
**Status:** primary Google DeepMind report; model-version transfer vs fresh adaptive evaluation.

Evidence:
- Before adaptive evaluation of Gemini 2.5, Google tested **1,799 prompts** whose triggers had been optimized against Gemini 2.0. The same set had **92% ASR on Gemini 2.0** but only **18% on Gemini 2.5**.
- The authors state that stopping there would have led them to incorrectly conclude Gemini 2.5 was more robust than it really was.
- Fresh defense-aware adaptive evaluation of Gemini 2.5 then found large residual vulnerabilities: on the held-out **Calendar Event / non-JSON / Passport** scenario, TAP reached **94.6% ASR** and Actor-Critic **59.6%**; Beam Search did not succeed in that table. Calendar was excluded from adversarial-training tools.
- This is not a direct paired estimate of 18% versus 94.6% on identical prompt sets: the 18% is an aggregate over 1,799 transferred prompts spanning five tools and three private-data categories, while 94.6% is a specific fresh adaptive Calendar scenario. The failure claim is therefore about evaluation-suite staleness, not a 76.6-point causal delta.

Failure implication: a predecessor-optimized red-team corpus can become stale after a model update and systematically miss vulnerabilities discoverable by re-optimization. Model-version safety claims should re-run attack search against the new target rather than only replaying previously successful triggers.

### FE-072 — A representation-level jailbreak defense with 0% ASR in its reported GCG settings was bypassed by a defense-aware objective, but a redesigned defense provides a positive control showing adaptive robustness can improve
**Status:** primary arXiv v1; Llama-3-8B representation-level jailbreak setting.

Primary source:
- Derya & Sunar, *Revisiting JBShield: Breaking and Rebuilding Representation-Level Jailbreak Defenses*, arXiv:2605.03095v1 (2026-05-04): https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03095

Evidence:
- The original JBShield configuration reported **0% ASR** against its GCG-based settings on Llama-3-8B.
- A defense-aware GCG variant, JB-GCG, changes the objective to jointly suppress the refusal direction and regularize JBShield's toxic-concept score. Across five tested JBShield configurations it achieves **46.2% average ASR**, peaking at **53.4%**; against JBShield-M it reaches **30.7%** in the evaluated settings.
- The bypass persists across several JBShield recalibrations (`N=10–50`), supporting the paper's claim that the weakness is structural rather than just a single threshold choice.
- Positive control: the authors redesign the defense as multi-layer **Representation Trajectory Verification (RTV)**, report AUROC **0.99** against JB-GCG, then white-box adapt against RTV; the strongest tested adaptive attack reaches only **7% ASR** at roughly **13×** computational cost.

Failure implication: a zero-ASR result against an attack whose objective does not directly target the deployed representation-level detector is not evidence of adaptive robustness. At the same time, this study does **not** support the broader claim that representation defenses are futile: its RTV positive control is substantially harder for the tested white-box attacker to bypass, at much higher cost.
