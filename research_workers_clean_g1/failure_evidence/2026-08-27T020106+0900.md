## New claims

### FE-142 — Shared-role self-play can under-train the attacker and overstate defensive robustness; role separation improves adversarial pressure but current evidence is still architecture-confounded
**Status:** arXiv v1, 8 May 2026; Qwen2.5-{3B,7B,14B}-IT comparison of Self-RedTeam versus Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play (ABS).

Primary source:
- La Malfa et al., *The Attacker in the Mirror: Breaking Self-Consistency in Safety via Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play*, arXiv:2605.08427v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08427

Evidence:
- Against the **same base Qwen defender family** on 100 Harmful Behavior examples, Self-RedTeam attackers reach `18%` ASR at 3B and `9%` at 7B, while ABS attackers reach `35%` and `24%` respectively.
- In cross-play against the opposing method's equal-scale defender, Self-RedTeam attackers reach `6%` ASR at both 3B and 7B, while ABS attackers reach `17%` and `18%`.
- Self-RedTeam also lowers XSTest benign compliance more than ABS at all three reported scales: 3B `0.932` vs `0.952`, 7B `0.888` vs `0.915`, 14B `0.968` vs `0.980` (base scores `0.960/0.912/0.984`).
- The paper's first-order analysis explains a plausible mechanism: when one parameter vector serves both attacker and defender, defender updates also alter the attacker and vice versa, creating self-consistency pressure rather than independent best-response pressure.

Failure implication: convergence or low in-training ASR under a shared attacker/defender policy can be a misleading robustness signal if the attacker is being weakened by the same parameter updates that strengthen/refusal-train the defender. Cross-play against a role-separated attacker is a useful stress test.

Scope guard: this is **not** the strict single-factor parameter-sharing ablation sought in the frontier. Self-RedTeam uses shared full-model fine-tuning while ABS freezes the backbone and trains separate LoRA adapters; optimization parameterization, effective trainable-parameter budget and some training dynamics therefore differ. The evidence supports a concrete shared-role failure mode in the reported setup, not a universal causal effect of parameter sharing alone.

### FE-143 — High attack-success rank is only weakly aligned with the value of an attack as safety-training data in a real LLM setting
**Status:** July-2026 arXiv v1; defender-centric jailbreak evaluation.

Primary source:
- Zhou et al., *How Jailbreak Attacks Inform Safety Alignment: A Defender-Centric, Shapley-Based Evaluation of Jailbreak Contributions*, arXiv:2607.17152v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17152

Evidence:
- The primary abstract reports that across controlled utility landscapes and real LLM safety settings, attacker-centric ASR rankings are only weakly aligned with defender-centric utility: the downstream safety improvement obtained when attacks are used as red-teaming/defense data.
- The paper therefore separates two objectives that are often conflated: `break the current model` versus `produce data that most improves a later defense`.
- A secondary methodological review of the v1 paper locates the real-setting statistic at Spearman `rho=0.215` over `n=10` attack categories and notes rank changes such as Roleplay rising from ASR rank 9 to utility rank 2 while Optimized Suffix drops from rank 1 to rank 7. Because the primary PDF numeric table was not re-extracted in this invocation, these exact numeric/rank details are retained only as a locator pending primary-table verification, not as an independently verified headline value.

Failure implication: choosing red-team data solely by ASR can optimize for attacks that break the current model yet contribute little marginal information to safety hardening, while lower-ASR attacks may cover more useful failure modes. Red-team optimization and defense-data selection require different acceptance objectives.

Scope guard: the real-model evidence is small (`n=10` attack categories according to the independent review), depends on the paper's chosen defender-side utility and defense pipeline, and is not a universal theorem that ASR is always poorly correlated with safety-training value. This claim is narrower than the already-observed SPARED-style result that attacker win rate can diverge from downstream defender improvement: here the contribution is an explicit defender-centric utility/ranking formulation.
