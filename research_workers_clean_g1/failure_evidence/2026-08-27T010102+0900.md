### FE-139 — “Uncensored” is not a strong-red-team control: willingness to emit harmful text can be far weaker than learned adversarial strategy
**Status:** June-2026 arXiv v1; held-out AdvBench/HarmBench evaluation against the same GPT-4.1 defender.

Primary source:
- Bullwinkel et al., *Learning to Attack and Defend: Adaptive Red Teaming of Language Models via GRPO*, arXiv:2606.09701v1 (8 Jun 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09701

Evidence:
- Against GPT-4.1 on HarmBench, a Qwen2.5-14B attacker trained with AdvGRPO reaches `79.5%` ASR single-turn and `91.0%` multi-turn.
- The same-size Qwen2.5-14B family after GRP-Obliteration reaches only `25.5%` single-turn and `55.0%` multi-turn; Llama-3.1-8B Abliteration reaches `14.0%` single-turn and `13.5%` multi-turn; GPT-4o Unsafe-SFT reaches `6.5%` single-turn and `11.5%` multi-turn (or `35.5%` with Crescendo).
- The paper therefore separates “will produce unsafe content” from “can discover prompts that strategically elicit unsafe content from a defended target.”

Failure implication: evaluating a defender only with an uncensored/unaligned attacker can materially understate exposure to a strategically trained attacker. Removing refusal behavior is not, by itself, a valid proxy for red-team strength.

Scope guard: these are different attacker families/training recipes and model sizes in the paper, not a single-factor causal ablation of alignment removal versus adversarial training. The claim is limited to the reported GPT-4.1/AdvBench/HarmBench setup and does not imply AdvGRPO is universally stronger than every uncensored or hand-engineered attacker.

### FE-140 — Single-turn attack evaluation can materially understate exposure to response-conditioned multi-turn attackers, but the effect is not query-budget matched
**Status:** same AdvGRPO paper; same Qwen2.5-14B base attacker family and held-out defenders.

Primary source:
- Bullwinkel et al., arXiv:2606.09701v1, Tables 2/7 and appendix full transfer table.

Evidence:
- For Qwen2.5-14B AdvGRPO attackers on **HarmBench**, single-turn versus multi-turn transfer ASR is `67.5%→82.0%` on Phi-4-mini, `61.0%→88.5%` on Llama-3.1-8B, and `68.5%→86.0%` on Gemma-2-9B.
- On **AdvBench**, the corresponding pairs are `77.5%→90.8%`, `71.6%→92.5%`, and `69.1%→88.3%`.
- The multi-turn attacker observes defender responses and can adapt for up to five turns, so the evaluation changes both interaction structure and effective target-call budget.

Failure implication: a one-shot red-team suite can be an optimistic safety estimate for systems exposed to iterative attackers who observe refusals and adapt. A defensible report should state whether the attacker is single-turn, open-loop multi-turn, or response-conditioned closed-loop, and should not compare their ASRs as if query budget were equal.

Scope guard: this does **not** isolate response-conditioning from extra attempts, longer context, or the different multi-turn training curriculum. The result is an operational threat-model contrast, not a single-factor causal effect of “adaptivity.”

### FE-141 — Human-only red teaming can severely under-cover an automated attacker’s reachable failure set in a held-out prompt-injection arena
**Status:** July-2026 OpenAI GPT-Red publication; replicated indirect-prompt-injection arena on held-out scenarios.

Primary sources:
- Wallace et al., *GPT-Red: Automated Red Teaming via Self-Play at Scale*, arXiv:2607.26115 (28 Jul 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26115
- OpenAI, *GPT‑Red: Unlocking Self-Improvement for Robustness* (15 Jul 2026), https://openai.com/index/unlocking-self-improvement-gpt-red/

Evidence:
- In a replicated version of the Dziemian et al. indirect prompt-injection arena, on scenarios/goals distinct from GPT-Red training and targeting GPT-5.1, GPT-Red succeeds on `84%` of scenarios while human red-teamers succeed on `13%`.
- The paper/publication also reports generalization to held-out environments, defender models and harnesses, so the gap is not presented only as memorization of its training scenarios.
- Positive-control context: the same program reports that adversarial training with GPT-Red data improves later model robustness rather than merely finding failures, so this is not evidence that automated red teaming is futile.

Failure implication: “our human red team did not find a failure” is not a robustness certificate when automated search can explore orders of magnitude more variants and adaptively refine attacks. Human and automated red teaming should be treated as complementary coverage channels rather than interchangeable substitutes.

Scope guard: the `84%` versus `13%` comparison is specific to OpenAI’s internal replicated arena, GPT-5.1, the tested scenario set, success criteria and available red-team budgets. It is not a population estimate that automated red teaming always beats expert humans by 71 percentage points.
