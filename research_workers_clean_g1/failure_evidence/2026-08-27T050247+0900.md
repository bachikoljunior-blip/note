### FE-150 — An independent ACL 2026 evaluation reproduces the failure of static prompt-injection tests under defense-specific adaptive search
**Status:** peer-reviewed ACL 2026 primary paper + official code/docs; exact PIArena conditions only.

Primary sources:
- ACL paper: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1533/
- PDF: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1533.pdf
- Official code: https://github.com/sleeepeer/PIArena

Evidence:
- PIArena evaluates the same defense suite under Direct, Combined heuristic, and a defense-feedback-guided Strategy attack using Qwen3-4B-Instruct as attacker.
- Averaged across the paper's 11 PIArena datasets, No Defense ASR is `0.56` under Direct, `0.72` under Combined, and `0.99` under Strategy.
- PISanitizer shifts from `0.11` Direct / `0.04` Combined to `0.86` Strategy.
- SecAlign++ shifts to `0.21` Strategy ASR while retaining only `0.45` average utility under that attack.
- In the SQuAD-v2 search-attack comparison, the Strategy attack reaches ASR `0.85` against PISanitizer versus `0.05` for PAIR and `0.05` for TAP; against PIGuard it reaches `0.71` versus `0.15` / `0.14`.
- The attack is not a mere larger static template set: after warm-start rewrites it conditions further rewrites on whether the defense detected/sanitized the prompt, ignored it, or otherwise failed.

Failure implication: static or non-defense-specific prompt-injection tests can materially overstate robustness. This is an independent replication of the broader adaptive-attacker failure mode using a separate peer-reviewed platform and attack implementation.

Scope guard: the Strategy method also differs from Direct/Combined in search procedure and query use, so this is not a single-factor estimate of “adaptivity alone.” It is a threat-model comparison under PIArena's released defaults, not evidence that this exact strategy is universally strongest.

### FE-151 — Defense rankings and residual ASR change sharply across agentic benchmarks even when the same PIArena defense modules are ported across them
**Status:** peer-reviewed ACL 2026 Table 5; exact benchmark/model/attack mappings in the paper.

Evidence:
- PIArena ports the same defense modules into InjecAgent, AgentDojo, AgentDyn, and WASP.
- PISanitizer ASR is `0.03 / 0.07 / 0.07 / 0.38` across those four benchmarks.
- PromptGuard ASR is `0.00 / 0.22 / 0.28 / 0.02`.
- PIGuard ASR is `0.00 / 0.15 / 0.04 / 0.00`.
- DataFilter ASR is `0.00 / 0.08 / 0.03 / 0.27`, while its reported utility is `0.68` on AgentDojo but only `0.06` on WASP under the paper's static extraction protocol.
- The benchmark backends/attacks are not identical: InjecAgent uses Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with ENHANCED; AgentDojo/AgentDyn use GPT-4o with their important-message attack; WASP is reduced to a static single-page evaluation of 84 cases.

Failure implication: a defense's headline ASR on one agent benchmark is not a portable property of the defense. Benchmark/harness/task/backend differences can reorder which defenses appear effective and can expose utility costs invisible in the original setting.

Scope guard: because the benchmark backends, tasks and attack definitions differ, these numbers are not a causal estimate of benchmark identity alone. The claim is precisely that cross-benchmark transfer cannot be assumed without matched re-evaluation.

### FE-152 — ASR-only evaluation can reward a defense that destroys benign utility
**Status:** peer-reviewed ACL 2026 PIArena aggregate result.

Evidence:
- AttentionTracker is the only tested detection defense with near-zero average ASR against the adaptive Strategy attack, but the paper reports only about `15%` average no-attack utility for it.
- In the 11-dataset aggregate row, AttentionTracker's No-Attack utility is `0.15`; its attack-side outputs are often N/A because detected inputs produce no answer.
- By comparison, No Defense no-attack utility is `0.74` average.

Failure implication: minimizing attack success alone can select a defense that obtains safety by refusing or breaking ordinary task execution. Any optimizer/evaluator that treats low ASR as sufficient can misclassify denial-of-service behavior as robustness.

Scope guard: this is not a claim that AttentionTracker is intrinsically bad or that detection defenses cannot be useful. It is a metric-design warning under the PIArena task mix and utility definitions.

### FE-153 — When the injected task aligns with the target task, instruction-level defenses can lose the signal they are designed to detect
**Status:** peer-reviewed ACL 2026 NQ knowledge-corruption experiment; exact task-alignment setting only.

Evidence:
- On the NQ knowledge-corruption condition, No Defense has utility `0.43` and ASR `0.81`.
- PISanitizer remains at ASR `0.67`; SecAlign++ `0.58`; DataFilter `0.78`; PromptArmor `0.82`; PIGuard `0.81`.
- The authors explain that the injected content can reduce to disinformation with no explicit injected instruction, so instruction-level detection/sanitization lacks a separable malicious-instruction feature.
- Some detection defenses lower ASR only by withholding responses; AttentionTracker is `0.01` ASR but has `0.00` no-attack utility in this table, making it a degenerate positive control rather than evidence that content truth was verified.

Failure implication: a prompt-injection benchmark that assumes attacks contain instruction-like text can miss a harder content-integrity regime where malicious content is semantically aligned with the legitimate task. In that regime, low instruction-detection error is not equivalent to trustworthy answers.

Scope guard: this does not prove all RAG misinformation attacks are impossible to defend. The experiment tests the released NQ knowledge-corruption construction and the listed defenses; content verification or provenance-aware system defenses were not established as ineffective by this result.

### FE-154 — Short adaptive campaigns can make model-reliant prompt defenses look durable even when they later fail abruptly under sustained pressure
**Status:** arXiv primary study, April 2026; direct secret-extraction threat model only.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23887

Evidence:
- The study's attacker uses populations of 10 prompts per round, carries the top 5 forward, and mutates them using defender-response feedback across hundreds of rounds.
- In the initial 25-round campaign, model-reliant defenses leaked only low-to-moderate severity and none was yet fully compromised.
- In extended campaigns, every model-reliant defense reached severity `>=0.9` in under 300 rounds: input sanitization/security directive/delimiter failed at rounds `39/49/84`; instruction hierarchy and sandwich defense held until rounds `247/277` before abrupt critical breakthroughs.
- The sandwich defense's leak rate rose from `0.4%` in 25 rounds to `3.8%` over 277 rounds, while worst severity rose from `0.05` to `0.95`.
- The reported trajectory is two-phase: a long near-zero probing period followed by a sudden jump to critical severity.

Failure implication: short-run red-team success rates can substantially overestimate long-horizon robustness when an attacker can learn from repeated failures. Time/query horizon is an evaluation variable, and “nothing serious happened yet” is not evidence of a robust boundary.

Scope guard: this study targets exact recovery of known embedded system-prompt secrets with a heuristic substring-based severity score; it is not a general estimate for all prompt-injection consequences, indirect injection, or multi-turn conversational attacks.

### FE-155 — Deterministic output filtering is a positive control under exact-secret leakage, but its success cannot be generalized to fuzzy or encoded disclosure
**Status:** same arXiv study; stress-tested positive control with explicit limitation.

Evidence:
- Output filtering alone and the full stack including it were each run three independent times for 500 rounds; across six runs and `15,000` total attacks, no exact secret leak was observed.
- Removing only output filtering from the full stack produced `23/5,000` leaks (`0.5%`), though worst severity stayed at `0.10`, showing the remaining layers still limited damage.
- The authors explicitly note that both scoring and output filtering use hardcoded substring matching and can miss paraphrase, indirect disclosure, ROT13/base64, or character-by-character spelling.

Failure implication: deterministic post-response controls can outperform model-self-policing for a finite known secret set, but a perfect exact-match record should not be promoted to a general semantic exfiltration guarantee. Evaluation must match the protected output space and include transformations the filter is expected to stop.

Scope guard: this is a positive control, not evidence that output filtering is generally sufficient. The tested filter protects exact known substrings in a direct prompt-leak setting; broader semantic leakage and client-side bypasses remain outside the demonstrated guarantee.
