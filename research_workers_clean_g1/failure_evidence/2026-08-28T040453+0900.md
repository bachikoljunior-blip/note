## New failure evidence

### FE-295 — Security hardening can turn formerly successful agent runs into timeouts and wrong solutions rather than safe early abstention
**Primary source:** Dotan Davidovich, Yair Amar, Hai Rozencwajg, Or Hiltch, “Permission Denied: Policy-Graded Evaluation of Coding Agents in Hardened Environments,” arXiv:2608.02670v1, 2 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02670

On the 82 Terminal-Bench 2.1 tasks for which the authors establish a strict-policy solvability witness, moving from control to the NIST-derived-high runtime policy reduces success from **72.5% to 65.1%**. The extra failures do not primarily appear as clean refusal: timeout rises **12.0%→16.4%**, wrong-solution runs **12.8%→15.5%**, while early stops barely change **2.4%→2.7%**. Among successful matched bundle/task runs, hardening adds about **13% wall-clock, 14% tool calls, and 26% tokens**. Across all 12 tested bundles, strict-policy success loss and cost inflation are strongly bundle-dependent; headline extremes reach **−18.3pp success** and **+167.3% cost**.

**Failure implication:** an agent that behaves adequately in a permissive benchmark may not fail safely when deployment policy blocks familiar actions; it may instead consume most of the execution budget searching for workarounds and then time out or submit a wrong solution. A production evaluation should therefore measure policy-constrained success, timeout/wrong-answer mode, and budget inflation rather than treating policy denial as a benign stop.

**Scope guard:** Terminal-Bench 2.1, the paper’s 12 model–harness bundles, three tested policy levels, and its wall-clock classification rules only. The evidence does not imply security hardening is net harmful; it shows the operational cost and failure mode are heterogeneous and must be measured.

### FE-296 — Hardening benchmarks can falsely attribute impossible-policy tasks to model degradation unless task solvability is separately witnessed
**Primary source:** same as FE-295.

The strict policy makes **7 tasks blocked by design**, guaranteeing failure for every tested bundle. Removing those policy-impossible tasks and conditioning on the 82 witnessed-solvable tasks raises hardened success by **3.4–5.8pp** across bundles. The apparent control→hardened success drop shrinks from **7.1–18.3pp** on the full task pool to **2.4–13.5pp** on the solvable pool.

**Failure implication:** a benchmark can overstate “the agent became worse under safety controls” by counting tasks that the control contract intentionally makes impossible. Policy-constrained evaluation needs a separate task-feasibility/witness channel so model failure, policy-impossible work, and provider/verifier errors are not collapsed into one failure number.

**Scope guard:** this correction applies to the specific reference/witness procedure and policy definitions in the study. A solvability witness itself can be incomplete; the result supports separating feasibility from agent performance, not treating the paper’s witness set as universal ground truth.

### FE-297 — Semantically correct tool plans can be physically invalid under hard memory caps; prompt advice and retry are weak substitutes for an execution-boundary resource check
**Primary source:** Yinuo Wang, Yiyu Shi, “SkillEffect: Checked Lowering for Memory-Bounded Agent Tools,” arXiv:2608.17007v2, 22 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17007

Across six data-processing operator families, eager and bounded implementations return the same verified results while largest-input peak memory differs by **3.75–24.25× (median 8.45×)**. On 24 tasks under externally frozen 64–2048 MiB caps, direct eager execution completes **0, 0, 4, 20, 20, 24** tasks; the bounded-lowering reference completes **12 at 64 MiB and all 24 from 128 MiB onward**. At 128 MiB, the lowered tasks all succeed while the corresponding eager executions remain physically infeasible. In the model-plan construction study, simply pinning a resource clause constructs verifier-valid bounded programs for only **9/24** tasks, while ordinary retry with resource feedback succeeds for **4/24**. In an external smolagents harness, all **16/16 otherwise valid tool calls** choose eager access when no resource feedback is given.

**Failure implication:** “the model produced a correct algorithm” is not enough for a sandboxed automation: the same logical task can be impossible under the actual per-call resource envelope. Resource obligations should be enforced at tool dispatch by a checked physical plan/capacity boundary; prompting the model to remember a budget or retry after OOM is not a reliable enforcement mechanism.

**Positive control:** the checked bounded path completes all 24 tasks from 128 MiB upward and the paper’s admission/publication checks reject adversarial proposals in the tested relation registry.

**Scope guard:** six registered data-processing relation families, the paper’s caps/platform and audited lowering plugins. This is not evidence that arbitrary programs can be automatically lowered safely; the paper explicitly requires relation-specific audited plugins.

### FE-298 — A positive headline “self-evolution gain” can hide capability regressions, missing mechanism evidence, and improvements smaller than run-to-run variance
**Primary source:** Shuhan Xue et al., “PAST-Bench: Benchmarking the Foundations of Recursive Self-Improvement in Personal Agents,” arXiv:2608.04003v1, 4 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04003

PAST-Bench uses fresh-session paired persistence-on/off runs with the same prompt, grader, tool stack and seed, then separately checks write/read/update telemetry. Holding MiniMax-M2.7 fixed and varying agent frameworks, **nanobot** has Overall persistence gain **+0.13** while Procedural is **−0.06** and its mechanism-evidence score is **0.57**; **Hermes** has the same Overall **+0.13** but all four capability deltas are positive and Mech is **0.64**. **ZeroClaw** is Overall **+0.12** while Procedural is **−0.04**. **Hermes+** reports Overall **+0.15** but still has a small Procedural **−0.02**. For MiniMax-M2.7 across three runs, Hermes is **+0.13±0.04** and Hermes+ **+0.15±0.06**; the authors explicitly do not interpret the **+0.02** difference as a stable overall gain because it is smaller than run-to-run variation.

**Failure implication:** a recursive optimizer that selects changes from one aggregate “improvement” score can accept systems that regress on an important capability, improve through the wrong persistence pathway, or win only inside run noise. Acceptance should therefore require per-capability outcomes, evidence that the intended save→retrieve→update path was actually used, repeated-run uncertainty, and rollback on protected dimensions.

**Scope guard:** PAST-Bench’s 26 scenarios/204 episodes, its synthetic fresh-session task families and tested frameworks. Mechanism telemetry supports attribution but does not prove causal necessity; the paper itself calls for stronger artifact deletion/replacement interventions.

### FE-299 — Skill-retrieval precision can collapse by nearly an order of magnitude while downstream task success stays flat or improves
**Primary source:** Zhiyuan Jiang et al., “Demystifying Agent Skills: Why They Work—Until They Don’t,” arXiv:2608.14036v1, 14 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14036

In the execution-time skill-use experiment, increasing the candidate pool from 5 to 100 causes averaged actual-use precision to fall from **29.6% to 3.3%**, while downstream success moves only **36.4%→39.3%**. For the reported Codex pairing, actual-use precision drops **42.3%→5.9%** while task success rises **35.4%→42.0%**. The authors conclude that exact annotated ground-truth skill invocation is neither sufficient nor strictly necessary for success; agents may inspect multiple artifacts and related skills can provide partial procedural support. Offline top-1 retrieval and explicit selection degrade much less than execution-time precision, so these are distinct measurements rather than one monotonic pipeline.

**Failure implication:** optimizing a skill system against “did it retrieve/use the canonical skill?” can be almost orthogonal to the actual task objective. Retrieval quality should be tracked, especially for confusable distractors, but optimizer acceptance must remain grounded in downstream verified execution and should not assume one canonical artifact is the only valid causal route.

**Scope guard:** SkillsBench and the paper’s reported agent/model pairings and skill-use parser. Stable downstream success despite low precision does not mean retrieval errors are harmless in every task; it shows retrieval precision alone is not a sufficient proxy for outcome quality.
