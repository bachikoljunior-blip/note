## New claims

### FE-056 — Closed-loop hardening can improve aggregate ASR while worsening specific attack categories through training-distribution shift
**Status:** peer-reviewed 2026 primary source; single-seed headline experiments on RoLLMRec/LLM4Rec offline benchmarks, not a universal adversarial-training claim.

Primary source:
- Shehmir & Kashef, *Automated adversarial red-teaming for evaluating robustness in LLM-based recommender systems*, Discover Artificial Intelligence 6, 759 (published 2026-06-03): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-026-01546-z

Matched evidence:
- The attacker/judge/defender loop adapts for up to 8 rounds: each round generates 160 candidates across 16 categories; successful attacks are fed back as positive examples and failures as negatives; bypasses drive discriminator retraining/signature expansion/threshold tightening.
- Aggregate ASR improves substantially after hardening: Amazon Books **42.4% -> 18.5%**, MovieLens **48.1% -> 12.3%**, Yelp **52.0% -> 16.2%**, while reported block-level FPR stays <=0.6%.
- Despite the aggregate improvement, Yelp **recursive injection regresses 8.3% -> 14.6%** and **semantic manipulation 2.0% -> 10.2%** after hardening. The paper also reports Amazon few-shot poisoning rising from a pre-hardening 22.2% to a substantially higher post-hardening rate, and MovieLens chain-of-thought manipulation ending at 14.3% post-hardening; exact paired pre/post values for those latter two were not promoted here when not explicit in the accessible text.
- The source attributes the Amazon few-shot regression to retraining-data imbalance: many token-level/direct-instruction bypasses shift the discriminator boundary toward those classes, making structurally different few-shot poisoning harder to catch. Proposed bounded mitigations are reservoir sampling across all 16 categories and per-category early stopping.
- A stronger suffix optimizer also finds more residual vulnerability after the same hardening: GCG retains **11.5%** ASR versus **3.2%** for GPT-4-generated suffixes; both were 100% before hardening in the reported Amazon comparison.
- Headline experiments use **one random seed (42)**; offline simulated-user benchmarks only.

Failure implication: an aggregate hardening curve can conceal category-level regressions, and adaptive retraining can move the decision boundary in ways that improve common attack classes while making underrepresented structures worse. Robustness claims should retain per-category trajectories and attacker-strength provenance rather than treating aggregate ASR as monotonic protection. This does **not** show iterative adversarial hardening is generally harmful; the same experiment has large aggregate gains and low measured FPR.

### FE-057 — Control-protocol feedback and resample aggregation can reverse the safety effect; detailed monitor rationale can be exploitable while max-score resampling improves safety
**Status:** May 2026 arXiv v2 primary experimental source; BashArena 1.1 control setting with specified models and 0.3% audit budget, not a general theorem about retrying/resampling.

Primary source:
- Lucassen & Kaufman, *Retrying vs Resampling in AI Control*, arXiv:2605.26047v2: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26047

Matched evidence:
- Setting: BashArena, Claude Opus 4.6 as the untrusted model and MiMo-V2-Flash as the trusted monitor; headline audit budget **0.3%**.
- Retrying with detailed feedback exposes the previous action/suspicion score/monitor rationale. The authors find a strategic attacker can use that rationale to construct sneakier attacks, **negating the retrying safety gain**; retrying without detailed feedback is harder to exploit directly, though the paper leaves open stronger attack-selection strategies.
- Pure resampling does not insert rejected-action feedback into the ongoing context. Drawing **5 samples per step** and auditing using the **maximum** suspicion score raises safety from about **61% to 71%**, a paired gain up to **+10.2 pp** with **95% CI [+6.1,+14.3]**, with no usefulness cost in that comparison.
- A selective two-stage variant recovers **+6.2 pp** while drawing about **0.4 extra samples/step** rather than 4.
- The direction of score aggregation matters: in the headline data, **max aggregation helps and min aggregation hurts**, opposite to the earlier Ctrl-Z setting. This is therefore direct evidence against assuming a resampling heuristic transfers unchanged across control settings.
- Executing the least-suspicious resample adds only **+3.9 pp** over information-only max aggregation in the N=2 paired comparison, **95% CI [-2.1,+10.9]**, so the interval includes zero; with a perfectly consistent attacker the paper's identified execution mechanism would provide no gain.
- Under an intentionally inconsistent attack, changing only the honest-score/aggregator regime produces the opposite incentive: with shuffled honest data and min aggregation, safety drops **-20.5 pp** at N=2 (95% CI [-30.2,-10.9]) and **-37.9 pp** at N=5 (95% CI [-46.8,-29.0]), while the headline max-aggregation regime does not show that degradation.

Failure implication: a control protocol can leak useful adaptation signal through monitor feedback, and even the sign of a resampling heuristic's effect depends on the score-correlation/aggregation regime and attacker incentives. Evaluations should red-team the exact feedback channel and aggregation rule rather than importing a protocol component from a previous benchmark. This does **not** imply all retrying is unsafe or all max aggregation is optimal outside this tested setting.

## Bounded positive control — iterative co-training can improve both attack strength and target robustness
- Guo et al., *MTSA: Multi-turn Safety Alignment for LLMs through Multi-round Red-teaming*, ACL 2025: https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1282/
- The red-team attack strengthens across iterative rounds on AdvBench (reported average ASR **58.08 -> 61.10 -> 63.92** from R1 to R3), while the Zephyr target's ASR under the MTSA-R3 attack drops **74.50 baseline -> 42.50 MTSA-T1 -> 30.50 T2 -> 23.50 T3**. Out-of-domain ActorAttack and RedQueen ASRs also decline across T1–T3 in the reported table.
- This is retained specifically to prevent overclaiming FE-056: iterative attacker/defender co-training can yield monotonic robustness gains in other tested settings, so the category regressions above motivate disaggregated monitoring rather than rejection of co-evolution as a family.
