### FE-052 — Defense robustness can collapse when the attacker is adapted to the specific defense; several near-zero static ASRs become 71–100%
**Status:** peer-reviewed USENIX Security 2026 primary source; defense-specific adaptive attacks, not one universal attack and not a claim about every deployment.

Primary source:
- Nasr et al., *The Attacker Moves Second: Stronger Adaptive Attacks Bypass Defenses Against LLM Jailbreaks and Prompt Injections*, USENIX Security 2026: https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/nasr
- Paper PDF: https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity26-nasr.pdf

Matched evidence from Figure 1 / paper evaluation, comparing weaker/static attack evaluation against an attack adapted to the named defense:
- Spotlighting: **28% -> 99% ASR**.
- Prompt Sandwich: **21% -> 95%**.
- RPO: **0% -> 99%**.
- Circuit Breaker: **8% -> 100%**.
- StruQ: **62% -> 100%**.
- MetaSecAlign: **5% -> 96%**.
- Protect AI: **15% -> 90%**.
- PromptGuard: **26% -> 94%**.
- PIGuard: **0% -> 71%**.
- Model Armor: **0% -> 90%**.
- Data Sentinel: **0% -> 80%**.
- MELON: **0% -> 89%**.
- The paper also reports a human-red-teaming condition where a case with **0% static ASR reaches 100%** under human adaptation.

Failure implication: static or generic-attack ASR can be a severe overestimate of defense robustness when the attacker moves second and adapts to the defense. The result does **not** establish that every possible deployment of these defense families has the same ASR, nor that a single attack transfers unchanged across them; the attacks are adapted to the tested defense/threat model.

### FE-053 — Adaptive prompt injection can erase most of the safety gain from monitor-based AI-control protocols, and a resampling heuristic can create a best-of-n evasion channel
**Status:** ICLR 2026 peer-reviewed primary source; exact quantified result below is APPS Backdooring under the paper's control protocol and attacker/monitor models, with BashBench providing a second benchmark-level qualitative/curve replication.

Primary sources:
- Terekhov et al., *Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols*, ICLR 2026: https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/7f75c2be9528d0154c041446b2f6c0c9-Abstract-Conference.html
- arXiv/PDF: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09462 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09462

Matched evidence:
- Setup uses an attacker that knows the control protocol, trusted monitor, and thresholds; monitoring budget is `c=0.02`. Benchmarks include APPS Backdooring (552 problems) and BashBench (257 tasks).
- On APPS with GPT-5 attacker and GPT-4o-mini monitor, the paper reports Trusted Monitoring safety **0.33 without prompt injection -> 0.19 with adaptive prompt injection**; Upfront Auditing is **0.15**. Thus the monitor-based protocol's safety gain over upfront auditing shrinks from **0.18 to 0.04** in this tested condition.
- The zero-shot attacker generates a new injection per query rather than copying a provided in-context attack template.
- Defer-to-Resample (DTR) samples extra candidate actions and executes the least suspicious one. Under a per-sample evasion probability `p`, this induces a best-of-n evasion channel with probability `1-(1-p)^k`; the paper tests up to `k=3` and reports that under prompt injection DTR can underperform Defer-to-Trusted (DTT), adding control cost without clear safety benefit. BashBench curves likewise show prompt injections subverting DTT/DTR across tested thresholds.
- A max-suspiciousness resampling variant removes this particular min-score best-of-n effect in the shown setting, serving as a bounded intervention/positive control; the authors caution ordering remains attacker-dependent.

Failure implication: monitor-based safety estimates are not robust to an attacker that directly optimizes against the monitor, and apparently conservative resampling can increase the attacker's chance of finding an evasive candidate if the protocol executes the least suspicious sample. This does **not** show monitor-independent control protocols fail, nor that every resampling rule backfires.

### FE-054 — Defensive fine-tuning can increase unsafe delegated actions under contextual manipulation, while reducing explicit attack recognition
**Status:** 2026 arXiv v1 primary experimental source with public code; independent extension of defense-backfire evidence, but not a replication of the exact AgentDojo/tool-budget intervention in FE-049.

Primary sources:
- Abdelnabi & Bagdasarian, *AI Agents May Always Fall for Prompt Injections*, arXiv:2605.17634: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17634
- HTML experimental paper: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17634v1
- Author-linked code: https://github.com/compass-group-tue/prompt_injections_so_back

Matched evidence:
- The study constructs **4,200 scenarios / 8,400 email variants** emphasizing contextual/authority manipulation rather than explicit prompt-injection vocabulary.
- Prompt Guard / Prompt Guard 2 discriminate authority-asserting versus neutral text only weakly in the reported setup, **AUROC 0.43–0.59**.
- In the same conditional-delegation task, permissive policy yields >97% send rates for both baseline LLaMA-3.3-70B-Instruct and SecAlign-70B. Under a restrictive delegation policy, however, baseline sends **54.0%** of authority-asserting and **49.8%** of neutral messages, while SecAlign sends **88.2%** and **85.1%**, respectively.
- On LLMail-Inject suspicious-content reporting, both tested models send 0%, but explicit attack flagging is **89.8% baseline vs 54.6% SecAlign** (−35.2 pp).
- A context-parameter adaptive attack over 150 scenarios, 3 streams and up to 15 iterations achieves **96.7%** success versus **0.67%** for an unoptimized baseline; first-email success is **61.4%**. Without reoptimization, transfer success is **63.3% GPT-5.4, 89.3% GPT-5.2, 92.6% Gemini-3-Pro**.
- Adding relevant history lowers out-of-scope execution from **29.9% -> 7.0%** for Claude Sonnet 4.6, **32.2% -> 24.2%** for Gemini-3-Pro, and **36.2% -> 24.5%** for GPT-5.4 while reported in-scope utility remains roughly in the low-to-mid 90s, giving a bounded contextual-grounding intervention.

Failure implication: a defense trained for canonical prompt-injection patterns can backfire under a different contextual-manipulation threat model, increasing delegated action rates while recognizing fewer attacks. This does **not** establish SecAlign is globally worse than the baseline or invalidate its original tested threat model; the failure is scoped to the paper's email-assistant/conditional-delegation contexts.

### FE-055 — Environmental-interference experiments can show large downstream degradation while still being unusable for component-level causal attribution because several factors changed together
**Status:** August 2026 preprint primary source; useful as a negative result about evaluation design/provenance, not evidence that any single decoy mechanism caused the entire effect.

Primary source:
- *SysEvolve: An AI-native, safe, autonomous adversarial attack-defense co-evolutionary system*, arXiv:2608.15012: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15012

Matched/held-fixed elements explicitly stated for the L1 comparison include the same manifest, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, L1 context, 300-turn limit, 3600-second timeout, seed 1 and temperature 0. However, the compared conditions also change **parallelism (8 vs 4), 43 decoys, omission of one topology hint, and fixed ordering**, so the authors explicitly say it is not a pure decoy-effect estimate.

Reported outcome difference in the current none vs high-interference configurations (50 cases each):
- all-three completion: **2/50 -> 0/50**;
- objective achieved: **1/50 -> 0/50**;
- timeout: **6/50 (12%) -> 19/50 (38%)**;
- mean agent time: **1417.6 s -> 2428.5 s (+71.3%)**;
- median agent time: **1091.7 s -> 2536.5 s (+132.3%)**;
- high-interference runs record some decoy interaction in **50/50**, direct contact in **38/50**.
- The paper separately reports stage progression on 50 L2 cases: Kimi reaches targets 1/2/3 in **22/18/16** cases versus DeepSeek **19/10/6**; conditional continuation from target 1 to target 3 is **72.7% vs 31.6%**, showing that final success can compress materially different failure stages.

Failure implication: environment/configuration perturbations can preserve initial entry while substantially worsening downstream progress, timeouts and latency, but a benchmark comparison that changes decoys, parallelism, hints and ordering simultaneously cannot support a clean causal claim about decoys alone. This is primarily evidence for matched-design discipline and stage-wise failure measurement, not a claim that decoys themselves cause the full observed regression.
