## New failure evidence

### FE-199 — At fixed long-context length, harmful-goal position alone changes jailbreak success
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04707v2

`Jailbreaking in the Haystack` holds the synthesized long context at 20k tokens and systematically moves the same harmful goal through the prompt. Across Llama-3.1 and Qwen2.5, front placement gives the highest ASR while end placement lowers ASR; Qwen shows a middle-position regime where non-refusal can be high while actual harmful completion is low. The paper repeats the position-only manipulation in BrowserART with a GPT-4o-based browser agent: the prompt content is held fixed and only the goal location changes, and front placement again raises ASR across direct, GCG, human-rewrite, and prefix attack families.

**Failure implication:** long-context safety evaluation can confound content, length, and position. A deployment/eval that always places the user goal at one location can materially misestimate risk at another location even with the same content and context length.

**Scope guard:** the paper reports the controlled direction robustly but the HTML text does not tabulate exact Figure 3/4 position-cell values. This claim therefore preserves the direction and matched design, not an invented percentage-point effect. It does not imply front placement is always worse for every model or prompt architecture.

### FE-200 — A production self-evolving guardrail still failed a low-resource-language threat after two autonomous rounds
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08471

SESG reports a real production evolution from April–June 2026. It autonomously closed 14 of 15 new threat scenarios, but one low-resource-language attack failed two rounds and was handed to the human blue team because the synthesis ensemble was weak in that language and the generated batch inherited the weakness. The system also deliberately requires human confirmation before opening a round, explicitly because that gate keeps the evidence bundle clean and makes poisoning harder; the deployed loop is currently single-turn only.

**Failure implication:** self-evolving defense does not remove coverage bottlenecks in its own data-generation stack. A loop can be fast and generally successful yet still fail systematically where its generator/validator competence is weak, and fully automatic triggering would add a poisoning surface the production system intentionally avoids.

**Positive control / scope guard:** this is not evidence that continual guardrail adaptation is ineffective. The same production system closed 14/15 threats, retrained from retained data to preserve prior competence, and uses deploy/regression gates. The negative claim is limited to the observed low-resource-language miss and the stated single-turn/trigger constraints.

### FE-201 — One-class agent monitors can fail completely under deployment transfer even on a matched plan
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464

`Real-Time Detection and Repair of LLM Agent Failures` evaluates a one-class telemetry monitor on live agent traces. Calibrating on Qwen2.5-7B and transferring to Llama3.1-8B on a matched task/tool/injector plan gives AUROC `0.527` with healthy false-alarm rate `0.75`; recalibrating on the Llama target itself raises AUROC to `0.885`. The authors therefore state that the healthy null must be collected under the exact serving distribution (model, temperature, toolset, probe pattern, latency regime).

**Failure implication:** a monitor checkpoint that looks model-agnostic at the feature level can become essentially chance after a serving-model change. Monitor calibration is deployment state, not a one-time artifact.

**Scope guard:** this is one telemetry-monitor family, two local model families, and the paper's matched task plan. It does not imply every monitor requires full retraining after every model update; it establishes that zero-shot calibration transfer failed badly here.

### FE-202 — Assuming a strong escalation judge can more than double the estimated recovery of a monitor pipeline
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464

The study's controlled escalation analysis originally stipulates a judge with `p_detect=0.90` and `p_false=0.02`. A real Gemini-2.5-Flash judge measured on 161 labelled prompts instead has `p_detect=0.548` (95% CI `0.44–0.65`) and `p_false=0.052` (`0.020–0.13`). Substituting those measured rates while holding the rest of the five-seed pipeline fixed preserves call savings but drops detection recovery from about `82%` to `43%`; the original escalation hypothesis is supported in `0/5` seeds under the measured judge.

**Failure implication:** monitor-funnel safety estimates can be dominated by an assumed downstream reviewer. A simulated/idealized judge should not be treated as an implementation detail when it determines end-to-end detection recovery.

**Scope guard:** this is the paper's specific monitor+judge escalation model and labelled subset. It does not establish that Gemini judges generally have these rates outside the tested prompts or rubric.

### FE-203 — High AUROC can coexist with near-useless low-FPR detection, and slow/semantically plausible failures can be structurally invisible
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02464

On imported AFTraj-2K, the channel-max ESN reaches episode AUROC `0.745` (best hybrid `0.760`) yet detects only `0.048` of failures at the paper's 5% false-alarm budget. The horizon analysis explains part of this: `61%` of failures end within three steps of onset, where neither sequential monitor has time to accumulate signal. The same paper reports that slow goal drift evades every tested per-step-surprise monitor and that a wrong-but-well-formed numeric value is undetectable from telemetry by construction without an external reference.

**Failure implication:** ranking metrics such as AUROC do not certify a useful deployment operating point, and generic anomaly monitors cannot detect failures that do not perturb their observable channel. Evaluation should report low-FPR recall, available post-onset horizon, and mechanism-specific blind spots.

**Positive control / scope guard:** deterministic checks and grounding verifiers repair some blind spots: on a disjoint 120-episode held-out set, frozen checks catch `93%` of failures with coverage and `0/64` false positives. This is not a claim that telemetry monitoring is useless; it supports heterogeneous verification rather than a single universal detector.

### FE-204 — Removing access to early task-defining tokens can collapse long-horizon behavior while weights and user script remain fixed
**Primary source:**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12922

`When Attention Closes` applies an inference-time sliding-window mask while holding model weights, system/user scripts, and response sampling protocol fixed. Under a 4096-token window, goal-token attention reaches the numerical floor at predictable crossover turns. On Mistral-7B, 5-fact recall is near 100% before crossover but ends near `45%` at turn 50; the controlled-complexity task ends at `11.2%` recall while the default full-attention condition stays at 100%. Across architectures at turn 50, recall under closure is `0.0%` for Llama-3.1-8B, `6.0%` for Qwen2.5-7B, `19.2%` for Mixtral-8x7B, and `45.2%` for Mistral-7B. Policy compliance is architecture-dependent: Mistral falls `60.8%→6.2%`, while Llama is nearly invariant (`79.7%→78.2%`).

**Failure implication:** long-horizon agents can retain apparently normal local behavior while losing effective access to early task/policy state; the failure magnitude is strongly architecture- and task-dependent. Context-window/harness changes therefore cannot be treated as neutral serving optimizations.

**Scope guard:** the strongest causal interpretation is for Mistral, which was natively trained with sliding-window attention. For Llama/Qwen/Mixtral the authors explicitly frame the forced mask more as an architectural perturbation than a native-state probe. These results do not imply default full-attention production models will exhibit the same turn-50 collapse.
