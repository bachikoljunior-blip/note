## New claims

### FE-166 — A brittle string-matching grader can move a defense utility score by about three points without any model change
**Status:** primary arXiv v1 (2026-08-24), author-identified scoring defect in a prompt-injection benchmark.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23873
- https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23873v1

Evidence:
- In the Semantic Overlays paper's SEP re-analysis, the published substring witness rule scores utility at `93.0%` and clean false positives at `1.7%`.
- A strict word-boundary rule removes mid-word false matches but drops measured utility to `89.7%`.
- The authors' corrected boundary-plus-inflection rule restores utility to `92.3%` while keeping clean false positives at `0.7%`.
- Thus the evaluator alone moves the measured utility by `3.3pp` between two plausible rules, without changing the underlying model outputs.

Failure implication: safety/utility conclusions based on lexical witness matching can be materially scorer-dependent. Evaluators should validate morphology/token-boundary behavior and retain raw outputs so scoring can be replayed after grader repair.

Scope guard: this is a defect in SEP's witness-scoring family as analyzed by this paper. It does not imply all benchmark graders have comparable error magnitude, and the corrected rule is still a task-specific heuristic rather than a general semantic oracle.

### FE-167 — Byte-identical prompts on the same frozen setup can produce a roughly 2–3 point benchmark swing
**Status:** same primary Semantic Overlays paper; paired rerun reliability analysis.

Evidence:
- On a `300`-item paired subset, the authors reran the same frozen configuration on byte-identical prompts.
- `27.9%` of completions differed between the two runs.
- `2.0%` of witness verdicts flipped, producing a `2.6`-point SEP-score gap.
- The paper therefore treats roughly `2` points as the run-to-run noise floor for that benchmark/configuration.

Failure implication: small defense deltas can be swallowed by generation/evaluation stochasticity even when prompts and model state are unchanged. Single-run differences near this scale should not be interpreted as stable improvement without repeated seeds or paired uncertainty.

Scope guard: the measured noise floor is specific to the reported SEP setup and decoding configuration; it is not a universal 2-point threshold for deterministic decoding or other benchmarks.

### FE-168 — Same-source cross-validation can overstate malicious-prompt detector generalization by 8–16.5 AUC points
**Status:** primary arXiv v2, published at the ICLR 2026 AIWILD workshop; 105K-sample static distribution-shift study.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14161
- https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14161v2

Evidence:
- Across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-3-27B, Qwen-3.5-4B, and Qwen-3.5-2B, standard CV AUC is `0.996 / 0.999 / 0.998 / 0.998`, while Leave-One-Dataset-Out (LODO) AUC is `0.912 / 0.920 / 0.845 / 0.829`.
- The resulting CV-to-LODO gap is `8.4 / 8.0 / 15.4 / 16.5pp` respectively.
- Per-dataset held-out-test versus LODO accuracy gaps reach `25.4pp` (Jayavibhav) and `20.1pp` (Mosscap).
- A dataset-identity classifier reaches `96.6%` on SAE features, and `28–44%` of top SAE features are classified as dataset-dependent shortcuts.
- Seven mitigation attempts fail to close the gap: aggregate LODO changes range from `-1.75pp` for targeted subspace projection to only `+0.7pp` for SAE shortcut zeroing; DANN is `+0.4pp` aggregate but unstable across datasets/seeds.

Failure implication: a detector can look nearly perfect under ordinary CV by learning dataset provenance rather than transferable attack semantics. Safety classifier validation should hold out entire source datasets/domains in addition to random examples, and should report per-source failure rather than only pooled AUC.

Scope guard: LODO measures static source shift, not adaptive adversarial robustness. The study does not claim LODO is sufficient for deployment safety, and its tested mitigations used reasonable but non-exhaustive hyperparameter tuning.

### FE-169 — Binary attacker ASR can improve while the semantic quality of successful attacks collapses
**Status:** primary arXiv v1 CHASE co-evolution study; single base defender family.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05523
- https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05523v1

Evidence:
- Across three co-evolutionary iterations, the authors report attacker raw ASR rising approximately `0.51 → 0.62 → 0.76` averaged across BeaverTails and JailbreakBench.
- Over the same iterations, mean attack Convincingness drops `4.79 → 2.83 → 2.38` (out of 5), while the attacker's StrongREJECT-based semantic success score falls `0.47 → 0.24 → 0.22`.
- The paper attributes this divergence to increasing defensive pressure: the attacker finds more nominal bypasses but cannot preserve bypass effectiveness and intent fidelity simultaneously.
- The method's multiplicative reward was introduced specifically to suppress intent drift/reward hacking that can inflate a simpler success metric.

Failure implication: maximizing a binary or weak ASR can select attacks that no longer faithfully preserve the harmful goal, making the red-team appear stronger while its useful adversarial pressure deteriorates. Attacker evaluation should pair bypass rate with intent fidelity/specificity and audit successful examples semantically.

Scope guard: these dynamics are from CHASE's single Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct defender setup and its own reward/judge definitions. They do not establish that ASR and attack quality always diverge under adversarial training.
