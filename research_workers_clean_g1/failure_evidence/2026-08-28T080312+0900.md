## New failure evidence

### FE-312 — In-context capability routing can collapse as the tool/agent/skill registry grows, even when the downstream reranker remains competent
**Primary source:** Nazib Sorathiya, Daniel Zhang, Bardiya Akhbari, “Enrich-Retrieve-Rank: Scaling Capability Discovery Beyond In-Context Routing,” arXiv:2608.22695, 24 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22695

Across registries scaled from `N=10` to `N=7,278`, the paper reports in-context routing Match@1 falling from **0.85 to 0.12**. A fixed retrieve-then-rank pipeline falls more gently from **0.81 to 0.39**, and once retrieval contains the correct item its reranker still places that item first **0.70–0.87** of the time. In the Nova Micro sweep, the crossover where retrieval becomes preferable is around `N≈500`. At full scale, the pipeline beats Search&Pick by **6.5 pp** Match@1 at roughly half the cost and is reported as **70× cheaper than Full-Ctx**.

**Failure implication:** adding capabilities to an agent can lower realized capability if discovery remains a prompt-menu problem. A high-quality planner or tool executor cannot compensate for a missing capability that never enters the shortlist. Registry scale and retrieval recall therefore need to be treated as first-class execution bottlenecks, not merely prompt-engineering details.

**Scope guard:** the paper’s fixed enrichment/retrieval/ranking configuration and tested agent/tool/skill registries. It does not establish that retrieve-then-rank universally solves discovery, nor that the same crossover holds for every model, metadata regime, trust policy, or registry distribution.

### FE-313 — A monitor can have excellent ranking AUPRC yet be nearly useless for low-false-alarm early intervention
**Primary source:** Xinmiao Huang et al., “PrefixGuard: From LLM-Agent Traces to Online Failure-Warning Monitors,” arXiv:2605.06455, 7 May 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06455

PrefixGuard’s strongest monitors reach AUPRC **0.900 / 0.710 / 0.533 / 0.557** on WebArena / τ²-Bench / SkillsBench / TerminalBench. Yet the authors explicitly find that WebArena, despite the best ranking score, is not well separated for low-false-alarm early warning: its risk signal mostly becomes useful close to terminal failure. By contrast, τ²-Bench and TerminalBench retain more actionable early alerts even though their raw AUPRC is lower.

The paper formalizes this as an observability problem: some failed prefixes are only positive in hindsight because evidence distinguishing them from successful prefixes has not yet appeared in the observed trace. Better ranking cannot recover information that is absent from the prefix.

**Failure implication:** optimizer or monitor selection should not use AUPRC/AUROC alone as an intervention criterion. It needs a deployment-constrained metric such as failed-trajectory recall and time-to-alert at a fixed false-alarm budget, plus a representation-level observability diagnostic.

**Scope guard:** PrefixGuard’s four benchmark splits, fixed failure horizon and calibrated prefix-warning protocol. This is not evidence that AUPRC is generally useless; it is evidence that ranking quality and actionable early-warning utility are distinct quantities.

### FE-314 — “One security policy across frameworks” does not imply equivalent enforcement: some host hooks are blocking and others are only observational
**Primary sources:** Kai Wang et al., “ClawSentry: A Progressive Multi-Tier Security Monitor for Safeguarding Autonomous LLM Agents,” arXiv:2608.21101, 21 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21101 ; public reference implementation `Elroyper/ClawSentry`, https://github.com/Elroyper/ClawSentry

The paper reports substantial tested protection—for example Codex/GPT-5.4 SkillInject contextual ASR **39.55%→2.61%** while TSR changes **83.78%→83.05%**, and residual ASR across five work agents of **9.09–15.03%** from **33.5–49.7%** unprotected. But the public implementation documentation makes the enforcement boundary explicitly host-dependent.

In the documented Codex integration, managed `PreToolUse(Bash)` preflight and `PermissionRequest(Bash|apply_patch|Edit|Write|mcp__.*)` can gate actions, while non-Bash `PreToolUse`, `PostToolUse`, `UserPromptSubmit`, `Stop`, `SessionStart`, `PreCompact`, and `PostCompact` remain advisory/observational by default. Kimi CLI similarly supports blocking `PreToolUse`/prompt hooks but treats several lifecycle hooks as observation-only and lacks native rewrite/true defer parity. Gemini and OpenClaw carry their own host/configuration caveats.

**Failure implication:** a framework-agnostic policy abstraction is not evidence of framework-agnostic control authority. Safety cases should inventory the exact pre-effect blocking hook for every action class and mark observation-only paths separately; policy equivalence without enforcement-capability equivalence can create false assurance.

**Scope guard:** the ClawSentry release documentation and integrations inspected here. This does not negate the reported benchmark improvements and does not establish that the listed hosts are unsafe in general; it identifies a documented mismatch between policy portability and enforcement parity.

### FE-315 — Concurrency caps alone can fail under shared-API contention; uncoordinated per-agent retry can amplify the outage
**Primary source:** Justice Owusu Agyemang et al., “HiveMind: OS-Inspired Scheduling for Concurrent LLM Agent Workloads,” arXiv:2604.17111, 18 Apr 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17111

Across seven injected-contention scenarios with 5–50 concurrent agents, uncoordinated execution fails at **72–100%** once contention is present, while the coordinated proxy reduces failures to **0–18%**. The replay-11 ablation is especially diagnostic: full HiveMind has **0%** failure; removing transparent retry raises failure to **63.6%**; using admission control alone yields **81.8%** failure. The paper also explains why independent per-agent retry can create a synchronized “thundering herd” after 429/502/reset failures.

A useful positive control is the local-server validation: at 10 agents, both direct and HiveMind modes are **0% failure** on the tested Ollama and MLX servers because those servers queue gracefully. Therefore the failure is not “parallel agents are inherently unreliable”; it is a resource-coordination failure that appears under particular provider/error regimes.

**Failure implication:** concurrency limit, retry, rate-limit accounting, backpressure and circuit behavior should be evaluated together against the actual shared provider. A local retry library in every worker is not equivalent to a coordinated retry budget and can make correlated transient faults worse.

**Scope guard:** HiveMind’s injected API contention/error model, seven scenarios, replay-11 ablation, and two local-server checks. Do not generalize the 72–100% rates to providers or servers that internally queue or have materially different limits.

### FE-316 — Rich traces and a sophisticated debugger still leave most exact root-cause locations unresolved
**Primary source:** Kunlun Zhu et al., “AgentDebugX: An Open-Source Toolkit for Failure Observability, Attribution, and Recovery in LLM Agents,” arXiv:2607.18754, 21 Jul 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18754

On the Who and When benchmark, DeepDebug reaches the best tested strict agent-and-step attribution accuracy but only **28.8%** on qwen3.5-9b, versus **21.7%** for the strongest single-pass baseline. Its closed-loop debugging is nevertheless useful: on GAIA it repairs **13 of 73** failed tasks in one rerun, compared with **4–6** for three decoupled self-correction baselines, increasing overall accuracy **55.8%→63.6%**.

**Failure implication:** observability/replay logs are not the same as reliable causal attribution. Automated recovery should preserve uncertainty about root cause and verify the proposed repair against the environment rather than treating the debugger’s chosen agent/step as ground truth.

**Scope guard:** the paper’s Who and When and GAIA settings and tested open-weight debugger backbones. This does not show debugging is ineffective; the recovery improvement is a positive control. It shows that even the strongest tested method leaves more than 70% of strict exact locations unresolved on the cited setting.
