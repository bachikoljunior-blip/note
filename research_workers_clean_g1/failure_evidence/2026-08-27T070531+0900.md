## New claims

### FE-161 — Terminal failure rate can hide a >2× difference in attacker effort and early survival
**Status:** primary arXiv paper (2026-06-23), 45-seed automated red-team study; intentionally weak Llama 3.1 8B target.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24589
- https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24589

Evidence:
- AdversaBench uses 45 seeds: 15 each for reasoning, instruction-following, and tool-use.
- All three categories eventually reach confirmed failure (`15/15` each), so terminal binary failure rate is `100%` for every category.
- Yet instruction-following needs `2.4` attacker iterations on average versus `1.1` for reasoning and tool-use.
- After iteration 1, `60%` of instruction-following seeds remain unbroken versus `10%` in the other two categories.

Failure implication: endpoint ASR can erase material differences in attack difficulty and time-to-break. Evaluations under sustained pressure should report an iteration/query-to-failure distribution or survival curve in addition to terminal success.

Scope guard: the target is intentionally weak, only 45 seeds are studied, expected behavior was author-constructed rather than independently human-validated, and the result does not establish the same iteration ratios for frontier systems.

### FE-162 — Compute-limited red-team evaluation can systematically overstate safety when right-censored samples are naively treated as safe
**Status:** primary arXiv v5; population-metric/censoring experiment with oracle full-horizon comparison.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06605
- https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06605v5

Evidence:
- DAPRO defines unsafe-event rate and restricted mean time-to-unsafe over a maximum horizon of `t_max=200` turns.
- The paper notes that stopping a conversation early creates right censoring and that treating early-stopped samples as safe increases perceived safety.
- In the population-level experiment, oracle outcomes are first computed with unlimited horizon, then allocation methods are constrained to an average budget of `20` turns per sample and evaluated over `50` random calibration/test splits.
- On the Toxicity setting with Qwen 2.5 14B Instruct as target and attacker, the unweighted uniform estimator “severely underestimates true vulnerability.” Proper inverse-censoring reweighting removes the bias; adaptive allocation reduces variance and observes more unsafe events under the same resource constraint.

Failure implication: a limited red-team budget is not just a power issue; if censored runs are scored as safe, it is a directional measurement bias. Population safety estimates need explicit censoring treatment, not only a fixed per-prompt query cap.

Scope guard: the zero-bias/minimal-variance statements are for the paper's estimator and experimental/replayed conversation setups under its assumptions. This does not prove DAPRO is optimal under arbitrary non-exchangeable production traffic or miscalibrated event models.

### FE-163 — Provider/API failures can masquerade as safety refusals and create a false model-level safety signal
**Status:** primary arXiv paper; 400-run autonomous pentest study with full-log correction.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30096
- https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30096

Evidence:
- The study runs four models `100` times each against an identical isolated honeypot while holding prompt, orchestrator, and target fixed.
- Claude Sonnet 4 encountered `91/1,135` HTTP 529 `overloaded_error` API calls during a documented capacity event, truncating `39/100` Claude runs.
- An earlier draft had classified these events as a `42%` stochastic safety-refusal rate; full-log audit showed they were upstream service failures, and the claim was withdrawn.
- The paper explicitly recommends logging API error categories separately from model refusal behavior.

Failure implication: transport/provider availability, tool/runtime failure, policy refusal, and model completion behavior must be distinct outcome classes. Otherwise infrastructure instability can be mistaken for improved safety or stochastic alignment behavior.

Scope guard: this is an authorized offensive-security scaffold with one-shot authorization re-prompting; it does not establish refusal rates under other framings. Cross-model behavior comparisons are additionally confounded by an unintended Anthropic temperature difference (`1.0` vs `0.3` for the other providers).

### FE-164 — Very high harmful-query safety alignment can coexist with roughly one-third benign-query over-refusal
**Status:** primary arXiv v1 (2026-08-20), unified black-box cross-dimensional evaluation; not yet independent replication.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20554
- https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20554v1

Evidence:
- aiXamine evaluates more than `120` LLMs in `5,000+` black-box runs across nine services.
- Its abstract reports a model scoring `99.3` in Safety Alignment while refusing about one in three benign queries.
- The main leaderboard identifies Claude-3.5 as the `99.3` Safety Alignment model and gives it an Over-Refusal score of `67.2`, consistent with the paper's stated safety–utility trade-off.

Failure implication: maximizing a harmful-query refusal/alignment score alone can select a system that materially degrades benign utility. Safety acceptance should therefore include paired benign-query false-refusal/availability criteria rather than a one-axis safety threshold.

Scope guard: these are aiXamine's normalized service metrics and benchmark mix, not a claim that Claude-3.5 refuses one-third of all real-world benign traffic. The paper is currently arXiv v1.

### FE-165 — Off-policy strong-to-weak distillation can preserve the base architecture while collapsing adversarial robustness from 56.9 to 2.6
**Status:** same primary aiXamine arXiv v1; controlled same-base-architecture comparison plus representational diagnostic.

Evidence:
- Llama3.1-8B has aiXamine Adversarial Robustness `56.9`; the R1-distilled Llama3.1-8B derivative scores `2.6` on the same service.
- The paper compares same-base-architecture derivatives and reports that R1-style off-policy distillation omits on-policy KL correction, whereas IDA/Cogito-style distillation retains more on-policy correction.
- On 50 AdvGLUE-SST2 adversarial prompts, last-layer representation cosine similarity to the base model is `0.094±0.161` for the R1 Llama derivative versus `0.834±0.013` for IDA-Llama, consistent with a major geometry shift.

Failure implication: capability/reasoning distillation can radically alter robustness even when base architecture is held fixed; post-distillation safety cannot be inherited from the base model. Robustness needs fresh evaluation and, where possible, on-policy correction/positive controls.

Scope guard: this is an observational controlled-family comparison plus the authors' theoretical mechanism, not a randomized training ablation that changes only the on-policy KL term. The `56.9→2.6` value is specific to aiXamine's adversarial-robustness service and these Llama derivatives.
