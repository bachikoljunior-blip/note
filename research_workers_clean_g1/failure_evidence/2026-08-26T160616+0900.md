## New claims

### FE-103 — The sign of attacker/defender parameter coupling is design-dependent; a single slogan about shared-parameter self-play is unsafe
**Status:** peer-reviewed Findings of ACL 2026 ablation, interpreted jointly with own prior FE-098; matched within SSP but not a cross-paper single-factor causal estimate.

Primary source:
- Wang et al., *Be Your Own Red Teamer: Safety Alignment via Self-Play and Reflective Experience Replay*, Findings of ACL 2026, DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.findings-acl.933: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.933/

Evidence:
- SSP normally uses one unified model for attacker and defender. Its `w/o UM` ablation instead splits the two roles into independent models while keeping them interacting through self-play and co-evolving jointly.
- Across all 24 reported backbone × attack cells, the unified SSP has lower final defense ASR than `w/o UM`. Examples: Qwen2.5 GCG **5.8 → 1.7**, PAIR **6.9 → 2.4**, TAP **6.3 → 1.4**; Vicuna PAIR **13.8 → 6.7**; Llama3 GCG **5.5 → 1.5**; Mistral3 SAA **6.3 → 2.7** (`w/o UM` → full SSP).
- The paper reports experiments averaged across three independent trials with p≤0.01 significance testing for its experiments.
- This points in the opposite operational direction from own FE-098's ABS study, where role-separated attackers were stronger than shared-parameter Self-RedTeam attackers against the same base Qwen target. The two studies measure different outcomes and use different training recipes, so the disagreement is itself the useful evidence.

Failure implication: treating parameter sharing as intrinsically good or bad for safety self-play is not justified. It can suppress attacker pressure in one recipe yet improve final defender robustness in another; update coupling, role objectives, replay and optimization geometry must be evaluated explicitly.

Scope guard: `w/o UM` changes the number/placement of trainable model instances and Table 4 reports final defense ASR, not a controlled attacker-strength transfer metric. FE-098 also differed in LoRA/full-finetuning and frozen-base structure. Therefore this is evidence of design dependence, not proof that unified models are superior or inferior in general.

### FE-104 — Recent-opponent-only self-play can cycle through catastrophic forgetting; replay of historical failures materially changes the dynamics
**Status:** peer-reviewed Findings of ACL 2026 ablation plus training-dynamics analysis; three-trial experimental setup.

Evidence:
- Removing reflective experience replay worsens final ASR relative to full SSP in every one of the 24 reported backbone × attack cells. For example, Qwen2.5 `w/o Replay` is **4.7/5.5/5.2/4.3/4.9/5.8%** across GCG/PAIR/TAP/DAN/DI/SAA versus full SSP **1.7/2.4/1.4/1.3/2.1/3.0%**.
- The appendix directly compares training curves. Without an experience pool, attacker and defender rewards are almost mirrored, oscillate around 0.5, and the authors diagnose overfitting to the current opponent state and a cycle of catastrophic forgetting with no clear upward trend.
- With the experience pool, historical failures are revisited, the simple mirrored pattern disappears, and the reported reward curves show an overall upward trend.

Failure implication: evaluating a self-play safety loop only at the current opponent can hide non-transitive forgetting. A system may appear locally balanced while repeatedly losing robustness to earlier attack modes. Historical failure replay or a separate frozen-opponent evaluation set is therefore an important acceptance check.

Scope guard: this is one SSP framework and one replay mechanism. It does not prove that every self-play method requires experience replay, nor that the plotted reward trend directly equals deployment robustness.

### FE-105 — Near-zero static prompt-injection ASR can coexist with catastrophic per-defense adaptive failure, and defense rankings can reverse
**Status:** May 2026 primary arXiv preprint with explicit fresh per-target adaptive GRPO evaluation, equal compute per prompt-injection cell, held-out test examples, security and utility metrics.

Primary source:
- Shah, Brinkmann & Angell, *Mitigating Adaptive Attacks against Reasoning Models with Activation Consistency Training*, arXiv:2605.28467: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28467

Evidence:
- The paper trains adaptive attackers directly against frozen defended targets rather than reusing static attack templates. For prompt injection, the attacker is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct + rank-16 LoRA and the reported schedule is **1,200 GRPO steps / about 19,200 suffix–target-response pairs per cell**, explicitly an equal-compute budget; evaluation uses best-of-8 suffixes on held-out examples.
- Qwen3-8B + BCT looks nearly solved under static suites: **0.4% OPI, 2.5% AlpacaFarm, 4.0% SEP ASR**. Yet its fresh adaptive attacker reaches **94.4% test ASR [88.4, 97.4]**. ACT on the same base is **0.7/0.7/2.7%** static but **59.3% [49.8, 68.1]** adaptive.
- GPT-OSS-20B shows a ranking reversal. On all three static suites BCT is lower-ASR than ACT (**4.4 vs 29.6**, **27.6 vs 39.3**, **14.7 vs 21.7**), but under adaptive attack BCT is worse: **57.4% [48.0,66.3]** versus ACT **37.0% [28.5,46.4]**.
- The appendix further states that adaptive evaluation uses a **fresh GRPO attacker trained against each defended target**. In the jailbreak setting it is even more explicit: the attacker is trained **from scratch against each (target, defense) pair** on 15 seen behaviors and evaluated on 85 held-out behaviors, with Wilson intervals.
- Utility is reported alongside adaptive ASR, preventing universal refusal from automatically counting as successful defense.

Failure implication: fixed-template safety evaluation can both understate residual vulnerability and mis-rank defenses. Re-optimizing an attacker against the actual frozen defense is not a stress-test luxury; in these tested cells it changes the substantive conclusion.

Scope guard: the prompt-injection adaptive attacker uses AlpacaFarm-style suffix attacks and a specific RL recipe; static and adaptive protocols are not identical prompt sets. The paper does not report repeated independent attacker-training seeds or a full attacker×defender transfer matrix, so it still falls short of a complete exploitability estimate.
