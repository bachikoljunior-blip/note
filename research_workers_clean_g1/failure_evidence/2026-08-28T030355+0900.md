## New failure evidence

### FE-294 — Summaries can preserve the words of a blocker while weakening its force; semantic retention is not operational state preservation
**Primary source:** Yiheng Sun, Huifei Wang, Yancheng Zhu, Zhenyu Li, Zebin Zhao, Yifan Yuan, “When ‘Must’ Becomes ‘Maybe’: Constraint Weakening in LLM Agent Workflows,” arXiv:2608.24569v1, submitted 25 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24569

The study conditions on correct upstream identification of an action blocker, then varies only how that state is handed off to a downstream executor that sees the resulting artifact. Across 1,296 controlled synthetic episodes, direct handoff preserves every blocker, while compression, plan assimilation, convergence, ownership deferral, and precedent substitution repeatedly transform binding prerequisites into caveats or non-binding considerations. The ordinary handoff-compression condition reports **100.0% blocker deactivation and 54.2% forbidden action**. Restoring all four state fields—prerequisite, authority, fallback, and execution consequence—raises preservation to **100.0%** and reduces forbidden action to **0.0%**. A fixed-artifact downstream-verification intervention also drives forbidden action to **0.0%** even while artifact deactivation remains **95.3%**, separating state preservation from runtime containment.

**Failure implication:** a durable automation can mention an unresolved requirement and still silently weaken “must resolve before execution” into “something to consider.” This is a different failure from forgetting. Summaries, plans, tickets, memories, and handoff notes need an explicit operational-state schema that preserves authority and execution consequence, or an independent downstream gate that re-enforces the prerequisite at commit time. For long-running optimization systems, compressing state text without preserving binding semantics can convert safety/ownership/rollback conditions into advisory prose while appearing semantically faithful.

**Scope guard:** this is a very recent arXiv v1 controlled synthetic study. The 100%/54.2%/95.3% figures apply to its constructed blocker scenarios, handoff transforms, and executor setup; they are not production rates. The positive controls show that full structured state or downstream verification repairs the tested failure, so the evidence does not imply summarization is generally unsafe.
