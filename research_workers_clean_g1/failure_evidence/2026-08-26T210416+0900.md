### FE-124 — Replaying an attack that a prior checkpoint learned to block can falsely credit a hardened model; fresh per-checkpoint re-optimization can immediately recover a successful attack
**Status:** August-2026 arXiv preprint; direct matched checkpoint/re-attack evidence in open-weight safety-removal setting.

Primary source:
- Russinovich et al., *Fool’s Gold: Defensive Deception Against Safety-Removal Attacks on Open-Weight Models*, arXiv:2608.17202v1 (17 Aug 2026).

Evidence:
- The evaluation contract explicitly re-derives an adaptive attack for every defended checkpoint and treats failure to find an accepted attack as attack-search failure rather than defense evidence.
- In a Qwen3.5-9B chain, an adaptive search first found an accepted attack at trial 33 with refusal `0.00`, judged compliance `0.50`, KL `0.295`.
- After one preference-hardening round, replaying that previously accepted attack produced refusal `1.00`, which would make the checkpoint appear to have blocked the exploit if evaluation stopped there.
- A fresh adaptive derivation against the hardened checkpoint was nevertheless accepted with judged compliance `0.625`, KL `0.628`; after another retraining step, the old attack again failed while another fresh attack was accepted at KL `0.877`.

Failure implication: `attack once, reuse everywhere` can manufacture a false monotonic hardening signal. For adaptive threat models, each frozen checkpoint should face a fresh best-response search rather than only a replay corpus.

Scope guard: this is a specific open-weight safety-removal/representation attack chain on Qwen3.5-9B, not evidence that every defense or jailbreak family has the same dynamics. The paper does not provide the full independently-seeded attacker×defender transfer matrix required by the strict frontier.

### FE-125 — Weak attack-success heuristics can mistake refusal suppression for harmful compliance, so failure to obtain a validated attack is not evidence of a robust defense
**Status:** same August-2026 primary source; direct attack-acceptance failure analysis.

Primary source:
- Russinovich et al., arXiv:2608.17202v1.

Evidence:
- The authors report multiple internal sweeps in which refusal-keyword style metrics indicated a successful safety-removal direction even though the resulting answers did not achieve judged harmful compliance.
- On Qwen3.5-27B, eight self-derived candidates produced no fully accepted attack. A public-recipe abliteration-derived direction reached judged compliance `0.688` but still failed the paper’s complete acceptance conjunction.
- On Qwen3.5-9B, a 48-variant self-derived sweep passed no full acceptance condition; compliance stayed at or below `0.25` and refusal never fell below roughly `0.25–0.31`.
- The paper therefore gates every claimed attack through a registered multi-part acceptance contract and explicitly records rejected/near-miss attacks as search failures rather than positive defense results.

Failure implication: red-team strength can be overestimated when an attack objective optimizes a proxy such as refusal tokens rather than validated harmful behavior; conversely, a failed search cannot by itself certify the defender.

Scope guard: these are attack-search diagnostics in the paper’s safety-removal setting. They do not establish that refusal-based metrics are always invalid; they show that proxy success and substantive compliance can diverge materially.

### FE-126 — Holding the judge model fixed does not make the evaluation stable: a holistic rubric can rate decoy and truthful outputs almost identically while a decomposed critical-element rubric separates the failure
**Status:** same August-2026 primary source; same pinned judge snapshot, different scoring contract.

Primary source:
- Russinovich et al., arXiv:2608.17202v1.

Evidence:
- The evaluation pins `gpt-4.1-2025-04-14` as judge.
- With a holistic quality rubric, the judge assigns approximately `8.36/10` to plausible-but-false decoy answers versus `8.30/10` to truthful answers, so the holistic score does not distinguish the deception target.
- A decomposed critical-element rubric exposes the fatal-flaw signal that the holistic score misses.
- Human calibration on a predecessor corpus reported `82%` agreement on fatal flaws and `70%` agreement within ±1 denial point, providing a positive but imperfect calibration control.

Failure implication: evaluator identity/version is not enough provenance. Rubric decomposition and human calibration can materially alter whether a failure is visible even when the underlying judge snapshot is unchanged.

Scope guard: the calibration is not perfect and is reported on a predecessor corpus. This does not prove the decomposed rubric is universally correct; it shows the holistic rubric is inadequate for this particular deception target.

### FE-127 — High refusal on original biosecurity prompts can collapse under meaning-preserving paraphrase, while some model families and specialized detectors remain robust
**Status:** August-2026 arXiv v2; matched original-vs-paraphrased stress test on a specialized biosecurity/protein-design funnel.

Primary source:
- *A Blind Spot in Alignment: Quantifying Biosecurity Risks in Large Language Models*, arXiv:2608.02684v2 (5 Aug 2026).

Evidence:
- On the same 50-prompt stress set, GPT-5 paper-specific FHR rises from `2.0%` on original prompts to `52.0%` under synonym substitution, `50.0%` under persona framing, `40.0%` under intent concealment, and `44.0%` under keyword removal.
- GPT-5-Mini rises from `0.0%` to `40.0% / 46.0% / 24.0% / 58.0%` under the same four strategies.
- Positive control: Claude-3.5-Sonnet and Claude-Opus-4.6 remain at `0.0%` FHR across the reported strategies, so paraphrase brittleness is not universal across all tested models.
- General-purpose guardrails show analogous support fragility: ShieldGemma-9B drops from `50.9%` detection on originals to `0.0%` under all four paraphrase strategies; Qwen3Guard-8B drops from `52.0%` to `0–4%`; Llama-Guard-3-8B falls from `84.5%` to as low as `0%` under intent concealment.
- Positive control: the specialized BioSafe-Guard is substantially more stable, from `98.9%` original detection to `96% / 96% / 92% / 80%` across the four strategies.

Failure implication: apparent safety on the canonical surface form can be a support artifact. Evaluation should include semantically equivalent surface transformations and report whether specialized controls transfer better than general guardrails.

Scope guard: FHR is the paper’s own funnel metric, not generic harmfulness/ASR. Each cell has 50 prompts; this is a specialized biosecurity setting and cannot be generalized to all safety domains.

### FE-128 — Optimizing an inference-time safety intervention on ASR alone can select a pathological solution that destroys utility or the intended steering effect
**Status:** August-2026 arXiv preprint; single-method constraint ablation for inference-time steering vectors.

Primary source:
- *Could Inference-Time Interventions Preserve Alignment? Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible*, arXiv:2608.08383v1 (9 Aug 2026).

Evidence:
- In the reported Llama-8B Corrigible `alpha=1` ablation, the full constrained method gives `ΔASR +1.57`, intended-effect change `+1.75`, and `ΔFRR -1.80` relative to the unsteered baseline.
- Removing only the FRR constraint makes ASR look better (`ΔASR +0.21`) but raises false-refusal rate by `+13.20` points.
- Removing the effect constraint gives `ΔASR -0.67` while intended effect becomes `-1.34` and FRR rises `+9.80`.
- Removing both constraints looks slightly better on ASR (`ΔASR -0.70`) while intended effect collapses to `-4.15` and false-refusal rate explodes by `+50.43` points.
- Positive control: the paper’s constrained optimization explicitly preserves the intended steering effect and FRR while sharply reducing the original steering safety cost.

Failure implication: a safety optimizer can appear to improve an attack-success metric by suppressing benign behavior or the intervention’s intended functionality. ASR, false-refusal, and task/effect preservation must be joint acceptance criteria.

Scope guard: this is a specific inference-time steering-vector ablation; it does not establish the same numeric tradeoff for post-training or all activation interventions.
