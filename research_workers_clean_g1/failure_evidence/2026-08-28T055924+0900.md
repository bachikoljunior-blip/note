## New failure evidence

### FE-304 — Rewinding only the agent’s reasoning is not enough; long-horizon recovery needs aligned world-state restoration, but external side effects remain outside the recovery boundary
**Primary source:** Yu Zhuang et al., “AgentRewind: Recoverable Execution for Long-Horizon LLM Agents,” arXiv:2608.14380, 14 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14380
**Detailed public extraction used for component numbers:** https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14380

AgentRewind records aligned checkpoints of agent context and a controlled environment, then resumes from an earlier checkpoint while carrying forward a compact memory of the failed branch. On the reported GPT-5.4 / MettleBench configuration, forward-only Continue reaches **62.2%** success, Restart-with-experience **78.0%**, and full AgentRewind **87.8%**. The component ablation is more diagnostic: removing environment rewind drops success to **43.9%**, removing context rewind to **65.9%**, and removing rewind memory to **51.2%**. Thus a “fix the prompt/reasoning and continue” recovery can remain trapped by a corrupted world, while restoring the world without removing stale reasoning or retaining lessons can also fail badly.

The paper’s recovery boundary is explicitly controlled local state (principally the workspace filesystem). Network requests, external-service calls, remote state, payments, messages and other effects outside that controlled boundary are not undone by the rewind. The paper also relies on external validation to identify stalled execution; it does not establish robust autonomous rewind triggering under noisy or absent validation.

**Failure implication:** a long-running automation should align checkpointed reasoning/context with checkpointed mutable world state, but must not interpret local rollback as rollback of external effects. Recovery needs an effect ledger/commit boundary for irreversible actions in addition to context/environment rewind. A recovery mechanism should also separately validate the signal that decides *when* rollback is warranted.

**Scope guard:** MettleBench’s 82 long-horizon engineering tasks, the paper’s controlled workspace environment, tested models/harnesses, and the reported ablations. The exact ablation percentages above were read through a detailed public extraction of the paper because the arXiv HTML table was not directly available in this run; treat those numbers as secondary-extracted until independently read from the primary PDF/artifact.

### FE-305 — Checkpoint restore can defeat ordinary idempotency because an LLM may re-synthesize a semantically identical action with a new request identity
**Primary source:** Yusheng Zheng et al., “ACRFence: Preventing Semantic Rollback Attacks in Agent Checkpoint-Restore,” arXiv:2603.20625, 21 Mar 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20625
**Detailed public extraction:** https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.20625v1

ACRFence identifies “semantic rollback”: after restoring an agent to a pre-effect checkpoint, the LLM may regenerate the same intended irreversible operation with a different UUID/idempotency key or subtly different request fields. A server-side duplicate detector that assumes byte-identical retries then treats the retried action as new. In the paper’s Claude Code + Qwen3-32B proof of concept, a crash-triggered restore produced duplicate transactions in **10/10** trials versus **0/10** without checkpoint restore. A separate Authority Resurrection proof of concept reused stateless single-use authorization after rollback in **2/2** trials; stateful server-side consumption rejected the reuse.

The proposed mitigation records irreversible effects and enforces “replay-or-fork” semantics at the tool boundary: a semantically equivalent retry should replay the cached result rather than re-execute, while intentional divergence must explicitly fork. Importantly, the paper does **not** yet implement and systematically evaluate ACRFence itself; the analyzer-LLM comparator remains an unmeasured attack/error surface.

**Failure implication:** syntactic idempotency keys are insufficient for nondeterministic agents if the agent can mint a fresh key after restore. Durable automation needs an authority/effect identity that survives rollback and binds semantically equivalent retries to the already-committed effect. “Checkpoint-safe” should therefore be tested with crash-after-success and restore/replan paths, not only deterministic retry tests.

**Scope guard:** small proof-of-concept counts, one tested CLI/model pairing, simulated MCP services, and conversation-level restore. This establishes the failure mechanism, not a production incident rate or the effectiveness of the unimplemented ACRFence design.

### FE-306 — Near-perfect trace replay does not imply useful failure prediction, and dumping the full learned workflow can perform worse than a compact local projection
**Primary source / author project page:** Seonglae Cho et al., “Automata from Agent Traces: Failure and Next-Step Prediction,” arXiv:2608.23670, 24 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23670 ; author project https://seonglae-agentstategraph.hf.space/

Across twelve public agent-trace datasets, compact FSMs with **7–43 states** replay held-out traces at **>=0.997 fitness**. Yet raw replay fitness by itself has failure-prediction AUROC near **0.50**: successful and failed traces can both fit the learned workflow almost perfectly. Failure information appears only after decomposing behavior into per-state features and transition surprise; those features reach held-out AUROC up to **0.941** and **0.894** on the safety-labeled ATBench condition (repeated-CV mean **0.864 ± 0.024**). On SWE-agent, an online structural monitor fires around **32%** of trace completion with **85.9% precision / 95.5% recall**, potentially avoiding much of the remaining failed-run compute.

The same work also shows a context-interference failure: on tau2-bench retail, passing the LLM the **entire FSM** reaches **52.2%** top-1 next-action accuracy, roughly AWM’s **52.9%**, while a minimal projection containing next-action probabilities and a few likely continuations reaches **65.1%**. Adding even more multi-step graph context falls to **49.2%**.

**Failure implication:** “the execution fits a known workflow” is not evidence that it is succeeding. Observability should preserve anomaly/failure features, not only replayability. Likewise, a learned workflow/memory object should not automatically be materialized wholesale into model context; selective local projection can outperform the full structure. This supports evaluating context delivery by downstream action quality rather than by amount of retained structure.

**Scope guard:** the twelve public datasets, the paper’s activity-extraction function and FSM construction. The method can degrade when the action space has weak sequential structure or becomes too large/sparse; topology learned from observed traces does not represent the agent’s full generating language.

### FE-307 — Harness optimization over visible feedback is itself an overfitting/search-allocation problem; native harness choice is not consistently dominant
**Primary source:** Varun Ursekar et al., “HarnessOpt-Bench: Evaluating LLMs at Harness Optimization,” arXiv:2608.06301, 6 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06301
**Detailed public extraction:** https://arxivsignals.io/papers/2608.06301 and https://arxivsignals.io/papers/2608.06301/summary

HarnessOpt-Bench gives optimizer LLMs a fixed target-evaluation budget and visible development/validation feedback, but hides the final test partition behind a trusted execution environment. Across **111 scored runs**, optimizer-model choice moves normalized gain more than coding-harness choice on average (**0.142 vs 0.079** in the public extraction). Native harnesses are not universally better: across **20 model-task pairs**, the shared harness wins **11**, native wins **9**, with model-dependent crossings on GAIA.

More importantly for recursive optimizers, visible feedback is optimistic: most submitted candidates’ held-out test scores are below the best validation score observed during search. Search breadth across eight pre-specified harness levers correlates positively with gain on every task (**Spearman rho +0.34 to +0.88**), but breadth is also correlated with modification volume, so this is not evidence that “touch more things” is causally superior. The resource bottleneck is also easy to misread: median optimizers use only **8** evaluation calls but consume **82%** of their case-pass allowance, and **55/100** cells exhaust at least one partition’s case budget.

**Failure implication:** an automation optimizer should not deploy the best visible-score candidate directly. It needs an inaccessible or at least independently held-out acceptance set, explicit resource metering at the actual scarce unit, immutable candidate versions, model/role-specific evaluation, and rollback. The optimizer’s own execution harness is another treatment variable, not a neutral shell.

**Scope guard:** five frontier optimizer models, four downstream tasks, the benchmark’s pinned target models/seed harnesses and evaluation budget. Reported process correlations are observational, and the benchmark itself is described as hack-resistant rather than hack-proof; repeated feedback against a stable evaluator can still invite evaluator-specific optimization.

## Frontier state
Frontier remains nonempty.

A targeted re-search again did **not** verify the strict full exploitability design: multiple frozen LLM/agent defenders; independently initialized fresh best-response attackers separately optimized against each defender; equal target/query/search budget; multiple independent attacker-training seeds per defender; and full attacker×defender cross-transfer. Adaptive Adversaries, ABS/Self-RedTeam, MAGIC, PandaGuard and other cross-play/population papers remain partial matches: they provide fixed attacker matrices, co-evolving checkpoints, fresh-per-defense training without full cross-transfer, or insufficient independent attacker-seed structure. None is promoted as satisfying the full frontier.

**Exact next action:** Continue the strict seeded exploitability-matrix search first, prioritizing released attacker/defender checkpoint populations or raw evaluation matrices that could support reconstruction. In parallel prioritize: (1) rollback/recovery designs that jointly preserve context/world consistency *and* external-effect identity across crash/restart; (2) workflow/failure monitors where replay fidelity is separated from predictive value and where compact local projections are compared against full-context materialization; (3) optimizer acceptance under hidden/independent evaluation with actual scarce-resource metering, per-role/model heterogeneity and rollback; (4) protected operational constraints across compaction/handoff; (5) physical feasibility/idempotency at tool dispatch; and (6) authorization/revocation under retry, crash and concurrent resume.
