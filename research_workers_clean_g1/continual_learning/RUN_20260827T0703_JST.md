## New provisional candidate: SAFE-Merge (August 2026) protects both pretrained knowledge and accumulated task updates during data-free continual model merging

New public primary source: Qiu et al., `SAFE-Merge: Data-Free Continual Model Merging with General Knowledge Preservation`, arXiv:2608.01184, submitted 2026-08-02 (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01184`).

Mechanism from the primary abstract: a stream of specialized model updates is incorporated without task data. Risk-aware sparse masking retains parameter updates judged to carry task-specific information while posing lower risk to pretrained general knowledge; masked low-rank recovery then restores task information lost through masking while leaving masked-out parameters unchanged; the recovered update is fused into the backbone, so the deployed model adds no inference-time parameters or latency. The primary abstract reports the best H-score across vision and language benchmarks and says that on longer CLIP task sequences SAFE-Merge substantially improves H-score over NUFILT while also obtaining the highest accuracy.

Why this matters to the current frontier: it is a new protected-write/model-composition mechanism that explicitly treats **pretrained general knowledge preservation** as a first-class objective rather than optimizing only interference among downstream task updates. It therefore belongs in the same falsifiable family as selective-write/de-correlation and post-hoc vector composition, and suggests adding a held-out general-knowledge preservation axis to the matched merge experiment rather than scoring only acquired-task performance.

Scope guard: SAFE-Merge is **continual model merging of incoming task-specialized checkpoints**, data-free at merge time. It is not evidence that the same method solves raw-sample online continual pretraining, unknown task discovery, or boundary-free streaming SGD.
