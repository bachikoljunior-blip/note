## C515 — Adaptive coalition selection is a credible alternative to fixed random sampling, but estimator identity and uncertainty must be first-class

`ShaplEIG: Bayesian Experimental Design for Shapley Value Estimation` (ICML 2026 / arXiv:2606.02247) models an expensive coalition-value function with a Gaussian-process surrogate and adaptively selects coalitions by expected information gain about the Shapley values. The authors derive closed-form EIG from Shapley linearity and a polynomial-time computation using elementary symmetric polynomials, reporting improved low-budget sample efficiency across costly applications.

This is not a proof-assistant result and it does not establish safe lemma pruning. It does show that “uniform random permutations with a fixed budget” should not be hard-coded as the only coalition-credit estimator when each future counterfactual proof evaluation is expensive. Any use here must retain estimator identity/version, uncertainty, samples observed, stopping policy, and model-misspecification scope.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02247

## C516 — Proof-DAG compression gives a concrete non-usage-count lemma prior with downstream prover evidence

Christoph Wernhard, `Generating Theorems by Generating Proof Structures` (IJCAR 2026), selects synthesized lemma candidates from a minimal proof DAG using a compression `save-value`. For production `a_k -> d_k`, the paper defines:

`save = (ref_G(a_k) - 1) * tsize(d_k)`

where `ref_G(a_k)` is the number of references to the shared subproof and `tsize` its tree size. Candidates are ordered primarily by save-value, then smaller MGT tree size and height, before subsumption reduction.

On a 1,374-theorem Metamath-derived benchmark, the paper reports that supplied synthesized lemmas improve prover solution rates, e.g. Vampire 74% -> 94% and leanCoP 7% -> 44%. It also reports that 50% of the first 64 LMM candidates ranked by save-value prove a benchmark POI theorem.

Design implication: a proof-DAG compression proxy is worth testing as an **advisory prior** distinct from raw usage count, transitive dependent count, support credit, and future causal utility. It may favor lemmas that collapse repeated proof structure even before chronological future-task reuse is observed.

Critical scope: condensed-detachment proof structures over a Metamath propositional fragment and first-order provers, not Lean; the reported downstream gains are not a compute-matched Lean library-policy ablation. Save-value is therefore a ranking feature candidate, not promotion authority.

Source: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-32589-1_3
