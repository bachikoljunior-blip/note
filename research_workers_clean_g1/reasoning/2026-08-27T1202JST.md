## C471 — compute-matched formal library learning can lose its apparent gain, and cross-problem lemma sharing can be causally irrelevant

Primary source: Berlot-Attwell et al., `Is This LLM Library Learning? Evaluation Must Account For Compute and Behaviour`, EACL 2026.

The paper re-evaluates LEGO-Prover, TroVE and AgentOptimizer under compute-corrected baselines and behavioural reuse analysis. For LEGO-Prover on miniF2F, the reported uncorrected accuracy is often accompanied by much higher inference cost than Draft-Sketch-Prove (DSP). Example cells from Table 3:
- GPT-4o: LEGO-Prover `29.3±3.1%` at `$125.4` average cost; DSP `23.1±1.2%` at `$22.9`; cost-reduced LEGO-Prover `23.8±1.2%` at `$22.6`.
- GPT-4o-mini: LEGO-Prover `25.8±1.6%` at `$69.9`; DSP `35.9±0.9%` at `$12.1`; cost-reduced LEGO-Prover `17.5±0.9%` at `$11.9`.

More important for reusable-lemma causality, the authors ablate **cross-problem lemma sharing** under Llama3.1 while preserving iterative refinement and the same-problem EVOLVER/PROVER database. On the full test split this no-cross-problem-sharing variant reaches `30.9±1.3%`, versus `29.0±2.1%` for non-ablated LEGO-Prover. Thus, within this tested Isabelle substrate, the observed advantage over DSP cannot be attributed to cross-problem lemma reuse.

Behavioural analysis also finds no verified direct reuse of learned lemmas in final proofs and evidence against meaningful soft reuse. At a moderate similarity threshold, soft-reuse curves rapidly fall to zero even while single-task soft use remains common.

Interpretation for the current frontier: **kernel/prover-valid library entries are not enough; the system must demonstrate actual future reuse or closure benefit under equal compute.** A controller should not reward `STORE_VERIFIED_LEMMA` merely for creating a verified item.

Scope guard:
- This is Isabelle/miniF2F, not Lean 4.
- It falsifies a specific library-learning explanation for LEGO-Prover; it does not imply that all reusable verified lemmas are useless.
- Compute accounting is system/model specific.

## C472 — in general-mathematics Lean, prior verified theorem/proof context strongly improves proving, but the effect can be guidance rather than direct lemma use

Primary source: Kasaura et al., `Discovering New Theorems via LLMs with In-Context Proof Learning in Lean`, NALOMA 2026.

The Conjecturing-Proving Loop (CPL) repeatedly adds verified theorem/proof pairs to a Lean library and then uses that library as context for later conjecturing and proving.

A controlled re-proving experiment takes 2,123 theorems generated by CPL and compares two contexts:
- context containing the library generated before the target theorem: `2106/2123 = 99%` re-proved;
- context containing only the notion definitions: `1935/2123 = 91%` re-proved.

The paper reports McNemar `p = 4 × 10^-35` for this difference.

For the harder alpha-open-intersection target theorem, the authors ran 80 re-proving attempts with generated-library context and 80 without it:
- with generated library: `7/80` successes;
- without generated library: `0/80` successes.

Crucially, the successful generated proof **does not use the other generated theorems as lemmas**. The authors interpret the library as in-context learning of proof strategies rather than a collection of directly invoked lemmas for this target.

This supplies the general-mathematics Lean transfer evidence that the predecessor frontier was missing, but it supports the **guidance** channel more directly than the **verified-fact reuse** channel. It strengthens the need to separate `STORE_GUIDANCE` from `ADMIT_REUSABLE_LEMMA` rather than treating all verified history as one memory type.

Scope guard:
- The library context changes prompt content and therefore input-token cost; the experiment is not a compute-matched library-versus-more-sampling comparison.
- The 7/80 result concerns one target theorem; do not generalize that magnitude universally.
- The 99% vs 91% re-proving result mixes direct theorem availability and strategy in-context learning at the aggregate level; only the highlighted target is explicitly shown not to use generated lemmas directly.

## C473 — Lean-checked status is a staging gate, not evidence that a lemma deserves permanent library admission

Primary source: `MathlibLemma: Folklore Lemma Generation and Benchmark for Formal Mathematics`, arXiv:2602.02561v3.

MathlibLemma reports a generated proof library of **1,506 Lean-checked proofs** that also pass a proof-bypass screen, derived from a benchmark of 4,028 non-trivial type-checked statements. Yet the paper describes only a **small curated pilot subset** as merged into Mathlib.

This is external evidence that verified library expansion at scale is feasible and that some generated artifacts can meet expert Mathlib standards. It is **not** evidence that all 1,506 verified items have useful downstream reuse, nor does the paper report a compute-matched downstream reuse rate for the whole generated library.

Controller consequence: introduce a two-stage distinction:
1. `STAGE_VERIFIED_LEMMA`: kernel-checked, provenance-pinned candidate becomes available for evaluation;
2. `ADMIT_REUSABLE_LEMMA`: promotion to durable/default retrieval only after a utility/admission criterion, rather than automatically on proof success.

Possible admission evidence includes observed downstream proof-term dependency use, repository-closure contribution, retrieval hit utility, human/library-quality review where required, and expected future value net of context/search/storage cost.

Scope guard:
- Curated merge into Mathlib is expert-quality evidence for selected outputs, not a quantitative estimate of general reuse.
- Absence of a downstream-use statistic does not imply the unmerged lemmas are useless.

## C474 — “reusable-looking” proof structure can improve without proving actual future reuse

Primary source: `Proof-Refactor: Refactoring Generated Formal Proofs into Modular Artifacts`, arXiv:2606.03743.

Proof-Refactor explicitly extracts proof fragments, designs generalized helper declarations, proves them, and repairs the original proof to use the verified components. On the authors' project-site table, human/rubric `Reuse` scores improve from `3.84→4.24` on 96 PutnamBench problems and `4.00→4.29` on 12 Putnam2025 problems, together with large gains in signature quality and readability.

This supports the feasibility of turning a successful monolithic proof into cleaner reusable candidates. However, the reported reuse metric is a **quality rubric**, not observed future downstream dependency reuse under matched compute. Therefore it should be used as an admission feature, not as the terminal utility target.

## New synthesis — stage verified facts, promote only on future utility; measure guidance and fact reuse separately

The new evidence creates a useful three-way distinction:

1. **Verified fact candidate** — mechanically true in the pinned Lean environment, but future utility unknown.
2. **Advisory guidance** — prior verified proofs can improve later proof strategy even when no prior theorem is directly invoked (C472).
3. **Admitted reusable lemma** — a verified fact promoted because it has demonstrated or credibly predicted downstream value, rather than because it exists.

This sharpens the predecessor's `STORE_VERIFIED_LEMMA` action into at least two actions:
- `STAGE_VERIFIED_LEMMA` after kernel/readback success;
- `ADMIT_REUSABLE_LEMMA` after a cost-sensitive utility gate.

A future utility signal should not rely on textual name matches alone. For Lean, direct reuse should be instrumented from elaborated proof-term/environment dependencies where possible, since tactics such as `simp`, typeclass search, or automation can use declarations indirectly. Separately record advisory-context benefit when the theorem itself is not in the final dependency closure.

The admission objective should include:
- observed downstream direct-use count and weighted task coverage;
- full-repository closure contribution;
- reduction in proof rounds/tokens/Lean execution/wall-clock under matched compute;
- generalization/refactoring/checking cost to create the candidate;
- retrieval/context overhead imposed on future tasks;
- regressions or version fragility;
- storage/indexing cost only if material.

The negative LEGO-Prover result makes one ablation mandatory: **disable cross-task lemma sharing while keeping same-task refinement and total compute matched**. Any claimed durable-library gain must exceed this control.
