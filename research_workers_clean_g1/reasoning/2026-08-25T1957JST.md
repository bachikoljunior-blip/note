## C17 — Compiler-Guided Adaptive Proof Search directly combines whole-proof exploration, repair/refinement, and restart under a budget

Primary source: `Compiler-Guided Adaptive Proof Search with Cross-Model Synergy on Context-Dependent Theorem Proving`, arXiv:2608.18084 (2026-08).

Mechanism:
- Project-level Lean 4 proof search on seven real-world miniCTX-v2 projects.
- Exploration uses two different prover models to create heterogeneous starting proofs, and resamples when refinement stagnates.
- Exploitation refines the current best whole-proof state with Lean compiler feedback.
- A pairwise comparison stage chooses whether a proposed refinement is better than the preserved incumbent, preventing destructive replacement of useful partial states.
- Thus the controller can effectively choose among fresh generation/resampling, continued refinement, and preserving/reusing the best current state.

Headline evidence:
- Within a pass@32 budget, average pass rate improves by **12.8 percentage points** while LLM calls are reduced by **21.9%** relative to the compared baseline setup.
- On RLMEval-FLT3, the adaptive method reports **39.3%** at **13.5 calls/theorem**, versus **36.9%** for Generalist pass@32 and **28.6%** for Specialist pass@32.
- Average cost is reported around **$1.96/problem**, versus **$2.43/problem** for GPT-5-mini pass@32 while attaining the higher solve rate.

Ablations / routing evidence from the primary paper:
- Removing the specialist, AutoSolve, stagnation-triggered resampling, or pairwise comparison each shifts the pass-rate/call tradeoff downward.
- Pairwise comparison ablation: **59.28% vs 57.16%** pass rate; generalist proof calls **6.54 vs 10.08**. Total LLM calls are **13.84 vs 11.05** because comparison itself costs calls, while total monetary cost is **$1.10 vs $1.14**. This shows a distinction between raw call count and expensive proof-generation cost.
- Successful trajectories reject about **50.4%** of proposed refinements. Among solved trajectories whose final success comes after comparison, **39.5%** verify from a preserved current-best state rather than the newest proposal. Preserving state is therefore causally relevant to avoiding destructive refinement.
- Source of solved theorems in the reported trajectory accounting: AutoSolve **28.36%**; initial generalist **30.34%**; initial specialist **9.44%** (initial subtotal **39.78%**); resampling subtotal **1.50%**; refinement generalist **18.91%**; refinement specialist **11.46%** (refinement subtotal **30.37%**). Both heterogeneous initialization and refinement contribute materially.
- Stagnation threshold sensitivity on mathlib-valid: K/12 **48% @30.24 calls**, K/6 **56% @27.12**, K/4 **60% @24.42**, K/3 **54% @27.60**, K/2 **60% @27.76**; K/4 gives the reported best balance.
- Wrong starting proofs differ materially in downstream repair effort: mean within-wrong-proof std **2.41** refinement iterations vs inter-wrong-proof std **3.36**, supporting the claim that which failed proof is selected matters.

Interpretation:
- This substantially closes the old broad frontier “does a Lean system directly mix exploration, compiler-guided refinement, and restart/resampling under a budget?” The answer is now yes at **whole-proof granularity**.
- It does **not** close the narrower frontier of CARTS/3D-Prover-style tactic/transition-semantic diversity jointly integrated with learned compiler-guided repair under the same verifier/token/wall-clock budget. Its diversity comes mainly from cross-model starting states and stagnation-triggered resampling, not explicit tactic-effect diversity.

Important limitations:
- Whole-proof granularity; no explicit tactic-level semantic search.
- Pairwise comparison uses an LLM judge, whose calibration as a proof-progress estimator is not guaranteed.
- Only two generation models are studied.
- Inference-time pretrained models only; no jointly learned router/repair policy is established.

Sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18084

## C18 — FormalRewardBench shows LLM proof judges are weak and position-biased even when generators are strong

Primary source: `FormalRewardBench`, arXiv:2605.10141.

Benchmark:
- 250 Lean correct-vs-incorrect proof preference pairs across five controlled error-injection classes.
- Tests whether a model can rank a valid proof above a subtly invalid one rather than generate a proof itself.

Evidence:
- Best frontier LLM pairwise accuracy is only **59.8%**.
- Best specialized theorem prover reaches only **24.4%**, showing that formal-proof generation ability does not automatically transfer to proof evaluation.
- Category-level failures remain severe: even strong frontier models are weak on forced mistakes and verbose incorrect proofs.
- Strong order/position bias appears. Claude Opus 4.5: **85.0%** when the correct proof is shown first vs **69.7%** when second, consistency **70.4%**. DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B: **94.9% vs 9.8%**, consistency **9.9%**.

Interpretation for routing:
- C17 shows an LLM pairwise judge can improve an end-to-end system empirically, but FormalRewardBench warns that this does **not** imply the judge is calibrated or reliable as a general proof-quality oracle.
- Future restart/repair routers should prefer verifier-grounded/process-derived progress signals where possible, and any LLM comparison should at least test swapped-order robustness, consistency, and calibration.
- This strengthens the case for 3D-Prover-style transition predictions, compiler error-class/repairability signals, or explicit Lean-derived progress features as alternatives/complements to pure pairwise LLM judging.

Scope:
- FormalRewardBench ranks correct vs incorrect finished proofs; C17 often compares two *failed or partial* proof states for expected repairability. Therefore these results do not directly measure C17's exact comparison task and should be used as a reliability warning, not as a refutation of C17.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10141

## C19 — BlueprintRepair: typed local graph edits preserve localized repair coverage at materially lower cost than patches or rewrites

Primary source: `BlueprintRepair: Typed Local Edits for Failed Lean Proof Blueprints`, arXiv:2607.28110.

Mechanism:
- Represents proof plans as dependency-graph blueprints.
- Defines ten schema-checked local edit operations. Each operation names the edited node; the target theorem cannot be changed.
- Lean validates every applied edit, and accepted repairs must declare every blueprint lemma used.

BlueprintTrace benchmark:
- **142** controlled failures with complete accepted/rejected repair trajectories.
- Matched source, feedback, model, and budget; compares typed edits, exact source patches, and complete module rewrites.

DeepSeek-V4-Flash evidence:
- Typed edits solve **104/142**.
- Exact patches solve **109/142**.
- Full rewrites solve **104/142**.
- On the localized-failure subset the interfaces solve almost the same number of states.
- Typed repair is cheapest per solved state; patching costs **1.30×**, full rewriting **2.06×** as much.
- Within **10,000 completion tokens/task**, typed edits reach nearly all final coverage while both free-form interfaces still lag.
- Qwen3.6-Flash solves fewer states overall but preserves the same qualitative cost ordering, with typed edits cheapest and relatively stronger on proof-authoring states.

Interpretation:
- “Repair” should not be a single undifferentiated action. A compute-aware controller can expose multiple repair granularities: typed local graph edit, source patch, whole-proof/module rewrite, fresh branch, or higher-level decomposition restart.
- Structured local actions can reduce the token cost of repair without sacrificing much localized-failure coverage, making them attractive before expensive rewrite/restart actions.

Scope:
- Controlled blueprint failures, not a generic theorem-proving pass@K benchmark.
- Typed repair does not dominate patches in raw solved count; its advantage is primarily efficiency and constraint preservation.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28110

## C20 — Goedel-Architect strengthens the case for preserving solved structure and refining only failed dependency-graph regions

Primary source: `Goedel-Architect: Streamlining Formal Theorem Proving with Blueprint Generation and Refinement`, arXiv:2606.06468.

Mechanism:
- Generate a Lean blueprint DAG of definitions/lemmas.
- Prove nodes in parallel with Lean compiler and Mathlib retrieval tools.
- Failed nodes carry structured diagnoses such as `PROOF_TOO_HARD`, `STATEMENT_WRONG`, or machine-checked formal negation.
- Blueprint refinement decomposes hard nodes, rewires missing dependencies, repairs/drops false intermediate statements, and **preserves already-proved nodes/signatures** so successful compute is not discarded.

Headline evidence with open-weight DeepSeek-V4-Flash:
- MiniF2F-test: **99.2% pass@1**.
- PutnamBench: **75.6% pass@1**; optional natural-language blueprint seeding raises to **88.8% (597/672) at pass@4**.
- Default PutnamBench run costs **$294 total / $0.44 per question**; NL-guided pass@4 effort costs **$985.67**, about **$1.65 per solved problem**.
- Refinement scaling on PutnamBench: initial blueprint solves **200/672 (29.8%)**; by 16 refinement iterations **508/672 (75.6%)** are solved, with roughly log-linear growth in solve count over refinement compute.

Same-backbone controls:
- On DeepSeek-V4-Flash, MiniF2F: direct inference **67.6%@32**, tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) **97.1%@32**, Hilbert **83.6%@1**, Hilbert with TIR **84.4%@1**.
- On a shared random 200-problem PutnamBench control subset: direct inference **6.5%@32**, TIR **54.5%@32**; the project reports that the blueprint/refinement curve reaches roughly **80%** on the same subset/token-budget framing, with NL guidance reaching **92.5%**.

Interpretation:
- This is independent support for hierarchical preserve-and-refine rather than repeatedly regenerating everything.
- Combined with C19, it suggests a multi-level action space: repair a local blueprint node when localized, refine/rewrite graph structure when diagnosis says decomposition/dependency is wrong, and preserve solved nodes as immutable anchors.
- It also strengthens C11/C17's lesson that *where* to spend the next compute unit is a first-class reasoning problem.

Scope / caution:
- Pipeline-level `pass@1` includes up to 16 internal refinement iterations, so it is not comparable to one-shot model pass@1 without accounting for internal compute.
- The 200-problem control subset is not the full PutnamBench distribution.
- NL-guided mode adds an extra proof-generation/correction pipeline and should not be conflated with the default blueprint result.

Sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06468
- https://goedelarchitect.github.io/
- https://github.com/astrio-labs/goedel-architect

## C11 supplement — router diagnostics: error diversity is especially informative, but the oracle gap remains large

Current primary version of `Optimizing the Cost-Quality Tradeoff of Agentic Theorem Provers in Lean` is arXiv:2606.04883v3; preserve **28.9%** as the current headline average budget reduction at approximately preserved accuracy on the 85-problem PutnamBench subset.

Earlier public-version ablations (do **not** silently relabel them as v3 results) report the router using proof similarity, compiler-error diversity, and attempt count. The reported cost-reduction sequence in that earlier version was:

- proof similarity + error diversity: **21.0%**;
- error diversity + attempt count: **18.4%**;
- proof similarity + attempt count: **16.2%**;

- Compiler **error diversity** appears to carry substantial marginal value as a routing signal, matching the broader clean-g1 hypothesis that realized failure modes are more useful than static lexical diversity.

## Updated synthesis

The evidence now supports a more concrete hierarchy of verifier-guided actions:

1. **Fresh branch / heterogeneous initialization** when current strategic coverage is narrow (C2, C15, C16, C17).
2. **Preserve the current best state** when a proposed refinement is likely destructive (C17).
3. **Local compiler-guided whole-proof repair** when the branch is promising and repairable (C1, C6, C17).
4. **Typed local graph repair** when failure is localized and structural constraints can be preserved cheaply (C19).
5. **Blueprint/decomposition refinement** when diagnosis points to wrong/missing intermediate structure or dependencies (C4, C20).
6. **Restart decomposition / alternate plan** when the current hierarchy is stagnating (C11, C17, C20).
7. **Use runtime/verifier-derived features for routing**, especially error diversity, transition effect, repairability, execution cost, and preserved-state success; do not trust static lexical diversity or uncalibrated LLM judging alone (C11, C12, C16, C18).

The old frontier “find any direct exploration + repair + restart composite in Lean” is now too broad and should be retired. C17 directly supplies such a system at whole-proof granularity. The high-value frontier narrows to **semantic/tactic-effect diversity + compiler-guided repair + hierarchical action routing under compute-matched conditions**.
