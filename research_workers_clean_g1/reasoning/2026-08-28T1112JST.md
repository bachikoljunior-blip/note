## Updated synthesis

The failed v1-scaled preregistration exposes a general controller-design lesson that is more important than another classifier score: a learned override cannot be trained in a region where the baseline policy deterministically prevents labels from being observed. The safe architecture is not to weaken the retained incumbent, but to keep it as the output fallback while buying a controlled amount of *audit-only counterfactual exploration* in rejected states. In formal proving this is unusually attractive because verifier-confirmed challenger wins can only improve the retained endpoint under the no-harm merge rule; the cost risk and quality risk can be separated.

The immediate synthetic question is now empirical support rather than model fitting: do fresh stop-region audits actually contain enough positive tail value to make a learned incremental controller identifiable? The v2 support gate answers this before another model is allowed to exist.
