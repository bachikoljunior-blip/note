## C522 — Exact Shapley/semivalue credit is not sufficient by itself to select a high-utility lemma subset

`Rethinking Data Shapley for Data Selection Tasks: Misleads and Merits` (Wang et al., 2024, arXiv:2405.03875) proves a general information-loss result: the map from a full set utility function to its Shapley vector is non-injective. Without structural assumptions on the utility function, any decision rule that sees only the Shapley vector cannot reliably determine which of two subsets has larger utility; their hypothesis-testing guarantee can be no better than random guessing in the paper's worst-case formulation. The paper also identifies structured utility families, including monotonically transformed modular functions, where Shapley-based selection can be optimal.

This is not a theorem-proving result, so transfer is deliberately narrow. It falsifies a tempting library-policy shortcut: even **exact**, zero-variance coalition credit per lemma does not generally identify the best retained lemma subset when future proving utility contains interactions, redundancy, search pollution or context-budget effects. Thus `WSMS/Shapley/support credit -> global promote/demote` remains invalid without an independently justified utility structure.

Design consequence:
- coalition credit remains an advisory prioritization signal;
- promotion/demotion remains grounded in chronologically future, fixed-compute counterfactual proving on the actual library/search substrate;
- if a cheaper Shapley-only policy is ever proposed, first test whether observed subset utility is adequately approximated by a pre-registered additive/MTM-like model rather than assuming it.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03875

## C524 — Full-library `getUsedConstants` extraction scales, but merged type/body graphs still cannot substitute for the proof-vs-statement split

ProofGraph reports a complete Mathlib extraction of 323,489 declarations and 7,516,850 edges. Its extractor walks Lean `ConstantInfo` and takes `Expr.getUsedConstants` from each declaration's type expression and definition body, then merges them into one `depends_on` edge set. This independently strengthens the feasibility of large-scale kernel-environment dependency instrumentation.

But the merge is precisely why it is not a drop-in replacement for the `result-graph` `UseEventV1` evidence model: a dependency appearing in a declaration's statement and one used only in its proof become the same graph relation unless the extractor preserves the provenance channel. For causal library evaluation, those channels remain distinct.
