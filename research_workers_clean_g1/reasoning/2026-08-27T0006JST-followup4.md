## C400 — a direct XACML/Rego formalization isolates the exact unverified compiler edge

**Observed.** Jakob Kreuze's 2024 Brown thesis formalizes executable XACML 3.0 semantics in Lean 4 and a formal Rego embedding. The XACML/Rego source-policy translators are implemented in OCaml. The thesis explicitly labels **Verified Compilation** as future work because translating policies into Lean structures is opaque to Lean; it proposes moving the compiler into Lean so one could prove that source-language restrictions are preserved. The XACML model had been exercised against 114 of 455 available translated conformance tests; several standard functions/elements were outside the implemented fragment. The Rego embedding likewise uses an OCaml compiler, and executable evaluation of the Rego semantics was still future work.

Primary source:
- https://cs.brown.edu/media/filer_public/00/df/00df3f96-8712-4ef6-be1b-4f7cc0f046a8/jakobkreuze.pdf

**Inference.** This is a concrete instance of the chain now targeted by `PolicyActivationCertificateV1`: even when the target Lean representation has formal/executable semantics, **the source parser/compiler can remain the unproved authority-widening edge**. Therefore a production activation path must bind the source policy, supported fragment, compiler revision/config, generated formal artifact, and translation certificate; the existence of a verified target evaluator does not certify the untrusted source translation.

## C401 — empirical agreement with a production policy compiler is useful but can reveal specification/compiler disagreement rather than establish a proof

**Observed.** IFCIL provides a formal semantics for the SELinux CIL type-enforcement fragment and empirically validates it against the reference manual and the production CIL compiler. The work documents corner cases where manual and compiler disagree, including behavior acknowledged by CIL developers as compiler bugs, and an example where contradictory type-attribute behavior still yields a kernel policy. The old standalone CIL compiler repository itself documents known issues including undefined behavior for blocks inside macros and problematic malformed policies. More recent SELinux userspace packages continue to ship `secilc` as the production CIL-to-kernel-policy compiler.

Primary/public sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15915
- https://github.com/SELinuxProject/cil
- https://packages.fedoraproject.org/pkgs/secilc/secilc/

**Inference.** Differential/empirical correspondence testing is not merely an inferior substitute for proof; it is also a **specification-discovery mechanism** that can show the production compiler and prose manual disagree. When they disagree, an activation system needs an explicitly chosen authoritative semantic source and a versioned exception/compatibility policy. It must not silently treat “matches production behavior” as equivalent to “matches intended policy semantics.”

New activation concepts:
- `semantic_authority_source`: e.g. certified model, standard version, or explicitly approved production-compatibility semantics;
- `known_correspondence_exception_set`;
- `production_deviation_approval_ref` when compatibility deliberately follows a known non-model behavior;
- `compiler_model_disagreement_blocks_unqualified_activation`.

**Scope guard.** IFCIL's formal semantics covers a fragment and is experimentally validated; it is not an end-to-end formal proof of `secilc` or the kernel policy loader.

## C402 — certified semantics plus conformance testing should be used as complementary evidence, not substitutes

**Synthesis.** C393/C398 and C400/C401 establish two recurring patterns:

1. machine-checked semantic/evaluator proofs can leave source/frontend/production correspondence unproved;
2. conformance/differential testing can discover bugs and compatibility mismatches that the formal model may not include.

A robust activation certificate should therefore carry both when available, but with separate claims:
- formal theorem claims over an exact model/artifact;
- supported-fragment claims;
- source-to-model translation proof/validation;
- production differential/conformance evidence;
- known disagreement/exception records;
- exact production compiler/runtime digest.

No evidence kind may be silently substituted for another.
