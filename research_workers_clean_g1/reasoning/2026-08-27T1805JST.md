## C499 — Reachability can greatly over-credit one declaration

A 2026-08-11 Mathlib dominator analysis reports that 116,766 theorems reach `lt_or_eq_of_le`, whereas only 2,018 would cease being classical if that declaration were rebuilt constructively, a factor-58 difference. It also reports that 60.1% of classically dependent theorems have no single intermediate declaration that dominates their path to `Classical.choice`. Design implication: transitive dependent count is an impact/priority feature, not per-lemma causal credit. Prefer controlled replacement/ablation and future fixed-compute counterfactual utility for authoritative promotion decisions.

## C500 — Isabelle Sledgehammer gives a cheap bounded alternative-support sampler

Current Isabelle Sledgehammer defaults include `max_proofs = 4` and `minimize = true`. Its minimizer repeatedly invokes the prover/proof method on subsets of returned facts to obtain a reduced/minimal fact set. This supports a low-cost first stage for support-family discovery: collect several proofs under frozen prover/method/budget settings and minimize each fact set before using expensive exhaustive methods such as hitting-set enumeration.

The resulting minimality is prover/method/time-relative, and the displayed proofs are sampled rather than exhaustive.

## C501 — MagnusData is an offline corpus for support-family diagnostics

MagnusData distinguishes human proofs from machine-generated alternative Sledgehammer proofs, and distinguishes `minimal` from `non-minimal` machine-generated data; minimal versions are produced by trying to remove unnecessary premises from a previously found proof. This can calibrate support-set similarity, redundancy and coalition-credit diagnostics before paying for live theorem-prover calls.

Scope: this is Isabelle data, not Lean, and the alternative proofs are search-produced rather than exhaustive.

Source: https://github.com/Simontwice/MagnusData
