## C385 — controlled English can be a genuine formal source language, but the authorization-lowering proof edge remains open

**Observed.** NIST RECON and Attempto Controlled English (ACE) are controlled-language systems designed around restricted grammars with unambiguous formal interpretations. RECON is described as a controlled English for business rules with a formal grammar and translation to formal logic. ACE is a controlled subset of English with deterministic translation to first-order logic and a public Attempto Parsing Engine implementation. Earlier access-control work has also used controlled natural-language frontends that parse policy statements into machine-readable logic/policy representations.

Public sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/recon-controlled-english-business-rules
- https://github.com/usnistgov/RECON
- https://github.com/Attempto/APE
- https://kar.kent.ac.uk/31980/

**Inference.** A high-assurance policy pipeline does not need arbitrary natural language to be the grant-authoritative source. A controlled language with a fixed formal semantics can reduce the semantic gap substantially. However, this search did not establish an end-to-end machine-checked theorem that a current controlled-language access-control frontend preserves authorization semantics all the way into the target authorization AST. Treat controlled-language determinism as useful structure, not as a proof-producing compiler by itself.

**Scope guard.** This finding does not claim RECON/ACE or the cited access-control CNL system is a verified authorization compiler.

## C386 — parser correctness can be moved out of the trust gap; semantic lowering remains a distinct obligation

**Observed.** There is mature precedent for formally verified or translation-validated parser infrastructure. TRX gives a formally verified PEG interpreter in Coq. A verified packrat parser interpreter has been developed in PVS with proof certificates for AST construction/semantic interpretation. Menhir supports generation of Coq-verified LR parsers, and LR parser translation-validation work verifies generated parser artifacts against the grammar specification.

Public sources:
- https://lmcs.episciences.org/686
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04457
- https://gallium.inria.fr/~fpottier/menhir/

**Inference.** `PolicyActivationCertificate` should not collapse parse correctness and semantic-lowering correctness into one opaque “compiler succeeded” bit. A safer evidence chain has separate artifacts for:
1. source bytes/text + grammar/version;
2. verified/validated parse to a canonical source AST;
3. fixed source-language denotation;
4. authorization-AST lowering;
5. machine-checked translation-validation/non-amplification evidence for that lowering.

A verified parser only establishes the syntax/AST edge. It does not prove that semantic actions or the authorization compiler preserve the intended policy meaning.

## C387 — proof-producing or translation-validating compilation is a plausible architecture for policy lowering

**Observed.** Proof-producing compilation exists in other domains: a recent verified compiler for CairoZero/blockchain applications accompanies compilation with Lean proof machinery. Parser-generator ecosystems also use translation validation to verify generated artifacts after an untrusted generator runs.

Public source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15002

**Inference.** For authorization, the compiler itself need not be fully trusted if it emits `(AuthAST, certificate)` and a small deterministic checker proves the grant-relevant relation. Full source/target equivalence is stronger than necessary for some safety goals; an especially useful hard property is **authority non-amplification**: the target policy must not permit an effect that the authoritative source semantics forbids or leaves unestablished. Explicit denies and mandatory obligations must likewise not disappear during lowering.

Candidate certificate fields:
- `source_language_id/version`;
- `source_ast_digest` and source-semantics digest;
- `target_auth_ast_digest`;
- `lowering_tool/version/config_digest`;
- `proof_or_translation_validation_artifact`;
- `checked_relation` such as semantic equivalence or conservative/non-amplifying refinement;
- checker/version and result digest.

**Scope guard.** This is an architecture inference from proof-producing/validated compilation precedent, not evidence that a production CNL-to-authorization compiler with this exact guarantee already exists.

## C388 — AgentGuardUtil demonstrates useful executable policy compilation, but uncovered clauses create a fail-open coverage risk

**Observed.** `AgentGuardUtil: A Deterministic Policy Compiler for Reliable Agent Tool Use` (arXiv:2608.23282, submitted 24 August 2026) compiles per-conversation natural-language policy into typed rules, translates state-conditional rules into executable tuples, and applies deterministic pre/post-action gates. A second compilation pass has access to real tool signatures; an obligation whose arguments cannot be schema-validated is dropped from executable enforcement and retained only as advisory rather than forcing an invalid call. Its deterministic engine simulates the projected post-action state and checks missing obligations/granularity before execution. An LLM critic may confirm/discard advisories but cannot override hard findings or invent hard findings.

On hidden CAR-bench Track 1 (30 tasks, three trials), the paper reports AgentGuardUtil `Pass^3=53.3`, `Pass@3=80.0`, `Pass1=66.7`, versus organizer baseline `50.0 / 66.7 / 60.0`. The system is substantially more expensive in the reported evaluation (roughly 362k tokens and 19.2 s/turn versus 82k and 3.2 s/turn for baseline). Crucially, the paper states that checks are silent when no compiled rule covers the task; improving `Pass^3` therefore requires making more policy executable.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23282

**Inference.** This is strong evidence that deterministic enforcement over compiled rules can improve reliability, but it also exposes the exact missing guarantee for positive authority: **compiler silence/omission must not be interpreted as permission** when the source contains a grant-relevant condition, prohibition, or obligation. Dropping an invalid obligation to advisory is safe against forcing malformed calls, but a missing prohibition or missing conditional precondition can widen effective authority if coverage is not separately certified.

New hard requirements/regressions:
- `policy_clause_coverage_witness` binds the authoritative source-clause set to the compiled-rule set;
- `uncovered_grant_relevant_clause_cannot_default_allow`;
- `compiler_silence_requires_deny_or_separately_trusted_confirmation`;
- `rule_coverage_witness_must_bind_source_clause_digests`;
- an advisory rule can narrow/risk-flag behavior but cannot create positive authority.

**Scope guard.** CAR-bench is a small reliability benchmark, not a proof that the system is safe against all policy-translation omissions.

## C389 — production natural-language policy deployment uses validator-gated, human-confirmed publication rather than raw autonomous authority

**Observed.** `Natural-Language Policies to Executable Decisions: An Interpretable LLM Framework` (ACL Industry 2026) converts policy text into interpretable condition trees while keeping numeric pricing deterministic. Before publication, induced trees pass validators covering schema completeness, unit compatibility, numeric sanity, conflict detection, service/catalog grounding, and constraint format. The paper explicitly frames production onboarding as a **validator-gated, human-confirmed publication workflow**: operators review/edit trees before publication rather than treating raw model induction as final executable truth. It does not report standalone raw tree-induction accuracy. In deployment, the paper reports 3,960 orders and, on a 2,598-product information-consistent subset, 100% unit correctness, 85.2% unit-price accuracy, and zero fatal numeric errors, while also documenting substantial human/resource correction activity upstream.

Primary source:
- https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.114/

**Inference.** Current production evidence supports the existing boundary: arbitrary natural language plus validators is useful for proposing executable policy, but positive authority should not be created solely by autonomous induction. Trusted confirmation or a machine-checked semantic-preservation/non-amplification certificate remains necessary before publication/activation can widen capability.

## C390 — formal enforcement guarantees stop at the faithful-policy assumption; source-to-rule coverage must be a separate certificate edge

**Observed.** The current PCAS/FORGE work (`Formal Policy Enforcement for Real-World Agentic Systems`, 2026 revision) uses formal Datalog policies, deterministic reference monitoring, causal dependency reasoning, and contradiction/redundancy/reachability analysis. Its enforcement argument is conditional on the policy faithfully encoding author intent and on the environment contract. The current presentation treats natural-language-to-Datalog as a separate translation stage and associates formal rules with source clauses to support coverage/entailment checking.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19748

**Inference.** A proof that the reference monitor correctly enforces a Datalog/AuthAST policy cannot certify that an upstream LLM translated the human policy correctly. `PolicyActivationCertificateV1` should therefore bind both the **formal-policy verification** and a separate **source-faithfulness/coverage edge**.

Proposed activation fields:
- `authoritative_source_digest` and ordered/source-qualified `source_clause_digest_set`;
- `source_language_and_semantics_version`;
- `compiled_rule_digest_set`;
- `clause_to_rule_map`;
- `coverage_status` and explicit `uncovered_clause_set`;
- `entailment/equivalence/non_amplification_proof_ref` per grant-relevant clause or group;
- `trusted_confirmation_ref` where no machine-checked semantic certificate exists;
- exact compiler/parser/schema/checker version tuple;
- activation policy hash/epoch derived only after these checks.

Hard rule: a clause may contribute **positive authority** only if its lowering is machine-checked conservative/faithful or separately trusted-confirmed. An uncovered clause cannot disappear into an implicit allow.

## Updated synthesis

The critical missing edge is no longer merely parser correctness. The decisive boundary is **semantic and coverage faithfulness from the human/policy source to the activation-worthy formal authorization AST**.

A candidate high-assurance chain is:

`authoritative source -> (machine-formal or controlled-formal semantics, or trusted confirmation) -> verified/validated parse -> canonical source AST/denotation -> untrusted lowering proposal -> clause-complete translation-validation or non-amplification certificate -> typed authorization AST -> symbolic policy analysis -> PolicyActivationCertificate -> exact per-effect ToolDispatchAuthorizationWitness -> atomic witness consumption/effect commit -> EffectReceipt -> optional OutputReleaseAuthorizationWitness`.

Learned components may search for parses, mappings, proofs, clauses, or repairs and rank safe alternatives. They must not be able to turn an unproved omission, unsupported schema field, arbitrary-NL agreement, or failed translation-validation step into positive authority.
