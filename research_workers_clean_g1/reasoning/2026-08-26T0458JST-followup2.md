## C70 — Separate the OPE data-adequacy gate from estimator choice; weak overlap should produce abstention or truncated estimators, not a confident policy ranking

The current Lean-controller plan should not begin by choosing IPS/DR/FQE and then hoping the logged data are adequate. OPE work makes coverage/support a first-class precondition.

Primary/relevant sources:
- Sun et al., *When is Off-Policy Evaluation (Reward Modeling) Useful in Contextual Bandits? A Data-Centric Perspective* / DataCOPE: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14110
- Jiang & Li, *Doubly Robust Off-policy Value Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning*, ICML 2016: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/jiang16.html
- Mehrabi & Wager, *Off-Policy Evaluation in Markov Decision Processes under Weak Distributional Overlap*, arXiv:2402.08201v2 (revised 2025-09-28): https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08201
- Zhou et al., *Demystifying the Paradox of Importance Sampling with an Estimated History-Dependent Behavior Policy in Off-Policy Evaluation*, ICML 2025: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25f.html

Mehrabi & Wager introduce truncated doubly robust (TDR) estimators for weaker distributional overlap. When the target/data stationary-density ratio is square-integrable, their method recovers the familiar large-sample behavior available under stronger bounded-overlap assumptions; when it is not square-integrable, TDR remains consistent but converges more slowly, with truncation playing a major empirical role in their experiments.

Operational pipeline implied by the evidence:

1. **Eligibility/propensity audit:** exact legal-action mask, chosen action, behavior propensity and logging-policy version must be present for every structural controller decision.
2. **Coverage gate:** measure state-action support for the target controller before value estimation. Use task/difficulty/backbone/action-region breakdowns rather than a single aggregate support number.
3. **Estimator selection:** ordinary DR/WDR where ratios are well behaved; truncated/regularized DR variants when overlap is weak but not absent; fitted-Q/model-based estimates only with explicit extrapolation diagnostics.
4. **Abstention:** if the target policy puts meaningful mass on unsupported action regions, do not produce a precise offline ranking. Fall back to baseline/bootstrap policy or collect new randomized trajectories.
5. **Matched online confirmation:** any controller promoted from OPE should still be tested under the same real compute/action substrate.

This matters for Lean because expensive actions such as strong-model escalation, large worker fanout, global retrieval and restart may be rare under the baseline. A seemingly good learned policy can therefore be least identifiable exactly where it differs most from the behavior policy.

Scope guard: weak-overlap theory improves the range where estimation is possible; it does not make zero-support actions evaluable.

## C71 — Logging-policy design is itself a controller optimization problem; uniform epsilon is the reproducible baseline, not necessarily the efficient final collector

`Agentic-RL-harness` provides a clean starting point because its epsilon-perturbed baseline gives reconstructable propensities. However, recent *Logging Policy Design for Off-Policy Evaluation* work explicitly frames data collection as choosing a logging policy that balances reward and coverage for future OPE.

Source: *Logging Policy Design for Off-Policy Evaluation*, arXiv:2605.15108: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15108

For Lean, this suggests a two-stage collection strategy:

- **Stage A — uniform-safe epsilon:** within a narrow safe eligible-action subset, use a fixed small epsilon and uniform random choice. This is easy to audit and gives a clean first dataset.
- **Stage B — coverage-aware logging:** once rough action-value/variance and target-policy families are known, allocate exploration toward state-action regions whose uncertainty dominates downstream policy comparison, while keeping minimum support for every admissible candidate action.

The objective should not be immediate proof success alone. A logging policy optimized only for current reward can collapse onto the baseline's favored actions and leave future controller alternatives unevaluable. Conversely, indiscriminate randomization can waste scarce verifier/model budget. The collector should therefore explicitly optimize a reward–coverage tradeoff and report how much proof-quality cost was paid to gain policy-identification coverage.

A practical Lean logging record should include `eligible_actions`, `base_action`, `exploration_mode`, `epsilon_or_mixture_weight`, `chosen_action`, `mu_chosen`, and the full probability vector over eligible actions whenever feasible. This makes later OPE reproducible even if the target controller changes.

## C72 — Model/provider escalation should optimize expected verified progress per real service cycle, not a naive additive quality-minus-latency score

Recent contextual-bandit routing work on heterogeneous LLM services gives an independent warning about multi-objective router rewards. LQM-ContextRoute reports that additive quality/latency scoring can mis-rank services because latency is a queue/service property and can dominate or be dominated by arbitrary scale choices. Their contextual-bandit route instead optimizes a quality-per-service-cycle style objective under provider heterogeneity.

Source: *LQM-ContextRoute: Contextual Bandit Routing for Heterogeneous LLM Services*, arXiv:2605.14241: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14241

For Lean this is transfer evidence only, but the analogue is useful. An `escalate_model`, `spawn_more_workers`, or `buy_search` action should be valued by prospective **verified terminal/reusable-progress gain per real marginal resource unit**, where the resource vector includes at least model tokens/$, verifier/tool execution, state reconstruction, concurrency-adjusted wall-clock and occupied worker/service slots. A fast but low-quality proof attempt should not win merely because its latency is tiny; a very strong model should not win merely because quality is high while monopolizing the budget.

This reinforces the earlier vector-outcome recommendation: retain a vector of verified progress and real costs in the dataset, then compare explicit constrained or priced objectives. Do not collapse everything to an arbitrary scalar before the benchmark has shown which tradeoff predicts terminal proof success.

## Consolidated Lean-controller experiment after C65–C72

The strongest current experiment is now concrete enough to implement:

**Shared substrate:** OpenProver-compatible theorem set, exact Lean/mathlib/toolchain, same executor/worker/verifier models, same Whiteboard/repository/cache interface, same action eligibility rules and same total real budget.

**Controllers:**
1. deterministic fixed/rule policy;
2. released-style free-form LLM Planner parsed into typed actions;
3. lightweight learned typed policy (small MLP/tree/linear first), baseline-bootstrapped under low support.

**Collection:** collect a fresh training/OPE wave with a typed epsilon/mixed behavior layer and exact propensities, not free-form-text randomization. Keep expensive/risky actions outside random exploration unless explicitly bounded.

**State:** compact logged structural features only—proof/subgoal progress, recent verifier/compiler result class, verified reusable cache/DAG delta, retrieval state, cost/budget, worker/model tier and previous action. Hidden chain-of-thought is executor-internal, not unlogged policy state.

**Outcomes:** terminal kernel-verified solve plus verified reusable progress and separate cost vector. Compare terminal-only AW against verifier-grounded intermediate value/credit and sequential estimators.

**Offline evaluation:** coverage/DataCOPE-style gate first; DR/WDR/TDR/FQE only in supported regions; abstain/fallback elsewhere. Report support and baseline-fallback frequency as first-class metrics.

**Online matched test:** solve@real-budget, verified reusable-progress reuse, calibration/selective-risk, model/tool/token/$/Lean-time/state-reconstruction/concurrency-adjusted wall-clock. Stratify by theorem difficulty and backbone.
