## Reproducibility repair — raw truth bitsets are not invariant to variable relabeling

While constructing C589, the persisted v1 runner exposed a reproducibility defect: it compared assignment-indexed raw truth bitsets across different variable orders after relabeling. Hamming rank is permutation invariant, but the raw truth-bitset integer is not. The first attempted C589 case failed this invalid cross-order assertion before any successful holdout outcome was emitted.

The C589/C590 runners were repaired before their full outcomes: search candidates are checked against invariant Hamming rank, and each completed endpoint additionally matches an exhaustive direct truth oracle in that endpoint order's own relabeled coordinates. This does **not** by itself invalidate the older C588 numerical live/compile observations, but the persisted v1 runner is not replay-safe as written; a corrected replay is required before reasserting its old `oracle_all` claim as exact execution evidence.

## Development-only probe controller — strong retrospective fit, not confirmation

C589 suggested replacing the static one-stage classifier with sequential value-of-computation. I inspected only previously observed development cases and found that several final seeded-benefit cases showed an early seeded-neighborhood improvement, whereas C589's harmful quartic `884102` improvement appeared very late. This motivated a post-hoc development policy with budget `B=32`:

1. run full natural/RCM first stages;
2. buy seeded start + first 32 eligible seeded-neighborhood candidates;
3. complete the remaining seeded first stage only if the probe already beats the best conventional one-stage live count;
4. reuse all probe evaluations, then commit/continue normally.

On the 84 previously observed development cases, this `probe-complete32` policy retrospectively matched per-case `min(two-arm, always-three)` final live nodes while reducing always-three candidate compilations by roughly **13.9–21.3%** across cohorts. Because the rule and B=32 were developed after viewing those cases, those numbers are development evidence only.

Full always-three spent **296** more candidate compilations than two-arm to gain **6** live nodes on that case.

Because the probe never triggered, it produced exactly the two-arm final live vector while paying **792 extra candidate compilations**. On this untouched holdout it is therefore strictly dominated by two-arm on the observed live-node/compile metrics. This falsifies the development hypothesis that useful seeded restarts are reliably exposed by an early fixed-prefix probe at larger n.

## Updated synthesis

The evidence now disfavors both tested gating abstractions as deployment rules:

- A **static cheap classifier** can save compute but its one-stage label is not aligned with final quality and can miss a real final benefit.
- A **fixed early probe** can be highly selective on development cases but can miss a very late benefit under size/distribution shift; on C590 it added probe cost without ever changing the chosen final path.

The more defensible target is an explicit **quality–compute Pareto / value-of-computation controller**. A late gain such as `885103` should not automatically be treated as mandatory to recover at any cost: it bought 6 fewer live nodes for 296 extra candidate compilations. Future policies should compare that marginal quality gain with its charged search cost under preregistered risk/utility constraints rather than optimize a brittle binary `seeded wins stage 1` label or a zero-regret-at-all-costs rule.
