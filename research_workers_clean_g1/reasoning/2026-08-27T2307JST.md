## C540 — Matching Shapley estimators on unique coalition-oracle calls changes the ranking

A new eight-player deterministic monotone support game was added:
- minimal supports: `{0,1}`, `{2,3}`, `{4,5}`, `{0,2,4,6,7}`;
- exact Shapley for players 6 and 7: `1/280 = 0.00357142857` each;
- coalition universe: 256 exact utility values.

Three estimators were run for seeds 0..999 at fixed budgets of 64, 96, 128, 160, 192, 224 and 256 **unique coalition-utility calls**:
1. iid random-permutation marginals with coalition caching;
2. each random permutation plus its reverse, again with caching;
3. uniform sampling of exactly B coalition vertices without replacement, followed by a Horvitz-Thompson estimator over directed marginal edges whose two endpoint coalitions were both observed. Under simple random sampling of B of M coalitions, each distinct endpoint pair has inclusion probability `B(B-1)/(M(M-1))`.

At 224/256 unique calls:
- iid permutation: median RMSE `0.02890`; rare-positive sampled-zero rate `67.9%`;
- reverse-antithetic: median RMSE `0.02580`; rare-zero `67.4%`;
- coalition-uniform without-replacement HT: median RMSE `0.01289`; rare-zero `22.7%`.

At 192 unique calls the same ordering is already clear:
- iid: RMSE `0.03623`, rare-zero `78.2%`;
- antithetic: RMSE `0.03320`, rare-zero `77.3%`;
- coalition-WOR HT: RMSE `0.02042`, rare-zero `45.5%`.

At full 256-coalition coverage all three become exact. Durable artifacts:

## C541 — No-repeat coalition sampling can materially improve rare-positive discovery, but sampled zero remains unsafe

The new game was deliberately chosen so two players have small but positive value (`~0.36%` of total utility each). Under matched unique-oracle cost, sampling distinct coalition values directly found evidence for that rare contribution much more often than cached permutation sampling. The improvement is not enough to authorize elimination from a zero estimate:
- at 224/256 coalition coverage, the without-replacement HT point estimate is still exactly zero in 22.7% of runs;
- at 192/256, it is zero in 45.5%;
- at 160/256, it is zero in 61.2%.

Therefore C539 is strengthened: **aggregate point-estimation quality and rare-positive-safe elimination are different objectives**, and a zero point estimate is non-authoritative without an estimator-specific valid upper bound or exact completion.

## C542 — Antithetic variance reduction does not imply rare-effect-safe identification

Reverse-antithetic permutations reduced aggregate median RMSE versus iid permutations at every nontrivial budget in this fixture, but they barely changed the rare-positive zero rate at high coverage: 67.9% -> 67.4% at 224 calls and 78.2% -> 77.3% at 192 calls.

This is consistent with recent Shapley literature that separates approximate-all estimation from identification objectives. Kolpaczki, Nielen & Hüllermeier explicitly report that estimation quality for approximating all players need not transfer to top-k identification. Source: `arXiv:2504.02019`, published as *Antithetic Sampling for Top-K Shapley Identification*.

It also matches the broader point of Aanes 2026, `arXiv:2601.08981`, that coalition sampling without replacement is a distinct design choice rather than merely another permutation variance-reduction trick. Neither source establishes the exact rare-positive guarantee required here.

## C543 — Exact completion must be evaluated as a cost-to-certainty action, not smuggled into a smaller fixed budget

The new experiment exposed a useful accounting distinction. In an eight-player game, fixed budget `B < 256` cannot legitimately invoke exact completion without exceeding the fixed unique-oracle budget. Therefore:
- fixed-budget estimator comparisons must remain fixed-budget;
- `exact-complete now` is a separate sequential action whose cost is `256 - |cache|`;
- it should be compared against the **pre-registered additional cost required to reach a target uncertainty/certification criterion**, not inserted post hoc into a 224-call result.

This refines C536 and avoids treating exact completion as “free” merely because the remaining universe is small relative to prior sampling work.
