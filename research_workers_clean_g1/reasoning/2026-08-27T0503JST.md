## C442 — A Lean formalization of Rego already exists, but it is explicitly a subset model with an unverified OCaml Rego→Lean compiler

Jakob Louis Kreuze's Brown University 2024 thesis, *Software Verification Techniques for Analyzing Access Control Policies*, gives a Lean 4 embedding of Rego and uses it to prove properties/equivalences. The work also built an OCaml compiler from Rego policy text into the Lean representation.

The thesis explicitly limits the claim:
- Rego had no official language standard used as a full semantic oracle; the semantics were reconstructed from experiments/source behavior, and broad conformance-suite evaluation was left as future work.
- The embedding lacks many standard-library functions/external effects, has poor support for comprehensions and `with`, does not broadly handle imports, and has an intentionally inaccurate numeric model. The author states it is more accurate to view the model as an embedding of a subset of Rego.
- The Rego→Lean compiler is written in OCaml rather than inside Lean, so its translation is not itself established by a machine-checked semantic-preservation theorem.

Primary artifact:
- https://cs.brown.edu/media/filer_public/00/df/00df3f96-8712-4ef6-be1b-4f7cc0f046a8/jakobkreuze.pdf

**Interpretation.** This is valuable theorem-level semantics evidence and a possible formal oracle for a deliberately restricted fragment, but it must not be treated as a verified OPA implementation or verified full-Rego frontend. Any use in a differential harness first needs fragment alignment and calibration against production implementations.
