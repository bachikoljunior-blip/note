## C526 — DreamProver validates iterative library consolidation, but does not causally validate usage/LRU eviction as the demotion rule

DreamProver (arXiv:2604.26311, 29 Apr 2026) evolves a persistent Lean lemma library through wake/sleep cycles. The sleep stage abstracts clustered intermediate theorems, removes structural duplicates, formally verifies candidates, and keeps the library under roughly 100 lemmas using a usage-based/least-recently-used forgetting heuristic.

The most useful new ablation is Table 3 on three inequality benchmarks:
- full DreamProver: `55 + 33 + 16 = 104` solved;
- remove iterative library optimization/refinement: `31 + 30 + 15 = 76`;
- remove cluster-based evolution: `21 + 23 + 9 = 53`;
- no learned library: `23 + 24 + 8 = 55`;
- retrieval-style LEGO-Prover condition: `27 + 25 + 9 = 61`.

The paper attributes the 104→76 degradation to single-pass evolution leaving low-level/non-reusable lemmas and failing to optimize reusability across wake stages. It also reports substantial actual test-time reuse: about 58% of learned lemmas are reused and those lemmas contribute to proofs of about 71% of solved test theorems.

This is strong system-level evidence that iterative abstraction/consolidation of a compact persistent library can materially improve downstream proving relative to simply accumulating/retrieving premises. But it **does not isolate the forgetting/LRU heuristic**: the `- library optimization` intervention bundles iterative wake-sleep refinement, repeated abstraction, pruning/consolidation, and changed library contents. Therefore usage/LRU remains a cheap advisory prior, not authoritative causal evidence for demotion. Chronologically future fixed-compute counterfactual proving remains the promotion/demotion authority.
