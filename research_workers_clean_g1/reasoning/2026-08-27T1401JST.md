## C479 — counterfactual reproving is a direct, prover-specific utility signal for lemma admission, and selected lemmas materially enter downstream proofs in a controlled ATP setting

Primary source: Rawson, Wernhard, Zombori, Bibel, `Lemmas: Generation, Selection, Application`, TABLEAUX 2023.

The paper explicitly trains a lemma-utility model using **counterfactual reproving**: for conjecture `C`, axioms `As`, and candidate lemma `L`, reprove `C` after adding `L` and score the reduction in inference steps, normalized to `[-1,1]`. This was the best-performing utility target among the tested logical/proof-size proxies. It is important that the target measures the candidate under the actual base prover rather than only structural properties of the lemma.

Behavioural evidence is also much stronger than simple retrieval. For proofs that were not found during lemma generation but were found by SGCD after the selected lemmas were supplied, the authors report that **63–96% of distinct proof subterms originate from the supplied lemmas**. With `k=200` selected lemmas, transfer to other provers also improves solving: leanCoP gains over 40% at all tested time limits, Vampire about 8–15%, and Prover9/E smaller gains.

Controller consequence: `ADMIT_REUSABLE_LEMMA` should not use kernel validity, retrieval frequency, or embedding similarity as its main utility target. A stronger task-local label is a **counterfactual marginal effect** such as:

`delta_solve`, `delta_inference_steps`, `delta_generated_tokens`, `delta_Lean_execution`, `delta_wall_clock`, and `delta_context_cost`

measured under fixed prover/config/compute. Direct proof dependency use remains separate evidence.

Scope guard:
- The experiments are condensed-detachment ATP, not Lean or repository-scale LLM theorem proving.
- The reported 63–96% is proof-subterm origin in the specific SGCD setting, not a universal reuse rate and not necessarily cross-task reuse.
- The transfer experiments provide selected lemmas per problem; they do not establish an optimal persistent global-library admission policy.

## C480 — proof-graph downstream-use counts and chronological "fully honest" evaluation are strong precedents for marginal library admission

Primary source: Kaliszyk & Urban, `Lemma Mining over HOL Light`, LPAR 2013 / arXiv:1310.2797.

The HOL Light proof graph directly defines:
- `D(i)`: recursive dependency cost/amount needed to derive lemma `i`;
- `U(i)`: recursive downstream uses of `i` by later proof graph nodes;
- `S(i)`: symbol size.

Their quality functions combine these, e.g. `Q1(i)=U(i)*D(i)/S(i)` and related variants. This is a useful historical counterpoint to retrieval-hit metrics: `U(i)` is actual downstream graph use, while `D(i)` approximates reasoning saved by naming/reusing the result and `S(i)` penalizes bulky candidates.

The selection procedure is **marginal** rather than one-shot: after each lemma is named, the quality of all remaining candidates is recomputed relative to the new named set. This captures redundancy/substitutability among admitted lemmas. The paper notes this was expensive on Flyspeck: producing the first 10,000 lemmas by the dynamic method was about 180 CPU hours in that implementation.

Evaluation leakage is treated explicitly. The `fully-honest` scenario recomputes lemma quality for theorem `j` using only proofs that chronologically preceded `j`; future graph uses are not allowed to justify a lemma retroactively. In that evaluation, the original success rate was 61.7% and lemma mining increased it to 64.8% (about 5% relative); the best single quality method `Q2` solved 46.2% with 512 premises.

Controller consequences:
1. Treat admission value as **conditional on the already-admitted set**, not an intrinsic static property.
2. Credit a candidate only from opportunities after it existed; never let future tasks leak backwards into admission training/evaluation.
3. Keep `actual downstream use`, `estimated saved proof effort`, `candidate size/context footprint`, and `recomputation/index overhead` as separate fields before forming any scalar policy objective.
4. Prefer incremental/dynamic recomputation or approximation rather than permanently freezing a global ranking.

Scope guard:
- This is HOL Light proof-graph mining of existing intermediate proof nodes, not automatically generated LLM lemmas.
- The 2013 CPU costs are implementation/hardware specific and not estimates for modern Lean.
- Fully-honest ATP improvement does not imply the same magnitude for query-time neural retrieval.

Primary sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2797
- https://ckaliszyk.github.io/d/13/ckju-lpar13.pdf

## C481 — exact behavioural audits show that a large retrieved lemma library can exhibit use without cross-task reuse

Primary source: Berlot-Attwell, Rudzicz, Si, `LLM Library Learning Fails: A LEGO-Prover Case Study`, arXiv:2504.03048.

The behavioural audit distinguishes single-task use from cross-task reuse. Direct use exists: among final successful solutions, retrieved-lemma verbatim/name use is reported as GPT-4o `2/43` and `9/43` on one subset, GPT-4o `4/21` and `6/21` on another, o3-mini `0/20` and `5/20`, and 4o-mini `13/189` and `47/189`.

But the cross-task direct-reuse table is essentially zero despite hundreds of retrieved unique lemmas appearing in prompts:
- GPT-4o: 121 prompt lemmas, 0 verbatim reuse, 0 name reuse;
- GPT-4o smaller subset: 64, all zero;
- o3-mini: 75, all zero;
- 4o-mini: 583, 0 verbatim reuse and one name-reuse event, which manual inspection found was in an erroneous step removed before the final verified proof.

Soft-reuse analysis likewise approaches zero while 30–40% of tasks can still show single-task soft use. The paper also argues the originally reported performance benefit disappears against compute-matched baselines.

Controller consequence: an admitted lemma should not receive durable credit merely because it was retrieved, copied once, or improved the task on which it was generated. Maintain distinct counters:
- `retrieved`;
- `direct_used_same_task`;
- `direct_reused_future_task`;
- `soft_guidance_same_task`;
- `soft_guidance_future_task`;
- `counterfactual_net_utility_future_task`.

Promotion to default cross-task visibility should be driven by the last three future-task quantities, not by library size.

Scope guard:
- The audit is Isabelle/miniF2F LEGO-Prover behaviour, not Lean.
- Absence of reuse in this system does not prove persistent theorem libraries are generally useless.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03048

## C482 — indiscriminate lemma visibility can reduce proof success and increase proof time, so global admission is non-monotonic

Primary source: Einarsdóttir et al., `Lemma Discovery and Strategies for Automated Induction`, IJCAR 2024.

Across 486 TIP induction benchmarks, QuickSpec lemmas improve total solves under all three tested Vampire strategy families, but adding them is **not monotonic** per problem. The same strategy loses proofs after lemmas are added in 10 cases for the default structural-induction strategy, 6 for the specialized strategy, and 1 for the strategy trained with lemma-rich problems. In the latter loss, 459 conjectured lemmas were inserted and the authors attribute the failure plausibly to search-space explosion.

On tasks solved both with and without the added lemmas, average proof time can also rise: for the default strategy, 0.67s without versus 1.04s with lemmas; for the specialized schedule, 0.20s versus 1.53s. The trained schedule mitigates the damage but does not establish universal monotonicity.

Controller consequence: `STAGE_VERIFIED_LEMMA -> QUERY_STAGED_LEMMA` is safer as the default than immediate global visibility. Admission must price **pollution cost**: extra premise-ranking work, larger context/index, extra search branches and timeout-induced lost solves. A trained search/retrieval policy can reduce that cost but does not make it zero.

Scope guard:
- These are task-specific QuickSpec conjectures plus Vampire induction, not a persistent cross-task Lean library.
- The 459-lemma explanation is a plausible mechanism, not a controlled single-factor ablation.

Primary source:
- https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-63498-7_13

## Updated synthesis — persistent lemma admission should be a chronological, marginal, counterfactual decision

The evidence now supports a stronger lifecycle than a single static `reuse score`:

1. `STORE_GUIDANCE`: advisory strategy/context only; no proof authority.
2. `STAGE_VERIFIED_LEMMA`: kernel-verified and provenance/version pinned, but not globally visible by default.
3. `QUERY_STAGED_LEMMA`: on-demand eligibility with explicit retrieval/index/context cost.
4. `MEASURE_USE`: proof-term/environment dependency + explicit `rw`/`simp` references, plus guidance-only channels.
5. `MEASURE_COUNTERFACTUAL`: where feasible, matched with/without candidate under identical prover/config/compute to estimate solve and resource deltas.
6. `SHADOW_COMPATIBILITY_TEST`: T2-style successor/integration checks only when a candidate replaces/generalizes shared semantics; this is safety, not utility.
7. `ADMIT_REUSABLE_LEMMA`: globally/default-visible only when the candidate has positive marginal future-task utility conditional on the existing admitted set.
8. `DEMOTE_REUSABLE_LEMMA`: return to staged/query-only if future evidence or library growth makes its marginal utility nonpositive.

### Admission / demotion confidence rule V0 (design hypothesis)

Do not hard-code a universal utility weight yet. Record the Pareto components first and permit admission by either of two evidence routes:

**Route A — replicated unique utility**
- at least two chronologically future eligible tasks where a fixed-compute paired/shadow comparison attributes either a new verified solve or a material cost reduction to the candidate;
- no successor/integration incompatibility where such tests apply;
- no observed regression larger than the gained solve/cost benefit on the same evaluation window.

**Route B — aggregate marginal utility**
- enough chronologically future eligible opportunities for a one-sided 95% confidence lower bound of the chosen normalized net-utility estimator to be > 0 against both `stage/query-only` and equal-compute `no-cross-task-sharing` controls;
- the estimator must include retrieval/context/index overhead and any lost-solve pollution, not just successful uses.

**Demotion trigger**
- if the one-sided 95% upper bound on marginal net utility is <= 0 after an adequate future-opportunity window, or a newly observed incompatibility/pollution regression dominates measured benefit, remove the candidate from default visibility but retain it in the staged provenance store.

`adequate opportunity window`, normalization weights and minimum material cost delta remain empirical parameters to calibrate before deployment; this is not a validated theorem.

### Required anti-leakage / attribution rules

- Candidate `L` created at time `t` may receive reuse/utility credit only from tasks whose admissible context genuinely occurs after `t`.
- Retrieval is not use. Use is not reuse. Reuse is not counterfactual utility.
- If multiple admitted lemmas jointly enable a proof, preserve multi-candidate attribution rather than assigning full credit to every member.
- Recompute/approximate marginal value after library changes because redundancy makes admission order matter.
- Keep `query-only staged` as a live alternative to global admission; global visibility is not the default reward for being valid.
