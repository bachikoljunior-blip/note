## C492 — DreamProver supplies direct modern Lean evidence of persistent cross-test lemma reuse

DreamProver (arXiv:2604.26311, 2026-04-29) learns a persistent domain-specific lemma library with wake-stage theorem proving and sleep-stage abstraction/consolidation. Its RQ3 reports that, averaged across inequality, number-theory and combinatorics evaluations, 58% of lemmas learned from training sets are reused on held-out test sets and those reused lemmas contribute to proofs of 71% of successfully proved test theorems. This is materially stronger direct Lean evidence of cross-problem reuse than the older LEGO-Prover behavior audit that motivated the previous near-zero-reuse warning.

Scope: libraries are small/domain-specific and kept under 100 lemmas; this does not establish that the same mechanism scales to a large heterogeneous global library or that every reused lemma has positive causal marginal utility.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26311

## C493 — Compact library evolution beats naive accumulation; modern Lean already has promotion plus usage-based demotion

DreamProver's sleep-stage library update performs three operations: remove less-reused lemmas, insert non-duplicates, and formally verify new lemmas. It tracks usage across wake stages and removes least-used lemmas at capacity, empirically keeping the library below 100 entries so the full library can still fit input context. The paper's component ablations also show that iterative library optimization and semantic cluster evolution matter; a merely larger/less-curated library does not explain the result and can underperform a no-library condition.

This narrows the prior negative result. A modern Lean system *does* perform persistent library promotion and demotion and demonstrates downstream reuse. What remains unverified is a stricter mechanism: per-lemma causal admission/demotion based on chronologically future matched-compute marginal solve/cost utility rather than frequency/recency heuristics.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26311

## C494 — Usage frequency is an operationally successful baseline, but it is not causal credit

DreamProver's forgetting rule makes usage/recency an explicit modern baseline for `ADMIT_REUSABLE_LEMMA`/`DEMOTE`. It should therefore be included in any matched comparison rather than treated as a strawman. But usage can be confounded by retriever exposure, theorem order, dominant proof styles, and substitutable alternative lemmas. A lemma may be frequently selected without changing solvability, while an infrequently used lemma may unlock a rare expensive theorem.

Design implication: use frequency/recency as a cheap candidate-priority feature, not as authoritative causal credit. Preserve the separate `UseEventV0`, alternative-support family, and chronologically future counterfactual utility ledgers.

## C495 — Hard-to-reprove + generality is a complementary theorem-selection prior

Ota, Osa and Harada, `Self-Supervised Theorem Discovery in a Formal Axiomatic System` (arXiv:2606.28747, 2026-06-27), grow a theorem action library without human theorem priors. Their extraction prioritizes theorems that are sufficiently general and have low empirical reprovability; reprovability uses a Beta(1,1)-smoothed posterior mean `(m+1)/(n+2)` from how often a candidate sampled as a goal was successfully reproved. The intuition is compression: a hard-to-reconstruct theorem can be valuable as a primitive even before it has accumulated many downstream uses. The discovered theorems also improve several LLMs when supplied as prompt lemmas.

Scope: this is a propositional Hilbert-style formal system, not Lean/Coq/Isabelle; the library expansion is cumulative rather than a demonstrated promotion/demotion policy under retrieval pollution, and the criterion is still a proxy rather than per-candidate future causal utility.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28747

## C496 — UseFor independently defines theorem utility by reuse plus increased future proof likelihood

UseFor (NeurIPS 2025 MATH-AI workshop) defines theorem usefulness through two criteria: reuse in subsequent proofs and contribution to increasing proof likelihood, and uses usefulness testing inside self-play theorem generation across arithmetic, propositional logic and group theory. This is adjacent rather than direct Lean evidence, but it independently supports separating `was used` from `made future proving easier`.

Scope: the inspected official workshop page supports the high-level objective and domains; do not import uninspected PDF-only quantitative claims into the Lean evidence ledger.

Source: https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/131119
