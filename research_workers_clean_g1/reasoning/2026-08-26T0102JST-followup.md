## C43 — DT-Solver already provides direct dynamic branching in Lean/Isabelle: allocate more expansion to uncertain but high-value states

Primary source:
- `DT-Solver: Automated Theorem Proving with Dynamic-Tree Sampling Guided by Proof-level Value Function`, ACL 2023, DOI 10.18653/v1/2023.acl-long.706.

Why this older result matters to the current frontier:
- It is a direct theorem-proving predecessor of the newer AlphaProof/MPS/LeanProgress pattern and explicitly **changes how much branching a state receives** rather than using a fixed branching budget everywhere.
- The search uses a virtual child whose score combines (a) whether already expanded children look valuable and (b) how much policy probability mass remains unexplored. A state continues widening when the policy has not yet produced confident good children; it stops widening when strong children are already available.
- Search can backtrack to previously expanded states, avoiding the `empty queue` failure mode of one-way best-first expansion.

Lean Mathlib evidence with the same policy models:
- Expert-iteration policy, low-resource expansion setting: best-first baseline **39.9%**, classic MCTS **40.9%**, DT-Solver **48.4%**.
- High-resource setting: baseline **45.9%**, MCTS **47.1%**, DT-Solver **48.2%**; the relative benefit shrinks when the baseline already expands much more at every state.
- PACT policy, low-resource setting: **22.6% -> 37.3%** with DT-Solver.
- Isabelle/PISA Lisa policy, low-resource setting: **16.8% -> 26.7%**.
- The paper summarizes average success gain as **6.65%**, and **11.03%** under low-compute conditions.

Trajectory/value evidence:
- A RoBERTa critic predicts whether a trajectory/state lies on a successful proof path.
- Value-function test accuracy on Mathlib: current state only **66.43%**, root+current **68.01%**, previous+current **69.39%**, entire trajectory **70.70%**.
- End-to-end low-resource success does not exactly follow classifier accuracy: current only **48.2%**, root+current **48.9%**, previous+current **49.6%**, entire trajectory **48.4%**.
- The paper identifies a concrete reason: full trajectories exceed the model context often enough that left truncation occurs in **53.00% of value queries**, covering 53.54% of Lean test theorems. On a short-proof subset without this issue, entire trajectory reaches **93.22%** success vs **90.67%** for root+current and also has better on-track rate/time.

Interpretation:
- This directly closes a weaker version of the current `dynamic branching factor` question: **state-dependent branching driven by learned value + uncertainty has already improved theorem proving**, especially when compute is scarce.
- It also supplies useful negative evidence for naive `more history is always better`: trajectory features help prediction, but excessive history can hurt the downstream controller when context truncation/interference occurs.
- Combined with LeanProgress (C39), a modern controller should compress trajectory information into sufficient progress/error/transition summaries rather than necessarily feeding the entire raw history.
- Combined with MPS (C42), the remaining high-value gap is not whether dynamic widening exists, but whether a modern Lean controller can dynamically allocate *different action types/perspectives* (critic, semantic branch, repair, retrieval, restart) under calibrated uncertainty and real substrate cost.

Scope / caution:
- 2023 models/search infrastructure are substantially weaker than current provers; absolute rates are historical.
- The value classifier is evaluated by accuracy, not Brier/ECE/reliability/selective-risk, and dynamic widening uses handcrafted equations rather than a learned policy over arbitrary action types.
- Search compute is primarily controlled by expansion samples; it does not account for modern retrieval/model/verifier/state-reconstruction costs separately.

## Frontier refinement

Retire the broad question `does theorem proving benefit from state-dependent branching?` as already answered positively in the tested DT-Solver/AlphaProof settings.

Keep the narrower current frontier:
1. **modern variable-action allocator** — dynamically choose not just branch count, but action type/perspective among progress-guided continuation, semantic diversity, repair, retrieval escalation, and restart;
2. **compressed trajectory sufficiency** — compare raw history against learned Delta/progress/error summaries under context constraints;
3. **calibrated value** — measure whether value/remaining-step predictors remain trustworthy under policy/prover/domain shift and abstain when not;
4. **substrate-normalized control** — include model tokens, Lean execution/state reconstruction, retrieval, critic overhead and wall-clock.
