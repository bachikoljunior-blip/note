## C44 — BFS-Prover-V2 adds an explicit hierarchical alternative to hardest-subgoal routing: focus all prover compute on the current planned subgoal, then dynamically replan only when that bottleneck fails

Primary/public sources:
- `Scaling up Multi-Turn Off-Policy RL and Multi-Agent Tree Search for LLM Step-Provers`, arXiv:2509.06493 / OpenReview.
- Official BFS-Prover-V2 project/repository pages.

Mechanism relevant to the current controller frontier:
- A planner first decomposes the theorem into an ordered sequence of intermediate Lean `have` subgoals.
- Multiple prover agents do **focused parallelism**: all active provers work on the same current subgoal rather than assigning agents to different later subgoals.
- Once any prover solves the current subgoal, the proof is cached and added to context for subsequent subgoals; redundant searches terminate.
- If a current subgoal exhausts its budget, the planner is re-queried with already-proven subgoals and produces a refined plan that breaks the stuck step into smaller intermediate goals.
- This deliberately avoids spending compute on downstream subgoals that may become invalid when an earlier bottleneck forces replanning.

Evidence:
- BFS-Prover-V2-32B without planner: **86.1%** MiniF2F-test.
- BFS-Prover-V2-32B with planner: **95.08%** MiniF2F-test.
- ProofNet-test for the 32B prover without the planner condition is **41.4%**.
- The paper gives an illustrative IMO-1969-P2 case where the initial route remains stuck after **7,200 attempts** at one subgoal, while dynamic replanning decomposes that bottleneck and completes the proof in **800 attempts**. This is a case study, not an aggregate replanning ablation.

Training-side control pattern:
- Multi-stage expert iteration filters tactics by model perplexity, dropping both very-low-perplexity/easy examples and very-high-perplexity/noisy examples.
- When repeated expert iteration plateaus, the system performs a periodic `soft reset`: resynthesize/curate accumulated proofs using the current expert, then retrain a fresh model from the base checkpoint. The stated aim is to recover exploratory capacity after stylistic entrenchment/mode collapse.

Interpretation:
- This supplies a concrete alternative to AlphaProof's `hardest current AND branch` allocation. BFS-Prover-V2 instead commits to an **ordered plan**, concentrates parallelism on the earliest unresolved bottleneck, and changes the plan only after local search fails.
- The high-value unresolved question is therefore no longer simply `how should subgoals be decomposed?`, but **when should a controller use ordered focused execution, hardest-branch routing, parallel independent subgoal search, or global replanning?**
- Dynamic replanning is a distinct action type from ordinary local repair: it changes the intermediate-goal graph while preserving already-proven subgoals as durable facts.

Critical scope / caution:
- The 86.1% -> 95.08% planner comparison is **not a compute-matched ablation**. The planner condition adds a general-purpose planning model, hierarchical subgoals and multi-agent inference; the gain cannot be attributed solely to replanning or focused parallelism.
- The 7,200-vs-800 example is one theorem and must not be generalized as an average efficiency ratio.
- No Brier/ECE/selective-risk calibration of subgoal difficulty, planner confidence, or replan timing is reported in the surfaced primary material.

- https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TfwePIKzXJ
- https://bfs-prover.github.io/V2/
- https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/BFS-Prover-V2

## C45 — Proof compression can reduce both verifier cost and training noise, but proof token length is an imperfect proxy for execution cost

Primary/public sources:
- `ProofOptimizer: Training Language Models to Simplify Proofs without Human Demonstrations`, arXiv:2510.15700 / ICLR 2026.
- Official project page.

Mechanism:
- Combine a symbolic Lean linter, a trained 7B proof simplifier, Lean verification, expert iteration / RL, and iterative shortest-correct-candidate selection.
- The target is proof simplification after a proof is already verified, rather than proof-search state compression.

Evidence:
- Iterative shortening reduces average proof length by about **87% on MiniF2F**, **57% on PutnamBench**, and about **50% on released Seed-Prover IMO-2025 proofs**.
- In the paper's downstream SFT experiment, training on simplified proofs improves MiniF2F performance by about **2%** relative to training on the corresponding unshortened proofs.
- Execution-time profiling reports that **28%** of simplified proofs achieve at least **1.5x** Lean verification speedup.
- More detailed project-page counts: 50/75 PutnamBench and 114/194 MiniF2F proofs are at least 10% faster after simplification; 22/75 and 56/194 respectively exceed 50% speedup.
- Importantly, some shorter proofs are slower because they replace an execution-efficient tactic strategy with a syntactically shorter but more expensive one.

Interpretation for the current frontier:
- Verified proof artifacts can be aggressively compressed without losing correctness, and this can improve both future training quality and Lean execution cost.
- However this does **not** directly solve `compressed trajectory sufficiency` for online search. ProofOptimizer compresses completed proof artifacts, whereas the current router needs a compact representation of partial search history that preserves predictive information about future progress/repairability.
- It does strengthen a substrate-cost warning: **token length cannot be used as the only cost target**. A controller should separately measure generated tokens, Lean tactic/checking time, state reconstruction, and wall-clock/heartbeats.
- A plausible downstream experiment is to train trajectory/progress summaries from compressed verified proofs while evaluating whether online controller value is retained; this remains untested.

Critical scope / caution:
- The 2% downstream gain is a preliminary same-family SFT result, not evidence that proof shortening universally improves theorem-prover generalization.
- Shortening itself requires substantial iterative sampling; end-to-end net compute savings depend on how often simplified artifacts are reused for training/inference.
- Execution speed is not monotone in token count, so proof-token compression must not be substituted for direct execution-cost optimization.

## Updated synthesis

The current controller frontier now separates four routing levels more sharply:

1. **Within one proof-state branch:** use progress/value/error/transition signals to choose continuation, semantic diversification or local repair.
2. **Across independent AND subgoals:** choose hardest-branch, total-work/risk-sensitive, or parallel scheduling.
3. **Across a planned subgoal graph:** preserve solved facts but replan the unresolved bottleneck when local search fails (C44).
4. **Across completed proof artifacts:** compress verified proofs to reduce future training and checking cost (C45), while measuring execution cost directly rather than using length alone.

A useful unified controller experiment would therefore compare, under equal real compute:
- AlphaProof-style hardest-branch scheduling;
- BFS-Prover-V2-style ordered focused parallelism;
- independent parallel subgoal scheduling;
- dynamic replanning of a stuck subgoal;
with a calibrated gate deciding when to switch modes from trajectory/progress/error/coverage signals.
