## C535 — The previous adaptive-coalition comparison was matched on the wrong primitive for a cached value oracle

The frozen `support_family_v0` adaptive sampler was rerun for 1,000 deterministic seeds with one change to **accounting only**: every characteristic-function evaluation `v(S)` was cached by coalition identity. The raw adaptive point estimate and its action-selection rule were left unchanged. A second output was allowed to switch to exact Shapley only when the cache already contained all `2^5 = 32` coalition utilities; the switch incurred no additional oracle call.

This exposed a major mismatch between `player-level marginal observations` and actual reusable oracle work:

- 40 marginal observations: median 29 distinct coalitions observed; cache complete in 1.4% of runs. Raw rare-bridge zero estimate 79.6%; cache-aware exact-completion 78.4%.
- 80 observations: median 32 distinct coalitions; cache complete in 65.5%. Raw rare-bridge zero 64.5%; cache-aware 22.2%. Raw median RMSE 0.0850; cache-aware median RMSE 0 because a majority of runs already had the complete game.
- 160 observations: cache complete in **998/1000** runs. Raw rare-bridge zero 46.1%; cache-aware zero 0%. Raw median RMSE 0.05746; cache-aware median and p95 RMSE both 0.
- 320 and 640 observations: cache complete in **1000/1000** runs. The raw adaptive point estimate still reported the true `1/30` bridge as exactly zero in 25.9% and 10.1% of runs respectively, while the cache-aware result was exact in every run.

The important mechanism is not a better Monte Carlo estimator. It is that the sampler repeatedly paid attention to already-known finite-population items while failing to exploit the fact that the complete characteristic function was already in memory. On this tiny game, an estimator can therefore look statistically uncertain after it already possesses enough information for an exact answer.

## C536 — Exact-completion should be a first-class action, chosen by remaining unique-oracle cost rather than nominal sample count

The result above suggests a general hybrid controller for coalition attribution:

1. persist every evaluated coalition utility with exact identity/provenance;
2. account cost in **new unique value-function calls**, not just marginal observations or sampled permutations;
3. maintain `remaining_exact_cost = |U_exact \ U_cache|` for the currently evaluated candidate universe;
4. when the remaining exact cost is cheaper than the preregistered cost required to reach the sampling uncertainty target, enumerate the missing coalitions and terminate approximation error exactly;
5. otherwise continue an approximate estimator, but never discard cached coalition values.

No numeric switch threshold is authorized yet; it must be fixed from cost calibration before outcome inspection. For small post-shortlisting candidate universes this switch can dominate additional Monte Carlo sampling. A recent applied 2026 Shapley analysis likewise used screening followed by exact enumeration when the retained sets had 8 and 10 predictors (256 and 1,024 coalitions), showing that `screen -> exact` is operationally realistic in at least some expensive-attribution workflows. Public source: Risk Analysis, 2026, “Global Sensitivity Analysis of Societal Resilience Using Shapley Values and Polynomial Chaos Expansion.”

## C537 — Safe uncertainty should exploit sampling without replacement and finite-population exhaustion

The previous adaptive fixture samples `(player, coalition-size)` strata with replacement. That is especially wasteful when coalition values are deterministic and cacheable.

Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, NeurIPS 2020, develop time-uniform confidence sequences for **sampling without replacement** from finite populations and explicitly highlight two benefits relevant here: each population element need only be queried once, and the confidence sequence shrinks to zero width after all `N` items are observed. Their examples include Shapley-value estimation. Source: `arXiv:2006.04347`, “Confidence sequences for sampling without replacement.”

A newer 2026 preprint by Shekhar & Ramdas derives tighter finite-population confidence intervals using empirical inverse rate functions and provides nonasymptotic constructions beyond finite alphabets. Source: `arXiv:2603.14423`, “Tighter Confidence Intervals under Without Replacement Sampling via Empirical Rate Functions.”

Therefore the next safe estimator should not merely add a rare-effect exploration floor to the existing with-replacement sampler. It should compare a cache-aware without-replacement design, with finite-population confidence/stopping, against iid permutation and adaptive alternatives under **matched unique coalition-oracle calls**. For tiny universes exact completion remains the oracle.

## C538 — Recent Shapley estimators improve point-estimation efficiency, but their guarantees do not by themselves authorize rare-positive elimination

SIGMOD/PACMMOD 2026 `ASSS: Adaptive Stratified Sampling for Shapley-like Values` addresses the limited-budget bias/instability of static `n`-stratum designs. The paper notes that full coverage of the static design can require `Ω(n^2 log n)` samples and proposes adaptive stratification (ASSS-A/ASSS-B) that retains unbiasedness with fewer samples. This is directly relevant as a point-estimation baseline, but the paper's objective is not one-sided safe elimination of rare positive players. An estimator may be unbiased while still assigning zero in a particular low-budget run, so ASSS cannot be promoted to demotion authority without a valid upper-bound/stopping rule for the use case here. Source: DOI `10.1145/3802016`, published 18 May 2026.

ICLR 2025 Leverage SHAP supplies a different kind of guarantee: for constant `δ`, expected model evaluations are `O(n log n + n/ε)`; including failure probability, the paper states `O(n log(n/δ) + n/(εδ))`, with a high-probability `(1+ε)` regression-objective guarantee. The guarantee implies average squared attribution error via conditioning of the regression matrix, but it is not directly an individual one-sided lower/upper bound for a rare player's value. Therefore a global approximation guarantee is useful for estimation/ranking but does not, by itself, justify deleting a lemma whose true marginal utility might be small and positive. Sources: ICLR 2025 “Provably Accurate Shapley Value Estimation via Leverage Score Sampling,” `arXiv:2410.01917`.
