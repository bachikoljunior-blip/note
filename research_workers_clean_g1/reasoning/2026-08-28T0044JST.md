## C551 — Monotonicity turns safe Shapley certification into an exact maximum-closure problem

For a sampled set of exact coalition values, consider every monotone completion `x:2^[n]->[0,1]` satisfying the observations. For player `i`, Shapley value is a linear objective
`sum_{S not containing i} w_|S| (x(S union {i}) - x(S))`.

The feasible region is a face of the Boolean-lattice order polytope. Vertices of an order polytope are 0/1 indicator functions of upper sets, so maximizing this linear objective over the relaxation gives the exact worst-case monotone-binary completion, not merely a fractional relaxation. The objective can also be written as a maximum-weight closure problem on the Boolean-lattice DAG and therefore reduced to s-t min-cut/max-flow.

Public support for the two structural steps:
- order-polytope vertices are characteristic functions of filters/upper sets (Stanley characterization; reproduced for example in Proposition 7.4 of *Morphisms and Order Ideals of Toric Posets*): https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/4/2/39
- maximum-weight closure is reducible to max-flow/min-cut: Picard 1976, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.22.11.1268 ; Faaland et al. 1990, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.36.3.315

Implementation used integer scaling by `10!`, so all Shapley objective coefficients are exact integers, then `scipy.sparse.csgraph.maximum_flow`. Eight spot checks against the direct LP formulation matched to max absolute difference `<7e-16`.

## C553 — Decision-level implication: exploit trusted structure before paying for statistical certainty

On this fixed small game, the statistical per-stratum bound could not certify even epsilon `0.005`, while deterministic monotonicity constraints certified the same true rare credits below `0.005` in about 62% of holdout runs by 896 oracle calls. This suggests a broader controller principle:

1. separate **assumption-free/statistical** uncertainty from **structure-conditioned deterministic** uncertainty;
2. if a structural invariant is trusted and mechanically checkable, first compute the tightest completion-compatible bound;
3. charge exact completion separately as cost-to-certainty;
4. never let a learned predictor convert a structural assumption into authority;
5. if structure is untrusted or noisy, fall back to statistical bounds rather than silently retaining the deterministic certificate.

This is directly relevant to lemma-library demotion: when dependency/use structure is mechanically available, a certifier should exploit it before treating all unseen coalition effects as exchangeable random uncertainty.
