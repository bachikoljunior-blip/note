## C592 — replace arbitrary expensive-candidate order with a cheap learned ranker

The C590 failure showed that a useful seeded neighbor can occur extremely late in the deterministic remove/reinsert enumeration (the useful n=18 quartic case did not beat the conventional stage until candidate 242/290). Instead of only predicting whether to buy a tail whose order is arbitrary, this run tested whether the expensive BDD evaluations themselves can be reordered using cheap structural features.

Development data was new and separate from C589/C590/C591: 144 synthetic positive-monotone graph 2-CNF cases, 80 at n=14 and 64 at n=16, with 29,019 legal seeded-neighbor candidates. Whole graph cases, not candidate rows, were split across five OOF folds. A Ridge(alpha=10) model predicted candidate `live_nodes / seeded_start_live` from 33 cheap features: move position/degree, Kendall distances, vertex-separation width, frontier/cut/edge-span statistics and their deltas, plus already-known conventional/seeded-stage context. The frozen model, scaler, coefficients and exact development seeds are in `experiments/coalition_candidate_ranker_ridge_v0_model.json`.

OOF candidate ordering improved information density substantially. With K=24 expensive candidate evaluations, the ranked prefix contained a global minimum-live seeded neighbor on 70.83% of development cases versus 17.36% for the deterministic first 24. Among the 10 development cases having any seeded neighbor at or below the conventional one-stage live count, ranked K24 exposed one within the prefix on 90% versus 50% for deterministic order. A matched partial-three-arm simulation at K24 had aggregate final live 11,136 versus 11,145 for deterministic K24, with 88,027 versus 88,320 charged compilations. K=24 matched the OOF K=32 aggregate final quality/regret while charging 1,152 fewer compilations, so K=24 was frozen before confirmatory outcomes. This is development evidence only.

Interpretation: the late-tail problem is partly an information-ordering problem. A bounded probe should spend expensive BDD calls on candidates ranked by a cheap surrogate rather than on the accidental enumeration order. This does not by itself solve the final quality-versus-compute allocation problem.

## C593 — preregistered untouched n=18 transfer: ranking mechanism confirmed, deployment utility not established

Before viewing outcomes, `experiments/coalition_candidate_ranker_ridge_v0_holdout_protocol.json` froze a 24-case n=18 split (6 cubic, 6 quartic, 6 Watts-Strogatz, 6 Erdos-Renyi; seeds 992000–992305), K=24, the frozen Ridge model, candidate legality, ordering/tie-breaks, three-arm commit semantics and co-primary criteria. The protocol commit preceded all holdout evaluation. Results are in `experiments/coalition_candidate_ranker_ridge_v0_holdout_results.json`.

The co-primary mechanism criteria both passed:

- Ranked K24 contained a global minimum-live legal seeded neighbor on **11/24 = 45.83%** of unseen n=18 cases; deterministic original-order K24 did so on **0/24**. Absolute improvement was **+45.83 percentage points**, above the preregistered +20 pp threshold.
- The matched final K24 policies had equal aggregate final live (**3263 vs 3263**) and equal charged compilations (**21,177 vs 21,177**), so the ranked policy was not worse on either matched-policy axis.

However, deployment usefulness against the mandatory cheap baseline was **not** established. Plain two-arm also had aggregate final live 3263 while using 20,577 compilations; ranked K24 therefore added **2.916%** compute without improving aggregate final quality. Always-three had live sum 3260 using 27,704 compilations. Ranked K24 reduced always-three compute by **23.560%**, but the split contained only one always-three-benefit case, so the preregistered benefit-retention diagnostic is explicitly inconclusive.

The sole benefit case was Erdos seed `992303`: two-arm 162 live versus always-three 159, a 3-node gain. The frozen ranker moved the global seeded minimum from deterministic rank **295** to rank **30**, a dramatic ordering improvement, but K=24 still stopped six candidates too early and therefore captured none of the 3-node benefit. This rank-30 fact is a post-outcome diagnostic only; K must not be changed on this split and retested as confirmatory evidence.

## Public-source convergence

Recent public work independently supports treating bounded inference as a compute-allocation/optimal-stopping problem rather than a fixed search budget. Zhai et al. (arXiv:2604.14853, 2026-04-16) formulate test-time compute allocation as constrained optimization and use Lagrangian pricing plus a learned cheap allocator; reported matched-budget improvements reach 12.8% relative on MATH. Yu's CC-AOS (arXiv:2607.22774, 2026-07-24) explicitly models finite-horizon stopping with state, remaining horizon and acquisition cost. `Learned Interventions in Lean 4 grind` (arXiv:2607.22972, 2026-07-25) reports that always-on learned replacement can regress non-monotone proof search, while failure-triggered bounded intervention preserves the symbolic fallback and can improve cost/coverage. These papers motivate, but do not prove, the controller design below.

## Updated synthesis

1. C589/C591 showed static case-level gating can save compute yet either incur regret or fail to beat two-arm.
2. C590 showed a fixed B=32 prefix can miss a useful candidate very late in an arbitrary enumeration.
3. C592/C593 now show that cheap learned **candidate ordering** materially raises the information rate of each expensive BDD evaluation and transfers from n=14/16 development to untouched n=18 cases.
4. But ranking alone is not a deployment controller: on C593 it spent 2.916% more than two-arm for equal aggregate quality and missed the sole benefit because its useful candidate was ranked 30, just beyond frozen K24.
5. The next abstraction should therefore be **rank first, then sequentially decide how many ranked candidates to buy**. The controller should not predict an unordered far tail; it should observe a score-sorted acquisition stream whose cheap model score is known for every remaining candidate.
