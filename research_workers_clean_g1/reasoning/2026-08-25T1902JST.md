### C10 — Training objective can explicitly trade pass@1 precision for pass@K coverage instead of accepting RL mode collapse

Primary source: `Whatever Remains Must Be True: Filtering Drives Reasoning in LLMs, Shaping Diversity`, arXiv:2512.05962 / ICLR 2026.

Mechanism:
- Define the target as the base-model distribution filtered by a verifier, then train an autoregressive policy toward that target with an alpha-divergence.
- Reverse-KL-like objectives are mode-seeking; forward-KL-like objectives are mass-covering. Intermediate alpha values explicitly control the precision/coverage tradeoff.

Lean quantitative evidence (Table 4, pass@1 / pass@256):
- Base SFT: 54.2 / 86.0.
- GRPO: 71.5 / 81.0 — large pass@1 gain but lower high-budget coverage.
- GRPO High-KL: 59.8 / 86.5.
- alpha-DPG alpha=0.5: 61.4 / 88.5 — higher coverage than both Base and GRPO while retaining some pass@1 gain.
- alpha-DPG alpha=0.999: 73.0 / 83.5 — highest precision among listed methods but lower coverage.

Diversity diagnostics:
- The paper computes per-problem Shannon entropy and Gini-Simpson diversity over tactics and premises across 256 generated proofs.
- Higher tactic/premise diversity is generally associated with better pass@256 and lower pass@1.
- A concrete GRPO example collapses to 16 identical sequences for one problem despite stochastic sampling.

Interpretation:
- C2's inference-time tactic-skeleton intervention is not the only way to recover coverage. Training can preserve a wider proof-policy support directly.
- This suggests a gateable architecture: detect low strategic diversity, then choose between (a) distribution-preserving post-training and (b) inference-time structural diversification.

Scope / caution:
- The precision-diversity tradeoff is real: maximizing entropy/coverage blindly sacrifices pass@1.
- Diversity metrics are correlational diagnostics, not yet demonstrated as a causal threshold for deciding when tactic-skeleton prompting helps.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05962

### C11 — Failed Lean trajectories can support cost-aware routing between “continue” and “restart”

Primary source: `Optimizing the Cost-Quality Tradeoff of Agentic Theorem Provers in Lean`, arXiv:2606.04883v3.

Mechanism:
- Data plane decomposes problems, formalizes lemmas, and samples proofs.
- Control plane observes failed Lean attempts, estimates success likelihood and future cost, then chooses whether to keep spending on the current target or restart from a new decomposition.

Current v3 headline:
- Evaluated on an 85-problem PutnamBench subset with a Goedel-Prover-V2-8B backend.
- Current v3 abstract reports 28.9% average cost reduction vs a fixed-step baseline at approximately preserved performance.
- Released repository: https://github.com/eth-sri/optimizing-lean-agents

Interpretation:
- This independently reinforces C6's restart-vs-repair routing result, but at a higher agent/decomposition level: failed trajectories are informative not only for local repair, but also for deciding whether to abandon the current proof breakdown.
- A useful hierarchy may therefore have routing at multiple levels: branch repair vs fresh proof; lemma target continuation vs new decomposition.

Scope / caution:
- The study is on a subset of PutnamBench and one proof backend; generality to other benchmarks/backends remains open.
- Earlier public versions reported 25.8%; v3 reports 28.9%, so preserve the version when quoting the number.

Sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04883
- https://github.com/eth-sri/optimizing-lean-agents

### C12 — Learned search interventions are safer after symbolic failure; dynamic lookahead beats static split prediction

Primary source: `Learned Interventions in Lean 4 grind`, arXiv:2607.22972v2 / AI4Math@ICML 2026.

Mechanism:
- `grind` search is non-monotone: a locally attractive heuristic can alter future search enough to make previously solved goals fail.
- Instead of replacing stock heuristics globally, run the learned intervention only after stock `grind` fails.
- For case splits, bounded lookahead actually executes candidate splits on throwaway states instead of predicting from static features.

Evidence:
- Cost-aware e-match filtering on an 855-theorem held-out suite runs about 5% faster and solves 2 additional theorems.
- Failure-triggered split lookahead proves 5 theorems that stock `grind` times out on, with no regressions to already-solved stock cases by construction of the cascade.
- Across four static feature-based split models, predicting the right split is no better than random; the failure mode depends on runtime search dynamics rather than static local features.

Interpretation:
- This is strong negative evidence against replacing reliable symbolic search with an always-on learned heuristic.
- Learning/search should often be layered as `reliable baseline -> failure trigger -> bounded adaptive probe`, especially for non-monotone symbolic search.
- This complements C6/C11: control decisions should be informed by realized runtime trajectories, not only static problem features.

Scope:
- Gains are modest in absolute solve count and specific to `grind` internals; do not generalize to whole-proof LLM search without matched tests.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22972

### C13 — Machine-checked proofs do not make benchmark scores intrinsically trustworthy

Primary source: `Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking: Dataset Defects and Evaluation Failures in Lean Theorem Proving`, arXiv:2606.29493, ICML 2026.

Audit evidence:
- Five widely used Lean benchmark families plus forks; 10,318 problems across audited variants.
- 4,833 static-checker findings.
- 398 mechanically certified issues, including counterexamples, vacuity, and unsound-axiom problems.
- Examples include 208 certified issues in the audited ProverBench deepseek variant and nonzero certified issues in multiple miniF2F variants.
- Corrected-subset experiments show defects can both inflate and deflate prover scores.

Interpretation:
- A benchmark-leader-first research worker should treat benchmark validity as part of evidence strength.
- Small pass@K deltas can be dominated by benchmark/harness defects, version drift, or specification mismatch.
- Future comparisons should pin Lean/mathlib/benchmark variant and, where feasible, use audited/corrected subsets or checker passes.

Scope:
- A flagged static finding is not automatically a confirmed semantic defect; the paper explicitly separates findings from mechanically certified issues.
- This does not invalidate all existing leaderboard results; it raises the burden of provenance/version-specific interpretation.

Sources:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29493
- https://github.com/Shashi456/atp-checkers

### C14 — Feedback Distillation is a second training-side route to preserve Lean proof diversity, complementary to GRPO

Primary source: `Distilling LLM Feedback for Lean Theorem Proving`, arXiv:2605.30861.

Mechanism:
- Student matches its own distribution conditioned on privileged, actionable LLM feedback about an attempted proof plus Lean compiler output.
- Provides token-level supervision instead of only sparse terminal verifier reward.

Evidence verified from the public paper text/abstract:
- Feedback Distillation maintains higher policy entropy than GRPO and better pass@K scaling.
- It discovers a broader set of successful tactics/Mathlib lemmas during training.
- Initializing GRPO from a Feedback-Distillation checkpoint outperforms GRPO alone; the paper reports a LeanWorkbook test comparison of 59% pass@1 for GRPO alone vs 75% for Feedback-Distillation + GRPO after training on LeanWorkbook.
- Training setup reported in the paper uses 10,000 LeanWorkbook training statements, 256 LeanWorkbook test statements plus MiniF2F, and one node of 8x H200 GPUs.

Interpretation:
- Diversity-preserving supervised/token-level guidance and high-precision RL appear complementary rather than mutually exclusive.
- This suggests a training analogue of the inference composite: first broaden/retain useful policy support, then sharpen with verifier reward.

Scope / unresolved verification:
- Need next-run extraction of exact pass@K curves and checkpoint-matched entropy values from the primary PDF/HTML to quantify the diversity/coverage gain beyond the reported pass@1 combination result.
- Feedback is generated by Claude Opus 4.6 in the reported setup, so cost and teacher dependence matter.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30861

## Updated synthesis

The frontier now separates into four coupled control problems:

1. **Policy support control (training):** avoid collapsing useful proof modes while improving precision (C10, C14).
2. **Branch allocation (inference):** detect strategic collapse and diversify proof openings only when useful (C2).
3. **Failure-conditioned local search:** choose fresh generation vs repair based on compiler/runtime evidence (C6, C12).
4. **Hierarchical budget routing:** decide whether to continue a lemma/decomposition or restart at a higher level (C11).

A plausible unified system is therefore not “one better prover” but a hierarchy of routing decisions over a verifier-observable search process. The high-value missing evidence is a compute-matched Lean experiment combining these layers rather than evaluating each in isolation.

## Rejected / narrowed leads this run

- **Use raw entropy alone as a universal quality metric:** rejected. Higher diversity improves high-K coverage but usually trades against pass@1; it should be a diagnostic/control signal, not the objective by itself.
- **Static split-scoring as a replacement for symbolic runtime search:** strongly weakened by C12; bounded execution/lookahead sees runtime effects that static features miss.
- **Treat leaderboard pass@K as self-authenticating because Lean verifies proofs:** rejected by C13. Kernel verification does not validate problem formalization or harness integrity.
- **Assume one routing level is enough:** weakened by C6 + C11; restart/repair decisions occur both inside a proof branch and across lemma/decomposition plans.

## Nonempty unresolved frontier

1. **Direct matched composite search:** find a Lean study with explicit structural/tactic diversity + learned compiler-guided repair/value routing under the same verifier-call/token/wall-clock budget. This remains the top frontier.
2. **Causal diversity gate:** find whether first-tactic entropy, tactic/premise Shannon diversity, or another mode-collapse statistic predicts the *marginal benefit* of inference-time skeleton diversification across both RL and SFT provers. Current papers show correlation/asymmetry but not a calibrated gate.
3. **Training-method head-to-head:** compare alpha-DPG / distribution matching vs Feedback Distillation vs GRPO on the same base model, same Lean data, same train compute, and pass@1-through-pass@256 curve. Determine whether they preserve the same modes or complementary modes.
