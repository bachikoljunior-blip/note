## C483 — LeanAgent: persistent Lean knowledge accumulation with premise-use annotations, but no utility-gated promotion

LeanAgent (ICLR 2025) exports tactic traces annotated with the premises used, persists a dynamic database across repositories, and adds newly proved `sorry` theorems back to that database for future progressive retriever training. It reports 155 newly proved theorems across 23 repositories and positive backward-transfer metrics.

This is a modern Lean precedent for exact dependency instrumentation plus persistent cross-repository accumulation. However, inspected sources do not show utility-gated promotion/demotion or report how often newly generated theorems themselves later become direct dependencies of chronologically future proofs. Therefore `generated -> stored -> future training` is not evidence of actual cross-task reuse or positive marginal utility.

Sources: https://leandojo.org/leanagent.html ; https://github.com/lean-dojo/LeanAgent ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06209

## C484 — TacMiner: cross-proof learned tactic libraries can have measurable downstream automation value

Xin et al., OOPSLA 2025 (`Automated Discovery of Tactic Libraries for Interactive Theorem Proving`) learn reusable tactic abstractions from Tactic Dependence Graphs across multiple proofs. Published results report 3x as many learned tactics as the baseline, 26% proof-size reduction, and a 172% relative success-rate increase for a downstream proof automation system.

This supports evaluating learned reusable abstractions by held-out downstream proof behavior rather than validity or aesthetic reuse scores alone. Scope: TacMiner learns tactics, not theorem declarations; it does not establish dynamic theorem promotion/demotion or multi-lemma causal credit.

Sources: https://2025.splashcon.org/details/OOPSLA/142/Automated-Discovery-of-Tactic-Libraries-for-Interactive-Theorem-Proving ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24036

## C485 — Isabelle Sledgehammer fact minimization is a practical primitive for multi-lemma attribution

Current Isabelle2025-2 Sledgehammer code reruns provers on subsets of used facts and applies linear or binary fact minimization before returning a smaller sufficient support. This is a concrete precedent for removing redundant exposed premises before assigning reuse/utility credit.

But one minimized support is not a unique causal decomposition: alternate prover strategies/orderings may yield different sufficient supports. Thus a lemma absent from one support can still be substitutable, and a lemma present in one support need not be globally necessary.

Source: https://isabelle.in.tum.de/website-Isabelle2025-2/dist/library/HOL/HOL/ISABELLE_HOME/src/HOL/Tools/Sledgehammer/sledgehammer_prover_minimize.ML.html

## Support-family attribution V1 — design hypothesis

For multi-lemma credit, do not grant every lemma in a successful proof the full terminal reward.

1. Record exact proof/environment dependencies plus tactic-explicit references.
2. Under frozen prover/config/compute, minimize the used candidate set by repeated reproving.
3. Repeat under pre-registered strategy/order/seed variations within a fixed total attribution budget and store every distinct sufficient support.
4. Distinguish `observed_essential` (in every discovered support), `observed_substitutable` (some supports), `observed_synergy_group` (coalition sufficient while tested proper subsets are not), and `exposed_but_redundant`.
5. Measure future-task solve/cost deltas for candidate coalitions. Small sets can use exhaustive/leave-one-out tests; larger sets require pre-registered sampled coalition/permutation estimates.
6. Global admission remains conditional on chronologically future marginal utility relative to the current admitted set.

`Observed essential` is only essential across the sampled support family, not across all possible proofs.
