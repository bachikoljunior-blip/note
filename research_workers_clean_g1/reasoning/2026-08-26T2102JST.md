## New evidence and design updates

### C350 — FAVA gives a real monotonic safety guarantee for *observed taint*, not for learned extraction completeness

FAVA's formal layer is more specific than the prior checkpoint captured. For fixed policy and required capability set, define `G <=_t G'` as a taint-only graph extension that may add nodes, edges, labels and evidence but does not introduce new trusted sanitizers, declassifications or positive obligation facts. FAVA states a taint-monotonic property: if an action is rejected on `G`, it remains rejected on `G'`. Runtime repair is append-only and the JIT gateway re-authorizes immediately before effectful execution. Ambiguous/sensitive extracted risk is handled conservatively rather than treated as an automatic allow.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27267

**Scope guard:** FAVA explicitly limits finite-graph soundness to the validated permission graph and fixed policy translation. It does not prove completeness of upstream natural-language extraction or implicit-flow discovery. Its own failure analysis reports false negatives when labels/obligations are omitted upstream. Therefore monotone runtime taint growth protects only facts that actually enter the graph.

### C351 — ChainCaps supplies the clean non-amplification algebra needed for value-flow authority

ChainCaps assigns every value a sink-specific capability budget. When tool `t` combines inputs `x_1 ... x_k` into output `y`, it sets

`B(y) = Pass(t) ∩ B(x_1) ∩ ... ∩ B(x_k)`.

An effectful sink is allowed only when its call-specific required sink privilege remains in the intersection of all contributing values' budgets. The paper states a non-amplification theorem: without explicit declassification, composition cannot create sink authority absent from any contributing source's initial budget. Context tracking conservatively intersects authority for model-visible context as well.

On 82 tasks across five frontier models, live attack success falls from 25.2–67.8% without the defense to 0–4.8% with ChainCaps while benign completion remains 96–100%.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26542

**Scope guard:** the theorem is a paper theorem/proof sketch under trusted manifests and proxy-visible explicit flows. It does not cover covert channels, hidden model-state leakage, compromised tools, incorrect manifests or OS-level effects that bypass the proxy.

### C352 — policy-specification quality remains the load-bearing boundary even under a correct monotone algebra

ChainCaps' manifest-quality ablation is unusually diagnostic. Expert/gold manifests achieve 100% attack blocking and 100% benign completion; checklist-guided manifests achieve 90.9% blocking and 100% benign completion; naive/auto-generated manifests drop to 27.3% blocking and 50% benign completion. Residual failures cluster in execution laundering, indirect injection and shell exfiltration—cases where the proxy cannot fully see the effective route/dataflow.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26542

Combined with FAVA, this narrows the useful safety architecture: the trusted substrate should establish the maximum authority and mechanically derivable source/sink/route facts; runtime composition may only attenuate authority. A learned semantic stage may add taint, risk or constraints and may request escalation, but it should not create new sink authority, declare a declassifier/sanitizer trusted, erase an established label/edge, or manufacture a positive obligation fact without independent trusted evidence.

This **monotone-restrictive learned stage** is a design hypothesis, not a published theorem for the proposed CSSC controller. Its benefit is directional: for mechanically covered grant-critical dimensions, extraction mistakes tend toward over-blocking rather than silent authority widening.

### C353 — monotone authority and semantic-route closure are orthogonal requirements

ChainCaps still sees residual failures when data/effects are re-expressed outside the proxy's effective visibility, such as shell/script execution laundering. Therefore even a perfect meet/intersection algebra is not sufficient if an equivalent effect can move through an unobserved route. The earlier `effect_route_id` requirement remains necessary: every semantically equivalent route that can realize a durable or external effect must cross the same authoritative contract, or the authority algebra simply does not observe the decisive edge.

This supports keeping three independent invariants rather than collapsing them:
1. **authority non-amplification** over observed value lineage;
2. **semantic route closure** over all effect-realizing execution paths;
3. **flow/temporal closure** over provenance and committed history.

### C354 — durable effects need a separate commit-time authorization check

`Temporary Authority, Permanent Effects` studies stale authority witnesses at the durability boundary. In a controlled 54-task matrix, 262/270 runs reach the visible endpoint, but only 55/270 are authorized completions; among 216 rows where the authority relation is invalidated before durability, 207 still commit. The proposed fail-closed CommitGuard works when runtimes expose witness freshness, dependency, binding and eligibility signals at commit time.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487

**Implication:** authorization at proposal/generation time and even immediately before execution is insufficient for long-running or staged effects. `EffectContractV1` should carry an authority witness identity/version and revalidate freshness, causal priority, binding and eligibility immediately before the durable commit. This extends the existing temporal/policy closure rather than replacing it.

## Revised safety architecture candidate

The evidence now supports a sharper separation of authority sources:

1. **Trusted maximum-authority facts:** mechanically derivable source/sink/route/destination/workspace/credential/policy facts and explicit operator policy establish the upper bound of what may ever be allowed.
2. **Monotone attenuation:** derived values/actions inherit authority by meet/intersection or equivalently conservative taint propagation. Composition cannot widen authority.
3. **Explicit authority-increasing exceptions:** declassification, sanitizer trust, positive obligations and policy activation are separate typed events with evidence, versioning and auditability; they are not ordinary learned annotations.
4. **Learned advisory/restrictive layer:** models may rank safe actions, add conservative taint/risk, predict no-ops/cost/value and route to review, but must not silently widen the trusted authority envelope.
5. **Commit-time revalidation:** durable effects re-check the exact witness/policy/binding at the durability boundary.
6. **Route and flow closure:** all semantically equivalent effect routes and all provenance-preserving data transformations must stay inside the same trusted enforcement surface.

A useful versioned `EffectContractV1` candidate therefore needs, in addition to prior V0 fields:
- `max_authority_ref` / source budget or policy-capability envelope;
- `semantic_effect_id` and `effect_route_id`;
- `source_labels` / lineage refs / sink identity;
- `declassification_event_ref` or trusted-sanitizer evidence when authority can increase;
- `authority_witness_id`, `authority_witness_version`, freshness/binding/eligibility fields;
- `history_automaton_state` and `policy_artifact_version`;
- pre-commit reauthorization result and exact durable-effect target.

Do not silently mutate V0 semantics; this should be a separately versioned proposal and only enter randomized support after deterministic regressions pass.
