## C532 — An uncertainty-directed coalition sampler improved average Shapley error but became worse at detecting the rare bridge

The existing five-premise `support_family_v0` fixture was used for a new deterministic 1,000-seed calibration. A stratified adaptive estimator was added over the 25 `(player, coalition-size)` strata. It warm-starts one uniform marginal sample per stratum, then repeatedly allocates the next marginal observation to the player with the widest aggregate confidence interval and that player's widest stratum. Budgets were matched to iid permutation sampling in **player-level marginal observations**: 40/80/160/320/640 observations correspond to 8/16/32/64/128 iid permutations.

At moderate/high budgets, the adaptive estimator reduced aggregate error. For example:
- 160 observations: median RMSE 0.05746 adaptive vs 0.06229 iid;
- 320: 0.03826 vs 0.04353;
- 640: 0.02499 vs 0.03161.

But the exact rare bridge `e_P_implies_Q` has Shapley credit `1/30`, and the adaptive **point estimate was exactly zero more often**:
- 160 observations: 46.1% adaptive vs 32.8% iid;
- 320: 25.9% vs 9.6%;
- 640: 10.1% vs 1.4%.

This is a useful negative result. Optimizing average attribution RMSE, or allocating only toward currently broad aggregate uncertainty, can systematically undersample a rare but real interaction even when the overall estimate looks better. Therefore aggregate-RMSE improvement cannot be used as a safe premise/lemma elimination criterion.

## C533 — Safe uncertainty and useful stopping efficiency are distinct; the naive anytime interval was far too conservative

The adaptive calibration used a deliberately safe time-uniform construction: for each of 25 strata and every sample count `m`, it spends `alpha_m = 0.05 * 6 / (pi^2 * 25 * m^2)` and applies two-sided Clopper-Pearson intervals. A union bound gives simultaneous all-time coverage across the stratum/sample-count grid.

Across the 1,000 calibration seeds, simultaneous coverage of all five exact Shapley values was 100% at every recorded budget. But the interval was practically useless for demotion. Even at 640 marginal observations, the median upper bound for the bridge whose true credit is `1/30 ≈ 0.0333` remained about **0.642**, and the median widest player interval was also about **0.643**.

The design consequence is twofold:
1. point-estimate zero is not safe evidence of zero; a lemma cannot be demoted while a valid upper interval still permits material value;
2. an anytime guarantee can be so conservative that it has no useful stopping power, so estimator calibration must measure both coverage and stopping efficiency.

For this tiny fixture, exact Shapley should be used instead of any Monte Carlo method: only `2^5 = 32` coalition utility values exist. The adaptive result is therefore a warning for larger candidate universes, where exact evaluation is infeasible.

## C534 — Adaptive coalition evaluation now needs an explicit rare-effect guard and estimator-specific stopping rule

The prior frontier asked for iid, reverse-antithetic and uncertainty-aware adaptive estimators. The new calibration rules out a naive deployment criterion for the adaptive candidate. The next estimator must pre-register:
- a nonzero exploration floor so a currently quiet player/stratum cannot be starved solely because its point estimate looks small;
- an estimator-specific confidence sequence or finite-population bound with materially tighter stopping efficiency than the naive alpha-spending construction;
- a stopping/elimination criterion stated in confidence bounds rather than point estimates;
- exact/without-replacement enumeration whenever the candidate universe is small enough;
- matched accounting of coalition-oracle calls and reusable cached coalition evaluations.

No coalition estimator receives global library promotion/demotion authority. All support/Shapley attribution remains an advisory prioritizer until chronologically future fixed-compute proving confirms marginal utility.
