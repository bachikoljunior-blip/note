## New evidence and design updates

### C355 — FORGE/PCAS exposes a useful *translation-validation contract*, but not a machine-checked NL-to-policy correctness theorem

`Formal Policy Enforcement for Real-World Agentic Systems` (arXiv:2602.16708v3) cleanly separates natural-language policy translation from runtime enforcement. The deployed policy is Datalog over an execution substrate; the reference monitor and observability contract give deterministic enforcement once that policy/substrate are fixed.

The translation section is directly relevant to the remaining semantic-lowering frontier. Given natural-language policy `N` and environment specification `Σ`, an LLM produces a Datalog policy `P` with clause-level source annotations. The proposed validation surface contains:

1. **Coverage:** every natural-language clause must have at least one formal rule citing it.
2. **Entailment:** every formal rule must be justified by the source clause(s) it cites, preventing fabricated/over-broad rules.
3. **Contradiction / redundancy / subsumption analysis:** formal-policy structural defects are mechanically exposed.
4. **Conditional reachability / what-if analysis:** enumerate substrate conditions under which an action is permitted or denied so an auditor can compare the formal policy back against source intent.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16708

The paper is also explicit about the boundary: the LLM translator itself has **no formal correctness guarantee** and may omit clauses, fabricate rules, or misrepresent conditions; the case-study policies were produced with LLM assistance plus manual correctness review. The paper calls natural-language-to-formal translation a broader open problem and leaves stronger translator/validation guarantees to future work.

**Implication:** this closes part of the design gap. A useful `SemanticLoweringCertificateV0` can require clause coverage, source-rule provenance, reachability summaries and translation checks, but those artifacts are not themselves proof that the natural-language source was faithfully formalized unless the source-side entailment judgments are independently trusted. Runtime solver soundness remains conditional on the lowered policy being the right policy.

### C356 — roundtrip *formal equivalence* is a stronger independent semantic-drift signal, but still evidence rather than an end-to-end guarantee

`Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip Verification and Repair` (arXiv:2604.25031v2) addresses the no-ground-truth setting by formalizing a natural-language rule, translating the formal object back to natural language, re-formalizing it, and using the target formalism to check equivalence between the two formal endpoints. If the endpoints disagree, stage-level diagnosis selects the translation stage to repair. Across two statutory domains and two frontier models, rules failing the formal-equivalence check show 1.4x–2.5x more independent NLI drift than rules that pass.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25031

The authors describe a passing roundtrip as **evidence** of faithfulness rather than a proof that the first formalization exactly matches the original natural-language meaning. A shared/common-mode semantic error can in principle survive both formalization passes. Therefore this mechanism is valuable as an independent acceptance/repair signal, but it should not by itself create authority-changing policy facts.

### C357 — validator-gated NL→logic translation gives a measurable reliability/coverage knob, and the residual error remains too high for sole hard authorization

`VeriTrans` (arXiv:2604.10341) gives a quantitative falsification target for using learned semantic lowering behind deterministic validators. Its pipeline is NL→propositional logic, roundtrip PL→NL validation, deterministic PL→CNF compilation and SAT solving. On 2,100 SatBench specifications:

- overall SAT/UNSAT correctness: `94.46%`;
- median roundtrip similarity: `87.73%`;
- threshold `τ=75`: `67.7%` coverage with `94.2%` correctness on the accepted subset;
- roundtrip score ≥90: `98.3%` equivalence rate in the reported bin;
- score 80–90: `95.6%`;
- score 70–80: `85.2%`;
- score <70: `54.1%`.

Primary source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10341

Most importantly, the paper explicitly states that its roundtrip metric **does not test semantic equivalence between the natural-language input and the generated formula**; it is a system-relative consistency signal under a fixed prompt/vocabulary. This is exactly the desired scope guard. Deterministic validator gating can trade coverage for reliability and make the learned front end auditable/replayable, but even a high-confidence accepted slice is not a perfect semantic-authority oracle.

### C358 — the remaining semantic-lowering frontier should be reframed as *evidence composition and authority activation*, not “find one perfect translator”

C355–C357 change the strongest practical design hypothesis. There is now public evidence for three complementary validation surfaces:

1. **source correspondence:** clause coverage + rule/source annotations + conditional reachability (FORGE/PCAS);
2. **independent semantic stability:** formal-endpoint roundtrip equivalence and diagnosis-guided repair (Faithful Autoformalization);
3. **selective acceptance:** structural validators + consistency threshold with explicit coverage/error tradeoff (VeriTrans).

None provides a machine-checked proof of arbitrary natural-language semantic lowering. Therefore `PolicyActivationEventV1` / `SemanticLoweringCertificateV0` should compose evidence rather than collapse it into one scalar confidence. A candidate authority-changing formal policy should carry at least:

- source policy artifact digest/version;
- clause-level source spans and coverage witnesses;
- formal rule IDs and reverse source references;
- formal contradiction/redundancy/subsumption results;
- conditional-allow/deny reachability summaries or generated counterexamples;
- independent roundtrip/equivalence result where the target formalism permits it;
- structural/symbol coverage checks;
- explicit unresolved ambiguity/omission set;
- validator identities/versions and provenance;
- authority delta between currently active policy and candidate policy;
- activation witness from a trusted source when the candidate would *increase* authority.

The monotone safety rule remains: learned or automatically generated evidence may conservatively **remove/attenuate** candidate permissions or require escalation, but it must not increase the mechanically/trusted maximum-authority envelope solely because multiple learned validators agree. Authority-increasing activation requires an independently trusted witness or a source formalism whose semantics is already machine-checkable.

This also yields a sharper regression:

`common_mode_lowering_error_cannot_grant` — construct a candidate where two learned translation/roundtrip stages agree on the same over-permissive interpretation while a trusted clause-level/source fact conflicts; the candidate policy must remain non-activatable despite cross-model/roundtrip agreement.
