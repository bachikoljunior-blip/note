## New claims

### C592 — Retained incumbent + interruptible challenger removes the main safety failure of commit-style diverse search

Reformulated the optional seeded-diversity branch as an independent challenger around a fully completed two-arm incumbent. The incumbent endpoint is never discarded. Every challenger endpoint is kernel-like verified by exact ROBDD construction plus Hamming-rank invariance; the final answer is always the lower-live verified endpoint among the incumbent and actually evaluated challenger orders. Consequently optional challenger work has a hard per-instance **no-harm invariant relative to two-arm** in this objective. A challenger candidate whose live-node count is already below the incumbent is a positive certificate: it can be returned immediately without completing the neighborhood.

This architecture matters because C589/C591 showed that committing based on a one-stage seeded win can move into a worse final basin. Retaining recall turns the problem from unsafe branch replacement into a pure **value-of-computation decision: how much additional search should be bought while the incumbent remains available?**

Development preregistration: `experiments/coalition_seeded_challenger_voc_dev_v0_protocol.json`.
Development result: `experiments/coalition_seeded_challenger_voc_dev_v0_results.json`.

On 80 new n=12 development graphs, exhaustive seeded challenger search improved the two-arm live-node sum by 30 across 12/80 cases at 20,609 challenger compilations. Uniform fixed prefixes were poor: B=64 recovered 0/30 gain with 5,200 compiles; B=128 recovered 3/30 with 10,272. A development-only staged rule derived before any new confirmation (`initial gap<=25`, after64 `best_gap<=11`, after128 `best_gap<=9`, stopping on first positive certificate) recovered 16/30 gain with 10,381 challenger compilations = 50.37% of exhaustive challenger compute. Median first positive certificate among beneficial cases was index 164; range 98–242. Thus useful challenger evidence is typically late, not an early-prefix event.

### C595 — A 20% uniform audit floor preserved support but was too sparse for rare heavy-tail tail-benefit detection

To avoid deterministic zero support in rejected branches, a second untouched n=18 experiment overlaid a pre-outcome 20% salted audit on v1 stop decisions. The salt and exact SHA256 selection rule were persisted before outcomes.

Protocol: `experiments/coalition_seeded_challenger_voc_v2_audit_holdout_protocol.json`.
Result: `experiments/coalition_seeded_challenger_voc_v2_audit_holdout_results.json`.

On 40 n=18 cases, exhaustive challenger gain was 36 at 23,595 compiles. Frozen v1 recovered 16/36 = 44.44% at 3,137 compiles = 13.30%. Three of 39 would-be rejected branches were selected by the precommitted audit. All three happened to have zero missed gain, so v2 recovered the same 16/36 while spending 4,749 compiles = 20.13%. Actual missed gain among base-policy rejections was 20, while the realized Horvitz–Thompson estimate from audited cases was 0.

This is not surprising under sparse tails: there were exactly three beneficial rejected cases. Under independent p=0.2 Bernoulli auditing, probability of missing all three is `(0.8)^3 = 0.512`; obtaining >=95% probability of auditing at least one of three requires `p >= 1 - 0.05^(1/3) = 0.6316`. Therefore **nonzero propensity is necessary for learning/evaluation support but a small uniform audit cannot be interpreted as reliable one-batch tail monitoring**. Audits should accumulate across a stream with anytime-valid inference and/or be risk-stratified while retaining a nonzero floor.

The three missed n=18 quartic benefits were especially adversarial to prefix heuristics: start gaps 62/64/67, gap64 23/24/28, and first positive certificates only at indices 300/390/575, with gains 5/9/6. A fourth Erdős case with gap64=5 was continued and yielded gain16 at index313. This confirms a heavy-tail pattern: large eventual improvement can occur after long apparently unpromising prefixes.

### C596 — Public test-time allocation literature independently converges on the same controller formulation and evaluation cautions

Fresh public-source checks:

1. **CC-AOS (Yu, arXiv:2607.22774, 2026-07-24)** formulates finite-horizon stopping with a learned continuation value conditioned on current state, absolute time, remaining horizon and acquisition cost. A shared amortized checkpoint beat separately fitted Convex Function Learning at all six unseen FordA cost-horizon pairs, average objective reduction 15.75%. This supports replacing fixed gap gates with a cost/horizon-conditioned continuation-value model.
2. **Strategic Scaling of Test-Time Compute: A Bandit Learning Approach (Zuo & Zhu, ICLR 2026 / arXiv:2506.12721)** explicitly allocates more compute to hard-but-solvable queries and reports up to +11.10 points on MATH-500, +10.82 on AIME25, and +11.23 on LiveCodeBench relative to uniform allocation. This is direct LLM evidence that inference allocation is a learning/scheduling problem rather than a global fixed budget.
3. **Sampling Luck Masquerades as Allocation Gain (Bae, arXiv:2608.13087, 2026-08-13)** is particularly relevant methodologically: in-sample allocation appeared to gain 2.2–2.6% on three TSP solvers but vanished out-of-sample; a preregistered shifted-workload confirmation retained real 11–12% gains, while charging a 20-sample probe reduced exploratory gains to 3.4–4.6%. This independently supports this reasoning worker's strict split freezing, out-of-sample confirmation and charging of every probe/candidate compilation.
4. **Anytime-valid off-policy inference for contextual bandits (Waudby-Smith et al., arXiv:2210.10768)** gives confidence sequences valid at arbitrary stopping times under adaptive logging. This is a better statistical target for accumulating sparse audit evidence than treating a single 40-case audit batch as decisive.

Scope guard: these public results support allocation/stopping methodology, not the transfer of the synthetic ROBDD numeric gains to theorem proving.

## Updated synthesis

The strongest tested architecture is now: **verified incumbent with recall + optional diverse challenger + charged sequential continuation + finish-after-positive-evidence**, not commit-after-probe. The retained incumbent removes quality downside from exploration itself; the remaining problem is estimating the opportunity cost of stopping. Fixed prefixing is repeatedly weak because useful improvements can appear hundreds of candidate evaluations after unpromising prefixes. The n=16 v1 confirmation shows that a simple staged VoC policy can recover most available challenger gain at about one-quarter of exhaustive compute, but n=18 exposes heavy-tail late jumps that defeat deterministic prefix-gap pruning.

The next model should therefore estimate `E[future verified improvement | progress trajectory, absolute search time, remaining search mass, problem structure, cost]`, not merely classify whether the current prefix looks close to the incumbent. It should retain a nonzero exploration/audit floor so rejected regions remain observable, but sparse audits must be accumulated with valid uncertainty rather than interpreted as zero benefit when no event is observed.
