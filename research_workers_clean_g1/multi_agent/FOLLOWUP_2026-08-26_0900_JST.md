## C54 — matched-budget evaluator-switch ablation

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. It extends C52's exact 16-cell archive family and 1,200 evaluator-call budget. The reproducibility script is persisted as `SYNTHETIC_EVALUATOR_SWITCH_2026-08-26_0900.py`.

### Switch model

- Evaluator A handles the active challenger for at most `cap_A=3` calls using the same Bernoulli likelihood-ratio gate as C52, with `alpha=0.20`.
- Unresolved challengers enter a reversible per-cell reservoir; 50% of eligible empty-active visits revisit the highest-evidence reservoir item, while the rest remain available for new proposals.
- `sameA` uses evaluator A again on timeout revisits.
- `switchB` uses evaluator B on timeout revisits, under the same total 1,200-call budget.
- A and B each have challenger-level within-evaluator lock probability `rho_A=rho_B=0.6`, matching C52's adverse repeated-same-judge correlation mechanism.
- Cross-evaluator dependence is a mixture construction: with probability `cross_mix`, A and B's first verdict threshold the same uniform random variable; otherwise they use independent uniforms. This preserves each evaluator's marginal pass probability exactly while increasing positive dependence.
- The switch gate continues multiplying A and B likelihood ratios. This is intentionally a **naive conditional-independence aggregation rule**; when errors are correlated, it can overstate evidence. Therefore the experiment measures system-level outcomes under this rule rather than claiming calibrated posterior validity.
- Symmetric A condition: `q_FA(A)=q_FR(A)=0.40`. B sweeps symmetric error `q_B in {0.40,0.35,0.30,0.25,0.20}` and `cross_mix in {0,0.25,0.50,0.75,1.0}`. Each cell uses 150 paired replicates.
- Asymmetric high-false-admission condition: `q_FA(A)=0.40, q_FR(A)=0.20`; B uses `q_FA(B)=q_FR(B)=0.20`. Key endpoints `cross_mix=0` and `1` use 500 paired replicates.
- Paired comparisons are valid **within each B condition**. Absolute `sameA` means across different B rows should not be compared because each B block used a different seed range.

### Pure heterogeneity helps only when it actually reduces shared error

When B has the **same marginal reliability** as A (`q_B=0.40`), switching gives a measurable gain only near independence:

| B error | cross_mix | switch quality | paired quality gain vs sameA | approx 95% CI half-width | false-promotion fraction | mean B calls |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.40 | 0.00 | 0.6020 | +0.0230 | 0.0126 | 0.3251 | 244.5 |
| 0.40 | 0.25 | 0.5967 | +0.0177 | 0.0139 | 0.3487 | 239.5 |
| 0.40 | 0.50 | 0.5937 | +0.0148 | 0.0140 | 0.3603 | 235.2 |
| 0.40 | 0.75 | 0.5847 | +0.0057 | 0.0141 | 0.3695 | 226.9 |
| 0.40 | 1.00 | 0.5795 | +0.0005 | 0.0141 | 0.3779 | 222.2 |

At `cross_mix=0`, the quality gain is about `+0.0094` per 100 B calls and the false-promotion fraction falls by about `0.0265` per 100 B calls. At `cross_mix=1`, those marginal values shrink to about `+0.0002` quality and `0.0053` false-promotion-fraction reduction per 100 B calls. In this toy controller, **a nominally different evaluator with the same error profile is useful only to the extent that it supplies genuinely less-correlated evidence**.

This is the direct synthetic analogue of the public judge-panel result in Kohli (2026), where 9 frontier judges from 7 families produced only `n_eff=2.18` effective votes with mean pairwise error correlation `phi=0.391`, and the panel improved only `+0.2pp` over the best individual on ChaosNLI-MNLI. Primary source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800`. Related large-scale evidence across 350+ models also reports substantial shared wrong-answer structure: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962`.

### Better marginals can dominate correlation

When B is genuinely more reliable on **both** error classes, switching remains useful even at high cross dependence:

| symmetric q_B | quality gain at cross=0 | 95% half-width | quality gain at cross=1 | 95% half-width |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.35 | +0.0644 | 0.0142 | +0.0315 | 0.0133 |
| 0.30 | +0.0945 | 0.0129 | +0.0659 | 0.0138 |
| 0.25 | +0.1356 | 0.0131 | +0.1070 | 0.0127 |
| 0.20 | +0.1805 | 0.0136 | +0.1536 | 0.0138 |

For `q_B=0.20`, the gain per 100 B calls is about `+0.0810` quality at `cross=0` and `+0.0732` at `cross=1`. Thus **marginal evaluator quality and evaluator independence are separate resources**. High correlation does not erase the value of a substantially better verifier; conversely, model-name heterogeneity without a marginal or independence advantage can be nearly worthless.

### Asymmetric false-admission regime breaks the simple rule

The high-false-admission condition produced an important negative result. With A at `q_FA=0.40, q_FR=0.20` and B improved to `q_FA=q_FR=0.20`, 500 paired replicates showed:

- `cross=0`: sameA quality `0.6496`, switchB `0.6485`, paired delta `-0.0011 ± 0.0072`; false-promotion-fraction delta `+0.0002 ± 0.0027`.
- `cross=1`: sameA quality `0.6449`, switchB `0.6453`, paired delta `+0.00035 ± 0.00794`; false-promotion-fraction delta `+0.00339 ± 0.00289`.

So **improving B's false-admission rate alone did not yield a reliable archive-quality gain under this symmetric LR gate**, even when B was cross-independent. The switch changes throughput and promotion dynamics, not just single-call accuracy. This falsifies the naive controller rule `if evaluator B has lower q_FA, switch to B` for this model.

The stronger controller variable is therefore not a single evaluator-accuracy score. It should track at least:

1. marginal `q_FA` and `q_FR` separately;
2. cross-evaluator error dependence;
3. expected number of timeout calls actually redirected;
4. the irreversible cost of false authoritative promotion;
5. proposal-throughput loss caused by verification;
6. whether the evidence-combination rule is calibrated under dependence.

## C55 — controller implication

The evidence now supports a two-dimensional routing criterion:

- **Independence value:** if B is only comparably accurate, switch only when measured cross-error dependence is low enough that B adds effective evidence.
- **Marginal-quality value:** a materially stronger B can still be worth routing to even when its errors are correlated with A.

But C54 also shows that class-conditional asymmetry can defeat both heuristics. Therefore irreversible promotion should not use a single global `judge quality` or `judge diversity` scalar. A practical controller should estimate the **marginal system value of a redirected verification call**, ideally in units such as expected reduction in false authoritative promotion or expected archive-quality gain per call.

## Scope and limitations

- C54 is synthetic and does not establish real LLM judge error rates, causal effects, or universal thresholds.
- `cross_mix` is a controlled positive-dependence mechanism, not a claim that real LLM error dependence follows this distribution.
- The current switch gate multiplies likelihood ratios as if conditionally independent, so high-correlation cases intentionally expose miscalibration risk.
- The asymmetric negative result is specific to this proposal distribution, budget, gate, reservoir policy and error model.
- The public judge-panel papers measure real evaluation panels but do not test this archive-switch controller.

## Frontier (nonempty)

1. Replace naive A×B LR multiplication with a **correlation-aware evidence rule**. Compare a dependence-discounted B contribution, a separate-B posterior/gate, and an abstaining rule that refuses irreversible promotion when dependence is not calibrated.
2. In the asymmetric high-`q_FA` regime, test class-specific promotion/rejection thresholds rather than one symmetric alpha; measure whether B's lower false-admission rate becomes useful when the controller values false authoritative promotion explicitly.
3. Estimate an `effective_verification_calls` analogue of Kish `n_eff` for sequential archive decisions and compare it with raw verifier-call count.
4. Sweep B routing only on high-irreversibility decisions versus every timeout; test whether selective switching dominates blanket heterogeneous verification under the same budget.
5. Continue public search for correlation-aware LLM-judge routing or archive-level evaluator-switch experiments; do not treat multi-model labels as independent by default.
