## C65 — state-conditioned proposal opportunity cost preserves quality while reducing verification calls

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. It preserves the predecessor family: 16 archive cells, 1,200 evaluator calls, `rho_A=rho_B=0.6`, A active cap 3, B cap 4, reversible standby reservoir cap 4, revisit rate 0.5. The evaluator regimes remain:

- symmetric weak: A=B=`q_FA=q_FR=0.40`;
- asymmetric: A=`q_FA=0.40, q_FR=0.20`, B=`q_FA=q_FR=0.20`.

For every current cell/incumbent, the controller now estimates the opportunity cost of one evaluator call by Monte Carlo simulation of a **fresh A-only proposal pipeline from that current state**. A fresh proposal receives three A calls and is promoted/rejected by the same prior-aware expected quality-loss rule used by `noB`. The estimator uses 64 synthetic proposal pipelines per previously unseen cell/incumbent state and caches the resulting expected signed archive-quality gain per evaluator call. A separate exact enumeration of the same A-only three-call process is used only to audit calibration, not to route the `route` policy.

Important scope: the 64 internal Monte Carlo samples are simulator-side planning computation and are **not charged against the 1,200 evaluator-call budget**. Therefore this experiment tests routing quality under a better state-conditioned opportunity model, not total wall-clock/compute optimality. The simulator also knows the proposal distribution and evaluator error model; a real system would need to learn these quantities.

Policies compared with 500 paired seeds per condition:

1. `noB`: three A calls, then lower expected-quality-loss action; no B.
2. `valueB`: after A, call B whenever one-step exact B EVSI is positive; ignore displaced proposal opportunity.
3. `route`: call B only when one-step B EVSI exceeds the 64-sample state-conditioned estimate of fresh-proposal archive gain per evaluator call; otherwise finalize immediately.

### Asymmetric regime — B is useful, but selective routing is better than using every positive-EVSI B call

| cross_mix | noB quality | valueB quality | route quality | route−valueB (95% half-width) | valueB B calls | route B calls | B-call saving | route proposals |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 | 0.7447 | 0.8326 | **0.8375** | +0.00487 ± 0.00490 | 105.54 | 92.29 | 12.5% | 373.55 |
| 0.5 | 0.7453 | 0.8178 | **0.8248** | +0.00703 ± 0.00471 | 95.90 | 80.24 | 16.3% | 377.49 |
| 1.0 | 0.7442 | 0.8037 | **0.8142** | +0.01058 ± 0.00482 | 87.51 | 69.89 | 20.1% | 381.03 |

At `cross_mix=0.5` and `1.0`, the paired confidence interval for `route−valueB` excludes zero in this experiment; at `0.0` it nearly touches zero. The robust efficiency result is stronger: `route` saves 12.5–20.1% of B calls while launching 4.4–5.9 more proposals per run. Relative to `noB`, both B-using policies remain materially better in this asymmetric regime.

False-promotion / false-rejection fractions for `route` were respectively `0.165/0.187` at cross 0, `0.185/0.200` at 0.5, and `0.201/0.221` at 1.0. Increasing cross-evaluator coupling erodes B's residual value, but the opportunity-aware controller responds by spending fewer B calls rather than blindly maintaining the same verification rate.

At `cross_mix=1.0`, `valueB` is already barely above `noB`, consistent with the hypothesis that positive local EVSI is not enough when the global opportunity cost is comparable.

## C66 — the Monte Carlo opportunity estimator is approximately calibrated but not free

Across route decisions, the 64-sample state-conditioned estimator had small negative mean bias and low absolute error against exact enumeration of the A-only pipeline:

| regime / cross_mix | mean bias, predicted−exact | mean absolute error |
|---|---:|---:|
| asymmetric / 0.0 | −0.000134 | 0.001596 |
| asymmetric / 0.5 | −0.000125 | 0.001585 |
| asymmetric / 1.0 | −0.000086 | 0.001617 |
| symmetric / 0.0 | −0.000103 | 0.001171 |
| symmetric / 0.5 | −0.000045 | 0.001059 |
| symmetric / 1.0 | −0.000081 | 0.001024 |

The underprediction bias is small in absolute archive-quality-per-call units but can affect marginal routing decisions near the EVSI/opportunity boundary. This experiment deliberately did not charge the internal Monte Carlo planning cost, so a real controller should replace simulator-side resampling with a learned/cacheable opportunity model or explicitly include estimator compute in the routing cost.

## C67 — fresh public evidence: value estimation itself has an inspection cost

Fisch et al., **“Pandora's AI Model Routing Box: Efficient Allocation with Costly Value Estimation”** (arXiv:2608.20316, 20 Aug 2026) formalizes routing when estimating a specialist's expected return is itself costly. Under its Gaussian signal model, a value-of-information rule decides whether refining a value estimate is worth its inspection cost. Across multi-LLM, retrieval-specialist, and variable-reasoning domains, Pandora's Router reportedly matches exhaustive-estimation routing quality while invoking the expensive estimator substantially less often. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20316

This is not QD/archive evidence and does not validate the synthetic numbers above. It does support the narrower controller principle that **the cost of estimating action value belongs inside the routing decision**, not outside it.

## Controller implication

The strongest current hypothesis is now a two-level value comparison:

`residual evaluator EVSI per call > state-conditioned fresh-proposal realized archive gain per call + value-estimation cost`.

The previous fixed divisor was too coarse because proposal value changes sharply with cell and incumbent quality. The state-conditioned estimator recovers sparse useful B calls in the weak symmetric regime while cutting verification expenditure more aggressively as B becomes redundant. The next missing piece is to stop treating the opportunity estimator as a free simulator oracle.

## Scope and limitations

- C65–C66 are role-local synthetic results, not real LLM judge measurements.
- The simulator knows proposal-distribution parameters, evaluator error rates, dependence, and candidate-value magnitudes. Real deployment must estimate them without outcome leakage.
- Internal Monte Carlo planning calls are not counted in the fixed 1,200 evaluator-call budget; total compute is therefore not matched once opportunity-estimation cost is included.
- `cross_mix` is a generator parameter, not a directly calibrated real-world correlation coefficient.
- False-rejection accounting includes candidates explicitly finalized/evicted by the bounded reservoir; unfinished candidates at budget exhaustion are not counted as rejected.
- The route policy uses one-step B EVSI. Multi-step lookahead could change the optimum.
- Public Pandora routing evidence concerns model/specialist routing, not evolutionary archive replacement; transfer is a hypothesis.

## Frontier (nonempty)

1. Replace simulator-side 64-sample opportunity Monte Carlo with an **online learned state-conditioned predictor** trained only on past realized proposal pipelines; charge its update/inference cost and evaluate calibration drift as archive quality rises.
2. Add uncertainty around the opportunity prediction and route B only when `EVSI_B` exceeds an upper-confidence opportunity-cost bound, or explicitly solve the value of inspecting the opportunity estimate itself.
3. Compare one-step route against a two-step lookahead / dynamic-programming approximation for B cap 4, especially near the route boundary.
4. Add reversible defer: if B EVSI is below present proposal opportunity but could become useful after state change, keep the candidate in Standby rather than permanently finalize; measure whether deferred verification beats reservoir crowding.
5. Seek real judge datasets with repeated calls, human labels, per-call costs, and changing candidate difficulty to fit `q_FA/q_FR`, repeat dependence, residual evaluator value, and proposal/opportunity models jointly.
