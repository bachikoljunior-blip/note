## C20 — Controlled pairwise-label noise shows a nonlinear degradation region; ~40–50% is a useful empirical danger zone, not a universal evolutionary threshold

Primary source: Gao, Alon, Metzler, *Impact of Preference Noise on the Alignment Performance of Generative Language Models*, arXiv:2404.09824. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09824

This is not an evolutionary-search experiment, but it provides a rare controlled injected-noise sweep for pairwise preference supervision. The authors study Reddit TL;DR and Anthropic-Helpful with T5-Large policies and a T5-XXL gold reward model. They construct three noisy preference oracles — random label flips, stochastic/Boltzmann noise, and Gaussian reward noise — calibrated to target noise rates from 5% to 50% in 5-point increments, with 5–10 repeated random seeds and 95% confidence intervals.

The directly reported regime change is important for any preference-driven population selector: below roughly 30% noise, adding 10 percentage points of noise generally costs less than 10 points of alignment win rate; above roughly 40%, the same +10 pp noise can cost 20–30 win-rate points. At 45% noise the learned model can still remain above 50% win rate on both tasks, but at 50% random/noise-equivalent preferences alignment becomes detrimental (win rate below 0.5).

Scope: this is DPO-style preference alignment, not an injected-judge-noise sweep inside evolutionary/QD search. Therefore 40% must not be promoted as a universal search-collapse threshold. It is a quantitative prior suggesting that selection quality can degrade nonlinearly as pairwise error approaches chance.

### Mitigation ablation from the same controlled study

Stronger KL regularization does not repair the noise problem and can hurt; higher dropout also hurts. Confidence-based filtering behaves differently by noise structure. With a confidence threshold around 0.8 it yields consistent/significant improvement for stochastic and Gaussian noise, even though more than half of preference pairs are removed. It does not help random label flips because confidence does not identify them. Over-aggressive filtering (e.g. threshold 0.99) hurts because too much data is discarded.

Transfer hypothesis for population search: a selector should estimate whether evaluator errors are structured/confidence-predictable before filtering. If errors are essentially random, confidence gating alone will not recover signal; repeated independent evidence or a stronger measurement channel is required. Selection should explicitly trade evidence quality against candidate/evaluation quantity.

## C21 — Direct evolutionary evidence: noisy LLM pairwise preferences can guide search when comparisons are globally aggregated and uncertainty-aware

Primary source: Karlekar et al., *Duel-Evolve: Reward-Free Test-Time Scaling via LLM Self-Preferences*, arXiv:2602.21585 (ICLR 2026 RSI workshop). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21585

Duel-Evolve is directly in-scope: an evolutionary LLM search in which the same LLM acts as generator and noisy pairwise judge, without an external scalar reward. It aggregates local pairwise duels with a Bayesian Bradley–Terry latent-utility model, uses posterior uncertainty / Double Thompson Sampling to allocate comparisons among plausible optima, and confidence-prunes candidates while retaining uncertain recent arrivals. To reduce position noise, each pair is judged twice with presentation order swapped; only concordant comparisons are retained.

Quantitative results:

- MathBench final accuracy: Zero-shot CoT 57.3, Few-shot 64.0, Self-consistency 62.7, Best-of-N 65.3, GEPA 51.3, Feedback Descent 72.0, Duel-Evolve 94.0.
- LiveCodeBench final accuracy: 13.1, 16.2, 20.2, 31.3, 25.3, 24.2, and Duel-Evolve 37.4, respectively.
- On MathBench, Duel-Evolve rises from about 57% to about 90% within the first 10 generations and reaches 94% by generation 64.
- Best-of-N uses the same pairwise Bayesian selection mechanism but without evolutionary proposal refinement. Its 65.3% versus Duel-Evolve's 94.0% on MathBench and 31.3% versus 37.4% on LiveCodeBench isolates substantial additional value from iterative evolution in these settings.

Scope: Duel-Evolve does **not** inject known judge error rates, so it does not establish the requested collapse threshold. It does establish that pairwise-noise tolerance can be engineered through global aggregation, uncertainty-aware comparison allocation, confidence-aware pruning, and order-consistency filtering. Systematic judge bias remains a distinct risk that repeated self-comparisons may not remove.

Mechanism hypothesis: replace `one noisy score -> archive replacement` with a posterior over candidate utility. Spend pairwise comparisons where posterior overlap/decision sensitivity is high; require consistency checks for known presentation biases; keep uncertain newcomers alive long enough to avoid premature elimination.

## C22 — Preference reliability can be inferred rather than assumed, but the evidence is alignment-side, not search-side

Primary source: Cao et al., *RE-PO: Robust Enhanced Policy Optimization as a General Framework for LLM Alignment*, ICLR 2026 / arXiv:2509.24159. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24159

RE-PO estimates annotator/label reliability with an EM procedure and turns it into soft confidence weights. In a synthetic reliability experiment on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct / UltraFeedback, injected noise is swept from 0.0 through 0.8. With one annotator, estimated reliability closely follows the ground-truth decline through 80% injected noise; with two annotators, the clean annotator remains approximately stable while the perturbed annotator's estimated reliability declines with injected noise.

Selected alignment-side improvements also support the mechanism: for Mistral, standard DPO 28.5/28.6 LC/WR becomes 35.5/33.0 with RE-DPO; for Llama-3, IPO 43.6/41.6 becomes 48.3/48.6 with RE-IPO. However these are policy-alignment outcomes, not evolutionary archive-selection results.

Scope: use this only as a prior for evaluator-reliability inference. It does not show that an evolutionary search remains stable at 80% judge noise.

## C23 — Reversible Active/Standby/Terminated pruning has direct ACL 2026 ablation support; hard deletion is not the only efficiency strategy

Primary source: Zhang et al., *Taming “Zombie” Agents: A Markov State-Aware Framework for Resilient Multi-Agent Evolution* (AgentRevive), ACL 2026, Anthology ID 2026.acl-long.373. https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.373/

AgentRevive replaces irreversible hard pruning with three learned states: Active, Standby, and Terminated. Standby agents can be reactivated when later messages make them useful; Terminated agents are permanently removed. The policy is risk-aware and the graph masks messages/edges according to state. The paper reports task-averaged performance +2.33% versus strong pruning/dynamic autoregressive baselines and a 15% token-overhead reduction.

The component ablation is strong enough to promote this beyond a lead. On MMLU/GSM8K/TruthfulQA average:

- Llama3-8B: full AgentRevive 68.53; without state-aware edge optimization (SEO) 66.01; without learned state policy (SPL, replaced by random transitions) 62.80; without state-aware message passing (SMP) 64.62.
- Qwen2.5-72B: full 84.34; w/o SEO 81.86; w/o SPL 80.21; w/o SMP 81.09.

The learned state policy is the largest reported ablation loss: -5.73 average points on Llama3-8B and -4.13 on Qwen2.5-72B. TruthfulQA is especially sensitive: 65.49 -> 59.28 (-6.21) for Llama3-8B and 72.05 -> 65.82 (-6.23) for Qwen2.5-72B.

Their transition attribution offers a concrete revival controller. Standardized coefficients show Active->Standby is dominated by self-risk (+0.82), Active->Terminated by self-risk (+0.75) plus later round (+0.31), while Standby->Active is dominated by message information gain (+0.69) and has little self-risk dependence (-0.09). This is consistent with `suspend on local unreliability, revive when new external information becomes useful` rather than `low current score -> delete`.

Scope: the authors' empirical evaluation uses at most 5 agents, which they explicitly flag as a scalability limitation. The method is a learned MAS communication controller, not a quality-diversity island model. The ablation does not isolate a pure `standby versus hard delete` toggle while holding all other components exactly fixed; it supports the broader reversible-state package and learned transition mechanism.

Mechanism hypothesis: population controllers should distinguish **temporarily inactive/uncertain** from **permanently dominated/unreliable**. A dormant lineage or specialist can be cheap to keep as compressed state and reactivated upon information gain, reducing the false-negative cost of hard pruning.

## Updated synthesis

The evaluator-noise frontier is now sharper but still not fully solved. Three layers of evidence line up:

1. Controlled preference-noise injection (Gao et al.) shows nonlinear degradation as pairwise error approaches chance and a >40% danger region in two alignment tasks.
2. Direct evolutionary search (Duel-Evolve) shows that noisy pairwise self-preferences can still produce large gains when comparisons are aggregated globally, uncertainty controls comparison allocation, and known presentation noise is filtered.
3. Dynamic multi-agent pruning (AgentRevive) shows that irreversible elimination itself has a meaningful false-negative cost; learned reversible Standby states materially contribute to performance.

A stronger controller therefore should not use a single scalar threshold for candidate survival. It should maintain at least: posterior utility/uncertainty, evaluator reliability, evidence provenance/independence, and a reversible dormant state. Expensive verification should be targeted at archive-replacement / termination decisions rather than uniformly multiplied across all candidates.

## Rejected / downgraded hypotheses

- `40% judge noise is the universal evolutionary collapse threshold` is **not supported**. The ~40–50% transition is from preference alignment and is retained only as a prior.
- `Confidence filtering fixes arbitrary evaluator noise` is rejected: it helps structured stochastic/Gaussian noise in Gao et al. but not uniform random flips.
- `Regularization alone makes pairwise selection noise-robust` is downgraded: stronger KL/dropout did not solve the controlled preference-noise problem and could worsen it.
- `One noisy pairwise verdict is enough for archive replacement` is downgraded by Duel-Evolve's reliance on order-swapped concordance filtering, global Bradley–Terry aggregation, and posterior uncertainty.
- `Low-current-value agents/lineages should be permanently pruned` is downgraded. AgentRevive's learned reversible-state package materially outperforms its ablations, but the exact benefit of Standby alone still needs a matched hard-delete toggle.
- `RE-PO tolerates 80% noise in evolutionary search` is explicitly rejected as an inference. It only demonstrates reliability-estimation tracking under that injected-noise sweep.
