## Targeted check 1 — AgentConductor component evidence
Primary source: AgentConductor: Topology Evolution for Multi-Agent Competition-Level Code Generation, arXiv:2602.17100v1 (2026-02-19), PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17100

The paper's Table 4 provides an actual training/reward ablation, which is stronger than the earlier abstract-level evidence:
- Full model: APPS pass@1 `58.8`, HumanEval `97.5`, valid-topology rate `100%` on both; Scomplex `5.2/5.8` (larger = sparser).
- Without RL: APPS `29.8`, HumanEval `90.2`, valid topology `56.5%/57.2%`, Scomplex `2.7/3.2`.
- Without SFT: valid topology only `15%/13%`; code-performance cells are not reported, so do not infer a numeric pass@1 effect.
- Without the overall graph-density reward `r_g(G^(k))`: APPS `52.6`, HumanEval `97.2`, Scomplex `3.0/3.4`, valid topology `83.2%/85.6%`.
- Removing density subterms also degrades APPS: `w/o Snode=49.2`, `w/o Sedge=45.5`, `w/o Sdiameter=48.3`, versus `58.8` full.

Interpretation within tested scope: the RL stage is not merely a cost pruner; on APPS it is associated with a very large accuracy and topology-validity gain over SFT-only. The density reward is also not behaving like a pure sparsity regularizer in this test: the full model is simultaneously more accurate and sparser than the no-density-reward variant. The effect is much larger on competition-level APPS than on HumanEval, consistent with the controller hypothesis that richer/adaptive coordination has higher marginal value on harder tasks.

Figure 5 and the accompanying text show the learned policy using sparser topologies on easier instances and denser ones on harder instances across APPS, CodeContests, and LiveCodeBench. This supports difficulty-conditioned communication density qualitatively/empirically.

### Important unresolved isolation gap
I did not find a direct `K=1`/one-turn versus multi-turn-evolution ablation, nor a direct removal of difficulty-specific density bounds while retaining the rest of the reward. The main evidence therefore still couples: SFT priors, RL, per-task role/topology generation, difficulty-aware density control, and feedback-driven second-turn regeneration. Do not assign the full gain to any one of these mechanisms.

## Targeted check 2 — SAGE role ablations and a failure mode
Primary source: SAGE: Multi-Agent Self-Evolution for LLM Reasoning, arXiv:2603.15255v2 (2026-03-17), PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15255

Table 3 on Qwen-2.5-3B isolates three trainable roles while keeping the other components active:
- Full SAGE overall average: `42.0`.
- Without Challenger training: `39.5`; LiveCodeBench `16.9 -> 9.0`.
- Without Solver training: `38.2`; GSM8K `85.5 -> 81.2`, MATH `66.2 -> 60.4`, LiveCodeBench `16.9 -> 9.0`.
- Without Critic training: `40.4`; code average `49.4 -> 44.8`, with MBPP+ `62.4 -> 53.7` and LiveCodeBench `16.9 -> 14.1`.

Within this tested setup, Solver training is the largest overall contributor, Challenger training is particularly important for code/OOD curriculum generation, and Critic training contributes strongly to code-side quality control.

### Planner and verifier scope guard
Despite the framework having four roles, the reported component table does **not** include a `w/o Planner training` row. Planner contribution is therefore not isolated by this ablation and should not be inferred from the full-model gain.

The paper explicitly states that in verifiable domains, correctness is determined by an external ground-truth/executable verifier, not by the Critic. The Critic supplies format and quality scores. Therefore SAGE is evidence for co-evolution under strong external verification; it is not evidence that self-judging alone is sufficient for stable self-improvement.

## New negative evidence — self-generated curriculum quantity can rise while validation quality falls
SAGE's training-dynamics figure/text reports validation accuracy increasing from `29.1%` early in training to `65.8%` by step 80, peaking at about `69.5%` around steps 100–140, then declining to `61.6%` by step 240. Meanwhile the cumulative valid-question pool keeps growing from `1,136` to `20,532` by step 250.

This is a useful failure mode: continued self-generated-data growth is not a monotonic proxy for capability gain. The paper itself attributes the late decline to possible over-specialization on the self-generated curriculum and recommends monitoring/early stopping. This supports controllers that track external/held-out capability and curriculum diversity rather than maximizing generated-task throughput.

## Updated synthesis
The strongest cross-paper controller principle from this targeted check is now more specific:

**Use adaptive coordination as a difficulty-conditioned resource, not a constant topology; train the orchestrator with execution feedback; preserve external verification for correctness-sensitive self-evolution; and stop or diversify self-generated curricula when held-out capability turns down even if generation throughput is still rising.**

The main negative-scope constraints are equally important:
- AgentConductor does not yet isolate one-turn vs feedback-driven topology regeneration or difficulty-bounds-only effects.
- SAGE does not isolate Planner training and does not support replacing external correctness verification with its Critic.
- Neither paper establishes an optimal universal agent count or topology for domains outside its tested tasks.
