## New design refinement: recorded-result replay is not provider redispatch

A previous candidate rule implicitly required current authorization for every non-internal recovery action. That is too coarse.

In the new constructor:
- a *new provider dispatch* requires a current, unconsumed authorization bound to the active grant epoch and exact action;
- a *terminal recorded-result replay* requires exact operation/action/result binding but performs **no provider dispatch** and therefore does not resurrect or consume provider authority;
- an ambiguous/unreconciled result never becomes retry permission;
- a certified-no-effect predecessor permits redispatch only when the no-effect state is paired with a delivery fence.

In an honest synthetic slice of 5,000 exact confirmed-result replays with already-consumed authorization, the intent-aware constructor permitted all 5,000 as `REPLAY_RESULT_ONLY`, while `current_auth_everywhere` blocked all 5,000. This is a mechanism/conformance result, not a measured production workload benefit.

Public evidence supports the distinction but does not prove this synthetic controller:

- **ACRFence** records irreversible effects and, after restore, returns the recorded response without re-executing a semantically equivalent tool call; it separately treats reuse of consumed credentials as an error. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20625
- **AID-Guard** keeps one reservation authoritative through commit and ambiguity, retains it while the outcome is ambiguous, and permits release/one successor only after a terminal result or certified no effect with a delivery fence. In its declared evaluation, all 210 Stripe provider-contract trials matched predeclared outcomes; 40 terminalize-successor schedules, 30 overlapping races, and 10 crash-recovery schedules completed without duplicate effects. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21159
- **Agent libOS v3** treats a post-dispatch unknown as unknown on reopen; a timeout/missing response is not evidence of non-execution, and reopening never grants replay permission absent an explicit provider reconciliation contract. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03895

## New binding refinement: epoch/status bits are not enough by themselves

Two synthetic counterexamples forced additional binding requirements:

1. **Surface census replay.** A previously valid `complete` surface census must be bound to the active capture epoch/topology. Otherwise a new unregistered effect-capable wrapper can be hidden behind a stale complete census.
2. **Authorization replay.** `state=CURRENT` is insufficient if the grant itself belongs to a stale authorization epoch/attempt. The constructor now binds the authorization record to the active grant epoch and exact action digest.

This is consistent with SCITT action-receipt draft-01, which explicitly separates artifact validity from sufficiency for a consumer intent. The draft states that:
- a checkpoint binds chain id, sequence, head hash, timestamp, but not tenant, issuer, agent set, genesis hash, or manifest version; freshness therefore must come from deployment policy / external identity state;
- a single transparency inclusion does not establish non-equivocation; consistency proofs plus monitoring are needed;
- `governance.verdict == EXECUTED` records dispatch/attempt at the governed boundary and does **not** attest controller success or physical effect;
- an execution authorization must bind the specific attempt; the draft notes that grant id, single-use nonce, audience, expiry, and attempt binding are the kinds of evidence needed, though the receipt profile itself does not define them.
Primary source: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt/

## Scope / negative evidence

- All quantitative results above are synthetic mechanism/conformance results, not deployment failure rates.
- Positive signatures/bindings are assumed truthful. Compromised receipt issuers, colluding transparency logs, forged provider outcome evidence, and a census authority falsely claiming completeness are outside this model.
- The model does not yet include compensation semantics, multi-provider partial effects, semantic-equivalence classifier errors for recorded-result replay, or corrections proposed by a noisy semantic verifier.
- The public SCITT documents are Internet-Drafts/work in progress. AID-Guard and Agent libOS support the lifecycle distinctions only within their evaluated inventories/contracts.
- ACRFence's paper explicitly notes its analyzer-LLM mitigation was not implemented/evaluated in that work; only the rollback attack mechanism was experimentally validated there.
- The synthetic `result_hash` stands in for a provider-bound terminal-result identity; it is not evidence that arbitrary text replay is safe.
