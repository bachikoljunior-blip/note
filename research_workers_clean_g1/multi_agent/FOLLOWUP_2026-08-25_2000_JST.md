## C16 — Selective exchange has a measurable cadence/acceptance optimum; neither isolation nor indiscriminate migration is sufficient

Primary source: Wang et al., *Towards Diverse Scientific Hypothesis Search with Large Language Models* (EvoDiverse), arXiv:2606.10587. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10587

EvoDiverse uses multiple temperature-separated populations and Metropolis-Hastings-gated exchanges. The paper provides a direct LLM-era exchange-cadence sensitivity study on 10 Physics LLM-SRBench problems with DeepSeek-V3.2 under a fixed evaluation budget.

With swap size k=5:

- period 2: diversity 0.321, Best Acc@0.1 0.5, Top-10 Acc@0.1 0.3
- period 5 (default): 0.302, 0.5, 0.3
- period 10: 0.295, 0.3, 0.2
- period 20: 0.284, 0.3, 0.2

At fixed period 5, varying exchanged candidates gives:

- k=1: diversity 0.289, Best Acc@0.1 0.3, Top-10 0.3
- k=3: 0.291, 0.4, 0.3
- k=5: 0.302, 0.5, 0.3
- k=10: 0.302, 0.5, 0.3

Thus exchange every 2–5 iterations is materially better than 10–20 in this setting, while increasing swap size beyond about five gives no observed gain. This is direct evidence for a Goldilocks region rather than `more migration is better`.

The acceptance mechanism also matters. On Physics, two isolated pools (`Ensemble`) score diversity 0.289 / best 0.333 / top-10 0.240; unconditional `Random Swap` reaches 0.295 / 0.346 / 0.263; MH-gated exchange reaches 0.308 / 0.416 / 0.280. On Materials the respective best/top-10 values are 0.688/0.641, 0.684/0.675, and 0.706/0.700. Therefore the gain is not explained by communication alone: selective acceptance contributes.

The circle-packing comparison under the same 1,000-program oracle budget reinforces the interaction between separation and exchange. EA achieves best 2.4986 / top-100 average 2.4302 / diversity 0.596; a ring-migration Island baseline 2.4247 / 2.4241 / 0.471; isolated Ensemble 2.4105 / 2.3330 / 0.691; Random Swap 2.4507 / 2.4507 / 0.576; EvoDiverse 2.5461 / 2.5138 / 0.714. The island baseline's pools become comparatively homogeneous, while pure isolation can leave one pool contributing none of the top-100 candidates. Selective exchange preserves differentiated lineages without permanently stranding them.

Pool count is also not monotone. On the equation-discovery study, two-temperature EvoDiverse gives diversity 0.308 / best 0.416 / top-10 0.280, while three temperatures give 0.316 / 0.421 / 0.280. The marginal gain is small enough that the authors retain two pools for cost-benefit reasons under the fixed total budget.

Scope: the cadence table is for parallel-tempering-style swaps in EvoDiverse, not a universal optimum for all island-model LLM evolution. Exact periods will depend on generation cost, population turnover, landscape, and evaluation latency. The circle-packing Island baseline also differs algorithmically from EvoDiverse, so its underperformance is not a clean migration-frequency ablation. What is directly supported is that exchange cadence, exchange size, and acceptance policy have measurable, non-monotone effects under fixed budgets.

Mechanism hypothesis: maintain semi-independent lineages, estimate stagnation/diversity, and exchange a bounded number of high-information candidates through a quality-aware gate. Communication cadence should itself be a controlled variable rather than a fixed always-on broadcast.

## C17 — Low-fidelity evolutionary fitness can be nearly uninformative for champion ranking; hierarchical deep evaluation is a high-value control primitive

Primary source: Li et al., *Beyond Static Evaluation: Co-Evolutionary Mechanisms for LLM-Driven Strategy Evolution in Adversarial Games* (FAMOU), arXiv:2606.10389. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10389

FAMOU separates a fast evaluator from a deeper evaluator. Fast evaluation uses 3 games per opponent; deep evaluation uses 20 games per opponent. In a preliminary reliability check, the 3-game score has only Spearman rho=0.11 (p=0.69) against a 20–40-game deeper ranking. This is an unusually concrete example of an apparently useful scalar fitness estimate carrying almost no reliable ordering information at the top of a stochastic evolutionary search.

The exploratory Gemini-2.5-Flash ablation reports combined score (CS): full FAMOU 0.521; without co-evolution 0.450 (-13.7%, p=0.084); without deep evaluation 0.385 (-26.0%, p=0.002); without weakness pressure 0.424 (-18.6%, p=0.160); and a vanilla version with all three removed 0.432 (-17.1%, p=0.020). Removing deep evaluation is the largest individual drop in this table.

The main 400-iteration experiments also show strong final performance: with DeepSeek-V4-Flash, FAMOU reports CS 0.526±0.075, win rate 0.680±0.080, margin +0.83±0.32, and unseen-opponent win rate 0.617±0.072. The corresponding ShinkaEvolve and OpenEvolve unseen win rates are 0.342±0.054 and 0.312±0.041.

Important caveats: the main comparison is equal-iteration, not equal-compute, because FAMOU spends additional games on deep evaluation and co-evolution. The ablation is single-run and components interact, so the percentage changes must not be interpreted as independent additive causal effects. The rho=0.11 result also does not imply a universal judge-accuracy threshold; it establishes that very noisy cheap fitness can badly mis-rank candidates and that deeper selective evaluation can matter substantially in one adversarial domain.

Mechanism hypothesis: evolutionary search should use a cheap broad screen only for triage, then escalate plausible champions/uncertain comparisons to a materially higher-fidelity evaluator before selection or archive replacement. Evaluation budget should be allocated adaptively rather than multiplying low-quality votes.

## C18 — Evaluator-panel size can dramatically overstate independent evidence because LLM judge errors are strongly correlated

Primary source: Guneet Kohli, *Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels*, arXiv:2605.29800. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800

Across nine frontier judges from seven model families on 1,000 ChaosNLI-MNLI items, the paper reports Kish effective panel size n_eff=2.18 [2.07, 2.31], eigenvalue n_eff=2.16, mean pairwise error correlation phi=0.391±0.111, and an independence ratio of only 24.2%. Majority-panel accuracy is 72.0% versus 71.8% for the best single judge: nine nominal votes yield only +0.2 percentage points.

Correlated failure is visible directly: all nine judges are wrong on 51/1,000 items, whereas fewer than one such unanimous error would be expected under an independence model. Scaling also saturates quickly: the first five judges capture about 90% of the achievable effective independence (n_eff 1.96 versus 2.18 for all nine), so judges 6–9 add only +0.22 effective votes.

The pattern transfers to pairwise preference evaluation, which is especially relevant to evolutionary candidate selection. On RewardBench, n_eff is 1.99 [1.83, 2.20], phi=0.440, and majority-panel accuracy 92.7%. The best individual judge reaches 95.5%, so adding correlated voters actually reduces accuracy. Chain-of-thought does not decorrelate the panel in this study: it increases phi to 0.456 and lowers n_eff to 1.94.

Simple aggregation does not repair the problem. Majority vote, Dawid-Skene, and accuracy weighting fail to close most of the independence gap; the best single judge beats all aggregation methods on several datasets. Family diversity also helps much less than a naive `different model brands = independent evidence` assumption would predict: same-family versus cross-family error-correlation difference is modest, and a one-per-family seven-judge panel has n_eff 1.93.

The author's heuristic warning threshold n_eff/k < 0.5 is useful as a diagnostic but is not an experimentally established collapse threshold for evolutionary search. Likewise, this study measures classification/pairwise preferences rather than open-ended program or scientific-hypothesis ranking. The transferable result is narrower: evaluator multiplicity is not equivalent to evaluator independence, and correlated errors can erase or reverse the expected benefit of voting.

Mechanism hypothesis: before adding evaluator agents, measure effective independent votes/error correlation on held-out decisions. Spend additional budget on genuinely different evidence channels (tool execution, independent measurements, adversarial tests, stronger/deeper verification) rather than more correlated LLM opinions.

## C19 — Lead: reversible agent pruning may dominate hard deletion when agent quality is nonstationary

Primary lead: *Taming “Zombie” Agents: A Markov State-Aware Framework for Resilient Multi-Agent Evolution* (AgentRevive), ACL 2026. https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.?

The ACL abstract describes a state-aware framework with Active, Standby, and Terminated agent states, a risk estimator, and state-aware edge pruning, motivated by the failure mode where aggressive hard pruning permanently removes temporarily weak agents that could later become useful as the task state changes. The abstract reports improved performance and reduced token consumption, but exact tables were not extracted in this run. Therefore no quantitative claim is promoted yet.

## Updated cross-source synthesis

This run converts two previously vague control ideas into measurable design variables.

First, population communication should be **selective and rate-controlled**. EvoDiverse provides direct LLM-era evidence that exchange interval and exchanged-candidate count have non-monotone effects under fixed budgets, and that gated swaps outperform both isolation and unconditional exchange in the studied domains. More pools also show diminishing returns.

Second, evaluation should be **fidelity- and independence-aware**. FAMOU shows that a cheap stochastic fitness estimate can have almost no rank correlation with deep evaluation and that removing deep evaluation produces the largest reported individual ablation loss. The correlated-judges study shows that adding nominal judge agents can yield only about two effective votes out of nine and can even underperform the strongest single evaluator.

A stronger population controller therefore needs to adapt at least four coupled quantities: (1) lineage separation and exchange cadence, (2) exchange acceptance policy, (3) evaluator-fidelity escalation, and (4) evaluator effective independence. Raw population size or raw judge count is an insufficient control variable.

The emerging control principle is: **selective communication plus selective deep verification**. Preserve semi-independent search lineages long enough to generate genuinely different candidates; exchange only enough high-value information to prevent stranded subpopulations; and spend expensive evaluation only where selection/archive decisions are sensitive. This targets marginal independent information per unit cost rather than nominal agent count.

## Rejected / downgraded leads and hypotheses

- `More frequent migration is always better` is rejected for the studied EvoDiverse setting: periods 10–20 underperform 2–5, while unconditional/frequent information exchange can homogenize lineages.
- `Larger exchange batches are always better` is rejected in the direct k sweep: k=5 and k=10 are indistinguishable on the reported metrics.
- `More pools are always better` is downgraded: two to three temperature pools yields only a small gain under the fixed budget.
- `More judge agents imply more reliable selection` is rejected as a general rule. Nine judges can collapse to roughly two effective independent votes because errors are correlated.
- `Different model families or chain-of-thought automatically decorrelate judges` is downgraded by the measured error correlations.
- `Cheap 3-game fitness is sufficient for champion selection` is rejected in the FAMOU domain; its rank correlation with deeper evaluation is rho=0.11.
- No exact universal judge-accuracy/evaluator-noise threshold is claimed. The requested controlled noise-sweep frontier remains unresolved.
- AgentRevive is retained only as a lead until exact quantitative tables and ablations are extracted from the primary paper.
