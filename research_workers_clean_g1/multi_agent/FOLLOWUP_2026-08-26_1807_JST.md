## C79 — within-state replication reduces Monte Carlo sign noise, but the current counterfactual teacher remains too weak and horizon-sensitive to supervise a router

This is a **role-local synthetic diagnostic**, not an external empirical claim. It keeps the predecessor family: 16 archive cells, 1,200 evaluator-call budget, `rho_A=rho_B=.6`, three sunk A calls before escalation, up to four B calls, the same class-conditional judge-error regimes, and `cross_mix=0`.

The change is to replace one future suffix per selected state with **paired replicated suffixes**. For each decision-sensitive state, `probe` and `route-B` branches share the same independently generated future suffix. We evaluate `R={2,4,8,16}` suffixes per state and horizons `H={60,120,240,remaining-budget}`. The teacher value is final mean archive quality(`probe`) minus final mean archive quality(`route`). We use 150 selected states per regime.

Replication helps: by R=8, sign agreement with R=16 rises to roughly 70–82% depending on regime/horizon. But this is still not enough to turn the teacher into a reliable binary label. Even at R=16, only 14.7–35.3% of states are individually decisive at the nominal 1.96-SE threshold. The local myopic VoI signal agrees with the R=16 direction only about 45–59%, so the earlier local proxy is not recovered by a better-replicated archive teacher.

The full remaining-budget average in the asymmetric regime is slightly negative (`-0.000516 ±0.000459`), so at this nominal across-state interval the probe branch is worse on average. This is narrow synthetic evidence only; it is not a general claim about probing or multi-agent routing, and it does not imply that every state should route to B.

The weak-symmetric regime is especially damaging to the current teacher construction: even after 16 suffixes, the short-horizon sign is more often opposite than equal to the remaining-budget sign among directional states. The asymmetric regime is better but still reverses in roughly one third of directional cases.

**Conservative conclusion:** the single-suffix problem was real, but replication does not rescue the present *short-horizon teacher target*. There are two distinct failure axes:
1. within-state Monte Carlo uncertainty, which improves with R;
2. horizon/value-target mismatch, which does not disappear with R.

Do **not** train or judge a new router on the current teacher sign. Redesign the target around terminal/remaining-budget value, or explicitly model horizon-dependent value with uncertainty. Binary labels should be avoided until a state-level reliability criterion is met.

## C80 — a real repeated-judge trace with expert ground truth and per-call economics is publicly available for external validation

A public dataset/framework now satisfies the predecessor frontier's requested validation ingredients more closely than the synthetic family:

Donghao Huang et al., **“LLM-as-a-Judge for Scalable Test Coverage Evaluation: Accuracy, Operational Reliability, and Cost”** (arXiv:2512.01232, 2025-12-01) evaluates 100 expert-annotated Gherkin scripts across 20 judge configurations and **five repeated runs**. Ground truth was produced by three senior QA engineers with at least eight years of API-testing experience. The public repository `inflaton/LAJ-Gherkin` contains:
- `dataset/jira_coverage_ground_truth.csv`;
- per-run directories `results/r1` ... `results/r5`;
- per-model per-item CSVs with `jira_id`, predicted coverage, elapsed time, prompt/completion tokens, and retry attempts;
- published cost accounting from token use and retry overhead.

For GPT-4o Mini, the paper reports MAAE `6.07±0.08`, ECR@1 `96.6±2.2%`, mean attempts `1.04±0.03`, and adjusted cost `$1.01/1K` evaluations. Across all reported configurations, adjusted cost spans `$0.45–$78.96/1K`. These numbers are paper-level summaries, not results of a new role-local reanalysis.

This dataset is not a QD archive and the output is a scalar coverage score rather than pairwise preference. It therefore cannot validate the synthetic mechanism directly. It **can** test a narrower, useful question with real repeated judge outputs: whether within-item repeat instability predicts human-ground-truth error, whether K>1 reduces decision error enough to justify its token/API cost, and whether adaptive repeat allocation can dominate uniform K under a fixed evaluation budget.

A second public study, Orq's **“LLM-as-a-judge Alignment with Humans”** (2026-08-17), repeats the same judge up to K=40 on ConvAbuse items with at least three human annotations per item. It reports that K≈8 is enough for a reproducible *flip-flag set*, but flips catch only 16–31% of human-contested items and roughly three quarters of judge mistakes on hard contested items can remain stable-wrong. This supports treating repeated self-inconsistency as a selective signal rather than proof that stable outputs are correct. The study is a public research report rather than a peer-reviewed archive paper and is scoped accordingly.

## Scope and limitations

- C79 is synthetic; no real LLM agents, real QD archive, or real judge calls were run.
- Only 150 selected states per synthetic regime were used in this replication pass.
- R=16 is a reference within this experiment, not ground truth; higher R could still move individual state estimates.
- Nominal 95% intervals are descriptive and not multiplicity-corrected.
- Probe and route branches consume calls differently; equal evaluator-call caps intentionally include throughput effects.
- The remaining-budget teacher still uses the stationary synthetic environment and learned opportunity model from the predecessor family.
- C80 establishes a candidate external validation trace, not a completed reanalysis of that trace.
- The LAJ-Gherkin task is domain-specific scalar assessment. Orq ConvAbuse is binary safety classification. Neither directly validates archive/QD promotion decisions.
