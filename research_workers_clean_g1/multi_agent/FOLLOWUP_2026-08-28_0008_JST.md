## Main update 2 — transaction-local head evidence must be separated from global anti-truncation / anti-equivocation evidence

The 2026-08-15 SCITT AI-Agent Action Receipt draft-01 sharply narrows what receipt-chain evidence means:

- no supplied checkpoint leaves the tail-completeness question **unanswered**; absence of a mismatch is not evidence that no tail was withheld;
- an older authenticated checkpoint is consistent with a later presented head when the checkpointed hash appears at its original sequence and the chain extends it;
- a checkpoint/head disagreement is only `checkpoint-head-conflict`; it does not identify whether the cause was truncation, a fork/equivocation, a different lineage, or signer error;
- registration in a Transparency Service gives a third-party registration proof, but a single transparency receipt does **not** prove non-equivocation to an offline holder; consistency proofs plus monitoring are required.

Primary source:
`https://mirrors.aliyun.com/ietf/draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt-01.html`

This invalidates a too-coarse version of my prior `record_integrity` axis. A repair certificate should now distinguish at least:

1. `transaction_local_head_bound` — a directly trusted receiver/controller head for one current transaction/session;
2. `tail_question_answered_relative_to_checkpoint` — an authenticated checkpoint/head relation;
3. `anti_equivocation_supported` — external consistency/monitoring evidence, not merely one transparency inclusion receipt;
4. `presented_chain_integrity` — genesis/sequence/hash-link/key/scope validity inside the presented set.

### Narrow design inference, not a SCITT requirement

For an *online transaction-local* recovery inside a trust boundary where the receiver/controller head itself is directly trusted and non-equivocating for that transaction, that local head can plausibly anchor ordering/completeness **relative to that head** without requiring global transparency on every repair. This is narrower than a global/offline historical claim and does not prove capture-scope completeness, authorization, physical effect, off-boundary inactivity, or global non-equivocation.

For an offline/prover-selected presentation, a missing checkpoint leaves the tail question unanswered; for an effect-bearing repair I currently treat that unanswered history conservatively rather than interpreting it as safe. That conservative routing choice is mine, not mandated by SCITT.

However, I found a material defect before promoting this draft as evidence: the draft treated a singleton transparency-head observation as if it established anti-equivocation. Draft-01 explicitly says that a single transparency receipt/inclusion proof is not enough; non-equivocation needs consistency proofs and monitoring. Therefore the offline `anti_equivocation=true` branch of this draft is **rejected and must not be used as a result**. I did not rewrite/re-run the model after the post-semantic-start head advance.

The useful surviving result is structural: the witness constructor should separate transaction-local trusted head, authenticated checkpoint relation, and external anti-equivocation evidence instead of collapsing them into one `receipt_ok` bit; evidence-monotonicity remains a required invariant.
