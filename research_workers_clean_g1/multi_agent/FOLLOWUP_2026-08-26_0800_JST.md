## C52 — matched-budget promotion-threshold / timeout-Standby ablation

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. It follows C50's class-conditional archive-decision-noise question but fixes a fully specified proposal family so this and future follow-ups can be reproduced exactly. C50 did not persist its exact per-cell Beta parameters, so C52 absolute quality values must **not** be treated as code-identical continuations of C50; policy comparisons within C52 are matched and valid for this synthetic model.

### Fully specified model

- 16 archive cells.
- Initial incumbent in cell `i`: `Beta(a_i,b_i)` with `a_i = 2.0 + 0.35*(i mod 4)`, `b_i = 2.6 + 0.25*floor(i/4)`.
- Exactly 1,200 evaluator calls per replicate.
- New challenger: 55% independent draw from that cell's `Beta(a_i,b_i)`; 45% local mutation `clip(incumbent + Normal(0.04,0.18), 0, 1)`.
- If challenger is truly better, evaluator passes it with probability `1-q_FR`; otherwise it passes with probability `q_FA`.
- `rho=0` means fresh independent repeated verdicts. `rho=0.6` means 60% of challengers lock all later reevaluations to their first verdict, an intentionally adverse same-judge correlation model.
- Sequential gate uses Bernoulli likelihood-ratio evidence, promotes at `E >= 1/alpha`, rejects at `E <= alpha`, and otherwise times out after `cap` active evaluations.
- `discard`: unresolved timeout is discarded.
- `standby`: unresolved timeout enters a reversible per-cell reservoir of capacity 4, ordered only by current evidence `E`; when the cell has no active challenger, 25% of visits re-evaluate the highest-evidence Standby item and 75% remain available for new proposals. Any authoritative promotion clears stale same-cell Standby evidence because those likelihood ratios were constructed against the prior incumbent.
- Search grid: `q in {0.30,0.35,0.40}`, `alpha in {0.05,0.10,0.20}`, `cap in {3,4,6}`, both timeout modes and `rho in {0,0.6}`, 100 replicates per setting. A refinement used `q in {0.32,0.34,0.36,0.38,0.40}`, `alpha in {0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25,0.30}`, `cap in {4,6}`, 50 replicates per setting. A final paired check at `q=0.40` used 200 shared seeds per policy.

The exact reproducibility script for this parameterization is persisted beside this checkpoint as `SYNTHETIC_QD_NOISE_ABLATION_2026-08-26_0800.py`.

### Main phase-boundary result under fresh evidence

The quality-maximizing threshold systematically **relaxed** as the symmetric judge error approached randomness:

| q | best tested alpha | cap | timeout | mean archive quality | mean proposals | mean false-promotion fraction |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.32 | 0.15 | 6 | Standby | 0.806 | 252 | 0.150 |
| 0.34 | 0.20 | 6 | discard | 0.769 | 258 | 0.178 |
| 0.36 | 0.25 | 6 | Standby | 0.739 | 245 | 0.206 |
| 0.38 | 0.25 | 6 | Standby | 0.709 | 241 | 0.248 |
| 0.40 | 0.30 | 6 | Standby | 0.665 | 240 | 0.280 |

This does **not** mean a worse judge deserves more trust. It means that, under a fixed total evaluation budget, demanding very strong evidence from a nearly random judge can consume so much budget that proposal throughput collapses. If the same weak evaluator is the only option, the controller must trade false-admission control against search starvation; the safer system-level response is to switch evaluator, retain reversible candidates, or abstain from irreversible promotion rather than demand unattainable certainty from repeated same-judge calls.

### Paired `q=0.40` check — 200 shared seeds

Fresh independent verdicts (`rho=0`):

| policy | mean quality | mean proposals | false-promotion fraction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| one-shot permanent replacement | 0.565 | 1200 | 0.436 |
| two-verdict provisional | 0.625 | 812 | 0.381 |
| strict sequential `alpha=0.10, cap=6`, discard | 0.511 | 207 | 0.075 |
| strict sequential `alpha=0.10, cap=6`, Standby | 0.538 | 197 | 0.072 |
| relaxed sequential `alpha=0.30, cap=6`, discard | 0.655 | 249 | 0.270 |
| relaxed sequential `alpha=0.30, cap=6`, Standby | **0.658** | 239 | 0.283 |

Paired mean quality differences for relaxed Standby (`alpha=0.30, cap=6`), approximate 95% CI half-width from paired standard error:

- vs one-shot: `+0.0936 ± 0.0112`.
- vs two-verdict provisional: `+0.0336 ± 0.0110`.
- vs strict discard: `+0.1475 ± 0.0106`.
- vs strict Standby: `+0.1197 ± 0.0101`.
- vs relaxed discard: `+0.0030 ± 0.0119` — no reliable Standby advantage at this already-relaxed threshold.

The important decomposition is that **Standby only partially rescues an over-strict gate; threshold relaxation is the dominant recovery mechanism in this toy model**. Strict Standby raises quality from 0.511 to 0.538, but remains far below the relaxed gate. Once the gate is relaxed, Standby and discard are statistically indistinguishable here.

### Correlated reevaluation (`rho=0.6`) sharply limits sequential-evidence value

At `q=0.40`, 200 paired seeds:

| policy | mean quality | mean proposals | false-promotion fraction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| one-shot | 0.558 | 1200 | 0.437 |
| two-verdict provisional | 0.569 | 808 | 0.422 |
| strict sequential, discard | 0.551 | 207 | 0.394 |
| strict sequential, Standby | 0.558 | 201 | 0.393 |
| relaxed sequential, discard | 0.589 | 324 | 0.397 |
| relaxed sequential, Standby | **0.592** | 316 | 0.397 |

Relaxed Standby remains better than one-shot by `+0.0334 ±0.0122` and provisional by `+0.0223 ±0.0118`, but its false-promotion fraction stays near 0.40 because repeated evidence is largely redundant. Relaxed Standby versus relaxed discard is only `+0.0023 ±0.0115`.

Thus same-judge repetition can recover some selection pressure through threshold/cap tuning, but it cannot manufacture independent evidence. Under correlated error, the main unresolved lever is **evaluator diversity / correlation-aware accounting**, not more calls to the same judge.

## Updated controller hypothesis

The controller should treat evidence strength as an **adaptive resource-allocation problem**, not a fixed threshold:

1. broad cheap proposal;
2. reversible provisional/Standby state;
3. strong promotion evidence only while the evaluator can supply informative marginal evidence at acceptable cost;
4. if evidence accumulation stalls because the judge is weak/correlated, switch evaluator or abstain from irreversible promotion instead of consuming the search budget indefinitely;
5. never interpret repeated same-judge calls as independent merely because the call count increased.

The C52 phase boundary suggests that a static very-low `alpha` can be dominated by a looser gate when judge error becomes high. The correct operational reaction is not to lower standards silently; it is to **recognize an evaluator-regime change and route uncertain candidates to reversible storage or a genuinely different verifier**.

## Frontier (nonempty)

1. Add a two-evaluator model with controllable cross-evaluator error correlation. At timeout, compare same-judge Standby replay versus switching to evaluator B under the same total budget; locate the correlation threshold where switching stops adding effective evidence.
2. Repeat that evaluator-switch sweep under asymmetric `q_FA/q_FR`, especially high false-admission / low false-rejection, where irreversible archive corruption is most costly.
3. Sweep Standby revisit rate and reservoir depth separately from promotion alpha; C52 shows Standby is not automatically beneficial once the gate itself is well tuned.
4. Add a system-level loss that penalizes false authoritative promotion more than temporary Standby occupancy, so threshold selection reflects irreversibility rather than mean archive quality alone.
5. Continue public search for direct archive-level class-conditional decision-noise studies; do not substitute Gaussian fitness noise as if it were the same phenomenon.
