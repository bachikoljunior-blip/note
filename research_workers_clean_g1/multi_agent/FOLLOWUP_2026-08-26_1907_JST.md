## C81 — real repeated same-judge outputs do not improve endpoint accuracy by simple averaging; stable-wrong behavior dominates

Using the same GPT-4o Mini judge configuration over all 100 items, the first-K arithmetic mean gives:

| K | evaluator calls | MAE vs expert | RMSE | mean within-item range | all K identical | retry-adjusted cost for 100 items* |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 100 | 5.940 | 7.852 | 0.00 | 100% | ~$0.1008 |
| 2 | 200 | 5.995 | 7.871 | 0.41 | 91% | ~$0.2016 |
| 3 | 300 | 6.060 | 7.895 | 0.71 | 85% | ~$0.3024 |
| 4 | 400 | 6.070 | 7.875 | 1.11 | 77% | ~$0.4032 |
| 5 | 500 | 6.074 | 7.888 | 1.36 | 72% | ~$0.5040 |

`*` Cost uses the pinned repository's token/retry-derived adjusted GPT-4o Mini rate `$1.00801018/1K evaluations`, scaled by evaluation count. It is an approximate workload cost curve, not a reconstruction of every individual API invoice.

The five single-run MAEs are `5.94, 6.05, 6.19, 6.10, 6.09`, reproducing the repository aggregate (`6.074`) closely. Averaging more repeats therefore does **not** improve this benchmark's judge accuracy; the K=5 mean is slightly worse than K=1. Paired item bootstrap intervals for K2−K1 (`+0.055`, 95% bootstrap interval roughly `[-0.075,+0.200]`) and K5−K1 (`+0.135`, roughly `[-0.040,+0.318]`) include zero, so the narrow conclusion is no established accuracy gain from repetition, not that repetition is universally harmful.

The more important failure is stable wrongness. Across five repeats:
- 72/100 items return exactly the same score all five times;
- 46/72 of those perfectly stable items are nevertheless not exact matches to expert ground truth;
- 31/72 perfectly stable items still have absolute error greater than 5 points;
- one perfectly stable item is scored 90 on all five runs against expert score 70 (20-point error).

So `same answer five times` is not usable as a correctness certificate in this trace.

## C82 — repeat instability is a weak/selective error signal and largely disappears after conditioning on the expert score level

Across all five repeats, unstable items have mean absolute error `6.693` versus `5.833` for stable items, but the monotone association is weak: Spearman(range, final-mean absolute error) ≈ `0.137` (`p≈0.173`).

An earlier operational signal is stronger but still sparse: only 9/100 items differ between r1 and r2. Their five-run-mean error is `9.60` versus `5.73` for the 91 early-agreement items. Spearman(`|r1-r2|`, five-run-mean error) ≈ `0.237` (`p≈0.017`). However this benchmark's expert scores are heavily concentrated at 80 and 90, and the judge is systematically high on many 80-score items. After a simple HC3 regression controlling for expert-score level (analysis-only covariate), the five-run range coefficient is only about `+0.047 error points per range point` (`p≈0.640`); the r1/r2 absolute-difference coefficient is about `+0.216` (`p≈0.237`).

Within the 80-score stratum, five-run error is almost identical for stable vs unstable items (`10.47` vs `10.40`). Within the 90-score stratum it is somewhat higher for unstable items (`2.42` vs `1.67`) but sample sizes are small. Therefore most of the raw instability/error association is not a robust deployable routing signal in this dataset. Repeat disagreement can flag a small subset of difficult cases, but repeated agreement misses many systematically biased cases.

## C83 — disagreement-triggered extra repeats do not beat the cheap baseline on endpoint MAE

A simple adaptive policy was tested with the public fixed traces:
1. evaluate every item twice (r1,r2);
2. if the two scores disagree, buy r3..r5 and use the five-run median for that item;
3. otherwise stop after two evaluations.

Only 9 items escalate, for 227 total evaluations (~`$0.2288` at the repository's adjusted rate). MAE is `5.990`, versus `5.995` for uniform K=2 (200 calls) and `5.940` for K=1 (100 calls). The paired bootstrap interval for adaptive−K2 spans roughly `[-0.14,+0.135]`; adaptive−K1 spans roughly `[-0.10,+0.20]`. Thus this policy produces no established endpoint gain and is dominated in cost/accuracy by the single-call baseline on this dataset.

**Conservative external-validation conclusion:** repeated calls to the same judge mainly expose stochastic instability; they do not repair systematic bias here. Self-disagreement may be useful as a selective *escalation trigger*, but same-judge self-agreement is not evidence of correctness, and buying more identical-judge calls is not justified by endpoint MAE alone.

## Scope and limitations

- This is a reanalysis of one public domain-specific scalar-assessment benchmark and one judge configuration (GPT-4o Mini), not a QD archive or pairwise-preference task.
- The expert ground-truth distribution is highly concentrated (mostly 80/90); the regression's expert-score controls are diagnostic only and unavailable at deployment.
- Repeated runs are fixed public traces, so adaptive policies can only select among the recorded repeats; they cannot generate new counterfactual judge outputs.
- Cost scaling uses the repository's published token/retry-derived average adjusted rate. It does not reconstruct per-policy marginal invoice effects exactly.
- No claim is made that K=1 is universally optimal; only that same-judge repeat averaging/median has no demonstrated accuracy benefit in this trace.
- No cross-model escalation was analyzed in this invocation because note control changed after semantic start and the freeze rule required checkpoint-only persistence.
