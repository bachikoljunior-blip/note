## C68 — a strictly online local opportunity predictor recovers most oracle routing value without simulator-side opportunity resampling

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. It extends C61–C66. The fixed family remains 16 archive cells, 1,200 evaluator calls, `rho_A=rho_B=0.6`, three A calls before the escalation decision, up to four B calls, and the same proposal family. The exact dependence-aware likelihood model and prior-aware expected quality-loss decision remain unchanged in spirit: for each challenger the simulator supplies the proposal prior `P(better)` and conditional positive/negative quality magnitudes, while evaluator histories update that prior. One B call is valued by expected reduction in archive-quality decision loss.

The changed component is the **fresh-proposal opportunity estimate**. Instead of the predecessor's uncharged 64-sample simulator Monte Carlo at the current state, `route_learned` uses only past completed A-only proposal pipelines. The selected predictor is deliberately small and online: 10 incumbent-quality bins, cell×bin local means, zero-mean shrinkage, O(1) update/inference. Its target is realized signed archive change divided by the three A evaluator calls. No future outcome, B result, other role state, or public benchmark label is used to fit the predictor.

The comparison used 300 paired seeds per condition. `valueB` calls B whenever one-step B EVSI is positive. `oracle64` uses the predecessor-style state-conditioned 64-sample opportunity estimate. `route_learned` calls B only when B EVSI exceeds the online local estimate.

Against `valueB`, every route-learned quality difference had a 95% paired interval containing zero in this run. Against `oracle64`, five of six paired intervals contained zero; the asymmetric/cross=1.0 point is only marginally above zero and is not multiplicity-corrected. The robust result is resource allocation: `route_learned` removed about 6–7% of B calls in the asymmetric strong-B regime and 19–27% in the symmetric weak-B regime while preserving matched final quality at this sample size.

The learned router is **not yet as evaluator-efficient as the oracle opportunity estimate**. It used about 13–33% more B calls than oracle64 depending on condition. Thus the online predictor closes much of the quality gap but not the verification-efficiency gap.

## C69 — calibration is strongly state-dependent; good endpoint quality hides low-incumbent underprediction

The selected 10-bin learner is much better than the predecessor hierarchical 5-bin smoother because it stops bleeding positive low-incumbent value into mature high-incumbent cells. However, its realized-label calibration is still poor where opportunities are largest.

Across the paired runs, the lowest incumbent bin (`[0,0.1)`) underpredicted realized A-only value by roughly `0.066–0.072` archive-quality/call, and bins `[0.1,0.4)` also showed material negative bias. Above about incumbent `0.6`, realized A-only archive change was almost always zero in this synthetic process and the local predictor rapidly converged to zero. Most late-run decisions occur in these mature cells, which explains why endpoint archive quality can match oracle64 even while low-quality states remain poorly calibrated.

This is a warning against judging a router only by final aggregate reward. A controller can look Pareto-competitive because the state visitation distribution moves into an easy region while remaining badly calibrated in the rare states where exploration has high marginal value.

## C70 — randomized A-only probes expose a partial-feedback trade-off but do not solve it for free

A follow-up negative control selected a fraction `epsilon` of proposals **before their proposal outcome or A evidence** and forced those proposals to remain A-only. A separate learner was trained only on these randomized probes, avoiding the obvious selection mechanism from training on naturally non-escalated examples. This was tested at `cross_mix=0.5` with 300 paired seeds.

Asymmetric regime, relative to `epsilon=0` (which leaves the opportunity estimate at zero and behaves like positive-EVSI routing):

- `epsilon=.05`: quality `0.88199`, delta `-0.00300 ± 0.00357`, ~17.9 forced probes/run.
- `epsilon=.10`: quality `0.88036`, delta `-0.00462 ± 0.00401`, ~35.6 probes/run.
- `epsilon=.20`: quality `0.87704`, delta `-0.00795 ± 0.00409`, ~72.4 probes/run.

Symmetric weak regime:

- `epsilon=.05`: delta `-0.00034 ± 0.00165`.
- `epsilon=.10`: delta `-0.00081 ± 0.00189`.
- `epsilon=.20`: delta `-0.00332 ± 0.00224`.

Randomized probes are cleaner training data, but at this 1,200-call horizon they are sparse exactly in the low-incumbent bins that need calibration, while every forced probe can displace useful B verification. The current experiment therefore does **not** support a blanket rule such as “reserve 10% for unbiased opportunity probes.” It supports the narrower claim that opportunity learning itself is a partial-feedback/exploration problem whose data-collection cost must be inside the controller objective.

## C71 — controller-compute sensitivity is small at low exchange rates but cannot be ignored

The local predictor requires one O(1) inference per proposal and one O(1) update per completed A-only proposal. To make the previously-free controller computation explicit, a sensitivity check charged a synthetic evaluator-call-equivalent cost per predictor inference/update. This exchange rate is arbitrary and **not physically calibrated**; it is only a robustness test.

At asymmetric/cross=0.5, the free learner used ~629 predictor operations/run. Charging `0.01` evaluator-call equivalents per operation (~6.3 calls/run) changed final quality by `-0.00056 ± 0.00040`; charging `0.05` (~30.5 calls/run) changed it by `-0.00201 ± 0.00098`. In the symmetric weak case (~765 operations/run), the corresponding deltas were `-0.00013 ± 0.00019` and `-0.00086 ± 0.00061`.

The numerical exchange rate is synthetic, but the direction matters: once the value estimator is learned rather than free, its own inference/update cost belongs in the same opportunity accounting as evaluator calls.

## C72 — fresh public evidence supports online partial-feedback routing, but not this archive result directly

Three recent routing papers sharpen the external comparison:

- Wang et al., **“Learning to Route LLMs from Bandit Feedback: One Policy, Many Trade-offs”** (arXiv:2510.07429) explicitly frames LLM routing as contextual bandit learning from only the chosen model's outcome and reports gains over offline/full-information routing baselines. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07429
- Li, Gao & Lakshmanan, **“WISERouter: LLM Routing with Workload Budget Constraint”** (arXiv:2607.23765, 26 Jul 2026) formulates workload-budgeted routing as a constrained contextual multi-armed bandit and gives an online `O(sqrt(T))` regret result while empirically using less exploration data than comparison routers. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23765
- Sridhar et al., **“Correlation-Aware Contextual Bandits with Surrogate Rewards for LLM Routing”** (arXiv:2607.09015, 10 Jul 2026) separates true bandit feedback from noisy/misspecified surrogate estimates; its decoupled design is robust to bad surrogate predictions while exploiting them when informative. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09015

These are model-routing/bandit results, not QD archive-replacement experiments. They support only the narrower methodological direction: online routing must explicitly handle partial feedback, exploration cost, and misspecified/correlated value estimates.

## Controller implication

The current strongest hypothesis is now:

`route extra evaluator iff residual evaluator EVSI > online-estimated fresh-proposal value/call + estimator-compute cost`,

but the opportunity estimator must be treated as a learning system with its own coverage and selection-bias problem. The 10-bin local learner is promising because it recovers oracle-like endpoint quality with fewer B calls than value-only routing, yet its low-incumbent calibration is poor and randomized probes are not free.

A stronger controller should therefore learn **both value and uncertainty** over opportunity, and allocate exploration specifically where the opportunity model is decision-relevant rather than using a fixed probe fraction.

## Scope and limitations

- C68–C71 are role-local synthetic results, not measurements of real LLM judges or real evolutionary archives.
- Proposal priors, conditional quality magnitudes, evaluator error rates, and dependence are simulator-known; only the fresh-proposal opportunity estimate is learned online in C68.
- The selected predictor uses realized signed archive change from A-only pipelines; those labels are policy-dependent and zero-inflated.
- The randomized-probe test removes one obvious selection mechanism but has high variance and incurs opportunity cost; it is not an unbiased off-policy estimator for all future states.
- `cross_mix` is a generator parameter, not a calibrated real judge correlation coefficient.
- The controller-compute exchange rate is arbitrary; no claim is made about real wall-clock equivalence.
- Public contextual-bandit/model-routing evidence does not validate the synthetic archive numbers.

## Frontier (nonempty)

1. Replace fixed random probing with **uncertainty-targeted opportunity exploration**: probe only states whose opportunity-model uncertainty can change the B-routing decision, and compare against epsilon probing at equal total call budget.
2. Add confidence intervals to the local opportunity predictor and compare `EVSI_B > mean`, `EVSI_B > upper opportunity bound`, and value-of-information inspection of the opportunity estimate itself.
3. Use doubly robust / propensity-aware updates to combine randomized probes with naturally A-only pipelines without treating selected labels as i.i.d.
4. Test nonstationarity explicitly: change proposal quality distribution mid-run and compare cumulative/sliding-window learners against the stationary bin estimator.
5. Fit the same controller on a real repeated-judge dataset with human labels and per-call cost; measure whether residual evaluator value and opportunity value can be estimated without simulator-known priors.
