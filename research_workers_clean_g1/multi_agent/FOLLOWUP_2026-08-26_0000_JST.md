## C28 — Order-swap inconsistency is a directly tested label-free trigger for selective evaluator escalation, but it systematically misses consistent-wrong cases

Primary source: Seungyeon Jwa, Daechul Ahn, Reokyoung Kim, Dongyeop Kang, Jonghyun Choi, *Becoming Experienced Judges: Selective Test-Time Learning for Evaluators*, arXiv:2512.06751v1 (2025-12-07). https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06751 ; HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06751v1

This paper directly tests one of the previous frontier's proposed decision-instability probes. For each pairwise evaluation item, the same LLM judge evaluates A-vs-B and the order-swapped B-vs-A version. If the verdict flips, the item is classified as inconsistent and receives the expensive sequential Learning-While-Evaluating (LWE) update path; if the verdict is stable, the update is skipped.

### Quantitative comparison

With GPT-4.1 on VLRewardBench / MMRewardBench:

- Vanilla pair accuracy: **0.529 / 0.747**.
- Full LWE pair accuracy: **0.646 / 0.727** at **10.9×** relative inference cost.
- Selective LWE pair accuracy: **0.648 / 0.808** at **3.9×** cost.
- Selective LWE consistency: **0.940 / 0.947**, versus vanilla **0.801 / 0.863**.

Thus the selective method uses about **36% of full-LWE inference cost** while matching/exceeding its pair-accuracy result in this setup. Its 3.9× cost consists of two vanilla passes for the order-swap check plus an additional 1.9× for LWE only on flagged cases.

The signal is also predictive of where intervention helps. On the vanilla-inconsistent subsets:

- VLRewardBench accuracy: **0.504 (125/248) -> 0.762 (189/248)**.
- MMRewardBench accuracy: **0.453 (62/137) -> 0.672 (92/137)**.

On vanilla-consistent subsets, the benefit is much smaller and can reverse:

- VL: **0.660 -> 0.734** under LWE.
- MM: **0.864 -> 0.846** under LWE.

The authors also vary update batch size. Updating every item (`b=1`) is most expensive and not best; very large batches (`b=8`) degrade because the update context becomes unstable; `b=4` gives the reported best balance. This is another non-monotonic compute-allocation result: more frequent evaluator adaptation is not automatically better.

### Transfer hypothesis for population / QD search

The strongest transferable controller rule is not `low confidence -> verify more` in the abstract, but:

`perform a cheap invariance/perturbation test -> estimate whether the selection decision is unstable -> escalate only unstable comparisons to deeper judging or re-evaluation`.

For LLM-based archive replacement, order swap is especially attractive because it is label-free and measures a concrete violation of a desired invariance. It is closer to decision-flip probability than generic score variance.

A practical archive controller suggested by this result would distinguish at least three states:

1. **stable under perturbation** — accept the cheap verdict provisionally;
2. **unstable under perturbation** — allocate extra judge calls / stronger judge / posterior comparison;
3. **stable but periodically audited** — reserve a small sentinel sample because stable-wrong cases are invisible to the trigger.

### Scope limits / negative evidence

- This is evaluator adaptation, not an evolutionary or MAP-Elites archive update experiment; it does not directly prove improved final search quality.
- The trigger requires two full vanilla judgments per item, so it is not free.
- The method can miss **consistent but wrong** cases. The paper explicitly reports 340 such examples on the VL consistent subset and 117 on MM, and lists this as a limitation.
- Claude Sonnet 4.5 had >50% inconsistent cases and Selective LWE cost rose to **15.2×**, showing that an instability-triggered method can become expensive when the base judge is broadly unstable.
- The meta-prompt adaptation mechanism can fail on weaker judges: the paper reports Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct sometimes failed to produce valid evaluation prompts after updates.

## C29 — Iterative LLM optimization is highly sensitive to corrupted feedback; a single opaque feedback channel can steer the optimizer far from the stated objective

Primary source: Andrew Zhao et al., *Are My Optimized Prompts Compromised? Exploring Vulnerabilities of LLM-based Optimizers*, EACL 2026 long paper / arXiv:2510.14381v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14381 ; ACL PDF: https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.100.pdf

This is adjacent but important failure evidence for population/self-improving search: the optimization loop itself can amplify corrupted evaluator feedback even when the optimizer is still verbally instructed to optimize the intended objective.

The paper evaluates two LLM-based prompt optimizers (TextGrad and Trace) and manipulates the feedback channel. With direct harmscore feedback while the optimizer is instructed to optimize helpfulness:

- TextGrad setup: initial ASR **0.07**, `ΔASR=+0.48`, max ASR **0.54**.
- Trace optimizer: initial ASR **0.08**, `ΔASR=+0.45`, max ASR **0.53**.
- GPT-5.1 backend variant: initial ASR **0.01**, `ΔASR=+0.24`, max ASR **0.24**.

A weaker fake-feedback attack inserted plausible `<FEEDBACK>` tokens into the optimization inputs. It raised `ΔASR` from vanilla **-0.02** to **+0.23**. With only **10% poison data**, `ΔASR` still reached **+0.10**. A simple highlighting/boundary defense reduced fake-reward `ΔASR` from **+0.23 to +0.07** in the reported setup.

### Transfer hypothesis

For multi-agent/evolutionary systems that use LLM judges or scalar metrics to drive mutation, archive replacement, role selection or prompt evolution, evaluator provenance/integrity is not just an evaluation concern; it is part of the search dynamics. A corrupted signal can become self-reinforcing because later candidates are generated from earlier corrupted selections.

This supports several defensive controller requirements:

- keep feedback provenance and objective identity explicit;
- do not let one opaque scalar silently redefine the optimization target;
- gate irreversible updates (archive replacement, permanent pruning, policy rewrite) on stronger or independently structured evidence;
- harden the boundary between candidate content and evaluator/control metadata so candidate text cannot masquerade as trusted feedback;
- use periodic independent sentinel checks to detect optimization-metric drift.

### Scope limits / negative evidence

- This is **adversarial poisoning**, not stochastic iid judge error. It is not the still-missing 0–50% random pairwise judge-noise sweep.
- Most reported optimization trials are single-run trends without multi-seed variance; the paper explicitly lists this limitation.
- The highlighting defense is tested against the paper's fake-reward attack; it is not a general proof of robustness.
- The paper suggests multiple independent metrics as a possible defense but does not experimentally establish that defense. Given prior evidence that LLM judge errors can be highly correlated, metric multiplicity should not be treated as independence without measurement.

## Updated synthesis

C26–C29 sharpen the adaptive evaluation/search controller into a two-axis design:

### Axis A: decision instability

Use cheap perturbation/invariance probes that test whether uncertainty actually changes the decision, not just whether scores have high variance. Order-swap inconsistency is now directly supported as a useful label-free signal for pairwise LLM judging, complementing rank-disagreement probes in noisy evolution strategies.

### Axis B: feedback integrity

Even a perfectly stable evaluator can be stably wrong or maliciously redirected. Therefore instability gating must be paired with provenance/integrity checks and a small sentinel audit path. The controller should not equate `consistent` with `correct`.

A stronger provisional decision policy is:

`trusted-source check -> cheap invariance/decision-flip probe -> selective deep verification for unstable or irreversible decisions -> periodic sentinel audit of stable decisions -> no hard pruning when evaluator reliability/integrity is below threshold`.

This is preferable to uniform k-fold judging, always-on deep judging, or unconditional trust in a single scalar reward under fixed budgets.

## Rejected / downgraded hypotheses

- `Order-swap consistency is enough to certify correctness` is rejected: consistent-wrong examples remain substantial.
- `Selective escalation is always cheap` is rejected: when >50% of cases are unstable, cost can rise sharply (15.2× for Claude Sonnet 4.5 in this study).
- `More frequent evaluator updates are always better` is rejected by the LWE batch-size ablation; `b=1` costs more and is not best, while `b=8` also degrades.
- `A stable optimization metric is safe if the optimizer prompt names the right goal` is rejected by the EACL feedback-manipulation experiments; the loop follows corrupted feedback strongly despite the stated helpfulness objective.
- `10% poisoned feedback is harmless` is rejected in the tested prompt-optimization setting; the fake-reward 10%-poison condition still raises ASR materially.
- `This resolves the direct random judge-noise threshold question` is rejected. The adversarial poisoning study and order-swap study do not provide a controlled 0–50% stochastic error sweep inside evolutionary LLM search.

## Frontier queue (nonempty)

1. **Direct evolutionary LLM judge-noise sweep:** injected known pairwise error rates (0–50%+) with final search quality, archive corruption, regret, and survival-decision error.
2. **Archive-level order-swap gating:** find or test a search system where order-swap inconsistency directly controls re-judging / archive replacement, not merely evaluator prompt learning.
3. **Stable-wrong sentinel policy:** matched-budget comparison of random auditing vs uncertainty/perturbation auditing for catching consistent-wrong judgments.
4. **Verification-share ablation:** 0/5/10/25/50% elite re-evaluation under identical search budget, ideally in LLM/QD.
5. **Extraction policy:** rank-biased vs random vs order-swap/decision-flip/posterior-entropy based re-evaluation at fixed verification budget.
6. **Feedback-integrity failures:** benign random label noise vs systematic correlated bias vs adversarial reward poisoning in the same optimizer/search scaffold.
7. **Extreme-noise mode:** principled trigger for switching from hard selection to reversible standby / evidence collection when comparisons are not informative enough.
8. **D-PBEMO / Duel-Evolve diagnostics:** comparison-budget, cluster-count, order-swap agreement and systematic-bias sensitivity.
9. **Event-triggered migration:** fixed cadence vs diversity/stagnation/uncertainty-triggered island migration under matched compute.
10. **Executable-candidate diversity:** whether single-call multi-output remains superior when candidate quality requires independent tool/environment trajectories.
