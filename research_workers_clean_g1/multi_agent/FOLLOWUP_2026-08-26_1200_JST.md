## C61 — opportunity cost must be measured per completed proposal pipeline, not per raw proposal draw

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. It extends the exact implemented-generator likelihood from C58/C59. The fixed family remains 16 archive cells, 1,200 evaluator calls, `rho_A=rho_B=0.6`, A active cap 3, B cap 4, reversible standby reservoir cap 4, revisit rate 0.5. Two regimes were retained: symmetric weak evaluators A=B=`q_FA=q_FR=0.40`, and asymmetric A=`q_FA 0.40 / q_FR 0.20`, B=`0.20 / 0.20`, with `cross_mix=0.5`.

A first attempt compared one B call's expected decision-value directly with the raw expected positive quality of one newly drawn proposal. That systematically overprices the displaced proposal because a raw proposal itself needs multiple evaluator calls before it can safely affect the archive. Under the synthetic proposal process, typical raw positive proposal gain around incumbent quality 0.6 is about 0.05–0.06, whereas one-step B expected value is often 0.02–0.04. Treating the full raw proposal gain as the opportunity cost therefore suppresses useful verification even though the proposal pipeline costs roughly three calls.

The corrected controller compares B's **expected reduction in archive-quality decision loss per call** against expected positive proposal gain divided by a proposal-pipeline call divisor. This is still an approximation, but the units now match: expected archive-quality value per evaluator call.

## C62 — prior-aware quality-loss routing separates three different effects

For each challenger, the controller freezes at proposal creation:

- the proposal-process prior `P(challenger > incumbent)` for that cell/incumbent;
- expected positive magnitude `E[challenger-incumbent | better]`;
- expected negative magnitude `E[incumbent-challenger | worse]`.

The exact lock/cross-dependence history likelihood updates that prior. Immediate promote loss is `(1-p)*E[incumbent-challenger|worse]`; immediate reject loss is `p*E[challenger-incumbent|better]`. A hypothetical B call is valued by the expected reduction in the minimum of those two losses. This differs materially from a fixed posterior threshold: it accounts for candidate-specific upside/downside magnitude as well as class probability.

Three matched utility controllers isolate routing from the improved decision model:

1. `noB`: after three A calls, make the lower expected-quality-loss promote/reject decision; never call B.
2. `valueB`: call B whenever its exact one-step expected decision value is positive, ignoring proposal opportunity cost.
3. `route`: call B only when that expected value exceeds the estimated displaced proposal-pipeline value per call; otherwise finalize immediately with the lower-risk action.

`allB` was also checked as a failure control: always spend B calls up to the cap before finalizing.

## Asymmetric regime — stronger B has real residual value

At `cross_mix=0.5`, 200 replicates with proposal-pipeline divisor 4 gave:

| controller | final mean archive quality | false-promotion fraction | false-rejection fraction | proposals | B calls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| noB | 0.7404 | 0.2477 | 0.3485 | 404.2 | 0.0 |
| allB | 0.7086 | 0.1080 | 0.4511 | 323.9 | 240.7 |
| valueB | 0.8244 | 0.1593 | 0.2141 | 372.9 | 94.7 |
| route | **0.8276** | 0.2066 | 0.2353 | 387.9 | 49.3 |

A separate 100-seed paired check produced route quality `0.8221`; route exceeded noB by `+0.0813 ± 0.0093` (95% half-width). Against valueB, route was `+0.0066 ± 0.0118`, so the quality difference between selective cost-aware routing and routing every positive-EVSI case was not resolved at this sample size. The strong resolved result is that **some B use is valuable in the asymmetric regime, while always escalating is harmful under the same fixed evaluator-call budget**.

The route controller used roughly half as many B calls as valueB in the 200-replicate summary (49.3 vs 94.7) while preserving essentially the same final quality. This is a direct synthetic efficiency result; it is not evidence that divisor 4 is universally optimal.

## Symmetric weak-evaluator regime — extra B mostly has no value

At the same `cross_mix=0.5` and 200 replicates:

| controller | final mean archive quality | false-promotion fraction | false-rejection fraction | proposals | B calls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| noB | 0.6853 | 0.3042 | 0.3694 | 404.3 | 0.0 |
| allB | 0.6013 | 0.2538 | 0.4777 | 321.9 | 246.6 |
| valueB | **0.6993** | 0.3068 | 0.3510 | 396.5 | 23.3 |
| route | 0.6853 | 0.3042 | 0.3694 | 404.3 | **0.0** |

The paired 100-seed check gave route=noB exactly and valueB exceeded route by `0.0132 ± 0.0057`. Thus the current proposal-pipeline opportunity estimate is somewhat too conservative to capture the small positive-value B cases in this weak symmetric regime. However the same experiment also shows why unconditional escalation is bad: `allB` spends ~247 B calls, reduces proposal throughput by ~82 proposals, and ends far below noB/valueB.

This creates a useful controller target: estimate proposal opportunity cost accurately enough to retain the sparse B calls that matter without paying for the large majority that do not.

## C63 — baseline comparison shows the gain is not only routing

Using the predecessor fixed posterior-threshold family at posterior threshold 0.8 on the same regimes and 150 replicates:

Asymmetric: `same 0.6512`, `naive 0.6521`, `lock 0.7682`, `joint 0.7777` mean archive quality.

Symmetric: `same 0.5741`, `naive 0.5971`, `lock 0.4902`, `joint 0.4921`.

The utility controller is better than these fixed-threshold baselines in both regimes, but that difference cannot be attributed purely to routing. It also uses the proposal-process prior and candidate-specific quality-loss magnitudes. The clean ablation is therefore `noB` vs `valueB` vs `route`, not utility-route vs the older threshold controllers.

## C64 — fresh public evidence: failure prediction is easier than protocol-value prediction

Yang et al., **“LLMs Can Predict Failure Risk, But Struggle to Predict Which Collaboration Protocol Pays Off: Cost-Aware Protocol Routing Across Reasoning Tasks”** (arXiv:2608.14927, 14 Aug 2026) runs each problem under Baseline, self-correction, planner-executor-reviewer, and Broadcast while holding the solver fixed. A post-answer probe predicts Baseline failure with AUROC `0.8847`, but protocol-specific value is much harder: reported AUPRC is `0.1674` for PER-specific value and `0.1041` for Broadcast-specific value. A simple pre-answer confidence gate reaches 78.0% solve at 45K tokens versus 73.8% at 71.3K for a frozen router, while a retrospective fixed-order oracle reaches 92.4%. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14927

This is not an archive-search result, but it sharply supports the current synthetic finding: **knowing that the base process is uncertain is easier than knowing which expensive collaboration/verification action is worth its opportunity cost**. Cost-aware escalation needs action-specific residual value, not just a generic uncertainty score.

Zhu, Xie & Rao, **“Stopping and Routing LLM Judge Panels”** (arXiv:2608.19802, 20 Aug 2026) remains consistent with this direction: estimate each judge's conditional held-out gain, drop copies, route specialists only where their gain clears a threshold, and stop when no remaining call is worth its cost. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19802

## Controller implication

The strongest current controller hypothesis is now:

`proposal prior + exact dependence-aware evidence + candidate-specific error magnitude + residual evaluator value - proposal-pipeline opportunity cost`.

This is more specific than “add another judge when uncertain.” In the synthetic asymmetric regime, stronger B is useful but always calling it is worse than selective use; in the symmetric weak regime, B's residual value is sparse enough that a coarse opportunity-cost estimate suppresses almost all calls. Therefore the next useful quantity is not a global B accuracy score but a **calibrated estimate of expected archive-quality gain per routed evaluator call versus expected gain per displaced proposal-pipeline call**.

## Scope and limitations

- C61–C63 are role-local synthetic results, not measurements of real LLM judge error rates.
- Proposal priors and conditional quality magnitudes are known from the simulator; a real controller would have to estimate them without leakage.
- Dividing raw proposal gain by a fixed pipeline divisor is only a first-order opportunity-cost approximation. Actual proposal cost is state-dependent because easy candidates can terminate early and difficult candidates consume more calls.
- `allB` is intentionally inefficient and is a failure control, not a realistic optimized baseline.
- The apparent route≈valueB quality equivalence in the asymmetric 100-seed paired comparison has a confidence interval crossing zero; only the large reduction in B calls is clearly observed.
- Public routing papers evaluate reasoning/judge workflows, not QD archive replacement. Transfer remains a hypothesis.

## Frontier (nonempty)
