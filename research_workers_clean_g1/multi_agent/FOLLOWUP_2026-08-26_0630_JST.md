## C48 — conditional e-values can survive adaptive optimization when evaluation is fresh and the tested model is frozen first

Primary source:
Hyunjoo Kim, Sicheng Wu, Agastya Venkatraman, Guang Lin, Sehwan Kim, **“Deciding When to Switch: E-Processes for Adaptive Minimax Training for Generative Adversarial Nets,”** arXiv:2608.10096v1, submitted 2026-08-10.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10096
https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10096v1

### Formal mechanism
The paper is a direct optimization example of the missing validity interface identified in C45. For a filtration `F_t`, each sequential factor is chosen from past information and must satisfy
`E[E_t | F_{t-1}] <= 1`; the product is then a nonnegative supermartingale/e-process, permitting anytime-valid stopping.

In the adaptive GAN procedure, the current discriminator/generator state is fixed before its fresh evaluation pair or minibatch is observed. During discriminator phases, fresh real observations and fresh latent draws are used to build conditional e-values after the current networks are fixed. The paper explicitly states that population-level validity requires evaluation observations not used to train the networks; when only the observed training sample is available, it conditions on that training sample and proves a narrower empirical/operational null, again requiring the current discriminator to be fixed before the evaluation pair is observed.

This is important because the model parameters themselves are adaptive consequences of previous training history. The validity is recovered by **conditioning on the past, freezing the tested object/rule, and using fresh evaluation randomness**.

### Quantitative evidence
On CIFAR-10, each compared method receives the same budget of 100,000 generator updates and is evaluated over five seeds. The adaptive method dynamically chooses 1–10 updates for each discriminator/generator phase; total discriminator updates may therefore differ from fixed-ratio baselines.

Reported seed-wise best-checkpoint results:
- Softplus: fixed 5:1 gives IS `8.072±0.043`, FID `17.540±0.553`; adaptive gives IS `8.192±0.080`, FID `15.877±0.354`.
- Hinge: fixed 5:1 gives IS `8.281±0.033`, FID `13.542±0.316`; adaptive gives IS `8.330±0.079`, FID `13.208±0.357`.
- Fixed 1:1 and TTUR are substantially worse in the reported table.

The paper also shows visually on a 16-Gaussian mixture that fixed schedules are objective-sensitive while the e-process schedule adapts without a prespecified ratio.

### Evaluation caveats
The CIFAR comparison is **not strict matched-total-compute evidence**: methods share the generator-update horizon, but discriminator-update counts can differ. Also, the paper reports each seed's maximum IS and minimum FID over evaluation checkpoints, and those two metrics may come from different checkpoints. The adaptive hyperparameters are selected using the same five-seed evaluation setup. Therefore the result supports viability of conditional e-process scheduling, not a universal claim of compute efficiency or unbiased final-checkpoint superiority.

### QD/archive transfer hypothesis
A source-grounded candidate mapping is now sharper:
1. let an adaptive population proposer use arbitrary past role-local history to generate challenger `x_t`;
2. freeze challenger, incumbent, cell/constraint definition, and evidence-scoring rule before evaluation;
3. draw fresh evaluation randomness not used to generate/train/select the challenger;
4. construct evidence satisfying `E[E_t | F_{t-1}] <= 1` for a predeclared candidate-improvement null;
5. use an e-process for anytime-valid challenger evaluation;
6. separately decide whether statistically supported evidence is sufficient for archive mutation.

C48 is **not a proof for QD**. Its nulls concern GAN distribution discrepancy and update switching, not archive replacement. But it demonstrates in an adaptive optimization loop that past-dependent model generation and anytime-valid evidence are compatible when the tested object is frozen before fresh evaluation randomness.

## C49 — small reversible archive depth and delayed questioning beat uniform over-verification in Uncertain QD

Primary source:
Manon Flageat, Antoine Cully, **“Uncertain Quality-Diversity: Evaluation methodology and new methods for Quality-Diversity in Uncertain Domains,”** arXiv:2302.00463.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463
https://arxiv.org/html/2302.00463

### Matched-budget design
The paper introduces a per-generation sampling budget so reevaluations count against exploration. Archive-Sampling and Parallel-Adaptive-Sampling must pay for archive reevaluation from the same budget. The benchmark uses four tasks and 10 replications per algorithm/task/sampling-size combination, totaling 1120 runs.

### Mechanisms and failure modes
- Uniform MAP-Elites-Sampling spends a fixed `N` evaluations on every offspring. Under sampling-size 1024, `N=8` leaves only 128 offspring/generation. The paper finds this can block exploration by rejecting brittle early stepping stones.
- Archive-Sampling instead repeatedly re-evaluates only archive contents and keeps a depth-`D` reservoir per cell, giving unlucky candidates a second chance and allowing lucky incumbents to be removed later.
- Parallel-Adaptive-Sampling additionally increases offspring sampling as archive certainty grows, approximately matching newcomer/incumbent certainty.

### Quantitative / ablation evidence
- MAP-Elites-Sampling is dominated by vanilla MAP-Elites at sampling-size 256 (`p<5×10^-2`) and is equivalent in Hexapod and Ant in the stated comparisons; the paper attributes this to reduced exploration.
- Archive-Sampling and Parallel-Adaptive-Sampling outperform all other tested methods on the challenging Ant task in Corrected QD-Score (`p<5×10^-4`), though neither dominates the other because Parallel-Adaptive-Sampling pays higher time cost.
- Both methods produce more reproducible solutions than vanilla MAP-Elites / random MAP-Elites on Walker and Ant (`p<10^-3`).
- Reservoir-depth ablation tests `D∈{1,2,4}`; `D=2` is best on average across environments for both Archive-Sampling and Parallel-Adaptive-Sampling. Increasing `D=2→4` adds `2*N_cells` archive evaluations every generation and can materially alter or even prevent execution under a fixed sample budget.

### Scope guard
The benchmark covers stochastic fitness/descriptors in robotics/synthetic QD, not LLM-judge class-conditional errors. `D=2` is an empirical choice for these tasks, not a universal optimal reservoir size. Deep-Grid is stronger on simpler uncertainty regimes, while archive sampling becomes especially competitive in the difficult closed-loop Ant task. The correct principle is conditional allocation, not “always use depth 2.”

### Population-controller implication
This supplies the matched-budget counterpart to C47:
- do **not** verify every novel candidate heavily before it can become a stepping stone;
- provisionally retain a small backup/Standby reservoir;
- concentrate repeated evaluation on candidates that survive long enough to matter and on incumbent/challenger disputes;
- let the required evaluation fidelity grow with decision irreversibility and archive age;
- explicitly charge reevaluation against exploration budget.

In a multi-agent population, this suggests keeping cheap, diverse early lineages alive while making promotion to authoritative/irreversible archive status progressively harder.

## Combined controller hypothesis after C45–C49

A robust population/QD controller should separate four budgets:
1. **proposal budget** for independent/niche candidate generation;
2. **provisional retention budget** for small reversible reservoirs / Standby candidates;
3. **verification budget** focused on contested promotion/eviction decisions and using fresh evaluation randomness where statistical validity is claimed;
4. **global error budget** only if per-candidate evidence validity under adaptive generation is established.

The evidence now argues against both extremes:
- one-shot elitism creates lucky permanent winners;
- uniform heavy verification kills exploration and stepping stones.

The promising middle is **cheap broad proposal → reversible provisional archive → selective fresh-data verification → irreversible promotion only with stronger evidence**.
