## C58 — exact implemented-generator likelihood exposes the real bottleneck

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. Reproducibility script: `research_workers_clean_g1/multi_agent/SYNTHETIC_JOINT_LIKELIHOOD_2026-08-26_1100.py`.

The matched family remains 16 archive cells, 1,200 evaluator calls, `rho_A=rho_B=0.6`, A active cap 3, reversible standby reservoir cap 4, revisit rate 0.5, and base promotion threshold `alpha=0.20`. Each reported table cell uses 100 paired replicates.

### Generator-semantics correction

Reading the predecessor role-local script exposed an important implementation detail that changes the interpretation of `cross_mix`: A's first verdict and a latent `b_first` are coupled, but the first *observed* B verdict equals that latent only when B is locked. If B is unlocked, every observed B call is fresh. Therefore the implemented first-observed-A/B coupling is the mixture

`P(A,B_obs|Y) = rho_B * P_pair_cross_mix(A,b_latent|Y) + (1-rho_B) * P(A|Y)P(B_obs|Y)`.

Equivalently, for this mixture family the cross component is scaled by `rho_B * cross_mix`, not `cross_mix` alone. The predecessor C56 `discount` controller and information proxy treated every first B call as if the full `cross_mix` applied. The **archive outcomes from C56 remain outcomes of the actual code**, but the earlier interpretation of first-B redundancy was too strong, especially when `rho_B<1` and `cross_mix` is high. This correction is synthetic-model-specific.

### Controllers compared

- `same`: repeat A after timeout.
- `naive`: switch to B and add marginal log likelihood ratios.
- `discount`: predecessor scalar B discount `(1-cross_mix)`.
- `separate`: require B confirmation with a cap.
- `pair`: correct only the first observed A/B joint likelihood, but leave later repeat evidence marginal/naive.
- `lock`: exact history likelihood for within-evaluator A/B lock dependence while intentionally assuming cross-evaluator independence.
- `joint`: exact history likelihood under the implemented generator, including both within-evaluator lock dependence and cross-evaluator first-latent coupling.

The key decomposition is `pair` versus `lock` versus `joint`: it isolates first-pair dependence from repeated-verdict correlation.

## C60 — fresh public evidence favors conditional judge allocation over nominal diversity

A newly published public paper directly matches this controller direction: Zhu, Xie & Rao, **“Stopping and Routing LLM Judge Panels”** (arXiv:2608.19802, 20 Aug 2026). It estimates each judge's value **conditional on the current panel and deployable slice**, labels candidates as copies/complements/specialists, drops conditional copies, routes specialists only where their held-out cost-adjusted gain clears a threshold, and stops when no remaining call is worth adding. The construction explicitly requires deployment-time route keys to be available before the routed call. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19802

Selected held-out results from its main table: hard GSM8K rationale accuracy is `0.6843` at average cost `2.90` for the role-routed stop policy versus `0.6670` for flat-all; MBPP public-overfit is `0.9900` at cost `1.52` versus flat-all `0.9617`. Arena100K and SummEval stop at one judge because added signals do not clear the conditional-value threshold. These are evaluation-panel results, not archive-search results, but they independently support **conditional residual value per call and explicit stopping** rather than unconditional panel expansion.

A separate ICML 2026 paper, Zhang et al., **“Reasoning Is Not Free: Robust Adaptive Cost-Efficient Routing for LLM-as-a-Judge”** (arXiv:2605.10805), routes between reasoning and non-reasoning judges under a fixed budget and distribution-shift uncertainty, again supporting selective expensive verification rather than universal escalation. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10805

## Controller implication

The current evidence now separates at least five quantities that should not be collapsed into one `judge quality` scalar:

1. **Marginal class-conditional reliability:** `q_FA` and `q_FR` separately.
2. **Within-evaluator temporal dependence:** repeated answers from the same judge may be locked/correlated and must not be counted as fresh evidence.
3. **Cross-evaluator residual dependence:** B can be redundant with A even if B is individually accurate.
4. **Promotion cost / reversibility:** safer evidence can still hurt the system if it consumes proposal budget or freezes the archive too aggressively.
5. **Evidence-model-specific thresholding:** a promotion threshold must be calibrated to the likelihood model and downstream opportunity cost.

The strongest actionable synthetic result is that **within-evaluator correlation correction can matter more than adding a fancier cross-judge interaction term**. The strongest external result is that judge calls should be allocated by conditional held-out gain and stopped when the marginal call is no longer worth its cost.

## Scope and limitations

- C58/C59 are synthetic and do not estimate real LLM judge error rates or universal thresholds.
- The lock process is a deliberately simple mixture; real judge dependence can be richer, item-dependent, and nonstationary.
- `lock` intentionally misspecifies cross-evaluator dependence when `cross_mix>0`; its strong quality can reflect a favorable bias-throughput tradeoff, not superior calibration.
- `joint` is exact for the implemented synthetic generator, but exact local posterior evidence does not imply globally optimal search under a fixed evaluation budget.
- The first-pair `cross_mix` correction applies to this implementation because B's latent first verdict is observed only when locked. It is not a general formula for judge correlation.
- Public routing papers concern evaluation panels, not evolutionary/QD archive replacement; transfer to archive search remains a hypothesis until directly tested.

## Frontier (nonempty)

1. Build a **cost-aware exact-likelihood router**: after A timeout, estimate B's conditional expected reduction in decision loss per call under the current posterior and skip/abstain when it is below the displaced proposal value.
2. Replace fixed `alpha` with an archive-level utility threshold that trades false promotion, false rejection, reversibility, and lost proposal throughput.
3. Correct the synthetic information accounting to the actual implemented first-observed coupling (`rho_B * cross_mix`) and re-evaluate whether any earlier scalar-discount conclusion survives.
4. Add class-conditional and item/slice-dependent routing so a strong B is called only where its residual gain after A is positive, analogous to copy/complement/specialist allocation.
5. Seek real judge-output datasets with repeat calls and human labels that permit separate estimation of within-judge repeat correlation, cross-judge conditional dependence, `q_FA/q_FR`, and cost-adjusted residual value.
