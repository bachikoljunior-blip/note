## C24 — Under a fixed evaluation budget, selective elite re-evaluation can beat both uniform over-sampling and full-archive re-evaluation

Primary source: Flageat et al., *Extract-QD Framework: A Generic Approach for Quality-Diversity in Noisy, Stochastic or Uncertain Domains*, GECCO 2025 / arXiv:2502.06585. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06585

This is direct Quality-Diversity evidence about how to allocate a limited evaluation/verifier budget when candidate fitness and descriptors are noisy. It is not an LLM-judge experiment, so its numerical settings must not be promoted as universal agent-search constants.

### Matched-budget setup

The paper uses a common per-generation evaluation budget (`sampling-size`) rather than comparing methods by number of offspring. In the main UQD comparison, every method receives 1024 evaluations per generation across 10 seeds. Thus vanilla MAP-Elites can evaluate 1024 new offspring once, whereas ME-Sampling evaluates only 32 offspring 32 times each. Final archives are corrected for evaluation by using ground truth where available or the median of 512 re-evaluations when it is not.

This setup isolates the central exploration-versus-verification trade-off: every extra re-evaluation consumes budget that could otherwise generate a new candidate.

### Extract-MAP-Elites (EME) mechanism

EME reserves a fixed fraction of the evaluation budget for re-evaluating existing elites — 25% in the reported implementation — and leaves 75% for new offspring. At 128 evaluations per generation this means 96 new offspring plus 32 elite re-evaluations.

Re-evaluated elites are extracted from the archive and then reinserted from their new observations, so uncertainty can move a previously lucky/misplaced elite to a different cell or remove it. Each candidate accumulates its evaluation history rather than discarding previous measurements. EME also maintains archive depth `d=8`; only the top layer is returned, while lower layers act as a memory buffer. Candidates are selected for re-evaluation with probability exponentially proportional to their rank within a cell, concentrating verification on promising archive occupants instead of sampling the whole archive uniformly.

### Quantitative comparison

Under the same 1024 evaluations/generation:

- Vanilla MAP-Elites is among the worst methods because it does not account for uncertainty.
- ME-Sampling can perform well under descriptor noise but loses exploration capacity on harder control tasks such as Ant because many evaluations are spent repeatedly sampling few offspring.
- Archive-Sampling is **undefined** on Hexapod and Ant under this budget because re-evaluating the entire archive consumes too many evaluations.
- Adapt-ME performs well on the low-dimensional Arm task but struggles on higher-dimensional tasks; the paper attributes this in part to a large fraction of sampling budget being consumed by re-evaluation, severely hampering exploration.
- EME matches the best-performing method across the tested tasks and outperforms all compared approaches on Ant (`p < 10^-3`). On Arm Descriptor Noise and Walker, final EME elites receive significantly more samples than Archive-Sampling elites (`p < 10^-3`), despite not re-evaluating the whole archive; the authors interpret this as better sample allocation.

The same principle transfers to QD-RL in their EPGA experiment. ME and PGA use all 128 evaluations/generation for 128 new solutions. EPGA instead uses 96 new solutions plus 32 elite re-evaluations, keeping total evaluation cost identical. Across Walker, HalfCheetah and Ant, EPGA outperforms PGA on final Corrected QD-Score (`p < 5×10^-3`) with no additional evaluation cost.

### Mechanism hypothesis for population / multi-agent search

The strongest transferable principle is not `use exactly 25% verification`. It is:

`fixed total budget -> keep exploration alive -> selectively spend verification on archive/selection decisions whose uncertainty can change survival or placement`.

This is stronger than either extreme:

1. `verify every new candidate many times`, which can starve exploration; or
2. `re-verify the whole archive`, which can become infeasible as the archive/population grows.

A population controller should therefore expose **verification-budget share** and **which candidates receive verification** as first-class adaptive variables. Candidate promotion, archive replacement, permanent pruning, and other hard-to-reverse decisions are natural high-value targets for deeper evaluation.

Scope limits:

- The uncertainty here comes from stochastic/noisy fitness and descriptor evaluation, not LLM judge hallucination, correlated evaluator bias, or semantic preference labels.
- The reported 25% allocation is a design choice in EME, not a demonstrated globally optimal fraction.
- The work focuses experimentally on Performance Estimation and explicitly leaves detailed Reproducibility Maximisation and Fitness–Reproducibility Trade-off studies for future work.
- These results do not establish a pairwise judge-noise collapse threshold.

## C25 — Stochastic dueling-bandit preference learning offers an adjacent evolutionary mechanism for preserving uncertainty instead of collapsing feedback to a point score

Primary source: Huang, Wang, Li, *Direct Preference-Based Evolutionary Multi-Objective Optimization with Dueling Bandits*, NeurIPS 2024. https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/dd0ef9308bd10c964bd14c0000438460-Abstract-Conference.html

D-PBEMO is adjacent direct evolutionary evidence. It avoids a learned scalar reward model and instead uses stochastic pairwise preference feedback inside multi-objective evolutionary optimisation. The consultation population is clustered into `K` subsets and the preference learner uses a stochastic dueling-bandit formulation; the paper reports a regret bound `O(K^2 log T)`, rather than treating all `N` population members as independent bandit arms. Preferences remain probabilistic and are transformed into a form used by the evolutionary algorithm. Experiments cover 48 benchmark problems including synthetic tasks, RNA inverse design and protein structure prediction.

Transfer hypothesis: when evaluation is intrinsically preference-like and noisy, preserve a distribution/posterior over preference or candidate utility and allocate comparisons adaptively rather than converting each judgment immediately into a deterministic scalar ranking. Clustering can reduce comparison complexity when the population is large.

Scope: this is stochastic **human** feedback in preference-based multi-objective optimisation, not an injected 0–50% LLM-judge error sweep. The regret result should not be translated directly into final-search-quality guarantees under systematic model bias.

## Updated synthesis

C20–C25 now separate two related but distinct failure modes:

1. **evaluator reliability** — pairwise labels can approach chance or contain structured/systematic errors; and
2. **evaluation allocation** — even a useful verifier can destroy search throughput if too much of a fixed budget is spent uniformly re-checking candidates.

The current best-supported control pattern is therefore:

- maintain uncertainty/reliability rather than a single noisy point estimate;
- preserve exploration budget explicitly;
- escalate evaluation selectively when uncertainty can change an archive/survival decision;
- retain reversible/dormant candidates when uncertainty is high rather than hard-deleting them;
- avoid whole-population/whole-archive verification when its cost scales with population size unless evidence shows the budget can support it.

This strengthens the earlier `selective communication + selective deep verification` hypothesis with direct matched-budget QD evidence.

## Rejected / downgraded hypotheses

- `More verifier calls always improve population search` is rejected. Under fixed evaluation budget, repeated sampling can reduce exploration enough to hurt performance.
- `Full-archive re-evaluation is the safest default` is rejected as a generally scalable default: Archive-Sampling is not even feasible on two tested tasks at the shared 1024-evaluation budget.
- `25% of budget should always be verification` is explicitly unsupported. The fraction should remain a tunable/adaptive variable until a matched fraction ablation establishes a robust optimum.
- `Uniformly verify every candidate before selection` is downgraded. EME's rank-biased archive extraction and EPGA's matched-budget gain support selective allocation.
- `The current public repository configuration is necessarily the exact published experiment` is rejected. Public code evolves; paper configuration and repository-head constants must be provenance-separated.
- `D-PBEMO establishes LLM-judge noise robustness` is rejected. It provides stochastic human-preference evolutionary evidence, not LLM evaluator-error injection.
