### Router definitions

Two deliberately small success calibrators were trained only on the 12,800 train runs:

1. **Marginal-only router** sees an independence-like union-recall feature from the two marginal recalls: `depth * log(1-(1-r_observed)(1-r_static))`.
2. **Joint-aware router** sees the complement feature: `depth * log(1-(1-r_observed)(1-q))`, where `q=P(static hit | observed miss)`.

Both use the same train-only linear call-cost model. Their threshold was chosen on train only to maximize correct endpoints per 100k calls subject to train failure `<=1%`. No test retuning was permitted.

### Held-out threshold transfer fails for both

On 44,800 held-out runs:

| router | local/union route fraction | endpoint failure | mean calls | correct endpoints / 100k calls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| marginal-only | 13.393% | 1.790% | 115.586 | 849.67 |
| joint-aware | 14.732% | 1.746% | 116.192 | 845.62 |

Both exceeded the 1% train risk constraint after held-out graph/overlap shift. Therefore **a calibrated joint-miss feature does not by itself make an in-distribution risk threshold transport safely**. Graph reach, cost distribution and blind-spot structure also shift.

This is the cleanest result from the run: **conditional complement coverage improves safety ranking at fixed local-replay capacity**, reducing endpoint failure about 19–28% relative in this synthetic family. But it is not a free Pareto improvement: the joint-aware router often selects safer yet costlier local-replay cases, so its correct/100k throughput is slightly lower at the same route fraction.

## Updated controller conclusion

A rollback controller should not treat `number of provenance mechanisms`, marginal recall, or implementation diversity as enough evidence for destructive local replay. The second provenance mechanism is useful in proportion to its **conditional residual coverage of the first mechanism's misses**.

However, this run also rejects the stronger claim that joint-miss calibration alone is sufficient. The held-out risk failure shows that a practical controller also needs a proof/risk state over graph reach and provenance coverage. A promising next shape is:

- use local replay only when the corrected datum is sufficiently reliable **and** the affected closure is supported by high-confidence observed/complement provenance;
- distinguish `proved_new_version`, `known_uncaptured`, and `unknown` resources;
- if joint blind-spot coverage or graph reach is unknown, fail closed by expanding the closure or doing whole redraw rather than silently accepting a local replay;
- treat false-negative provenance as a safety error and false-positive provenance mainly as a cost/blast-radius error.

## Public-source cross-check

Public work supports these architectural distinctions without validating the synthetic thresholds above:

- GRADE (arXiv:2606.22741) explicitly separates execution edges from dependency edges and grades dependencies as observed, declared or inferred: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22741
- Cordon (arXiv:2606.17573) binds task-level semantic transactions to runtime-tracked result lineage, reversible state and recovery metadata: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17573
- `auditable` documents observed LangGraph dependency edges as channel-level read-after-committed-write touches, matched superstep-aware and reducer-aware, while warning that an observed channel touch is not itself a precise causal claim: https://github.com/yzhao062/auditable/blob/main/docs/architecture.md

These sources justify keeping provenance *grade* and runtime lineage explicit. They do not establish this harness's 1% threshold, 19–28% failure reduction, or any production crossover.

## Scope guards / negative evidence

- All numeric router results are synthetic mechanism-study outputs, not production LLM-agent estimates.
- The critical endpoint is one root-to-leaf dependency path; real agents may have reducers, cross-branch joins, conditional routing, dynamically created resources and cross-run memory.
- Static false-positive dependence is simplified and does not yet model structured inference errors.
- The source correction itself is assumed correct here; verifier false replacement remains a separate safety axis from provenance completeness.
- Logistic success calibration and linear cost prediction are deliberately small baselines, not claimed optimal routers.
- The held-out risk miss is evidence against treating train-calibrated risk as portable without shift detection/proof state.
