## Quantitative findings from the pinned public artifacts

### 1. Heterogeneous panel size is not a substitute for conditional residual value

For the v1 factuality prompt on the 147-item common set, judge-human Cohen kappa is:

- Llama-3.3-70B: 0.5342
- GPT-OSS-120B: 0.5146
- Kimi-K2.6: 0.3354
- Nemotron-550B: 0.2806
- Gemma-4-31B: 0.2794
- Cogito-671B: 0.2117

Yet the six-judge majority-vote ensemble has kappa **0.4008**, about **0.1334 below the best single judge**. Unanimous and any-flag rules are still lower (0.2720 and 0.2776). Pairwise judge-judge kappas range from about 0.096 to 0.608, so model-family heterogeneity does not automatically convert into useful independent evidence.

The repository's separate independence diagnostic reports observed `0.3523` versus independence expectation `0.1248` (95% simulation interval `[0.0736, 0.1803]`, 2,000 simulations), an excess `0.2275`. I am preserving the repository's diagnostic without broadening it into a universal error-correlation estimate.

### 2. A prompt that adds another criterion can look like a judge change while actually changing the task

Prompt v1: independently verify **factual correctness**.

Prompt v2: require both `(A) Truth` and `(B) Responsiveness`; fail if either fails.

On the **89 factuality-assessable items**, switching v1→v2 decreases kappa for **all six judges**:

- Cogito: -0.1649
- Gemma: -0.0573
- GPT-OSS: -0.1812
- Kimi: -0.2439
- Llama: -0.5490
- Nemotron: -0.1735

Mean delta on this assessable subset: **-0.2283 kappa**.

Across all 147 common items, the mean delta is only about **-0.0235**, because model-specific movements have opposite signs (e.g. Gemma +0.2094 while Llama -0.4654). Meanwhile v2 raises the mean flag rate by about **+0.403** overall; on the 58 elicitation/no-factual-claim items the mean flag-rate increase is about **+0.666**.

Interpretation within exact scope: this is not evidence that "more explicit rubrics are bad". It is evidence that comparing prompts by one agreement number is invalid when the prompt changes the construct. A routing controller must first verify that candidate evaluators are judging the *same semantic claim* before treating their disagreement as independent evidence.

### 3. Construct applicability dominates calibration quality

The public audit partitions the corpus because R3 declined to score factual correctness on 58 items with no applicable factual claim. Under the original factuality prompt:

- On **89 assessable items**, judge-human kappa ranges **0.4532–0.6658** across the six models.
- On **58 elicitation/no-factual-claim items**, it ranges only **0.0206–0.2835**.

Examples on the assessable subset: Kimi 0.6658, Nemotron 0.6506, Gemma 0.6287, GPT-OSS/Llama 0.6165. On the elicitation subset, the best is GPT-OSS at 0.2835 and several are near zero.

So calibration should be *construct-conditional*: a judge can be serviceable where the target construct is actually instantiated and nearly meaningless where it is not. Mixing the two can make aggregate metrics answer the wrong question.

### 4. Fresh external evidence: high reliability can coexist with weak construct sensitivity

`A Judge Should Know What Changed: Construct Validity for LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation` (arXiv:2608.24419, submitted 2026-08-25) separates:
- invariance `S`: verdict should stay fixed under construct-preserving edits;
- construct sensitivity `R`: verdict should change under minimal construct-changing edits.

Across 7 judges × 4 domains, at matched `S >= 0.90` the paper reports mean `S=0.945` but only `R=0.319`; scope-edit sensitivity is `0.383` versus strength-edit sensitivity `0.262`. It also reports that surface-only predictors reproduce 55–67% of labels in five public label sets in paired mode. The paper explicitly argues that agreement/test-retest consistency does not constrain `R`.

This directly changes the semantic-correction gate design: agreement with humans, self-consistency, and pairwise judge diversity are insufficient proof for destructive canonical replacement unless the verifier is also demonstrated to react correctly to the *kind of semantic change being repaired*.

### 5. Fresh external evidence: abstention/localization is preferable to forced selection when confidence is not calibrated

`Localize-Then-Decide Guarantees for LLM Judgments` (arXiv:2608.25824, submitted 2026-08-26; EMNLP 2026 Findings) observes that simple confidence-thresholding can fail as candidate-set size grows. It first conformally localizes a shortlist, then makes a calibrated decision or abstains, and reports better guarantee success and coverage than single-stage baselines across multiple candidate sizes/datasets/judges.

I do not transfer its numerical guarantees to repair verification; the useful mechanism is narrower: *localize uncertainty before forcing a semantic commit, and preserve an abstain route when the calibration assumptions fail*.

## Updated controller hypothesis

Semantic correction should be gated in this order:

1. **Construct applicability:** is the claimed property actually instantiated in this case?
2. **Construct identity:** do A and B judge the same semantic criterion, or did a prompt/rubric change introduce another criterion (e.g. responsiveness)?
3. **Construct validity:** has the verifier shown both invariance to irrelevant edits and sensitivity to the semantic edit class that matters?
4. **Conditional residual value:** on cases where A is wrong, does B actually correct A more often than it harms A-correct cases, with minimum support and uncertainty bounds?
5. **Decision impact:** destructive replacement requires stronger evidence as descendant fan-out, external-effect irreversibility, or replay cost rises; otherwise keep provisional/standby or abstain.
6. **Repair admissibility:** only after semantic truth is sufficiently supported should the existing provenance/dependency/authority/effect/KV repair certificate decide local/enlarged/redraw/block scope.

This is a stronger condition than `model diversity`, `judge agreement`, `confidence`, or `majority vote`.

## Scope / negative claims preserved

- The 147-item corpus is machine-generated tutoring data, not a general human-production benchmark.
- v2 changes the rubric, so its kappa delta is **not** a clean same-construct prompt-quality ablation.
- The six-judge majority-vote result does not prove all ensembles are bad; it disproves the assumption that more heterogeneous judges automatically improve the decision.
- The construct-validity paper studies specific intervention families and domains; its `S/R` numbers are not universal thresholds.
- No production repair threshold is inferred from these values.
