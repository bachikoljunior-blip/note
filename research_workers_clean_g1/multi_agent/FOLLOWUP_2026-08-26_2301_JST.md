## New primary evidence — MANTA inference-time topology adaptation
Primary source: Mao-xun Huang, Jerry Wang, Yi-Cheng Lai, Zhengxin Zhang, Claire Cardie, Hen-Hsen Huang, **MANTA: Multi-Agent Network Topology Adaptation for Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Systems**, arXiv:2607.28527v1, 2026-07-30, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28527 .

MANTA is directly relevant to the current controller frontier because it adapts collaboration structure **during inference** while preserving a bounded agent budget. It separates three mechanisms: task-conditioned initial topology planning, trace/audit-triggered bounded mutation during a run, and a two-horizon playbook that reuses structural experience across runs.

### Component ablation under the paper's tested setting
Table 2 averages 30 tasks from each of BrowseComp, WorkBench, PlanCraft, and StableToolBench:
- Full MANTA: success `71.7%`, mean total tokens/run `100,315`.
- No initial Topology Planner: `57.5%`, `112,040` tokens.
- No topology mutation: `60.8%`, `72,105` tokens.
- No long-term playbook update: `67.5%`, `102,620` tokens.
- No long-term playbook: `66.7%`, `78,356` tokens.

Within this exact setup, task-conditioned initialization is the largest isolated success contributor (`-14.2pp` when removed), while execution-time repair is also large (`-10.9pp`). Long-term structural memory contributes a smaller `4.2–5.0pp`. Importantly, the no-mutation variant uses materially fewer tokens yet performs much worse, so the repair benefit is not explained by simply spending more compute. Conversely, do **not** interpret full MANTA's higher token count over the no-mutation ablation as proof of matched-compute superiority; the paper does not provide an equal-token reallocation control for this ablation.

### Bounded mutation rather than unlimited self-modification
The method validates each structural edit before instantiating agents. Its reported implementation caps initial agents at `5`, total agents after repair at `10`, and each mutation at at most `3` structural operations. The paper's progressive mutation-budget study reports that the first mutation opportunity gives the largest average gain, with later opportunities adding smaller coverage. Exact per-budget values are plotted rather than tabulated in the accessible text, so I do not assign numeric deltas beyond that qualitative ordering.

This supports a controller rule stronger than "let topology self-modify": **permit small, validated, trace-grounded structural edits, and treat additional mutation opportunities as diminishing-return compute rather than an always-on entitlement.**

### Repair targets are local and mechanism-specific
The paper gives concrete trace-backed repairs rather than only whole-network scaling:
- expand only an overloaded branch into a subgroup;
- insert a verifier when an unvalidated singleton is low-confidence;
- serialize parallel agents when duplicated state-changing actions are a risk;
- rewire a missing worker→verifier edge when consensus appears premature;
- change context visibility without necessarily adding agents.

This is evidence against using raw agent count as the sole adaptation variable. Useful control dimensions include role count, communication edges, execution order, information visibility, and validation paths.

### Cross-run structural memory has limited but positive transfer in MANTA
At mutation budget zero, transferring MANTA's long-term playbook yields `+3.3pp` mean gain both in-domain and cross-domain in the reported PlanCraft/WorkBench experiment. The compared adaptive baselines have neutral or negative cross-domain transfer in that table (ADAS mean `-3.3pp`, AgentSquare `-13.3pp`, MASS `-58.3pp`). This suggests reusable **structural lessons** can transfer better than frozen workflows in this setting, but the experiment is small (30 source runs and 30 target tasks), benchmark-specific, and not enough to claim universal transfer.

### Token accounting
Across four benchmarks, Table 4 reports MANTA at `77,652` mean tokens/run total, lower than the tested static multi-agent and offline workflow-optimization baselines; meta-level operations are `9,416` tokens versus `68,236` inner-agent tokens (~12% meta overhead). However, the single-agent baseline is still far cheaper at `21,811` tokens. Thus MANTA is evidence for **more efficient multi-agent coordination**, not evidence that multi-agent execution dominates single-agent execution on cost.

## Updated synthesis
The new evidence strengthens a controller architecture with two distinct adaptation horizons:

1. **Before execution:** choose a task-conditioned sparse topology and bounded agent count from structural experience.
2. **During execution:** use process/audit signals to make small local repairs only when the current organization is insufficient.
3. **Across executions:** store portable structural lessons, not frozen task-specific workflows.
4. **Budget discipline:** cap mutation count/operations and require deterministic structural validation before irreversible activation.
5. **Evaluation discipline:** compare success gain against the opportunity cost of extra repair tokens; MANTA shows repair helps, but does not provide a matched-token ablation that isolates repair quality from additional compute.

This complements the prior AgentConductor result. AgentConductor supplies RL-trained difficulty-conditioned topology generation and execution-feedback refinement; MANTA supplies a cleaner decomposition of initial planning vs repair vs structural memory and shows that the first bounded repair carries most of the reported mutation-budget benefit. Neither establishes a universal optimal agent count or mutation budget.
