## C37 — Direct controlled corruption inside a self-evolving LLM-agent lifecycle: asymmetric false-pass bias has a hard retirement cliff

Primary source: Xing Zhang et al., *The Blind Curator: How a Biased Judge Silently Disables Skill Retirement in Self-Evolving Agents*, arXiv:2607.07436v2, revised 2026-08-18, COLM 2026 Workshop on Agent Behavior. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07436

This is the closest direct result found so far to the unresolved evaluator-corruption frontier. The study runs a closed-loop self-evolving skill library and injects corruption only into the reward used during evolution while keeping evaluation on the true deterministic grader. It separately controls symmetric label noise and asymmetric false-pass bias rather than collapsing judge quality into one scalar error rate.

### Mechanism and quantitative sweep

The curator retires a skill when its observed pass rate falls below `pi_tau=(1-tau)/2`. Under a false-pass channel, the observed statistic is displaced upward; retirement becomes impossible once `rho_F->P >= (1-tau)/2`. With the tested `tau=0.10`, the predicted cliff is a false-pass rate of **0.45**. Crucially, this is a bias cliff: once crossed, more samples cannot restore the retirement decision under the modeled channel. Symmetric noise instead attenuates signal and increases the sample requirement approximately with `(1-2 rho)^-2` while preserving direction for `rho<0.5`.

The behavioral experiments run the loop for 12 rounds with `tau=0.10`, `N_min=24`, active-skill cap `C=12`, and multiple seeds. The controlled channels include symmetric noise `rho in {0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4}` and false-pass bias `q in {0.2,0.45,0.7}`. Across report-writing subsets and a code-generation cross-check, symmetric noise preserves genuine contribution-retirement much better than false-pass corruption. False-pass bias drives genuine retirement to approximately zero around/past the predicted cliff. The paper explicitly separates true contribution-retirement from cap-eviction churn, so a full library cap cannot masquerade as healthy governance.

A key negative result is that aggregate task quality can stay apparently healthy while the curator mechanism is already disabled. Outcome harm is regime-dependent because severe corruption can also starve skill synthesis; therefore a silent lifecycle failure is possible even without an obvious headline-score collapse.

### Judge audit and asymmetry

The paper's defect-injection audit shows why total judge error is not enough. The deterministic QC catches structural injected defects but misses semantic claim negation and nearly all number swaps; a held-out LLM judge catches the semantic defects at high rates. Conversely, the audited strict binary judge has near-zero false-pass but very high false-fail, placing it in a conservative/starvation-prone rather than retirement-disabling regime. Thus the dangerous axis is specifically failures being misreported as passes, not merely high aggregate error.

### Transfer to population/QD control

For a QD/archive controller, false-pass is analogous to **false admission / failure to retire a bad elite**, while false-fail is analogous to **false rejection / starvation of useful candidates**. These should be swept separately. Re-evaluation can fight stochastic variance but cannot fix a stable displacement past a decision threshold. Therefore the archive safety design should measure class-conditional evaluator errors and process-specific lifecycle metrics (genuine elite retirement, false archive writes, true-elite survival, cap-eviction churn) rather than only endpoint score or judge consistency.

### Scope limits

- This is a self-evolving skill lifecycle, not MAP-Elites or an island/QD archive.
- Corruption is exogenously injected on top of deterministic ground truth; it is not a co-adapting adversarial judge.
- The main contribution is behavioral/mechanistic safety, not a claim that one architecture improves task performance.
- The direct requested `0/5/10/20/30/40/50%` QD archive corruption sweep with archive-regret metrics remains unfound.

## C38 — Loreley public evidence can replay selection-layer corruption, but not the full online QD archive dynamics

Primary public artifact: NeapolitanIcecream/loreley, `paper/evidence/README.md`, `zstd_formal_records.json`, `zstd_method_efficacy.json`, `build_zstd_formal_records.py`. https://github.com/NeapolitanIcecream/loreley

The previous checkpoint identified Loreley as a promising replay scaffold. This run inspected the released evidence boundary more closely and narrows that claim.

The public evidence README says the formal Zstandard record contains **126 finalist groups, 439 validation evaluations, 126 validation-to-holdout selections, 56 unique holdout evaluations, 21 final endpoints**, the finalist-width sensitivity, and public parent/inspiration graphs. The released validator can replay the complete validation-winner rule for all 126 groups and recompute the reported endpoint statistics.

The builder confirms that each public finalist record retains source-qualified evaluator identity, commit, training rank, logical job, training score, root status, validation pass/fail and summary metrics; the public selection groups retain block, policy arm, checkpoint and winner identity. That is enough to perform a meaningful **offline selection-layer corruption replay** without rerunning generation.

However, the README and builder are explicit that candidate source, prompts, hidden corpora, private paths/provider state and several private archive-database diagnostics are not distributed. The public export is designed to replay finalist selection, endpoint mapping/statistics and lineage counts — not every online archive insertion, eviction, parent sample or intermediate candidate state across all 48 generated jobs.

### What can be replayed honestly from public data

A public synthetic corruption experiment can take the frozen finalist groups and perturb only their validation/admission layer under a matched candidate set. Suggested corruption channels:

- iid eligibility/verdict flips at `p={0,.05,.10,.20,.30,.40,.50}`;
- asymmetric false-pass versus false-fail channels;
- block-correlated verdict errors;
- stable metric offsets that emulate a biased evaluator.

Then re-run the frozen validation-selection rule and measure:

- winner-identity error versus the clean selection;
- selected-invalid / false-admission rate;
- false root fallback or missed non-root winner;
- holdout endpoint regret using the public holdout mapping where the corrupted winner has a released holdout measurement;
- distortion of the 48-job policy contrasts;
- winner-lineage changes for public lineage records.

### What cannot be claimed from the released record alone

The public aggregate does **not** support a faithful replay of full online QD archive corruption, true-elite loss over every generated candidate, parent-sampling feedback loops, or migration dynamics. Likewise, PACE-style paired/anytime-valid gates require repeated per-instance evidence; the public Loreley summaries do not obviously expose the raw paired instance sequence needed to compare those acceptors without adding synthetic assumptions. A replay using only public summaries should therefore be labeled `selection-layer corruption replay`, not `full QD archive replay`.

This is a useful correction: the scaffold is still valuable, but its public causal resolution is narrower than the earlier hypothesis.

## C39 — Current arXiv version of the former GSME line is HarnessBank v2; code is not yet public

Primary source: Xiaotian Luo et al., *HarnessBank: Semantic Gene-Bank Search with Gated Verification for Agent-Harness Self-Evolution*, arXiv:2607.13683v2, revised 2026-07-30. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13683

The current arXiv v2 has been substantially retitled/reframed from the v1 GSME presentation. It now describes a semantic Harness Gene Bank with reinvention/recombination and Gated Harness Screening, reports **5.1% to 15.4%** improvements across seven agent benchmarks, and explicitly states that code will be publicly available upon acceptance.

Therefore the earlier v1 GSME quantitative observations remain version-scoped evidence and must not be silently attributed to the current v2 formulation. More importantly for the current frontier, no public frozen candidate/evaluation trace was found from the current HarnessBank release, so it cannot currently serve as an artifact-level corruption replay scaffold.

## Updated synthesis

1. The broader direct-noise frontier is now partially resolved: a self-evolving LLM-agent lifecycle has a controlled corruption sweep with symmetric noise through 40% and false-pass bias through 70%, and the effect is sharply asymmetric.
2. **Bias direction matters more than a scalar judge error rate.** A stable false-pass displacement can cross a threshold where more data is useless; symmetric variance remains statistically recoverable below 50% in the modeled channel.
3. Population/QD evaluation should separately report false-admit and false-reject rates. A single symmetric-flip sweep is insufficient because the two errors can produce opposite lifecycle pathologies: library/archive drift versus search starvation.
4. Aggregate endpoint quality can hide a dead governance mechanism. Archive/lifecycle metrics must therefore be first-class safety/evaluation outputs.
5. Loreley's released evidence supports a concrete finalist-selection corruption replay with matched frozen candidates, but not a faithful replay of the full online archive feedback loop.
6. HarnessBank v2 currently does not supply public code/traces; v1 and v2 evidence must remain version-qualified.

## Rejected / downgraded hypotheses

- `No direct controlled reward-corruption experiment exists inside a self-evolving LLM-agent loop` — rejected by The Blind Curator.
- `Total judge error alone predicts self-evolution safety` — rejected; class-conditional false-pass versus false-fail behavior is materially different.
- `More re-evaluation always fixes an unreliable judge` — rejected for stable bias beyond a decision cliff; more samples reduce variance, not systematic displacement.
- `Loreley's public evidence is sufficient to replay the entire online QD archive under synthetic noise` — rejected. It is sufficient for finalist-selection/endpoint replay, not full archive dynamics.
- `Current HarnessBank/GSME code is already public` — rejected by current v2 source wording; code is promised upon acceptance.
