## New empirical update from SymFail: anchor location and repair semantics are different uncertainty axes
The full arXiv HTML for `Repair or Resample? Rethinking Failure Debugging in LLM Multi-Agent Systems` (arXiv:2608.25920v1, submitted 2026-08-26) exposes substantially more annotation and anchor-selection detail than was used in the predecessor checkpoint.

The 536 SymFail failures were independently annotated by three software-engineering-background annotators and then adjudicated by a fourth reviewer using the task, full trace, event graph, all three annotations, rationales, and evidence. Annotators are instructed to choose the **earliest trace-supported actionable node**, not the latest visible manifestation.

### Human localization agreement
Among the three independent annotators:
- exact same failure-node ID for all three: `396/536 = 73.88%` (paper Wilson 95% CI `70.00%–77.42%`).
- at least two choose the same node: `514/536 = 95.90%`.
- exact strict diagnosis tuple `(exact category set, primary category, node ID, node type)` agrees for at least two annotators in only `424/536 = 79.10%`.

The node can therefore be comparatively stable while the **intervention semantics attached to that node remain much less stable**.

### Majority vote is not adjudication
Appendix B Table 5 reports the relationship between the three initial strict tuples and the adjudicated final tuple:
- unanimous initial strict tuple: 210 cases; the unanimous tuple was retained in 187 and modified in 23.
- 2–1 split: 214 cases; majority retained 124, minority retained 48, and a new tuple chosen in 42.
- all distinct: 112 cases; one proposed tuple retained in 77, and a new tuple chosen in 35.

Thus, in a 2–1 split, the majority strict tuple becomes the final adjudicated tuple in only `124/214 = 57.94%`. Across all 536 cases, the final strict tuple is absent from all three initial strict tuples in `100/536 = 18.66%`.

This is important for the current repair controller: **top-k union over already-proposed diagnoses is not a completeness proof**. A higher-quality adjudicator can construct a new repair tuple rather than selecting one of the candidates. This does not imply that the final *node* was unseen in all such cases—the published table is for the full strict tuple—but it does show that location certainty and repair-instruction certainty must not be collapsed.

The category ambiguity is also strongly nonuniform. C2 (repeated/stalled progress) has the lowest category-specific Fleiss kappa (`0.374`) and accounts for `86/123` category additions made by the adjudicator that were absent from all three initial annotations. The controller should therefore treat symptom-class reliability as slice-specific rather than a single localizer accuracy.

## Symptom score is ordinal, not a repair-success probability
The paper's Suspicious-Node policy combines deterministic symptom rules, semantic-judge confidence, node type, graph position, propagation evidence, and actionability into a fused score. The authors explicitly state that these are **ordinal evidence scores, not calibrated probabilities**, and that the coefficients and threshold were manually specified rather than validation-optimized.

The primary threshold `theta=0.50` retains all 536 selected historical targets and yields 108 repairs (`20.15%`). A post-hoc abstention analysis at `theta=0.95` retains only 78 cases (`14.55%` coverage) and 19 repairs (`24.36%` conditional repair). That analysis fixes the historical rank-0 target and observed repair outcome; it does not rerank or rerun alternative targets. Therefore the higher score should not be reused as `P(anchor correct)` or `P(repair succeeds)` in the risk-vector controller.

The paper also reports that Suspicious-Node Intervention materially outperforms Random-Node and Last-Node intervention and the three-attempt full-rerun baselines on all three tested MASs, but target selection and repair guidance are evaluated jointly, so the paper does **not** isolate the causal value of the ranking function itself.

## Replay evidence relevant to current rollback work
SymTrace's replay fidelity audit uses the human-adjudicated target ID. Across 536 cases × 3 replay attempts, all 1,608 replay plans used that exact target, all 6,159 reused prefix nodes matched source content hashes, and every attempt preserved parent/edge topology. The authors explicitly note that this audit measures **replay fidelity, not automatic localization accuracy**.

This distinction matters for the current architecture:
`semantic diagnosis/localization -> repair-scope selection -> replay admissibility`
should remain layered. Perfect prefix replay cannot compensate for a wrong intervention tuple.

## Controller update
The candidate controller is now refined to keep two uncertainty objects upstream of rollback:
1. **anchor-location uncertainty**: which earliest actionable node(s) contain the causal intervention point.
2. **repair-semantics uncertainty**: which symptom/category/evidence tuple defines the corrective action at the selected node.

A top-k anchor union can increase location coverage, but it cannot solve semantic novelty when the correct repair tuple is not among current proposals. Under high-impact repair-risk vectors, disagreement or unsupported symptom slices should therefore trigger a new adjudication/escalation step, provisional repair, broader replay, or abstention—not merely a larger union of existing candidate tuples.

The paper's own repairability-aware ranking also supports separating `suspicious` from `repairable`: propagation-only nodes and late graph position are penalized, while regenerable agent actions and tool-call boundaries are favored. A strongly suspicious late manifestation need not be the best anchor.
