## Frontier action 1 — exact matched-output single-agent vs MAS evidence

### C12 — Nominal agent multiplicity can reduce semantic diversity even when output count and prompt conditioning are held fixed

Primary source: Cui Encheng et al., *Single-Agent Generation Surpasses Multi-Agent Systems in Semantic Diversity*, Findings of ACL 2026. https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1894/

The controlled protocols separate agent count from perspective prompts and total output count. SI-MO is one model invocation producing multiple outputs, while MI-SO uses multiple parallel agents; the same perspectives and fixed output volume are used. Across the divergent-thinking tasks, SI-MO is the highest-diversity protocol and MI-SO is the weakest.

The cost table makes the scaling penalty concrete when SI-MO is normalized to 1.0 total token cost. At output count k=2, MI-SO costs 1.5885x and serial SI-SO 1.6895x. At k=4 the ratios are 2.7013x and 3.1820x; at k=8, 4.5098x and 5.7339x; at k=16, 8.4914x and 12.1092x. Thus the MAS variants pay substantially more token cost while failing to produce greater semantic diversity in this task family.

A hybrid agent-count/output-count table also shows a monotonic diversity penalty as a fixed total output count is split across more agents. For GPT-4.1-mini at k=16, the 1x16 condition starts at 6.67/7.31/5.62 across the three embedding metrics, while 16x1 is roughly 16.5–21.5% lower at round 1 and remains roughly 15.5–18.3% lower at round 4. The paper also reports that four rounds are enough for convergence in these experiments; additional rounds have diminishing returns and can sometimes degrade performance.

A post-hoc validity audit finds no evidence that SI-MO's higher diversity was purchased through a material increase in obvious logical defects.

Scope: divergent/open-ended idea-generation tasks under the studied backbones and sampling setup. This does not show that a single model invocation is superior for decomposable tool use, adversarial verification, or tasks where agents truly have different information. The paper's proposed information-visibility explanation is plausible but not a complete causal decomposition.

Mechanism hypothesis: before allocating slots to nominally separate agents, test whether a single inference can generate decorrelated candidates more cheaply. Agent multiplicity should be justified by genuinely distinct information, models, tools, environments, or lineages rather than role labels alone.

## Frontier action 2 — adaptive workflow depth / conditional multi-agent compute

### C13 — Query-conditional scheduling can recover much of a heavy workflow's accuracy at far lower token and latency cost, but cheap gating alone is unsafe

Primary source: Dawei Xiang et al., *LLM-as-Scheduler: Agentic Workflow Dynamic Scheduling*, ACL 2026. https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.581/

LAS dynamically decides whether to stop or invoke further workflow stages using a lightweight gate followed by an LLM scheduler. Against the strong fixed AFLOW workflow, the paper reports:

- MBPP: 94.2% / 6643.2 tokens / 134.28s for AFLOW versus 93.7% / 2430.3 / 77.99s for LAS: -0.5pp accuracy, -63.4% tokens, -41.9% latency.
- HumanEval: 95.9% / 9390.8 / 171.56s versus 94.5% / 5876.5 / 108.34s: -1.4pp, -37.4% tokens, -36.8% latency.
- GSM8K: 95.9% / 17582.5 / 198.26s versus 95.1% / 8624.1 / 125.15s: -0.8pp, -50.9% tokens, -36.9% latency.

The MBPP ablation is a useful failure result: fixed AFLOW is 94.2% accuracy; `Only Gate` falls to 86.7% while using 2516.4 tokens; `Only LAS` reaches 93.9% at 3678.1 tokens; Gate+LAS reaches 93.7% at 2430.2 tokens. The cheap gate therefore saves compute but is too error-prone to be the sole controller. The full gate reports high recall (98.60%) but only 66.29% precision, while scheduler routing accuracy is 65.72%; the paper reports harmful routing errors at 2.7%.

The task distribution itself supports conditional allocation: roughly 60% are solvable by the first agent, about 20% need the full workflow, and about 20% remain unsolved by the workflow.

Scope: code/reasoning benchmarks and the tested workflows; it does not directly choose arbitrary population size N or arbitrary graph topology. It is evidence for adaptive depth/conditional compute, not universal superiority of the specific two-stage scheduler.

Mechanism hypothesis: treat multi-agent work as an escalation resource. Use a conservative low-cost gate to identify likely-easy cases, but retain a stronger scheduler/verifier for uncertain cases; optimize expected marginal value of the next agent/stage rather than invoking the whole graph on every query.

## Frontier action 3 — island population structure and elite migration

### C14 — Island-based elite selection materially improves LLM heuristic evolution; removing the whole island+elite mechanism hurts every tested heuristic

Primary source: Xuanang Chen et al., *Experience-Driven Reflective Co-Evolution of Prompts and Heuristics for Autonomous Algorithm Design (EvoPH)*, Findings of ACL 2026. https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.245/

EvoPH partitions the population into independent island subpopulations, maintains local elite archives, and periodically migrates selected elites. The published implementation uses K=5 islands and T=20 iterations. Table 2 removes the island model plus elite selection as one ablation (`w/o IES`). Relative error, lower is better:

- Nearest-insertion: EvoPH 4.41% vs w/o IES 9.70%
- 2-opt: 4.20% vs 5.17%
- Christofides: 5.17% vs 7.03%
- Farthest-insertion: 4.05% vs 5.99%
- Nearest-neighbor: 4.41% vs 6.48%
- Random-insertion: 4.41% vs 6.90%

Other ablations also matter: no prompt evolution gives 5.17/5.17/9.24/5.50/5.17/5.17%; no strategy sampling gives 5.17/4.38/5.17/5.17/5.17/4.41%. Thus the strongest tested system combines population separation, elite migration/selection, strategy diversity, and evolving mutation prompts rather than attributing all gains to islands alone.

Scope: automated heuristic design on the paper's TSP/bin-packing benchmark setting. Because `w/o IES` removes both island structure and elite selection simultaneously, the ablation cannot isolate migration from local elite archiving. Migration interval/frequency is still unresolved.

Supporting older evolutionary evidence (not LLM-specific): De Jong & Skolicki's island-model experiments report migration interval as more influential than migration size; overly frequent migration collapses global diversity, while overly rare migration slows convergence. This is a useful prior, not direct evidence that the same optimum holds for LLM populations.

Mechanism hypothesis: maintain semi-isolated lineages and migrate only high-value elites; do not continuously share all state. The missing variable to measure next is migration cadence conditioned on stagnation/diversity.

## Frontier action 4 — evaluator uncertainty / moving evaluators

### C15 — Pairwise uncertainty-aware selection can sustain evolutionary search without a calibrated scalar reward, but an explicit judge-noise threshold remains unmeasured

Primary source: Sweta Karlekar et al., *Duel-Evolve: Reward-Free Test-Time Scaling via LLM Self-Preferences*, arXiv:2602.21585. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21585

Duel-Evolve replaces scalar reward with pairwise self-preferences from the same LLM, aggregates noisy comparisons through a Bayesian Bradley-Terry posterior, allocates comparison budget with Double Thompson Sampling, and selects parents using both estimated quality and uncertainty. It reports +20 percentage points over existing methods/baselines on MathBench and >12 points over comparable iterative methods on LiveCodeBench, without ground-truth labels or a hand-crafted scalar scorer during search.

This supports a weaker but useful claim: noisy evaluation need not be fatal if uncertainty is modeled explicitly and comparison budget is concentrated around plausible optima. It does **not** establish how inaccurate a judge can become before population search fails; no controlled synthetic judge-noise sweep was verified in this run.

Additional primary lead: Li et al., *Beyond Static Evaluation: Co-Evolutionary Mechanisms for LLM-Driven Strategy Evolution in Adversarial Games*, arXiv:2606.10389. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10389 . The authors explicitly target evaluator drift in adversarial games using opponent-pool co-evolution, deeper repeated evaluation, and weakness pressure; the abstract reports best combined score 0.526 and 61.7% win rate on unseen opponents, with ablations contributing. Detailed sample-count/noise-response curves still need primary-table extraction before stronger claims.

Scope: Duel-Evolve is test-time discrete-output optimization and FAMOU is one adversarial game domain. Neither currently supplies the desired general judge reliability threshold.

## Updated cross-source synthesis

The new evidence sharpens resource allocation into four distinct decisions. First, do not instantiate separate agents merely for diversity when a single multi-output call can produce more diverse candidates at much lower cost. Second, when a task sometimes needs extra reasoning/verification, make additional stages conditional on estimated difficulty rather than fixed. Third, for open-ended evolutionary search, semi-isolated islands plus selective elite movement are supported better than globally shared populations, although migration cadence still needs direct LLM-era ablation. Fourth, when scalar evaluators are unavailable or noisy, uncertainty-aware pairwise comparison is a viable alternative, but a reliability threshold for when search becomes self-deceptive remains an open empirical question.

The common control variable is therefore **marginal independent information per unit cost**, not agent count. Add agents/stages/islands only when they create a new useful channel or preserve a search lineage; prune or avoid them when they merely duplicate correlated outputs.

## Rejected / downgraded leads

- The hypothesis `more role personas => more diversity` is further downgraded by the matched-prompt SI-MO vs MAS results.
- LAS's lightweight gate is not accepted as a standalone controller: its MBPP accuracy loss is large enough to require a stronger second-stage scheduler/verification path.
- EvoPH does not isolate migration frequency or migration itself from elite-selection effects; do not cite Table 2 as a migration-cadence ablation.
- Classical island-model migration findings are retained only as a prior for search direction, not transferred quantitatively to LLM evolution.
- Duel-Evolve is not recorded as evidence for a specific evaluator-noise threshold because no explicit noise sweep was found.
- A 2026 OpenReview multi-island symbolic-regression submission surfaced useful adaptive-frequency numbers, but it is not promoted to a core candidate here until title/version and peer-review status are cleanly resolved from the primary record.
