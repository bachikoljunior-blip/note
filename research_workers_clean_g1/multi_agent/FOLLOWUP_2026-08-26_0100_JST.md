## C30 — SCOPE provides a direct random-label-noise sweep for a selective pairwise judge, with a desirable conservative failure mode but catastrophic coverage collapse at higher corruption

Primary source: Sher Badshah, Ali Emami, Hassan Sajjad, *SCOPE: Selective Conformal Optimized Pairwise LLM Judging*, arXiv:2602.13110v4, revised 2026-08-18, accepted at ICML 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13110

SCOPE uses Bidirectional Preference Entropy (BPE): judge each pair in both response orders, average the implied preference probability to remove response-position asymmetry, then use entropy as an uncertainty score. A conformal threshold chooses which pairwise judgments to accept versus abstain so accepted-set error is controlled at a target risk level under exchangeability.

### Main risk/coverage evidence

At target risk `alpha=0.10`, SCOPE reports accepted-set empirical risk typically around **0.097–0.099** across MT-Bench, RewardBench and Chatbot Arena and multiple judge scales. Vanilla full-coverage judging on MT-Bench has much larger risk (**0.217–0.269** across the tested judges). SCOPE more than doubles MT-Bench coverage relative to a naïve finite-sample threshold for Qwen-7B (**0.246 vs 0.102**) while remaining under the 0.10 risk constraint. The paper reports up to **2.4×** more accepted judgments than naïve baselines under the same target risk.

### Random corruption sweep

Appendix F injects uniform random flips into the **calibration preference labels** at `epsilon={0,5,10,15,20}%` while keeping test labels clean. This is the closest direct random-noise sweep found so far, though it is calibration-label noise rather than LLM-judge output noise inside an evolutionary loop.

At `alpha=0.10`, empirical accepted-set risk remains at or below the target at every injected noise level, but coverage contracts aggressively. Examples:

- Qwen-14B / RewardBench: `risk/coverage = 0.099/0.891` at 0% noise, `0.055/0.668` at 5%, `0.003/0.195` at 10%, and approximately zero coverage by 15–20%.
- Qwen-32B / RewardBench: `0.098/0.982` -> `0.055/0.816` -> `0.005/0.154` -> near-zero by 15–20%.
- Llama-70B / MT-Bench: `0.099/0.548` -> `0.053/0.147` -> `0.022/0.022` -> `0.008/0.006` -> `0.004/0.002`.

The system therefore fails conservatively: it abstains rather than silently exceeding the requested risk bound. At 20% corruption many configurations have essentially zero coverage, which is operationally a **stop/human-or-stronger-verifier trigger**, not a success mode.

Calibration-set size ablation also matters for sentinel design. With only `n=50` clean calibration examples SCOPE remains under alpha=0.10 but has lower coverage; coverage is largely converged by roughly `n=200–500`. Cross-benchmark calibration transfer can violate the bound under distribution shift (e.g. easier RewardBench calibration -> harder target can reach risk about **0.20**), so the guarantee is not portable across arbitrary search-distribution changes.

### Transfer hypothesis for population/QD search

A population controller can use an explicit three-way selection state rather than forcing every comparison to winner/loser:

`accept cheap preference if calibrated-safe / abstain-and-escalate if uncertain / enter conservative freeze-or-standby if calibration support collapses`.

For irreversible archive replacement or permanent pruning, a risk-controlled abstention mechanism is more defensible than treating a low-entropy or order-consistent judgment as automatically correct. Under worsening evaluator/calibration quality, graceful throughput reduction is preferable to silently corrupting the archive.

### Scope limits

- The injected 0–20% noise is in calibration labels, not judge outputs generated during evolutionary search.
- The conformal risk guarantee assumes exchangeability; cross-distribution transfer can fail.
- Near-zero coverage under heavy noise preserves risk by refusing to decide; it does not preserve search throughput.
- SCOPE is an evaluation method, not a demonstrated MAP-Elites/evolution archive controller.

## C31 — Fixed judge budgets should be allocated by observed variance, not uniformly; one study reaches uniform-100k error at roughly 50k queries

Primary source: Aadirupa Saha, Aniket Wagde, Branislav Kveton, *LLM-as-Judge on a Budget*, arXiv:2602.15481v2, 2026-04-13. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15481

The paper formalizes repeated LLM judging under a fixed total budget `B` as an allocation problem over `K` prompt-response pairs with unknown heterogeneous score variances. ROBIN-HOOD uses an initial warm-up to estimate per-pair variance, then allocates more judge calls to high-variance pairs. The target is minimax worst-case estimation error rather than average error.

The theoretical rate is approximately

`O_tilde(sqrt(sum_i sigma_i^2 / B))`

for the worst-case estimation error, with additional logarithmic overhead for estimating unknown variances.

On the reported HelpSteer2 experiments, ROBIN-HOOD achieves an error around that of uniform allocation at **100k** judge queries using approximately **50k** queries. The authors average experiments over **50 runs** and report statistically significant differences between ROBIN-HOOD and uniform allocation across tested model/attribute settings. They also show a failure mode: a poor confidence parameter (`delta`) can cause adaptive allocation to plateau and eventually be overtaken by uniform allocation.

### Transfer hypothesis

Under a fixed verification budget, repeated evaluation should be concentrated on candidate comparisons whose uncertainty/variance can still change an archive/pruning decision, rather than assigning identical `k` to every candidate. This complements previous Extract-QD evidence that fixed-budget re-evaluation should be selective rather than uniform.

A practical controller should separate:

1. **warm-up**: enough repeated judgments to estimate evaluator variability;
2. **adaptive allocation**: concentrate calls on high-variance/high-decision-impact pairs;
3. **hard cap / fallback**: if uncertainty does not resolve, abstain or use stronger evidence rather than indefinitely sampling.

### Scope limits

- Objective is accurate mean-score estimation, not final evolutionary search quality.
- Variance is only one uncertainty dimension; systematic stable bias can have low variance.
- The paper's empirical 50k-vs-100k result should not be treated as a universal 2× saving.
- Hyperparameter miscalibration can erase the advantage.

## C32 — Elo-Evolve exposes a concrete opponent-selection diversity/focus trade-off: greedy curriculum can score higher internally yet suffer catastrophic downstream degradation

Primary source: Jing Zhao, Ting Zhen, Junwei Bao, Hongfei Jiang, Yang Song, *Elo-Evolve: A Co-evolutionary Framework for Language Model Alignment*, arXiv:2602.13575v2, 2026-03-02. https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13575v2

Elo-Evolve trains a Qwen2.5-7B policy through pairwise competition against an adaptive opponent pool. Opponent sampling is controlled by a temperature over Elo-derived difficulty.

### Opponent-temperature ablation

- `T=20` (greedy): opponent probability changes sharply toward a single dominant opponent. It reaches the highest final reported Elo (**2400**) but suffers a **50.8 -> 43.6** performance degradation at step 900 after the dominant Qwen3-8B opponent itself weakens.
- `T=200` (moderate): opponent probabilities transition gradually; final Elo is about **2300**, with stronger performance stability.
- `T=2000` (near-random): probabilities flatten around 0.3–0.4, final Elo about **2000**, and learning is consistently weaker because selection does not focus enough on useful difficulty.

This is useful negative evidence against both extremes: **over-focused selection is brittle, while near-uniform diversity can dilute the training signal**. Moderate selective diversity performs better in the tested trajectory.

The same paper compares LLM reward formats on an expert-annotated creative-writing set. Absolute-score ranking has estimated effective noise `sigma_abs,eff≈35.65`, while direct pairwise comparison at quality gap 1 has `sigma_comp≈7.85` and 55.1% accuracy. Pairwise accuracy varies non-monotonically with quality gap: **55.1%, 63.5%, 64.4%, 56.2%** for gaps 1–4. This is not an injected-noise sweep but reinforces that comparison reliability is state/difficulty dependent rather than constant.

### Transfer hypothesis

For island/population search, migration/opponent selection should be neither fully greedy nor fully uniform. The controller should maintain enough diversity to avoid dependence on a degraded lineage/evaluator/opponent, while still concentrating interactions where they create useful learning pressure. Internal ranking/Elo alone is insufficient: the `T=20` result shows an internally high rating can coexist with a large downstream performance collapse.

### Scope limits

- This is alignment training against model opponents, not a MAP-Elites archive or autonomous research population.
- Elo is endogenous to the competition; high Elo is not a ground-truth quality metric.
- The noise analysis compares natural LLM scoring/comparison noise, not controlled injected judge-error rates.
- Exact temperature values are setup-specific, not transferable constants.

## C33 — High self-consistency is not a safe sentinel substitute: frontier models can be highly consistent and still wrong almost half the time

Primary source: Kaihua Ding, *When LLMs Agree, Are They Right? Auditing Self-Consistency and Cross-Model Agreement as Confidence Signals*, arXiv:2607.08065v2, 2026-07-28. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08065

The study aggregates **265,000 samples** from 53 runners across GPQA Diamond and AIME. Self-consistency is positively but weakly related to majority correctness, with Spearman correlations only about **0.20–0.59** across studied cells.

The most important stable-wrong result is GPQA with gpt-4.1: high agreement (`C>=0.8`) occurs on a large fraction of cases, yet those high-agreement results are wrong **48%** of the time (95% CI approximately **0.40–0.56**). In a logged-snapshot rerun, gpt-4.1 is more self-consistent than gpt-4.1-mini (`0.89 vs 0.82`) at identical majority accuracy (`0.48` each), and is worse calibrated.

An exploratory Claude-family check shows the same qualitative pattern. Claude Opus is the most self-consistent (mean `C=0.94`, `C>=0.8` on 88% of GPQA cases) but has majority accuracy **0.58**, below Sonnet's **0.63**, and worse calibration. Confident wrong answers also recur across providers above a marginal-preserving null in some model-pair checks.

### Transfer hypothesis

Self-consistency/order agreement is useful for **allocating compute**, but cannot be the only sentinel that certifies archive updates. A small independent calibration/sentinel channel is still needed specifically for stable-wrong failures. The controller should distinguish `low variance` from `high correctness`.

### Scope limits

- GPQA/AIME answer correctness is not identical to open-ended pairwise judging.
- The cross-family Claude analysis is exploratory and smaller than the main runner study.
- Agreement remains a useful conditional proxy in some unsaturated regimes; the evidence rejects only standalone auto-trust, not all consistency-based routing.

## Updated synthesis

The evaluation controller now has four empirically supported pieces that address different failure modes:

1. **Order/perturbation instability** (prior C28): cheap label-free trigger for escalation, but misses stable-wrong cases.
2. **Calibrated abstention** (C30): if accepted-set risk cannot be supported, refuse the decision; under noisy calibration this degrades by coverage rather than hidden error.
3. **Variance-adaptive budget allocation** (C31): spend repeated judge calls where stochastic uncertainty is high rather than uniformly.
4. **Independent/sentinel calibration** (C33): low variance/high agreement cannot certify correctness because systematic errors can be stable.

Population interaction itself shows the same balance principle (C32): too much concentration on one lineage/opponent is brittle; too much uniform mixing weakens learning pressure. This mirrors earlier migration evidence and suggests a common controller objective: **selective concentration subject to diversity and reliability constraints**.

A stronger provisional archive decision policy is therefore:

`provenance/integrity check -> bidirectional/order perturbation -> calibrated risk/abstention -> variance-adaptive extra queries only when decision-relevant -> sentinel calibration for stable-wrong detection -> reversible standby when evidence remains insufficient -> irreversible archive/prune only after stronger evidence`.
