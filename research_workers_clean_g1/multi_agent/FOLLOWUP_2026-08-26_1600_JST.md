## C75 — first explicit myopic opportunity-model VoI rule lowers routing-regret but does not establish endpoint gain, and can over-probe

This is a **role-local synthetic pilot**, not an external empirical claim. It preserves the predecessor simulator family: 16 cells, 1,200 evaluator-call budget, `rho_A=rho_B=.6`, three A calls before escalation, up to four B calls, A/B class-conditional error regimes, and the predecessor 64-sample fresh-A opportunity proxy.

The experiment adds two changes requested by the previous frontier:

1. A pre-materialized common-randomness stream generates proposal-cell choices, proposal mixture draws, evaluator latent uniforms, lock uniforms, and probe uniforms before policy decisions. Different policies still consume different numbers of evaluator calls and therefore can terminate at different points in the common proposal stream; this reduces but does not eliminate paired-path variance.
2. A myopic VoI controller estimates the reduction in future routing regret from obtaining one A-only opportunity label. It uses the current opportunity mean/variance, a small deterministic quadrature over possible true opportunity values and labels, and a remaining-horizon multiplier `H`. It probes only if:

`H × remaining_budget_fraction × expected_future_routing_regret_reduction > immediate_lost_B_EVSI + 0.1 × estimated_proposal_value + controller_cost_proxy`.

The `0.1 × proposal value` and `5e-5` controller-cost terms are **heuristic exchange rates**, not physically calibrated costs. This is deliberately a first falsification pass, not a claim that the rule is optimal.

Main pilot: 30 paired seeds per condition. `±` is the paired 95% half-width versus no-probe. Because `n=30`, small differences are not treated as established effects.

The conservative conclusion is **negative**. The first explicit myopic VoI rule did not establish an endpoint-quality improvement over no-probe or the simpler target05 controller. It often reduced the oracle-relative routing-regret proxy substantially, but achieved that by forcing many more A-only probes and cutting B calls. In the weak, independent (`cross_mix=0`) symmetric-judge regime, all tested VoI multipliers produced a sizeable endpoint loss; for `H=4`, the paired pilot difference was `-0.00803 ±0.00509`. This is the clearest falsification signal in the run.

The result separates two objectives that had been partially conflated:

- **local routing-regret relative to an oracle threshold** can improve;
- **final archive quality under a hard global evaluator budget** can simultaneously worsen.

Therefore reducing local routing disagreement is not sufficient evidence that an information-acquisition policy is globally useful. The current VoI approximation overprices repeated future routing decisions and underprices the archive-level consequence of suppressing B calls in weak-evaluator regimes.

The remaining-horizon multiplier is also not behaving like a stable transferable hyperparameter. Larger `H` monotonically increased probing and generally reduced B calls, but endpoint quality did not improve monotonically. This is evidence against treating an assumed future decision count as a simple scalar multiplier without calibrating which future states the acquired label actually generalizes to.

Rare low-incumbent calibration remains poor in this family: mean absolute error against the 64-sample opportunity proxy was roughly `0.055–0.060 quality/call` across policies in the audited low-incumbent states. VoI probing reduced this only weakly. The high-value rare-state calibration problem from C69/C73 therefore remains unresolved.

## C76 — fresh public evidence supports adaptive judge allocation, but not the current VoI formula

Two public sources observed before the semantic-freeze stop are directionally relevant:

1. Xu et al., **“Ask a Strong LLM Judge when Your Reward Model is Uncertain”** (arXiv:2510.20369, 2025). It routes uncertain preference pairs from a cheap reward model to a stronger expensive LLM judge and reports better cost-matched behavior than random judge calling. This supports selective escalation, but its routing signal is uncertainty in an RLHF reward-model setting, not archive-level opportunity VoI.
2. Saha, Wagde & Kveton, **“LLM-as-Judge on a Budget”** (arXiv:2602.15481, 2026). It allocates repeated judge queries adaptively according to estimated score variance and derives budget-dependent estimation guarantees. This supports nonuniform query allocation under a fixed budget, but optimizes score-estimation error rather than search/archive utility.

A third fresh production-oriented source, Kong et al., **“The Lifecycle of LLM-as-a-Judge for Large-Scale Recommendation Explanations”** (arXiv:2608.18300, 2026-08-18), emphasizes continuous monitoring and drift-triggered maintenance for production judges. That reinforces the frontier's nonstationarity concern but does not validate the synthetic controller.

The narrow transferable principle remains: **adaptive evaluation needs an objective aligned with the downstream decision, not merely uncertainty or local estimation error**.

## Scope and limitations

- C75 is synthetic; it does not measure real LLM agents, real judge calls, or a real QD archive.
- Only 30 paired seeds per condition were completed; the pilot is intentionally underpowered for small effects.
- The common-randomness stream is pre-materialized per proposal event, but policy-specific evaluator-call consumption changes how far each policy advances through that stream.
- The VoI model assumes a local opportunity distribution and deterministic small quadrature; it is not a calibrated Bayesian posterior.
- The immediate-cost terms are heuristic and dimension matching is approximate.
- The 64-sample oracle is a diagnostic proxy, not ground truth.
- Low-incumbent states remain sparse and poorly calibrated.
- Public judge-routing papers support their own objectives only; they do not establish the synthetic archive effects.

## Frontier (nonempty)

1. Replace the scalar future-horizon multiplier with an **empirical generalization footprint**: estimate which future cell×incumbent states a new opportunity label actually changes, and value only those routing decisions.
2. Measure **archive-level counterfactual VoI** directly on a small exact state space: clone the current archive/controller state, branch `probe` vs `route-B`, replay a fixed short common-randomness suffix, and estimate downstream archive gain per consumed call. Use this expensive estimator only as a teacher/diagnostic, not as the production policy.
3. Train a lightweight online predictor of that counterfactual archive-level VoI from completed teacher branches, then compare it with local routing-regret VoI under the same 1,200-call budget.
4. Explicitly constrain probe rate or B-suppression rate and test whether the weak-symmetric failure disappears while preserving the asymmetric routing-regret gains.
5. Improve rare-state coverage with state-weighted diagnostics rather than blind probing; do not force random probes solely to populate low-incumbent bins.
6. Add mid-run evaluator/proposal drift and test sliding-window/discounted opportunity models against cumulative statistics.
7. Move to a real repeated-judge dataset with human labels and per-call costs when a suitable public trace is found.
