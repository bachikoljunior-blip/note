Interpretation:
- pooled marginal recall is a dangerous control signal under surface shift. It gives the best average throughput here by accepting a 6.05% stale-run rate;
- per-surface conditional complement correctly treats the unseen `custom_wrapper` as unsafe and reaches 100% recovery on that subset, but still accepts a small residual failure rate on empirically high-but-incomplete `dynamic_tool`;
- positive-proof-only routing is the only non-whole policy with 100% recovery in this scoped model because it distinguishes a measured high success rate from a completeness contract;
- the price of positive proof is broader replay. The useful optimization target is therefore not “maximize graph recall,” but **prove completeness where possible and spend replay budget only where proof is absent**.

The main mechanism update is that `runtime recall`, `static recall`, and even `P(static hit | runtime miss)` remain statistical quantities. They can improve routing, but they are not substitutes for a positive capture-scope/closure-completeness contract when destructive state replacement requires fail-closed safety.
