## C50 — matched-budget synthetic class-conditional archive-decision noise sweep

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. It was run to directly instantiate the `q_FA/q_FR` archive-decision noise question that remains absent from the located QD literature.

### Controlled setup

- 16 archive cells.
- Exactly 1,200 evaluator calls per replicate, 100 replicates per setting.
- Each cell has a heterogeneous proposal distribution. New challengers are generated 55% by broad independent Beta sampling and 45% by a local mutation around the current authoritative incumbent (`incumbent + Normal(0.04,0.18)`, clipped to `[0,1]`).
- Binary evaluator: if the challenger is truly better than the authoritative incumbent, it passes with probability `1-q_FR`; if it is not truly better, it passes with probability `q_FA`.
- `rho=0` gives fresh independent reevaluations. `rho=0.6` is an intentionally adverse correlated-evaluator model: each challenger has 60% probability that every reevaluation simply repeats its first verdict. This violates the fresh-evidence assumption and is used to test failure sensitivity.

Three policies share the same total evaluator-call budget:

1. **one-shot permanent replacement** — one verdict; pass immediately replaces the authoritative incumbent.
2. **two-level reversible provisional** — authoritative incumbent plus one provisional challenger; first pass creates a provisional, a second verdict on a later cell visit is required for promotion.
3. **fresh-evaluation e-process gate** — likelihood-ratio e-value with `p0=q_FA`, `p1=1-q_FR`; promote at `E>=10` (`alpha=0.1`), drop the pending challenger at `E<=0.1` or after 6 evaluations. This deliberately assumes the synthetic noise rates are known and fresh verdicts are conditionally valid; it is not a deployable calibration recipe by itself.

- `q=0.20`: provisional `+0.141 ±0.016`; e-process `+0.153 ±0.014`.

It averages only ~207 proposals and ~6.6 true promotions, versus ~808 proposals / ~129 true promotions for the reversible provisional policy. **Statistical caution can become search starvation.**

Correlation materially erodes the value of repeated verification because repeated verdicts cease to contribute independent information. Paired e-process advantage over one-shot falls from `+0.127/+0.153/+0.140` at `q=0.1/0.2/0.3` under fresh evidence to only `+0.045/+0.050/+0.056` under `rho=0.6`; at `q=0.4` it becomes negative (`-0.026 ±0.016`). This is a concrete synthetic warning against treating repeated same-judge calls as independent evidence.

### Asymmetric false-admission / false-rejection

Independent reevaluations (`rho=0`):

- Low false-admission / high false-rejection, `q_FA=0.10, q_FR=0.30`: one-shot `0.838`, provisional **`0.963`**, e-process `0.939`.
- High false-admission / low false-rejection, `q_FA=0.30, q_FR=0.10`: one-shot `0.715`, provisional `0.841`, e-process **`0.917`**.

This synthetic model makes the asymmetry operationally visible: when false admission is the dominant error, a strong promotion gate is highly valuable; when false rejection dominates and false admission is already low, the cheaper reversible provisional policy can beat the stricter gate because it preserves more proposal throughput. Therefore a controller should not collapse evaluator quality to one scalar “error rate.” It should estimate or bound **false-admit and false-reject separately** and allocate verification by the irreversible consequence of each error.

### What this does and does not establish

Supported within this synthetic setup:

- One-shot permanent elitism is fragile to class-conditional evaluator error.
- A small reversible provisional layer gives a broad robust gain over one-shot across the tested settings while consuming less exploration budget than strict sequential evidence.
- Sequential evidence can dominate at moderate independent error, especially when false admission is high.
- Sequential evidence can catastrophically under-explore when the judge is weak enough or the stopping rule is too strict.
- Correlated repeated judgments substantially reduce the benefit of repeated verification.

Not established:

- No claim that `alpha=0.1`, 6 evaluations, one provisional slot, or any tested threshold is optimal.
- The experiment is not a full MAP-Elites reproduction, does not model descriptor noise, and does not calibrate any real LLM judge.
- The e-value validity here relies on the deliberately simple known Bernoulli model; unknown/nonstationary/correlated real judge errors require a different validity construction.
- Quality values are synthetic and should not be compared numerically to external QD benchmarks.

## Updated controller hypothesis

The strongest current synthesis is now a **three-state promotion controller** rather than a binary admit/reject rule:

1. broad cheap proposal;
2. reversible provisional / Standby state that preserves exploration and can be revisited;
3. irreversible authoritative promotion only after evidence whose strength scales with estimated `q_FA`, correlation, and consequence.

Crucially, evidence allocation must have an explicit **search-starvation guard**. If verifier reliability is too low for a strong gate to reach decisions within budget, the controller should widen/retain provisional capacity, change evaluator, or abstain rather than silently consume nearly all proposal budget.
