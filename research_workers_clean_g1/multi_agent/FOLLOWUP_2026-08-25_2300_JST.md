## C26 — Under fixed evaluation budgets, noisy evolutionary search can benefit more from preserving search depth and integrating uncertainty at selection time than from spending many evaluations on denoising each generation

Primary source: Sichen Wang, Zhipeng Lu, *Depth over Fidelity in Fixed-Budget Noisy Evolution Strategies*, ICML 2026 / PMLR 306 / arXiv:2606.06555. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06555 ; full author-hosted paper: https://sichen-wang.github.io/publications/depth-over-fidelity/

This is direct peer-reviewed evolutionary-search evidence for the same exploration-versus-verification trade-off identified in C24, but with a different mechanism: rather than selectively re-evaluating archive elites, it keeps per-generation cost low and integrates ranking uncertainty into selection weights.

### Mechanism

Rank-based evolution strategies are vulnerable to noisy evaluations because misranking changes which candidates receive elite weight. Uniform `k`-fold resampling reduces observation noise but multiplies per-generation evaluation cost and therefore reduces the number of adaptive distribution updates completed under a fixed oracle-call budget.

The paper proposes Probabilistic Elite Membership (PEM): replace hard rank weights from one noisy ordering with conditional expected rank weights. Residual-bootstrapped PEM (RB-PEM) estimates these expected weights using a small capped reevaluation set plus an amortized residual bank reused across generations. A low-cost misranking probe then chooses between RB-PEM and vanilla CMA-ES (`Probe-and-Switch`).

### Matched-budget quantitative evidence

Across the main COCO comparison and six external tasks, Probe-and-Switch beats evaluation-stage denoising baselines strongly under fixed budgets. Aggregate pairwise win rates are:

- vs fixed Resample(`k=10`): **85.6%** (`1413/237` W/L)
- vs fixed Resample(`k=5`): **79.3%** (`1307/341`)
- vs UH-CMA-ES: **77.4%** (`1276/373`)
- vs vanilla CMA-ES: **54.3%** (`865/728`)
- vs always-on RB-PEM: **58.1%** (`954/688`)

Protocol: 30 COCO bbob-noisy functions × 15 instances at `d∈{10,20,40}`, `B=200d`, plus six external tasks with 50 seeds each. The comparison therefore does not buy accuracy merely by increasing total oracle calls.

A separate UH-CMA-ES sensitivity sweep uses `maxevals∈{1,10,30}` at `d=40`, budgets `B∈{200d,500d}`, 30 functions × 15 instances. Increasing `maxevals` monotonically *reduces* UH-CMA-ES win rate, supporting the depth-loss interpretation rather than a poor one-off hyperparameter choice.

### Adaptive verification/routing is more important than an always-on expensive mode

Always-on RB-PEM wins against CMA-ES on the selected high-misranking half of COCO problems **68.1% (460/675)** but only **35.3% (238/675)** on the complement. Probe-and-Switch retains **65.2% (440/675)** on the high-misranking subset while recovering to **47.4% (320/675)** on the complement by reverting to vanilla CMA-ES when ranks are stable.

The paper's rank-disagreement probe shows a broad robust operating region rather than requiring brittle tuning. On held-out COCO instances, empirical `Pr(RB-PEM wins | probe P)` increases monotonically with rank-disagreement; at high `P`, RB-PEM wins around **0.7–0.8**. Threshold sensitivity is broad: routing accuracy changes only about **2 percentage points** over `τ∈[0.08,0.18]`.

Probe budget also shows diminishing returns. Population sizes `λ∈{4,8,16,32}` correspond to probe cost `2λ`. Even `λ=4` (8 evaluations) gives **AUC 0.71, accuracy 0.72**; performance improves to about **AUC 0.76 at λ≈16**, then drops to **0.73 at λ=32**. More measurement is therefore not automatically more useful.

A key diagnostic ablation shows that generic variance is the wrong trigger when what matters is selection instability. Under state-dependent radial noise, a variance probe triggers on **0/54** problems while rank-disagreement triggers on **49/54**; rank-disagreement-based switching beats variance-based switching on **39/54** problems (`p=0.0007`).

On a nonconvex digits0 MLP task with identical total budget `B=40d=10,600` and 50 seeds, moderate noise (`batch=16`) gives Probe-and-Switch **66%** win rate over CMA-ES and a warmstart variant **76%**. Under extreme noise (`batch=4`) both are near parity at **54%**, suggesting a saturation region where all methods become noise-limited; under deterministic evaluation (`batch=256`) warmstart produces **48/50 ties (96%)** with CMA-ES.

### Transfer hypothesis

For population / multi-agent search, the strongest transferable controller principle is:

`measure decision-relevant ranking instability -> preserve search depth -> integrate uncertainty into selection -> spend extra verification only when the expected decision value exceeds the lost exploration depth`.

This sharpens C24. Verification-share should not be controlled only by raw evaluator variance or confidence. A better trigger is **probability that evaluation uncertainty changes survival/archive/routing decisions**.

Scope limits:

- Noise here is black-box objective noise, not semantic LLM-judge hallucination or correlated judge bias.
- RB-PEM is an evolution-strategy mechanism, not directly a multi-agent language-model workflow.
- The probe threshold `τ=0.12` is empirically robust in this study but is not a universal constant.
- Extreme-noise near-parity shows uncertainty integration cannot rescue a regime where all candidate comparisons are effectively uninformative.

## C27 — A 2026 LLM-native QD system independently adopts buffered evaluation plus periodic elite re-evaluation, but its main ablation does not isolate the re-evaluation mechanism

Primary source: Dongxin Guo, Jikun Wu, Siu Ming Yiu, *Parameter-Efficient Neuroevolution for Diverse LLM Generation: Quality-Diversity Optimization via Prompt Embedding Evolution*, arXiv:2605.09781 / GECCO 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09781

QD-LLM is directly LLM-native Quality-Diversity search. Its archive-management layer explicitly imports uncertain-QD ideas: each archive entry keeps a buffer of **k=3 evaluations**, archive comparisons use **median fitness**, and the algorithm **re-evaluates 10% of elites** periodically.

The authors evaluate with a common generation budget `B=500` across eight baselines. On HumanEval, QD-LLM reports QD-Score **26.3±0.9** and coverage **0.41±0.02**, versus QDAIF **18.6±1.0 / 0.28±0.02** and adapted CMA-ME **19.8±0.9 / 0.30±0.02**. They report +41.4% QD-Score and +46.4% coverage versus QDAIF (`p<0.001`, 30 runs, Vargha-Delaney `A=0.94`). On MBPP the reported QD-Scores are 58.7 vs 42.1 (+39.4%, `p<0.001`).

For creative writing, fitness uses GPT-4-as-judge and was compared with 300 human annotations; the paper reports Spearman `ρ=0.84` and Cohen's `κ=0.76` inter-annotator agreement. This is relevant because the archive is being maintained under an explicitly model-mediated quality signal rather than only deterministic code tests.

However, the published HumanEval ablation table removes behavior-characterization components, prompt evolution, crossover and targeted mutation, but **does not isolate buffered evaluation or the 10% elite re-evaluation policy**. Therefore the overall QD-LLM gain cannot be attributed quantitatively to that uncertainty-handling layer.

This is still useful adjacent evidence: a modern LLM-QD design independently converges on `small evaluation buffer + median aggregation + partial elite re-evaluation`, consistent with C24/C26. But it does **not** establish that 10% is optimal, necessary, or better than uncertainty-triggered extraction.

Scope limits:

- HumanEval code fitness is binary pass@1, whereas creative-writing fitness is GPT-4-judge based; judge-noise conclusions must not be transferred from code results without qualification.
- The common `B=500` generation budget does not by itself prove identical total LLM-call cost across methods because targeted mutation uses additional forward passes; the paper separately reports cost-efficiency, but the re-evaluation component is not independently budget-matched in ablation.
- The public algorithm description says 10% elite re-evaluation, but no matched 0/5/10/25% sweep was found in this run.

## Updated synthesis

C24–C27 now support a more specific adaptive-control model for multi-agent / population search:

1. **Preserve search depth explicitly.** Repeatedly denoising every candidate can lose more from fewer adaptive updates than it gains from cleaner local rankings.
2. **Trigger verification on decision instability, not generic noise magnitude.** Rank disagreement predicted when uncertainty-aware selection helped; a variance proxy failed under state-dependent noise.
3. **Use small probes and stop increasing verification once information saturates.** Probe AUC improved from λ=4 to ≈16 but degraded by λ=32.
4. **Use an always-cheap path plus an uncertainty-aware path.** Always-on uncertainty handling helps high-misranking cases but harms stable cases; adaptive switching recovers most high-noise gain while avoiding much low-noise overhead.
5. **Treat extreme-noise saturation as a distinct failure mode.** At sufficiently severe noise, extra sophistication can return to parity because the available observations contain too little ranking information.
6. **LLM-native QD already uses partial elite re-evaluation, but the precise share/policy remains unvalidated.** The next useful experiment is not another demonstration that re-evaluation can help; it is a matched policy/share ablation.

A plausible controller state therefore includes at least:

- estimated rank-instability / survival-decision flip probability;
- remaining evaluation budget and expected lost search depth from verification;
- candidate/archive decision reversibility;
- per-candidate uncertainty/reliability;
- whether the current regime is informative, stable, or noise-saturated.

## Rejected / downgraded hypotheses

- `Higher observation variance is sufficient to decide when to spend verification budget` is rejected by the radial-noise ablation; decision-relevant misranking can be high while a variance probe at the reference point is effectively zero.
- `More probe/evaluator calls monotonically improve routing` is rejected; probe quality saturates around λ≈16 and is worse at λ=32 in the reported sweep.
- `Always-on uncertainty handling is safe` is rejected; RB-PEM wins only 35.3% against CMA-ES on the stable/complement group while winning 68.1% on the selected high-misranking group.
- `Extra reevaluation can always rescue noisy evolution` is rejected; increasing UH-CMA-ES maxevals monotonically worsens fixed-budget performance, and extreme-noise MLP search remains near parity.
- `QD-LLM's 10% elite re-evaluation is proven optimal/necessary` is rejected; its published ablation does not isolate this component.
