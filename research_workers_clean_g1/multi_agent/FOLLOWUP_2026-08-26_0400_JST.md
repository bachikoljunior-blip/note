## C40 — Uncertain QD gives a direct archive-corruption metric and shows why naive elitism amplifies lucky false admissions

Primary source: Manon Flageat and Antoine Cully, *Uncertain Quality-Diversity: Evaluation methodology and new methods for Quality-Diversity in Uncertain Domains*, IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation 28(4), 2024; arXiv:2302.00463v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463

This is not an LLM-judge paper, but it directly studies MAP-Elites archives under noisy fitness/descriptor evaluations and therefore advances the exact archive-corruption frontier. Standard MAP-Elites evaluates offspring once and preserves apparent elites. Under uncertainty, single evaluations can be lucky outliers; because the archive is elitist, those lucky outliers survive. The paper states that final collections can contain a majority of lucky sub-performing solutions rather than truly high-performing/novel ones.

The paper provides a standardized archive-level corruption diagnostic: build a **Corrected Archive** by heavily reevaluating each archived solution and recomputing its expected fitness/descriptor, then measure **QD-Score loss = (training QD-Score - Corrected QD-Score) / training QD-Score**. Their final evaluation uses the median of `M=512` reevaluations per cell for the corrected estimate. This is useful for the current frontier because it quantifies how much the online archive overstates itself after noisy admissions, even though it does not by itself label individual false-admit/false-reject events.

Under matched per-generation **sampling-size** rather than raw offspring count, the study runs 1120 main comparison runs across Arm, Hexapod, Walker and Ant. MAP-Elites is consistently the worst method on QD-Score loss; even Random-MAP-Elites is significantly better except on Ant. The mechanism is revealing: ordinary MAP-Elites repeatedly searches near current elites until similar solutions receive lucky evaluations, whereas random search does not repeatedly exploit the same noisy neighborhood. In Walker, Random-MAP-Elites even gets a significantly better Corrected QD-Score than MAP-Elites (`p<1e-2`), illustrating selection-driven noise amplification rather than merely noisy measurements.

The paper also gives mechanisms that are close to explicit false-admission control. Adaptive-Sampling only considers a challenger for archive insertion after it has been evaluated as many times as the incumbent elite; unpromising challengers can be discarded early. When an elite is challenged, the elite itself is reevaluated; elites whose descriptor repeatedly falls outside their cell can be moved or discarded. Archive-Sampling periodically reevaluates the archive and reinserts elites using moving-average estimates. Deep-Grid instead weakens irrevocable elitism by storing depth `D` per cell and treating every occupant as questionable/replaceable.

The matched-budget negative result matters: simply reevaluating every newcomer with fixed `N` is not automatically optimal. MAP-Elites-sampling can lose exploration and is even dominated by vanilla MAP-Elites at small sampling-size in some tasks. In other words, reducing false admissions by brute-force repeated evaluation can create a **false-rejection/search-starvation** failure mode. This directly supports tracking both error directions rather than only archive overestimation.

### Transfer to LLM-agent/QD control

For an LLM-agent archive, a concrete measurement layer should separate:

- `archive_overstatement`: online QD/utility score minus corrected/deep-verified score;
- `false_admit`: challenger inserted online but rejected by later deep verification;
- `false_reject`: challenger rejected online but later deep verification would have admitted it;
- `elite_drift`: accepted candidate later maps to a different niche/behavior class;
- `true_elite_survival`: fraction of corrected elites preserved online;
- `exploration_starvation`: useful-candidate discovery lost because too much budget is spent on repeated checks.

The UQD results directly justify corrected-archive and reevaluation-based diagnostics, but class-conditional false-admit/false-reject must still be added explicitly for LLM judge corruption.

### Scope limits

- Noise is environmental/fitness/descriptor uncertainty, not an LLM judge with semantic systematic bias.
- The paper does not inject separate false-pass/false-fail channels at specified percentages.
- QD-Score loss is an aggregate archive-estimation metric, not a per-decision confusion matrix.
- The `M=512` corrected evaluation budget is for measurement, not a recommended online verification budget.

## C41 — Global error budgets across adaptive archive updates are statistically plausible, but local anytime-valid gates can become invalid under archive feedback

Primary sources:

1. Ziyu Xu and Aaditya Ramdas, *Online multiple testing with e-values*, AISTATS 2024, PMLR 238:3997-4005. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/xu24a.html
2. Hongjian Wang, Sanjit Dandapanthula and Aaditya Ramdas, *Anytime-valid FDR control with the stopped e-BH procedure*, Statistics & Probability Letters 226 (2025), arXiv:2502.08539v3. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08539

The unresolved frontier asks how to allocate error across hundreds of adaptive archive writes rather than giving every write an independent `alpha`. Online multiple-testing methods provide a candidate mathematical template. e-LOND controls FDR for an unbounded stream of hypotheses using e-values and is designed to tolerate arbitrary, possibly unknown cross-hypothesis dependence. This is conceptually closer to a long-running archive than a fixed one-shot significance test because decisions arrive indefinitely and later thresholds can depend on earlier outcomes.

However, the stopped e-BH result exposes a critical failure mode for directly transplanting per-candidate anytime-valid tests into adaptive search. A stopped e-process is an e-value for stopping times in its **local filtration**, but a global archive controller observes many streams and chooses actions/stopping times using the combined history. The authors show that applying stopped e-BH at a global stopping time is not valid in full generality: information can leak across streams/time. They provide a causal condition under which local e-processes are also global e-processes and FDR control is restored.

This is highly relevant to QD/self-evolution because the next candidate is generated from the current archive, and the archive itself depends on previous accept/reject outcomes. The candidate tests are therefore not a passive fixed stream. A controller cannot safely say `each candidate has an anytime-valid p/e-process, so repeated archive updates are globally safe` without proving the relevant filtration/predictability conditions.

### Design implication

A defensible archive-wide error controller should make the chronology explicit:

1. Freeze the candidate and decision question using only information available before its new evaluation evidence.
2. Construct a candidate-local e-process/test whose betting/test rule is predictable from the allowed past.
3. Record exactly what global history the candidate generator and evaluator observed.
4. Apply an online FDR/FWER rule only if its dependence/filtration assumptions are satisfied by that generation/evaluation process.
5. If candidate generation adaptively reuses evaluation outcomes in a way that violates those assumptions, treat the nominal global error guarantee as unsupported rather than silently reusing alpha.

This suggests that an archive-wide `error wealth` ledger could be useful, but the proof obligation is on the **generation/evaluation filtration**, not only on the numerical alpha allocation.

### Scope limits

- e-LOND/se-BH are statistical multiple-testing procedures, not QD algorithms.
- No result here proves that QD archive admissions are valid hypotheses/e-values under adaptive candidate generation.
- FDR control bounds the fraction of false discoveries under specified assumptions; it does not directly optimize search regret, diversity or stepping-stone preservation.
- This is a transfer candidate for controller design, not direct evidence of improved evolutionary LLM search.

## C42 — Loreley public artifact supports an exact deterministic selection-corruption target at the eligibility boundary

Primary public artifact: NeapolitanIcecream/loreley, `paper/evidence/build_zstd_formal_records.py` and `paper/evidence/zstd_formal_records.json`. https://github.com/NeapolitanIcecream/loreley

This run refined the previously proposed public replay to a deterministic decision boundary. The released builder defines a public `validation_eligible` rule for non-root finalists:

- validation `passed == true`;
- `compression_lower_95 > 1.0`;
- `decompression_geomean >= 0.995`;
- `worst_cell_speedup >= 0.98`.

Among eligible finalists, the winner is chosen lexicographically by highest compression lower bound, then root/non-root preference, smaller diff, commit and identity. Because this exact rule and the frozen finalist/evaluation identities are public, synthetic corruption can be injected **after the clean metric computation at the eligibility-decision layer** without inventing hidden candidate source or prompts.

A clean public-data-only replay can therefore define, for each of the 126 finalist groups:

- clean eligibility vector and clean winner;
- false-admit channel: flip a clean-ineligible finalist to eligible with probability `q_fp`;
- false-reject channel: flip a clean-eligible finalist to ineligible with probability `q_fn`;
- symmetric channel: set `q_fp=q_fn=p` for `p in {0,.05,.10,.20,.30,.40,.50}`;
- optional block-correlated channel: one latent Bernoulli corruption state shared by finalists in the same block;
- rerun the published deterministic winner rule using the unchanged public selection score/tie-break fields.

Report winner-identity error, selected-clean-ineligible rate, missed-clean-winner rate, root-fallback distortion, and holdout regret where the corrupted winner has a released holdout evaluation. This is now a precise **selection-layer** noise experiment; it still must not be called a full online archive replay because public artifacts omit the generation/parent/archive feedback trajectory.

### Scope limits

- Eligibility flipping is a synthetic decision-layer corruption model. It does not reproduce metric-level judge hallucinations unless those are separately modeled.
- Holdout regret is only measurable when the selected corrupted winner has a released holdout evaluation.
- The replay cannot recover hidden online candidate-generation/archive dynamics.

## Updated synthesis

1. Direct QD literature already provides a strong archive-corruption measurement pattern: corrected archive + normalized QD-score loss + reproducibility metrics. This is closer to the target than endpoint-only task scores.
2. Naive elitism amplifies lucky evaluations because repeated local exploitation creates repeated chances for false admission. Random exploration can sometimes look better after correction despite being less optimized online.
3. Brute-force repeated verification is not free: fixed newcomer resampling can suppress exploration, so false-admit reduction must be balanced against false-reject/search-starvation.
4. Global error-budget control is possible in indefinite statistical streams, but adaptive archive feedback creates a filtration problem. Per-candidate anytime-validity does not automatically compose into archive-wide validity.
5. Loreley's public selection rule is now precise enough for a reproducible synthetic false-admit/false-reject sweep at the eligibility boundary, while the full archive-feedback claim remains excluded.

## Rejected / downgraded hypotheses

- `No direct QD archive-level metric exists for noise-induced archive corruption` — rejected. Corrected Archive and QD-Score loss directly measure online archive overstatement after reevaluation.
- `More newcomer reevaluations monotonically improve QD under fixed budget` — rejected by MAP-Elites-sampling exploration losses.
- `Anytime-valid candidate tests can simply be unioned or globally stopped inside an adaptive archive` — rejected without a global-filtration/dependence argument.
- `Loreley corruption must perturb raw metrics to be meaningful` — downgraded. The public eligibility decision itself is a deterministic, auditable corruption target; metric-level corruption is optional and requires stronger assumptions.
