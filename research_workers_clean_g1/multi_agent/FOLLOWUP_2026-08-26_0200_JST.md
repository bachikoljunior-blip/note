## C34 — PACE directly quantifies false-commit drift in a self-evolving prompt loop and shows the acceptor can dominate reliability

Primary source: Zayx Shawn, *PACE: Anytime-Valid Acceptance Tests for Self-Evolving Agents*, arXiv:2606.08106, 2026-06-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08106

PACE reframes self-evolution acceptance as sequential hypothesis testing rather than `keep candidate if noisy dev score rises`. Each proposed prompt modification is compared with the incumbent on the same instances; a testing-by-betting e-process accumulates evidence and commits only after crossing an anytime-valid threshold, so optional stopping does not invalidate the per-candidate false-commit control.

### Quantitative evidence

On Qwen2.5 agents from 0.5B to 3B evolving prompts on GSM8K, SVAMP and ARC-Challenge:

- greedy score-improvement acceptance commits **30–42% false edits** and **10–33% harmful edits** when a genuine improvement is mixed with noisy proposals;
- PACE commits the genuine improvement and almost none of the false ones, while matching greedy held-out accuracy with lower variance and about **18% lower evaluation cost**;
- when no true improvement exists, greedy makes **13–21 spurious self-modifications per run**, of which **72–100% are false**, and the most fragile tested agent loses **4.9 accuracy points**; PACE stays at baseline.

This is closer to the current frontier than generic judge calibration because it measures the downstream consequence of noisy acceptance *inside a repeated self-evolution loop*: false persistence, harmful persistence, churn and final task degradation.

### Transfer hypothesis for population/QD search

Archive replacement and permanent lineage pruning are repeated adaptive acceptance tests. A population controller should therefore not reuse a small noisy dev estimate with a naive `delta > 0` rule. For irreversible transitions, use a paired sequential/anytime-valid acceptor or an equivalent error-budgeted gate; cheap noisy scores can remain navigation signals, but persistence must require stronger evidence.

### Scope limits

- PACE evolves prompts, not a full MAP-Elites archive or multi-island population.
- The reported noise arises from small held-out evaluation and repeated adaptive testing; the paper does not inject a controlled 0–50% LLM-judge flip rate.
- The guarantee is per decision under the stated paired null/interface; it is not a global guarantee against all distribution shift, evaluator misspecification or correlated systematic bias.
- Evidence is one Qwen2.5 model family and one proposer setup; cross-system validation remains future work.

## C35 — GSME provides a second direct self-evolution failure measurement: a single-run improvement gate can manufacture phantom wins about 60% of the time

Primary source: Xiaotian Luo, Fengxingyu Wang, Chuanrui Hu, Dizhan Xue, Yafeng Deng, *Self-Evolving Agent Harnesses via Gated Semantic Quality-Diversity*, arXiv:2607.13683, 2026-07-15. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13683

The framework separates proposal from credit. An LLM diagnoses pathologies and proposes harness patches, but deterministic code owns validity checks, activation checks, paired significance testing and persistent archive writes. The search archive is categorical QD over `(where × why)` pathology cells with gated elites and cross-cell recombination.

### Failure/ablation evidence

The paper explicitly asks whether a deterministic `mean improves` rule is enough. On TB2 candidate mechanisms, a single-run gate credits a truly neutral mechanism as a win **about 60% of the time**, and about **25%** of runs make that neutral mechanism look like a gain of at least **3 percentage points**. In the tested candidate set, train-time non-statistical acceptance can therefore create substantial phantom gains; paired significance and activation gates remove false credit paths.

The same system shows that diagnosis may itself be wrong without poisoning persistence: on AppWorld it diagnosed a knowledge gap, proposed a knowledge injection, and the measurement gate rejected the candidate. This is a useful architecture principle: `diagnosis proposes, measurement disposes`.

For six credited domains, the train-selected harness is evaluated once on a sealed test and shows held-out gains of **+9 to +15.5 pp**, with retained train lift of **86–147%**. A seventh SWE-bench result improves +5.1 pp held-out but does not clear the predeclared paired significance bar (`z=0.78`) and is explicitly not credited. On four of six credited domains the selected harness is a cross-cell recombination, so the QD archive demonstrably exposes stepping-stone combinations, though there is no matched naive task-keyed or no-archive control proving the archive causes the final gain.

### Transfer hypothesis

For evolutionary agent systems, proposal semantics and persistence authority should be separated. LLM diagnosis/critique can direct mutations, but archive writes should be owned by deterministic or statistically controlled gates with activation/provenance checks. A wrong semantic hypothesis should cost a rejected candidate, not contaminate the durable population.

### Scope limits

- The ~60% phantom-win figure is single-run evaluation noise in this harness setting, not an injected judge-output noise sweep.
- Several domains use deterministic verifiers, while Omni-MATH/BrowseComp+ use LLM judging; the paper does not isolate which evaluator type contributes how much noise.
- The archive evidence is mechanistic (cross-cell recombination appears in selected harnesses), not a matched-budget proof that GSME beats simpler search.
- The sealed test is used for final credit in the reported study; direct reuse of that exact protocol in an online archive would require preserving held-out integrity and avoiding repeated test peeking.

## C36 — Loreley supplies a public, replayable repository-QD scaffold and matched-budget negative evidence: QD engaged stepping stones but did not beat sequential champion search at 48 jobs

Primary source: Mohan Chen, *Loreley: Repository-Scale Program Evolution with Quality-Diversity Search*, arXiv:2608.19703, 2026-08-20. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19703

Public code/evidence: https://github.com/NeapolitanIcecream/loreley and `paper/evidence/zstd_method_efficacy.json` in that repository.

Loreley evolves complete Git repository states with planning/coding agents, a project-supplied evaluator, persistent ancestry, candidate identity and a MAP-Elites-style archive. It is directly useful for the unresolved future-noise-replay branch because evaluation is external to the agent proposal, candidate identities are durable, and the experiment evidence is public and machine-readable.

### Matched-budget controlled result

The Zstandard comparison holds the root and physical candidate-job budget fixed across three policies: Loreley QD, Sequential Champion and Independent Root. It uses **7 paired blocks × 48 jobs per policy = 1,008 total candidate jobs**, with validation-selection checkpoints at 8/16/24/32/40/48 jobs and agent-hidden holdout evaluation of the fixed winner.

At 48 jobs:

- QD versus Sequential Champion: **−0.1346%** effect, 95% BCa interval roughly **[−0.556%, +0.161%]**, adjusted comparison non-significant;
- QD versus Independent Root: **+0.3204%**, interval roughly **[−0.082%, +0.686%]**, also not establishing superiority;
- Sequential Champion has the highest observed endpoint mean and median in the seven blocks.

The stepping-stone mechanism nevertheless fires: four of seven final QD winners have a non-incumbent primary-parent state in ancestry under the paper's retrospective one-incumbent analysis; including inspiration edges raises this to six of seven. This is evidence of *mechanism engagement*, not evidence that the mechanism caused better endpoint quality.

### Why this is a strong future noise-replay scaffold

The public experiment contract already includes:

- fixed source root and explicit candidate-job budgets;
- deterministic Git ancestry and evaluator-defined artifact identity;
- correctness gates and structured numerical metrics;
- repeated paired benchmark rounds with a precision-extension rule;
- disjoint training, validation and holdout corpora;
- fixed selection rules and public machine-readable evidence;
- an evaluator-fit requirement that explicitly calls for runtime/noise compatible with the effect size.

A future synthetic corruption experiment could therefore replay identical candidate streams while perturbing only the acceptance/evaluator layer (for example controlled sign flips, biased score offsets, correlated block-level errors, or false-pass/false-fail gates) and measure archive corruption, winner survival, ancestry loss and endpoint regret under a fixed evaluation budget. No such corruption experiment was run here; this is feasibility evidence only.

### Scope limits

- The controlled endpoint is Zstandard throughput on a specific ARM64 host and evaluator; it is not a general result that QD is worse than sequential search.
- Equal physical candidate jobs do not imply identical wall-clock or token usage because native concurrency differs.
- The experiment evaluates project-supplied numerical performance, not an LLM-as-judge subjective objective.
- The public evidence enables a future replay but does not itself provide the requested 0–50% injected judge-noise sweep.

## Updated synthesis

The reliability picture is now stronger than the previous checkpoint:

1. **Repeated greedy acceptance is itself a failure mode**: PACE measures 30–42% false commits and 10–33% harmful commits in a prompt self-evolution loop; with no real gain, false commits can reach 72–100% of commits and cause measurable final degradation.
2. **Single-run evaluation can create phantom improvements at very high rates**: GSME reports ~60% false wins for a truly neutral mechanism under a single-run gate, ~25% appearing >=3 pp better.
3. **Semantic proposal and durable credit should be split**: GSME lets the LLM be wrong about diagnosis while deterministic/paired measurement prevents bad persistence.
4. **QD mechanism engagement does not imply endpoint superiority**: Loreley visibly uses non-incumbent stepping stones yet does not establish a 48-job QD advantage over matched sequential/root controls.
5. **A direct controlled-noise replay is now concretely feasible** on an open repository-evolution scaffold with fixed candidate budgets, durable ancestry and external evaluators.

A stronger provisional population controller is therefore:

`generate/diagnose freely -> cheap navigation score -> paired/repeated evidence on the same instances -> anytime-valid or significance-controlled acceptor -> activation/provenance gate -> reversible standby while unresolved -> durable archive commit only after credit gate -> sealed or independently protected holdout for system-level claims`.

## Rejected / downgraded hypotheses

- `The direct 0–50% judge-output noise sweep inside evolutionary/QD LLM search has now been found` — still rejected. PACE and GSME directly measure noisy self-evolution acceptance failure, but neither injects a controlled judge-flip sweep.
- `If QD ancestry visibly uses stepping stones, QD must improve final quality` — rejected by Loreley's matched Zstandard experiment at 48 jobs.
- `A single noisy dev improvement is acceptable if many candidates are tried` — strongly rejected; repeated adaptive testing makes the problem worse, not better.
- `LLM diagnosis needs to be trusted before persisting an edit` — rejected by GSME's propose/credit separation; diagnosis can remain a fallible search heuristic.
- `Matched candidate count alone proves compute parity` — downgraded; Loreley fixes physical candidate jobs, but concurrency, model usage and evaluation runtime can still differ.

## Frontier queue (nonempty)

1. **Direct controlled judge-output corruption inside evolutionary/QD LLM search:** inject 0/5/10/20/30/40/50% pairwise sign flips and separately correlated/systematic bias; report false archive writes, true-elite loss, endpoint regret and survival-decision error.
2. **PACE-style acceptor inside a population archive:** compare greedy replacement, fixed-k re-evaluation, paired significance, anytime-valid acceptance and conformal abstention under identical total evaluator budget.
3. **Loreley replay feasibility details:** identify the minimal public artifacts needed to replay a frozen candidate stream without rerunning generation; determine whether evaluator outputs/selection traces expose enough structure to inject synthetic acceptance noise offline.
4. **Global versus per-decision error budgets:** quantify how per-candidate alpha should shrink or be allocated across hundreds of adaptive archive updates.
5. **Stable systematic bias:** compare iid stochastic noise with low-variance correlated bias, because variance/consistency gates miss stable-wrong evaluators.
6. **QD benefit regime:** matched-budget study varying horizon, branching factor, evaluator noise and stepping-stone density; test when archive retention becomes worthwhile versus sequential champion editing.
7. **Reversible state policy:** combine Active/Standby/Terminated lineage states with evidence-controlled promotion/termination so uncertain candidates are not permanently discarded.
8. **Evaluator-cost allocation:** jointly optimize proposal count versus re-evaluation share rather than treating generation and verification budgets separately.
9. **Sealed-holdout discipline under continual evolution:** system-level evidence that avoids repeated peeking while still allowing online archive decisions.
