## C56 — correlation-aware promotion ablation

This is a **role-local synthetic experiment**, not an external empirical claim. Reproducibility script: `research_workers_clean_g1/multi_agent/SYNTHETIC_CORRELATION_AWARE_PROMOTION_2026-08-26_1000.py`.

The experiment preserves the C54 synthetic family: 16 archive cells, 1,200 evaluator calls, challenger-level within-evaluator lock probability `rho_A=rho_B=0.6`, A active-gate cap `3`, reversible standby reservoir, `alpha=0.20`, and paired seeds. Each table cell uses 150 paired replicates. Four timeout policies are compared:

1. `same`: keep replaying evaluator A.
2. `naive`: switch to B and multiply A/B likelihood ratios as if conditionally independent.
3. `discount`: switch to B but multiply B log-LR by the supplied dependence factor `1-cross_mix`.
4. `separate`: keep A/B evidence separate; B must independently confirm while A remains net-positive, otherwise abstain after a small B-call cap.

`effective_info_nats_per_call` is an explicit mechanistic **proxy**, not exact mutual information. It uses equal-prior Jeffreys information per evaluator; repeat-A/B calls are discounted by `1-rho`; only the first B call is discounted by `1-cross_mix` because this generator couples only the first A/B verdict.

Key result: when A/B first-verdict dependence is low, switching can improve final archive quality under the same call budget. But **dependence discounting by itself is not a controller**. At `cross_mix=1`, `discount` correctly assigns zero incremental weight to the first B evidence but still spends roughly 291.5 B calls; promotion nearly disappears, proposals fall, and archive quality collapses. Measuring dependence is therefore insufficient unless the measurement also changes *routing / abstention / verification allocation*.

The `separate` confirmation policy sharply lowers false authoritative promotion in the symmetric regime but loses too much proposal throughput and final quality under this budget. This is another direct instance of the earlier search-starvation effect: safer local decisions can worsen the global search outcome if confirmation is too costly.

## C57 — public dependence-aware aggregation evidence

A directly relevant public result appeared in Balasubramanian, Podkopaev & Kasiviswanathan, **“Dependence-Aware Label Aggregation for LLM-as-a-Judge via Ising Models”** (arXiv:2601.22336, 2026). The paper distinguishes class-independent from class-dependent judge coupling. Under class-independent coupling, dependence can be absorbed into correlation-adjusted linear weights. Under class-dependent coupling, the Bayes log-odds is generally **quadratic in the votes**, and conditional-independence aggregation can be confidently wrong even when per-judge marginals are correct. The authors prove nonvanishing separation from CI predictors and report improvements over classical baselines on three real evaluation tasks. Primary source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22336`.

This external result is not evidence that the current synthetic generator is literally Ising-distributed. It does, however, independently support the narrower controller lesson from C56: **a scalar dependence discount cannot in general repair class-conditional dependence; the aggregation rule may need interaction terms or an explicitly joint likelihood.**

## Controller implication

The current evidence now separates three distinct problems that should not be collapsed into a single `judge quality` scalar:

1. **Marginal reliability:** `q_FA` and `q_FR` separately.
2. **Dependence / redundancy:** how much incremental evidence evaluator B adds beyond A.
3. **Decision-rule adequacy:** whether the aggregation rule models the joint class-conditional vote pattern rather than merely discounting one evaluator.

For weak symmetric evaluators, dependence-aware routing can improve value per call. For class-asymmetric evaluators, C56 shows that even materially higher effective information can fail to improve the archive under a symmetric LR controller. That makes decision-rule structure, not only evaluator selection, the next bottleneck.

## Scope and limitations

- C56 is synthetic and does not estimate real LLM judge error rates or universal thresholds.
- `cross_mix` only couples the first A/B verdict; later unlocked B calls can add information, so `cross_mix=1` does not mean the entire B trajectory is identical to A.
- `effective_info_nats_per_call` is a transparent mechanistic proxy, not exact mutual information or calibrated effective sample size.
- `discount` intentionally changes evidence weighting but not B-call routing. Its high-dependence collapse therefore demonstrates a routing failure, not a theorem that correlation discounting is intrinsically harmful.
- `separate` is one conservative confirmation policy; its throughput loss does not reject all abstention/confirmation schemes.
- The external Ising paper concerns label aggregation, not archive search; the transfer here is a hypothesis about decision-rule structure, not a proven archive-level effect.

## Frontier (nonempty)

1. Replace marginal LR multiplication/discounting with an **explicit joint A/B likelihood** using the known synthetic `P(v_A,v_B|better)` and `P(v_A,v_B|not better)`. Compare it against `naive`, `discount`, and `separate` under the same 1,200-call budget.
2. Add a class-dependent interaction term (Ising/QDA analogue) and test whether it recovers value from the asymmetric `q_FA=0.40/q_FR=0.20` regime where C56's scalar rules fail.
3. Use dependence estimates to control **routing**, not only evidence weight: skip or abstain from B when expected incremental information per call is below the proposal-value opportunity cost.
4. Estimate system-level marginal value per verification call: expected reduction in false authoritative promotion plus expected archive-quality gain, divided by calls displaced from proposal generation.
5. Search for real LLM-judge experiments that fit class-dependent dependence models or route verification using calibrated joint error structure, especially under asymmetric false-positive/false-negative costs.
