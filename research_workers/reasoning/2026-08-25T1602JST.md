## Findings

### R-20260825-01 — Exact verifier diagnostics + trained sequential repair
Goedel-Prover-V2 trains self-correction on Lean compiler feedback and uses checkpoint averaging for accuracy/diversity. ICLR 2026 reports 32B at 88.1% MiniF2F pass@32 standard versus 90.4% with self-correction; 8B reaches 84.6%. The 32B model solves 86 PutnamBench problems at pass@184 versus 47 for DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B at pass@1024. Reported ablations show self-correction adds about 2 points on MiniF2F and 14 PutnamBench solves at pass@32 (43→57); removing specific compiler diagnostics hurts, while removing prior CoT has little effect. A model not trained for revision gains only 75.8→76.2 under the same correction mode. Scope: Lean formal proof generation.
Sources: https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/13e8be77982beb73d7ed0bbf122f9f3c-Abstract-Conference.html ; https://github.com/Goedel-LM/Goedel-Prover-V2
Transfer hypothesis: allocate verifier budget to learned repair trajectories rather than only independent restarts; preserve diversity explicitly.

### R-20260825-02 — Structural diversity beats prompt paraphrase for RL provers
On MiniF2F with DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL, iid k=32 and k=64 both solve 42/244. A fixed schedule of 15 tactic skeletons gives a reported +45% relative improvement at k=16, mean Δ=+12.3±4.2 theorems across three seeds. Controlled ablation: tactic skeletons help, paraphrased instructions match baseline, irrelevant Lean comments degrade. The effect is regime-specific: V1.5-Base proves zero either way; RL-trained DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B gains frontier solves, while an SFT-trained Goedel prover is reported to lose performance. Public code has pinned Lean setup, deterministic config hashes, append-only logs, and rerun scripts.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16172 ; https://github.com/zacn04/icml_hints_for_provers
Transfer hypothesis: spend test-time budget on semantically distinct proof/program structures, not synonymous prompts or iid samples, especially after RLVR.

### R-20260825-03 — Learn coordinated pass@K diversity
Coordinated Pass@K Policy Optimization (CPPO) trains a planner to emit K distinct approaches and a shared solver to execute one attempt per plan, rewarding verifier-confirmed outcomes rather than textual difference. Across APPS, CodeContests, and LiveCodeBench-v6 it reports gains over direct sampling and pass@K baselines at K=4. Largest reported cell: Qwen3.5-9B LiveCodeBench-v6, PKPO 0.588 pass@4 versus CPPO 0.748 (+0.160, paired-bootstrap significance reported). Planner-only SFT is mixed; the full joint planner-solver update improves over warm-up in every reported model–dataset cell.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27000
Transfer hypothesis: optimize diversity for complementary verifier-successful branches.

### R-20260825-04 — Dense feedback distillation can preserve exploration before/with RLVR
Feedback Distillation conditions an EMA teacher on failed attempts plus Lean compiler output and distills token-level distributions, then can apply GRPO. The May 2026 paper reports Qwen3.5-9B LeanWorkbook test pass@1 of 59% after GRPO alone versus 75% with FD+GRPO; it reports higher entropy, more unique tactics/Mathlib lemmas, and better pass@k scaling. Reported 8×H200 step times are about 300 s FD versus 550 s GRPO. Caveat: an external high-quality feedback model is part of the intervention.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30861
Transfer hypothesis: convert rich verifier/critic traces into dense supervision to reduce sparse-reward mode collapse.

### R-20260825-05 — Active discriminative tests improve program selection
S* sequentially debugs generated programs using execution feedback, then synthesizes inputs intended to distinguish pairs of surviving candidates and compares actual execution. It reports DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B + S* at 85.7% LiveCodeBench, GPT-4o-mini + S* beating o1-preview by 3.7 points, and Qwen2.5-7B + S* outperforming Qwen2.5-32B by 10.7 points in the paper's setup. Ablations attribute gains to sequential debugging and execution-grounded adaptive selection.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14382
Transfer hypothesis: use verifier budget to search for informative counterexamples/tests that separate candidates, not only terminal scoring.

### R-20260825-06 — Verified-data/curriculum engineering can substitute for brute scale
Pythagoras-Prover combines Lean-verified seed data, difficulty curriculum, failure-mode-conditioned distillation, dynamic proof/reasoning filtering, Lean-reward RL, and structured ALF mutations. June 2026 reports Pythagoras-4B at 86.1% MiniF2F pass@32 and 32B at 89.8% pass@32 / 92.62% pass@1024, with 93 PutnamBench solves at pass@2048. Reported matched ablations attribute +3 solved MiniF2F instances for 4B and +2 for 32B to dynamic proof/reasoning filtering. On MiniF2F-ALF, all models degrade; Pythagoras-32B is reported at 85.04% pass@32. Caveat: ALF training mutations are alignment-filtered but not all independently Lean-verified.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12594 ; https://github.com/Pythagoras-LM/Pythagoras-Prover
Transfer hypothesis: improve task distribution and verified-data efficiency before buying more inference samples.

## Follow-up frontier discoveries

### R-20260825-07 — OProver closes part of the repair/retrieval/training-loop composite
A follow-up search found OProver (May 2026), which iteratively revises failed Lean proofs using both retrieved compiler-verified proofs and Lean compiler feedback. Its post-training loop feeds successful repair trajectories back into SFT and unresolved hard cases into RL. The paper reports a corpus of 1.77M Lean statements and 6.86M compiler-verified proofs, and OProver-32B reports pass@32 of 93.3% MiniF2F, 58.2% ProverBench, and 11.3% PutnamBench, with second-place results on MathOlympiad and ProofNet among its comparison set. This is closer than Goedel alone to an agentic repair-memory loop, but it still does not establish the structural-diversity component under matched budget.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17283
Transfer hypothesis: successful repair trajectories can be recycled into the policy while hard unresolved cases become targeted RL curriculum; retrieval memory may reduce repeated failure modes.

### R-20260825-08 — Joint program-and-proof planning reduces downstream brittle repair
P3 (August 2026) plans the executable program and proof scaffold together before elaboration, rather than generating code first and proving it afterward. Across Verina, AlgoVeri, and Lean4Commit0 with four frontier backends, the paper reports the highest solve rate in every benchmark-model setting, +4.6 to +11.2 percentage points over the stronger baseline, up to roughly 40% lower API cost and 37% lower wall-clock time on difficult subsets. A targeted ablation reports +3.3 to +8.3 points over implementation-only planning, isolating joint program-proof planning. This complements repair-based systems by preventing some structurally hard-to-prove programs before repair is needed.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09277
Transfer hypothesis: search over proof-aware designs early; downstream verifier repair should be a second line, not the sole mechanism.

### R-20260825-09 — Lean itself can provide process-level RL credit, not only terminal reward
Process-Verified Reinforcement Learning for Theorem Proving via Lean (ICLR 2026) parses proof attempts into tactic sequences and uses Lean elaboration to mark locally sound tactics and the earliest failing step. It then propagates dense verifier-grounded process credit in a GRPO-style objective. The paper reports tactic-level supervision outperforming outcome-only baselines in most tested settings on MiniF2F and ProofNet. Exact effect sizes need extraction in a later pass.
Source: https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/554a5982e26d0129f4038ad7a68fb3aa-Abstract-Conference.html
Transfer hypothesis: replace some sparse binary RLVR credit with proof-assistant-derived local credit while preserving formal soundness.

### R-20260825-10 — Dedicated repair supervision is emerging as a separate training axis
APRIL (February 2026) frames Lean proof repair as supervised learning over erroneous proof + compiler diagnostics → corrected proof + grounded diagnosis, with 260,000 tuples generated from systematic failures. The paper reports a fine-tuned 4B model outperforming the strongest open-source baseline in its single-shot repair evaluation. Exact matched metrics and public training code remain a frontier item.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02990
Transfer hypothesis: treat diagnostic interpretation/repair as an explicitly trained skill, then combine it with broader theorem-proving RL rather than assuming a general prover will learn repair incidentally.

## Synthesis
The repeated mechanism is better allocation of verifier-coupled compute, not simply more compute: proof-aware or diverse high-level plans → verifier-executable branches → exact diagnostic repair → active distinguishing tests → diversity-preserving/process-verified learning → recycling successful repairs into training memory. No cited source establishes this full composite; it is a testable synthesis hypothesis.

## Rejected / deferred leads
- Generic prompt paraphrasing as diversity: rejected as primary mechanism for the cited RL Lean setup.
- Compiler feedback without repair training: insufficient in the Goedel revision ablation.
- Blind iid pass@k scaling: poor default when solved-set growth plateaus.
- Diffusion theorem proving as primary accuracy path: deferred; higher throughput but substantially lower pass@32 in the current Pythagoras comparison.
- Very-large TTRL sampling as first lever: deferred because reported redundancy/compute make repair and structural diversity more attractive for efficiency tests.

## Evaluation cautions
- Formal-statement benchmark pass rates are not end-to-end mathematical reasoning; autoformalisation and benchmark fidelity remain separate failure modes.
- Keep pass@1 and pass@k separate; RL/checkpoint choices can improve one while flattening the other.
- Compare iid sampling versus repair/diversity under matched model, generated-token, verifier-call, and wall-clock budgets.
- Scope negative evidence to the tested training regime/model.
- OProver/P3 headline results add mechanisms but do not yet answer the key matched-budget question versus fixed structural diversity + repair.
