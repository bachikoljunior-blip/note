## New evidence

### SA-20260825-07 — AutoResearchClaw component ablation: debate improves quality, self-healing improves completion, verification prevents fabricated evidence

**Primary source.** Liu et al., *AutoResearchClaw: Self-Reinforcing Autonomous Research with Human-AI Collaboration*, arXiv:2605.20025, component-ablation Table 5. Same 10 ARC-Bench topics; each configuration-topic pair rerun three times, with best result retained for the table.

**Exact results.**

| Configuration | Completion | Mean quality | Accept | Fabrication |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Full AutoResearchClaw | 10/10 | 5.62 | 3/10 | No |
| w/o Debate | 10/10 | 4.25 | 1/10 | No |
| w/o Self-Healing | 6/10 | 4.83 | 1/6 | No |
| w/o Evolution | 9/10 | 5.14 | 2/10 | No |
| w/o Verification | 10/10 | 5.48 | 5/10 apparent | **Yes** |
| w/o Debate & Self-Healing | 4/10 | 3.47 | 0/4 | No |

The paper reports debate removal as the largest quality loss (**−1.37**, p=0.003); self-healing removal as the largest completion loss (10/10 → 6/10); evolution removal as a moderate loss (**−0.48 quality**, and one fewer completed topic). Removing both debate and self-healing is worse than either single ablation, with completion falling to 4/10 and quality to 3.47, described by the authors as super-additive degradation.

The verification ablation is especially important: apparent acceptance rises to 5/10, but manual inspection found that **3 of those 5 accepted papers contained numerical values absent from any measurement record**. Thus an evaluator that only rewards surface paper quality can perversely favor a system with weaker evidence integrity.

**Mechanism implication.** Research-agent architecture should separate at least three functions instead of treating “reflection” as one undifferentiated loop:
1. **adversarial critique/debate** for proposal quality;
2. **execution self-healing** for recovery/completion;
3. **claim-to-measurement verification** as a fail-closed integrity gate.
The metrics should remain separate too: completion, scientific quality, and evidence integrity can move in opposite directions.

**Scope / confidence.** This is a recent preprint using the authors' own ARC-Bench and LLM-assisted judging; the ablation is controlled but not independent validation. The best-of-three selection also differs from a single-trajectory deployment regime. Treat the exact magnitudes as benchmark-local.

### SA-20260825-08 — Independent peer-reviewed corroboration: reflection/self-correction is a dominant execution module in scientific data-analysis agents

**Primary source.** Yang Liu et al., *Benchmarking LLM-based agents for single-cell omics analysis*, **Genome Biology 27, 123 (2026)**, published 25 Feb 2026, https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-03998-z . The study uses **50 real-world single-cell omics analysis tasks**, three agent frameworks (ReAct, LangGraph, AutoGEN), eight LLMs, and 18 evaluation metrics.

For functional attribution, the authors used Grok3-beta and ablated AutoGEN's retrieval, explicit planning, reflection/self-correction, and workflow-control modules; ReAct was ablated for retrieval and planning where structurally applicable.

**Quantitative / controlled findings.**
- Removing retrieval reduced AutoGEN task-success rate by **0.14** and total score by **0.054**; ReAct fell by **0.26** task success and **0.113** total score.
- Removing explicit planning increased interaction and self-correction rounds substantially in the multi-agent framework; planning's effect on final performance was framework-dependent.
- Removing reflection disabled correction after execution errors and caused a **significant performance decline**; the paper identifies self-reflection as the **largest overall functional contributor** among tested modules. Even relatively simple `scvi` and `harmony` tasks frequently failed without it.
- Removing explicit inter-agent speaking-order control did **not** change final task success and marginally increased overall score by about **0.011**. Well-specified roles/prompts could self-organize without rigid turn ordering.
- In the broader benchmark, ReAct required **2–3× more interaction/correction rounds** than the multi-agent frameworks but had **12–18% higher retrieval accuracy**, illustrating a coordination-efficiency/knowledge-fusion tradeoff.
- Across 24 framework×LLM configurations, code-generation quality correlated more strongly with task completion than plan quality; an example cited in the paper has AutoGEN+DeepSeek-R1 matching top models on plan score while having **66% lower task success**.

**Cross-study synthesis.** This is an independent, peer-reviewed corroboration for the self-healing result from AutoResearchClaw: scientific agents need an execution-time repair loop, not merely good initial planning. It also constrains the interpretation of “multi-agent orchestration”: rigid speaker scheduling itself may contribute little once roles are clear. The stronger transferable mechanism is **specialized roles + explicit planning where architecture benefits + executable feedback + bounded repair**, rather than multi-agent count or turn-order machinery per se.

**Scope.** Genome Biology evaluates computational single-cell analysis, not hypothesis-to-paper autonomous research. It independently validates the execution/self-correction mechanism, not AutoResearchClaw's debate or claim-verification result.
