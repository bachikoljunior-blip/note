## Candidate NS-001 — synaptic consolidation / metaplasticity -> EWC

**Status:** high-confidence direct neuroscience-to-AI transfer; useful but scope-limited.

Biological evidence is stronger than a loose analogy. Long-lived dendritic spines correlate with persistent memories (Yang, Pan & Gan, Nature 2009); task-specific dendritic plasticity can be causally disrupted by selectively reversing potentiated spines (Hayashi-Takagi et al., Nature 2015); and different motor tasks can recruit branch-specific dendritic Ca2+ events whose disruption increases interference (Cichon & Gan, Nature 2015).

Kirkpatrick et al. explicitly translate this stability/plasticity idea into **elastic weight consolidation (EWC)**: parameters estimated to matter for previous tasks are made less plastic. On sequential permuted-MNIST, plain SGD catastrophically forgets while EWC scales to many permutations. In the Atari experiment, 10 games were sampled from a pool of 19; plain gradient descent stayed below a total clipped human-normalized score of 1 because it did not retain more than one game at a time, whereas EWC retained multiple games. The experiment used 10 game sets and 4 seeds. EWC still did not reach the aggregate performance of ten separately trained DQNs.

**Scope/counterevidence:** This is an algorithmic abstraction of synaptic consolidation, not a literal dendritic model. Later class-incremental benchmarks show parameter-protection alone can fail badly where task identity is unavailable; replay complements it.

Sources:
- Kirkpatrick et al. 2017, PNAS, doi: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611835114 ; preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796
- Yang, Pan & Gan 2009, Nature, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08577
- Cichon & Gan 2015, Nature, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14251
- Hayashi-Takagi et al. 2015, Nature, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15257

## Candidate NS-002 — hippocampal/cortical replay -> internal generative replay

**Status:** high-confidence direct neuroscience-to-AI transfer; strongest current candidate in this run.

Primary neurophysiology shows experience-dependent reactivation: Wilson & McNaughton (1994) found place-cell ensembles that co-fired during behavior showed elevated co-firing during subsequent slow-wave sleep; Foster & Wilson (2006) found rapid reverse replay immediately after spatial experience, with a strong novelty effect; Ji & Wilson (2007) found coordinated replay of corresponding experience sequences in visual cortex and hippocampus during slow-wave sleep.

van de Ven, Siegelmann & Tolias (2020) explicitly build **brain-inspired replay (BI-R)** around this principle, replaying internal/hidden representations via feedback rather than storing raw examples. The mechanism remains competitive even with only 1 replayed sample for every 128 current samples in their tested setting. Their ablation identifies internal replay as the most influential component. On 100-permutation MNIST, BI-R+SI with replay-through-feedback reported 0.904 ± 0.005 versus 0.892 ± 0.004 when that component was ablated; on class-incremental CIFAR-100, 0.344 ± 0.002 versus 0.334 ± 0.002. The paper also shows replay and parameter-stability methods are complementary: replay preserves prior behavior in function/output space while SI/EWC constrain parameter space.

**Scope/counterevidence:** The CIFAR-100 continual-learning setup froze five convolutional layers that had been pre-trained on CIFAR-10, so the result must not be generalized to fully end-to-end continual vision without verification. Standard generative replay also degrades over long task sequences; the internal-replay modifications matter.

Sources:
- Wilson & McNaughton 1994, Science, doi: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8036517
- Foster & Wilson 2006, Nature, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04587
- Ji & Wilson 2007, Nature Neuroscience, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1825
- van de Ven, Siegelmann & Tolias 2020, Nature Communications, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17866-2

## Candidate NS-003 — entorhinal grid code -> vector navigation / path integration

**Status:** medium-high transfer evidence; narrow domain and biological-fidelity dispute.

Hafting et al. (2005) established medial entorhinal grid cells with repeating triangular spatial fields and systematic spacing/phase properties, supporting a metric/path-integration interpretation. Banino et al. (2018) trained a recurrent network for path integration; grid-like internal representations emerged and supported reinforcement-learning navigation, shortcut behavior, and performance above comparison agents and an expert human in the tested tasks. An author thesis reports mean path-integration error after a 15 s trajectory of 16 cm versus 91 cm for an untrained network; treat this specific number as author-side secondary evidence rather than the core Nature article.

**Scope/counterevidence:** Later analyses question whether all learned units satisfy strong biological grid-cell criteria and report failures of pattern continuation outside training arenas. Keep the behavioral navigation gain separate from the claim that the hidden units are faithful biological grid cells.

Sources:
- Hafting et al. 2005, Nature, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03721
- Banino et al. 2018, Nature, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0102-6

## Candidate NS-004 — predictive coding -> predictive networks / alternative learning dynamics

**Status:** medium evidence for mechanistic biological equivalence; strong enough to keep as an AI-transfer candidate.

Rao & Ballard's hierarchical predictive-coding model reproduced multiple V1-like response properties after training on natural images. Egner, Monti & Summerfield (2010) tested predictive-coding-style expectation effects in human FFA: the predictive-coding model fit better than the feature-detection alternative (log-likelihood -3.37 vs -8.62; likelihood-ratio chi-square 10.51, p<0.002), and unexpected-face versus unexpected-house responses were negatively correlated across subjects (r=-0.72, p<0.002).

AI transfers are measurable. PredNet, explicitly inspired by predictive coding, reported on rotating faces MSE/SSIM 0.0152/0.937 for its L0 objective, versus 0.0180/0.907 for a CNN-LSTM encoder-decoder and 0.125/0.631 for copy-last-frame. Lee et al. (2022) replaced backprop-style optimization with predictive-coding learning on several machine-challenging tasks. On split-MNIST, their reported five-seed average was 98.80% for PC-SGD versus 92.47% for BP-SGD. Reimplementing EWC with predictive coding improved their average from 95.64% to 97.52%; IMM-MEAN improved by 0.21 percentage points and IMM-MODE by 5.42 points.

**Scope/counterevidence:** Evidence for predictive processing in cortex does not establish that the exact optimization algorithm used in these ANN papers is the brain's learning rule. Treat the neuroscience link as hypothesis-supported, not settled mechanism. Large-scale modern comparisons against optimized backprop remain an open frontier.

Sources:
- Rao & Ballard 1999, Nature Neuroscience, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/4580
- Egner, Monti & Summerfield 2010, Journal of Neuroscience, doi: https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2770-10.2010
- Lotter, Kreiman & Cox, PredNet preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08104
- Lee et al. 2022, Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience, doi: https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2022.1062678

## Candidate NS-005 — complementary corticohippocampal representations -> CH-HNN

**Status:** strong recent engineering result; medium mechanistic confidence because several useful components are engineered abstractions.

Human and animal work supports complementary memory representations: prototype/abstract information has been observed in ventromedial prefrontal cortex and anterior hippocampus during concept generalization, while related mouse work finds more abstract/general task representations in mPFC and more task-specific representations in dorsal CA1 across structurally related problems.

Shi et al. (2025) translate this into a hybrid ANN+SNN system with generalized versus episode-specific representations, episode inference, feedback, and metaplasticity. Reported task-incremental sCIFAR-100 inter-episode disparity was 17.32% versus 48.76% for XdG. A pMNIST lesion analysis showed metaplasticity reduced inter-episode disparity from 29.77% to 12.53%; episode inference alone yielded mean accuracy 70.41%. In class-incremental sTiny-ImageNet, episode inference alone reached 70.70% and the full CH-HNN 70.72%, showing metaplasticity contributed little in that particular configuration. A robot robustness evaluation reported 82% ± 7.25% over 30 trials under varied positions/angles.

**Scope/counterevidence:** Some task similarities are manually specified, and class-incremental image similarity uses CLIP features; therefore the full gain cannot be attributed solely to the biological circuit abstraction. Independent replication is needed.

Sources:
- Shi et al. 2025, Nature Communications, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56405-9
- Bowman & Zeithamova 2018, Journal of Neuroscience, title: “Abstract Memory Representations in Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex and Hippocampus Support Concept Generalization”

## Candidate NS-006 — dendritic structured computation / restricted sampling -> parameter-efficient dANN

**Status:** promising direct transfer, now better grounded after follow-up; biological-to-architecture mapping is still only partial.

Chavlis & Poirazi (2025) build dendritic ANNs whose inputs are partially sampled into nonlinear dendritic units and whose dendrite-to-soma connectivity is sparse and structured. Across image-classification tasks they report matching or outperforming conventional ANNs with roughly 1–3 orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters, plus better overfitting/noise/sequential-learning robustness in tested configurations. Their local-receptive-field variant is especially parameter-efficient.

A follow-up trace in this run improved the biological grounding. The paper states that restricted input sampling is inspired by visual cortical receptive fields, while sparse one-soma-per-dendrite structure loosely mimics neuronal morphology. Wilson et al. (2016) used in-vivo two-photon calcium imaging of dendritic spines in ferret V1 and found spatial/functional clustering of orientation-tuned synaptic inputs. Park et al. (2019) reports that distinct L2/3 dendritic trees arborize in different cortical map regions/layers and sample largely non-overlapping inputs; causal 2-photon ablation further showed orientation tuning remains robust to large losses of dendritic input. In that experiment, 13/33 neurons survived single basal-dendrite ablation and 17/50 survived double ablation; removing the whole apical tuft did not consistently shift orientation preference, and two basal dendrites were needed to produce only a small shift. This supports structured, redundant dendritic sampling, although it does not uniquely imply the exact dANN wiring scheme.

**Scope/counterevidence:** Chavlis & Poirazi explicitly describe the architecture as *loosely* mimicking morphology/sampling. Visual receptive fields and dendritic redundancy support restricted/structured sampling generally, but are not causal evidence that the paper's exact sparse graph is optimal. Independent replication and architecture-matched controls remain necessary.

Sources:
- Chavlis & Poirazi 2025, Nature Communications, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56297-9
- Wilson et al. 2016, Nature Neuroscience, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4323
- Park et al. 2019, Nature Communications, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13029-0
- Cichon & Gan 2015, Nature, doi: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14251

## Rejected / downgraded leads

- **Successor representation:** scientifically important bridge between hippocampal representations and reinforcement learning, but the direction of influence is not a clean neuroscience-to-AI transfer; computational SR substantially predates much of the neural interpretation.
- **Dopamine reward-prediction error -> temporal-difference learning:** directionality is largely computational-RL models explaining dopamine signals, not a clean neural discovery transferred into AI.
- **Generic experience replay / prioritized replay:** useful AI mechanisms, but direct provenance from hippocampal replay is weak. Prefer BI-R, whose neuroscience motivation is explicit and experimentally evaluated.
- **“Learned grid units are biological grid cells”:** too strong; retain navigation benefits while treating representational fidelity as disputed.
- **“Predictive coding is the established cortical learning algorithm”:** unsupported as stated; keep empirical predictive-processing evidence distinct from claims about exact optimization rules.
